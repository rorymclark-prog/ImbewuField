# Newcomer-flow audit — confirmed gaps (2026-07-18, wf_f5f1032d-026)

## Leg verdicts
### first-touch
app/page.tsx:6 blind-redirects everyone to /home (no separate landing/role-hub despite the file's own comment claiming one exists). /home itself is auth-free and correctly gates two full-screen modals in sequence for a first touch — language pick (components/Onboarding.tsx) then POPIA consent + goal picker (components/PopiaConsent.tsx) — but neither modal creates an account or ever mentions one will be needed. Once past them, HomeHeroCard (components/home/HomeHeroCard.tsx:97-140) correctly renders a clean two-choice WELCOME variant with one unmistakable primary CTA ("Find your land") and the zero-state hiding is done right: FarmPlanCard returns null with no saved places (app/home/page.tsx:224-225) and TaskBoardCard only renders when boardTasks.length>0 (app/home/page.tsx:424), so no progress bar or task list clutters a brand-new screen, and the Dashboards role-picker stays collapsed by default (app/home/page.tsx:272). The critical flaw: that primary CTA, the persistent bottom-tab "Map" icon (components/TabBar.tsx:10), and the "My records" quick action all point at /farmer, which silently hard-redirects an unauthenticated visitor to /login (app/farmer/page.tsx:278-288, 339) — a wall nothing upstream warned them about, with zero sign-in-status indicator anywhere in the home screen's header, tab bar, or nav drawer chrome until the moment they hit it. Compounding this, the six-tile Quick Actions grid mixes three different unauthenticated behaviors on visually identical tiles: Finances shows a soft in-page SignInPrompt, Contact/Student hard-redirect to /login, and Journal/Crop-planner are fully open with no auth check at all. There are also two redundant "see an example" entry points live on the same screen simultaneously — the hero's small "See example" link (→ /example, a static read-only demo) and the dashed "Explore the sample farm" card (→ enters an interactive sandbox and routes through /farmer) — which promise the same thing but behave very differently. Net verdict: the zero-state layout itself is well-designed and appropriately quiet, but the one thing the whole onboarding narrative points a newcomer toward throws them into an unexplained authentication wall, which for first-time smartphone users is the leg's most damaging gap.

### survey-to-design
LEG 3 verdict: the "survey → design" ordering is hollow in the shipped product, and the design wizard is a strong middle with a weak ending. The ONLY code that turns survey answers into design guidance — generateGeometryDesignPlan (lib/design-studio.ts:511, loadSurvey at :536) — is called solely by components/GeometryDesignStudio.tsx:2886, an orphaned component no route renders. The live wizard (app/design/page.tsx) and everything it uses (DesignWizard, StepGuide, suggestZones, DesignAdvisor, TankCalculator) never read the survey, so filling the survey first does NOT make the design smarter; the survey's real payoff is the Report (ReportView.tsx:302 → report-doc.ts) and progress scoring. The wizard itself is otherwise good for a newcomer: clean sequential step order and every design step explains WHY via STEP_GUIDANCE + DESIGN_STEP_LESSONS + StepGuide substep checklists. But the flow does not close its loop: on the final glossy step StepGuide is hidden (app/design/page.tsx:1337), Lima is hidden (:1307), DesignGlossy only points to Print, there is no bottom nav, and the sole exit is a back-arrow to /farmer — so a finished designer is never guided on to crops, finances, or the report the owner explicitly wants them to reach. The single crops handoff ("Plan my crops") lives mid-wizard on the planting step and only appears once beds exist (StepGuide.tsx:136), so a newcomer who reaches the end finds it only by luck. Guided Next is also never gated on content, letting a newcomer click through to an empty glossy render with no nudge. Net: survey-before-design is not logically supported by the code, and design-then-report has no in-flow bridge.

### sheets-to-crops
The Print/Export entry point itself is solid: a header 'Print' button (app/design/page.tsx:1086-1109) is visible on every Design Studio step, including Glossy, and opens DesignPrint with a clear 8-sheet picker, live preview, and PDF/PNG/WhatsApp-share actions — a newcomer generating sheets on Glossy will find and use it without guesswork. Everything downstream of that is broken. Neither DesignGlossy.tsx nor DesignPrint.tsx contains a single mention of crops — after generating sheets or printing/sharing, the farmer is simply returned to the Glossy screen with no forward nudge, and the Design Wizard's own 'Next' button is dead (Glossy is STEP_ORDER's last entry, so canNext is false). The one working design-to-crops bridge (lib/design-beds-bridge.ts, wired via StepGuide's planCropsHref) only appears on the earlier 'planting' step and is invisible by the time the farmer reaches Glossy/Print. Far worse: every OTHER entry point into the crop planner — the /home 'Your farm plan' progress card's cropPlan action, the Task Board 'View Plan' link, the /home quick-action tile, NavDrawer, /cropplan, and /plan — links to bare `/facilitator/crops` with no `?canvasSite` param, and the crop planner's bed source (app/facilitator/crops/page.tsx:510-513) only reads Design Studio beds when that param is present; otherwise it falls back to the legacy Firestore/local facilitator design, which is empty for anyone who built their farm entirely in the new Design Studio. A farmer following the intended canonical journey who taps the progress card's 'Plan your crops' link lands on an EmptyState reading 'No beds designed yet... Place veg beds on the Planting step first' with a 'Back to design' button pointing at `/facilitator` — the OLD Konva canvas, a different product from `/design` — actively misleading them into thinking their work vanished. The fix is cheap and mechanical: every caller already has (or can trivially derive) the site's lat/lon, and the exact `site:${lat.toFixed(5)},${lon.toFixed(5)}` format the crops page already parses is used one call site away (app/design/page.tsx:1348, lib/design-studio.ts:131-134) — it just needs to be threaded through the other five/six links, and the two 'Back to design' links (app/facilitator/crops/page.tsx:740, :1234) need to point at /design instead of /facilitator.

### crops-to-report
Leg 5 (crops → finances → report) is discoverable-but-disconnected, not blocked-but-unguided. Finances is easy to find — /finances is a permanent TabBar entry (components/TabBar.tsx:11) and a home quick-action (app/home/page.tsx:313) — and it does connect back to the crop plan via HarvestReconciliation (loadCropPlan + bedsFromDesign). But nothing in the app's guidance layer ever points a farmer there or onward: the canonical 5-stage completion score (lib/completion-score.ts:7-12) ends at cropPlan with no finance/report stage, so both 'next step' widgets that read it run out of things to say exactly at the moment a real farmer would start selling produce. NextStepCoach's own copy says 'come back to log harvests and journal' but its only button just retires guided mode (components/NextStepCoach.tsx:154-160); home's FarmPlanCard instead sends a fully-planned farmer backward to reprint the design plan set (app/home/page.tsx:254-262) — the two guidance surfaces even disagree with each other. The 'report' side is worse: there are three separate, unlinked report implementations (ReportView's AI climate narrative, ReportDocView's 11-section 'north-star' structured report buried in a collapsed beta toggle inside the Design Studio, and MyRecords' own mini sales log duplicating /finances), none of which pulls in the farmer's actual recorded sales/expenses/invoices — grep confirms zero references to sales/expense/invoice data in either report engine. There is no dedicated /report route, no link to any report from /home, /finances, or the NGO/Funder dashboards (both explicitly 'demo data' placeholders), and nowhere does the app say 'your report is ready — show this to your funder.' A newcomer who diligently finishes the crop plan is left with no next signal; they would only discover finances and the report by independently exploring the tab bar and DataPanel's Reports tab, and even then the report they generate won't reflect the income they've actually logged.

### ordering-truth
Verdict on the owner's question: the app's encoded order — located → boundary → survey → design → cropPlan, then report (lib/completion-score.ts:7-12) — is objectively RIGHT, and the proposed "design maps THEN survey THEN report" is worse. The survey captures the farmer's own site ground-truth (water sources, roof catchment, soil condition, existing crops/livestock, fencing, goals, household size — lib/site-survey.ts:5-50), and its live hard consumer is the north-star REPORT, which is told to "treat this as authoritative ground truth about the site" (app/api/generate-report/route.ts:213); it also drives the WaterBalance card (DataPanel.tsx:1017) and the progress score. The live /design wizard does NOT data-bind to the survey (the survey-driven plan generator generateGeometryDesignPlan lives in GeometryDesignStudio.tsx, which is mounted by no route = dead code), so survey-before-design is a "design informed by your own context" ordering rather than a hard block — but it is still correct, and designing first would leave the report's ground-truth empty until the farmer backtracks. So: KEEP survey before design; do not move it after. On the analysis maps: the decision-relevant auto site-analysis is already pre-design (DataPanel Overview tab + the sun/wind Sector canvas overlay), so "analysis before design" is substantially honoured — only the printable numbered 01 Site / 02 Sector SHEETS sit in the terminal Glossy step (DESIGN_SHEETS at DesignGlossy.tsx:194; 'glossy' is last in STEP_ORDER at DesignWizard.tsx:37), a minor inconsistency versus the report's own analysis-first section order (report-doc.ts:177-186). The real newcomer danger is not the abstract order but the wiring: the guided pull-through is pointed at the WRONG survey (a /survey route that writes a different store), and the canonical survey has no deep link at all, which is the one thing that actually gets a newcomer stuck.
