- The proposed Xitsonga contrast said “one plant” where the English says “one crop”; it was not released. Another proposed sentence narrowed “productive species” to food-producing species, so it stayed English. The care-free-sounding title was held English.
- A proposed Tshivenda genetics sentence used a word suggesting a strong variety rather than a stable variety, and could imply saving a desired combination instead of offspring retaining traits. It stayed English. A proposed batch-identity sentence dropped the meaning of each seed batch; selection/mentor and quiz wording stayed English too.

The existing Seeds L1 diagram was inspected. It draws a simplified contrast between five similar-looking plants and five varied plants. Its former alt text asserted identical offspring from one packet, which conflicts with the lesson's conditional genetics wording. The English alt and exact Sesotho/Tshivenda held source pairs now describe the illustration as simplified and state that offspring depend on variety and pollination. The image itself was not replaced.

These drafts do not establish that a Shangani-speaking participant understands standard written Xitsonga. Facilitators still need to test the language and explain retained English terms.
