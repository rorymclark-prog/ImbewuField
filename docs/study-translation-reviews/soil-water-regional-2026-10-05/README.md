# Soil Health and Water Harvesting regional slide drafts: review record (5 October 2026)

Six silent slide decks: **Soil Health** and **Water Harvesting** in Sesotho (South African orthography), Tshivenda,
and standard written Xitsonga (a provisional starting point for the Shangani-speaking group; no Shangani
comprehension is claimed). This batch worked on the ordinary English that the decks still showed. Everything here
is an **unreviewed machine draft**, and the exact English source is shown beside every draft. No fluent-speaker or
local-farming approval is claimed. Facilitators review the published drafts and send feedback afterwards. The
lesson text of these two modules is outside this batch, which changes only the six slide decks.

## Method

1. **Scope and locks.** Some deck passages are word for word the same as the current learner paragraph for the same
   exact English, and six are paragraphs the learner text keeps in English. Those passages were not changed, so a
   slide never contradicts its lesson. Existing course headings that the tests pin were not changed either: Water
   slides 2 and 23, Soil slides 19 and 20 in Sesotho and Tshivenda, and Xitsonga Soil slide 2.
2. **Drafting.** Twelve Claude Sonnet agents drafted by language, module and slide range, under one guide:
   - translate ordinary prose fully;
   - keep difficult technical terms in English inside translated sentences;
   - keep every number, species name, sequence and piece of geometry;
   - keep qualifications such as "may", "can", "only", "before" and "at least", and every prohibition;
   - add no farming guidance.
3. **Blind back-translation.** Separate Claude Sonnet agents (29) translated every draft back into English without
   seeing the English source.
4. **Independent checks.** Claude Sonnet checkers (29) compared each draft and its blind back-translation with the
   exact English. Each checker was told to check:
   - negatives and prohibitions;
   - "can" against "may";
   - contour geometry: a level line across the slope, points at the same height, at least three points, not
     downhill;
   - water safety: drinking water, wastewater, dams and spillways, keeping used water away from people and animals;
   - compost conditions: heat, time, and the materials kept out of a home heap.

   Claude Sonnet revised the flagged passages (12 revision agents), and the revisions were checked again. Claude
   Opus adjudicated what the checkers still disputed (6 agents). Claude Haiku listed recurring words that had more
   than one rendering (6 agents).
5. **Repairs and harmonisation.** Claude Opus repaired the six passages that still failed, over two rounds. Each
   repair was back-translated blind and checked by Claude Sonnet. A Claude Sonnet harmonisation round then gave
   recurring words one rendering per deck, following the wording that was already checked, for example diverter,
   first flush, jar, heap, design and wastewater. Its edits were back-translated blind and checked again.

   An edit was rejected if a checker found a major problem, if it replaced a checked regional rendering with bare
   English, or if it brought in a term that is not in that passage's English. The evidence file lists the
   rejections. An independent Claude Sonnet re-check covered all eight harmonised Xitsonga Water edits.
6. **Final word fixes.** A Claude Sonnet check compared recurring Tshivenda words with the live course. Claude Opus
   then made a few small edits:
   - the Tshivenda adviser noun: mueletshedzi, plural vhaeletshedzi, replacing mulaedza, which means message;
   - the Tshivenda word for advice: nyeletshedzo, replacing ndaela, which means instructions;
   - "modulu" for module in Tshivenda;
   - the deck's own Xitsonga word for jar in a Soil heading;
   - the English species name "maize" restored in brackets after the regional crop word in Sesotho and Tshivenda;
   - on Tshivenda Water slide 19, Tsivhudzo for "guidance", using the lesson's own sentence for the same English, and
     the lesson's word "hayani" (at home) for household water;
   - two Tshivenda spelling and grammar fixes: "nḓila" (way) with its accent mark on Water slide 21, and "halwo",
     which agrees with luvhondo (wall), on Water slide 12.

   Each of these edits was back-translated blind and checked independently by Claude Sonnet before it was applied.

   A last sweep compared every word in the changed passages with the live course in the same language: the lesson
   drafts, the manual and the other slide decks. It found words from another of the three languages that the blind
   back-translations and checks had passed, all in the Tshivenda decks: "pula" (rain in Sesotho), and "munyu" (salt),
   "thanki" (tank) and "tsakama" (moist) as Xitsonga writes them. It also found spellings and word forms that the
   course writes differently, such as "khichini" for the course's "khishini" (kitchen), and one Xitsonga meaning error
   that no checker had caught: "khathala" (be tired) where the English says to care for the compost heap. Claude Opus
   replaced these with the course's own words, or with the regular form of a course word, in 43 passages
   (29 Tshivenda, 6 Sesotho, 8 Xitsonga). The words that agree with a replaced noun changed with
   it, for example "nhulu wa compost" (compost heap) and "tiphayiphi ta mati" (water pipes) in Xitsonga. Each passage
   was back-translated blind and checked independently by Claude Sonnet before it was applied. The evidence file gives
   every change with its course source, and lists the words in these passages that the course has not used before.

   Then every slide sentence was matched with the lesson sentence for the same exact English. Where the two
   differed, the slide took the lesson's wording only if the lesson sentence was fully translated, used the same
   spelling conventions, and passed its own blind back-translation and independent check. Sesotho Water slide 19,
   paragraph 1 now uses its two lesson sentences. Xitsonga Soil slide 18, paragraph 3 was considered and kept: a
   blind reader could not recover "its surface" from the lesson's "vuandlalo", and the independent check found that
   the manual uses that word only for a measured area, the surface of open water and bulk, so the slide keeps
   "xiphemu xa yona xa le henhla" (its top part). Soil slides 5 and 14 do use "vuandlalo" for the soil surface (see
   "Look at these first"). The 23 sentences that still differ from the lesson are listed under "Slide and lesson
   wording" below.

   Then six Claude Sonnet auditors, one per deck, read all 245 changed passages again through six lenses: negatives,
   "may" against "can", timing, water construction, sanitation and safety. They reported 13 problems. Claude Opus
   repaired 11 of them in 10 passages. For example, a Tshivenda Soil heading named aloes ("zwikhopha") where the
   English says seed pods; the Sesotho and Tshivenda first-flush headings said the first water goes into the tank;
   and a Xitsonga washwater sentence paired "prevent" with a negative, so it could be read as requiring contact. The
   other 2 are explained under "Look at these first". Claude Opus also repaired 5 passages where the earlier
   independent checks had found a problem in words that an edit left unchanged, such as Sesotho "metsi a tswang
   dinapining" (water coming out of the nappies), now "metsi a ho hlatswa dinapi" (water from washing nappies), as
   in the Sesotho glossary. Each repair was back-translated blind and checked independently by Claude Sonnet before
   it was applied.

   After the audit, the repo's South African Sesotho spelling check, which the Food Forest and Plant Guilds slides
   already pass, was run on the Sesotho decks. It found Lesotho spellings in 11 passages that this batch had drafted,
   for example "letsoapo" (slope) where the course writes "letswapo" (42 uses in the lessons and manual, none of
   "letsoapo"), "joang" (how) for "jwang" and "Mocheso" (heat) for "Motjheso", as the lessons write it (11 uses; the
   manual still writes "mocheso", 33 uses). Claude Opus respelled them. One clause was reworded: Soil slide 19 paragraph
   3 said "seo o sa kholisehang ka sona" (what you are not sure about), and the course has no South African form of that
   verb, so it now says "seo o se nang bonnete ka sona" (what you have no certainty about), with the manual's word
   "bonnete". A blind reader missed its negative; "o se nang" is the negative of "o nang le" (you have), as in the
   manual's "mobu o se nang letho" (bare soil, literally soil that has nothing). Each change was back-translated blind
   and checked independently by Claude Sonnet. That check also found a wrong concord on Water slide 4 paragraph 2: "Ha e
   bontshe" (it does not show) pointed at the swale instead of the picture ("setshwantsho", whose concord is "se"), so
   Claude Opus changed it to "Ha se bontshe", which was back-translated blind and checked in the same way. Eleven older
   passages that this batch did not change fail the spelling check and keep Lesotho spellings. Three more older passages
   use the Lesotho second-person "u", which the check does not test; none of this batch's drafts does (see "Slide and
   lesson wording").

   Last, Claude Opus changed two Xitsonga Water wordings found while these notes were written. Water slide 2
   paragraph 4 now writes plain "greywater", as slide 1 and the course do, instead of "mati ya greywater" (greywater
   water). On Water slide 22 paragraph 1, washwater is now "mati lama tirhisiweke ku hlantswa" (water that has been
   used for washing), the Water lesson's words for used water, instead of "mati yo hlantswa" (water for washing),
   which on its own could be read as clean water; the Sesotho and Tshivenda slides already say "used". Both were
   back-translated blind and checked independently by Claude Sonnet.
7. **Frames.** The existing paired-deck renderer re-rendered only the changed frames. Twelve Claude Haiku agents
   inspected all 125 for clipping, legibility, pairing with the English source, and labels. There were 13 flags,
   all about the illustrated English source at the top of the frame, which shows the artwork's own title (or none)
   instead of the slide heading:
   - Soil Sesotho slides 11 and 14–18;
   - Soil Xitsonga slides 5, 8 and 10;
   - Water Sesotho slides 9 and 12;
   - Water Xitsonga slides 16 and 18.

   That illustration is the unchanged English artwork. Because each changed frame is saved again as WebP, the
   illustration area is not byte-identical to main: its decoded pixels differ from main by an average of 0.3 of 255
   levels in the median frame (4.0 in the worst frame) and by at most 46 at any single pixel. The renderer and the
   English sources are unchanged, so this is compression noise. The exact English heading still appears in the English
   source panel.

   After the last word fixes, seven more Claude Haiku agents inspected the 53 frames redrawn since the first inspection.
   They found no clipping or missing paragraphs. Claude Opus checked their 8 flags: the artwork shows its own title on
   Soil Xitsonga slide 14, Soil Tshivenda slide 5 and Water Tshivenda slides 12 and 16; the artwork on Water Tshivenda
   slide 7 does show the slide heading; and the mark under the D of "TSHIVENḒA", reported missing from the banner and
   panel label on Soil Tshivenda slides 1, 5, 10 and 13, is drawn, as on main.
8. **Automated checks** in `tests/paired-draft-slides.test.ts`, `tests/soil-health-regional-silent-slides.test.ts`
   and `tests/studies-media-migration.test.ts`:
   - every changed passage is a marked draft, tied to an evidence row that holds its exact English and its blind
     back-translation;
   - word anchors on each former hold check that prohibitions, conditions, numbers and contour geometry survive;
   - the six learner-English panels stay exact English;
   - every Sesotho passage this batch drafted passes the repo's South African spelling check, and the eleven older
     passages that fail it are listed by name;
   - the offline refresh replaces only the changed frames.

`docs/study-translation-reviews/SOIL-WATER-ORDINARY-SLIDES-2026-10-05.json` lists every changed passage with its
exact English source, previous text, current text, blind back-translation and checker notes.
`docs/media/soil-water-ordinary-2026-10-05/frames.json` records every changed and preserved frame.
