## Source and meaning

- The final sentence of paragraph 1 describes Fair Share as taking only what is needed and returning surplus seeds, food, water and knowledge to the system. `surplus` stays in English alongside the ordinary Sesotho explanation.
- Paragraph 2 keeps the two examples: selling every egg and vegetable without keeping family food; fencing off a shared spring. The candidate phrase meaning “used by everyone” was rejected because the source only says “shared.” `shared spring` is retained beside the Sesotho description for facilitator review.
- Paragraph 3 keeps ethics as decision guidance, with the neighbour asking to graze cattle after drought and a flood damaging swales as examples. It gives no grazing permission, water entitlement or construction instruction. `swales` remains English.

## Backcheck and terminology

Two independent lower-cost model passes compared the draft with the source. The second identified the spring-access drift and uncertainty about `fulela`; the final draft uses `fulisa`.

The [Institute of Natural Resources / Serumula Sesotho grazing booklet](https://inr.org.za/wp-content/uploads/2018/05/Grazing-Managment-Booklet-Sesotho.pdf), produced for the USAID/IUCN Orange–Senqu programme, uses `fulisa` in grazing context. This supports the term choice only; none of that booklet's farming guidance is added to this lesson. Model checks and a terminology source do not establish fluent approval.

Facilitators should confirm the shared-spring phrasing, `fulisa dikgomo`, the ordinary explanation of surplus and overall naturalness. Feedback can be applied to the paired body without changing the English teaching.
