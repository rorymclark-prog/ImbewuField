# Vegetables and Staple Crops L3 — isiZulu review packet

**Status: UNREVIEWED AI DRAFT. Do not publish or use with learners.** This
packet compares the current English L3 lesson and narration with pages 12–14 of
the existing unpublished isiZulu draft. The isiZulu wording has not been
reviewed for fluency or accepted by an isiZulu-speaking farmer, educator or
agronomist. No fluency or local practitioner approval is claimed.

## Scope and sources compared

- English lesson body, key points and quiz: `lib/course-modules.ts`,
  `vegetables-staples-l3`.
- English narration: `docs/narration/vegetables-staples.en.md`, slides 12–14.
- Existing isiZulu draft: `docs/narration/vegetables-staples.zu.md`, pages
  12–14. The draft's terminology appendix already marks crop and seed-saving
  terms as unverified.

The comparison is limited to L3. The adjacent L2 and L4 lesson claims are not
reviewed here. The English lesson presents staple diversity as a way to keep
options when a crop fails, not a promise that a harvest or household food
supply is secured. Its crop-suitability statements are brief teaching claims;
they should not be used as a field recommendation without local advice.

### Quiz coverage

The English L3 module has two quiz questions: one explains that saved seed
from an F1 hybrid can germinate but may vary in the next generation, while a
stable open-pollinated variety with managed pollination is more predictable;
the other says amadumbe tolerates wetter ground than maize and may suit coastal
and high-rainfall conditions. The existing isiZulu narration has no quiz
translation. This packet proposes none. If quiz localization is commissioned,
translate the full qualifiers and answer rationales, and have the agronomy
claims reviewed alongside the lesson body.

The module key points also say that hybrid maize seed may not breed true and
specify amadumbe as suited to wetter KZN and coastal ground. The open-pollinated
seed-saving condition is in both English and isiZulu narration, but the hybrid
contrast and regional qualifier are not spoken in either script. Treat these as
module-content-to-script omissions, not isiZulu translation errors; decide
whether the English narration should carry them before preparing a matched
isiZulu version.

## Terms needing fluent and farming review

The existing narration's appendix already flags the following L3 terms as
uncertain: `isitshalo esiyisisekelo` (staple),
`ummbila wemvelo ovulelekile` (open-pollinated maize), `ibanga
lokuhlukanisa` (seed isolation), `ukukhetha kwezitshalo ezinhle kunazo zonke`
(selection), `ubhatata` (sweet potato), `izindumba` (cowpeas),
`ukumelana nezinkinga` (resilience), and `amadumbe`. A fluent isiZulu speaker
from the target community should confirm crop names and everyday comprehension;
a local seed-saving practitioner should check the seed terms. Keep English
terms in review notes where useful, but do not treat the parenthetical glosses
as learner-approved language.
