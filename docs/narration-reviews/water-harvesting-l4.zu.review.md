## Meaning and safety gaps for review

| Surface | Current English boundary | Existing isiZulu draft | Review decision needed |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Source, slide 19 | Used washwater may contain germs, salts and chemicals; exclude nappies, sick people, animal washing and harmful chemicals; ask the municipality which sources and uses are allowed. | Names bath, sink and laundry; calls it a free daily irrigation source if strong chemicals are absent. | “Sink” can include kitchen wastewater. Remove the unconditional free-irrigation promise and distinguish acceptable sources only after a qualified local source decision. |
| Exposure and destination, slides 20–22 | Where local rules allow, suitable water goes below mulch around **non-food** planting; keep people and animals away. Do not spray, pool or discharge to street, drain or watercourse. | Recommends a mulched basin around **fruit trees**, says organic matter filters it before roots, and names fruit trees as suitable. | Do not carry the older edible-crop or filtration assurance into a translation. | Treatment and storage | Mulch does not disinfect. Use promptly; stored greywater needs specialist advice and treatment. | Says the water is filtered through living material; gives no storage or no-disinfection warning. | Find natural wording for “mulch does not disinfect” and prompt use without suggesting soil makes wastewater safe. |
| Health and plumbing | Keep untreated water away from edible crops and drinking-water pipes; stop if it smells bad, pools or harms plants. | Avoids some raw-eaten crops and says to check municipal rules for permanent installation, but does not preserve the broader no-contact/no-cross-connection or stop cues. | Carry every safety cue through the eventual lesson, narration, quiz explanation and still captions together. |

Keep the keyed index if corrected. |

The [2017 national norms](https://www.gov.za/sites/default/files/gcis_document/201709/41100gon982.pdf)
exclude kitchen sinks and dishwashers from the glossary definition of
greywater (p. 9), yet §4.5(7) discusses introducing their wastewater to
biologically active topsoil (p. 17). The [Department of Human Settlements
Redbook, J.4.2.4 and glossary](https://www.dhs.gov.za/sites/default/files/documents/Redbook/REDBOOK_Section_J_Water_v1-1.pdf)
excludes kitchen sources as a potential reuse resource in that guide and
warns of health and plumbing risks. These passages do not establish a
universal statutory ban or permission for this learner. The English lesson now asks the learner to obtain qualified local source/use/site advice before any reuse and to avoid reuse if that advice is unavailable or unclear. A qualified local reviewer has not approved a particular route. Any isiZulu rewrite still requires fluent-language and qualified local review.

## Fluent-review questions

1. What spoken isiZulu distinguishes bathing/hand-basin washwater from
   kitchen-sink wastewater without implying every sink is acceptable?
2. How should the cautions for nappies, sick-person and animal washing, salts,
   chemicals, no spray/pooling/runoff, no edible-crop/contact route and no
   drinking-water connection be expressed plainly without dropping a condition?
3. Does the proposed wording for below-mulch **non-food** planting avoid
   promising filtration, disinfection or plant benefit?
4. Which local municipality and sanitation practitioner will check the
   practical route and any installation permission for the teaching context?
