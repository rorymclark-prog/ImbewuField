### Lesson Title & Body

* **Title**
  * **English**: The Three Ethics: Earth Care, People Care, Fair Share
  * **Sesotho sa Leboa / Sepedi**: Melao e Meraro ya Boitshwaro: Tlhokomelo ya Lefase, Tlhokomelo ya Batho, Kabelano ya Toka

* **Body Paragraph 1**
  * **English**: Permaculture rests on three ethics. Earth Care means treating soil, water, plants and animals as living systems to protect, not resources to use up. People Care means your family's needs come first, then your community's. Fair Share means taking only what you need and returning the surplus — seeds, food, water, knowledge — back into the system.
  * **Sesotho sa Leboa / Sepedi**: Permaculture e theilwe godimo ga melao e meraro ya boitshwaro. Tlhokomelo ya Lefase e ra go swara mmu, meetse, dimela le diphoofolo bjalo ka ditshepedišo tše di phelago tše di swanetšego go šireletšwa, e seng methopo ya go fetšwa. Tlhokomelo ya Batho e ra gore dinyakwa tša lapa la gago di tla pele, gwa latela tša setšhaba sa gago. Kabelano ya Toka e ra go tšea fela seo o se hlokago le go bušetša mašaledi — dipeu, dijo, meetse, tsebo — morago ka gare ga tshepedišo.

* **Body Paragraph 2**
  * **English**: These aren't abstract ideas. A farmer who sells every egg and vegetable but keeps nothing back for the family table is skipping People Care. A community that fences off a shared spring is breaking Fair Share.
  * **Sesotho sa Leboa / Sepedi**: Tše ga se mehopolo fela ye e se nago bophelo. Molemi yo a rekišago lee le lengwe le le lengwe le morogo fela a sa šiele lapa la gagwe dijo tafoleng o tshela Tlhokomelo ya Batho. Setšhaba seo se ageletšago mothopo wa meetse wo o abelanwago se roba Kabelano ya Toka.

* **Body Paragraph 3**
  * **English**: Ethics matter because they help you decide when there's no rulebook — a neighbour asking to graze cattle after a drought, a flood damaging your swales. Build these three into how you think before you build anything on the ground.
  * **Sesotho sa Leboa / Sepedi**: Melao ya boitshwaro e bohlokwa ka gobane e go thuša go tšea diphetho ge go se na puku ya melao — moagišani yo a kgopelago go fudiša dikgomo morago ga komelelo, mafula a senyago di-swale tša gago. Agela tše tše tharo ka gare ga tsela yeo o naganago ka yona pele o ka aga selo le ge e le sefe fase.

  * **Option 1**

    * **Sesotho sa Leboa / Sepedi**: Tlhokomelo ya Batho fela
  * **Option 2**
    * **English**: Fair Share — he returns nothing to the system
    * **Sesotho sa Leboa / Sepedi**: Kabelano ya Toka — ga a bušetše selo ka gare ga tshepedišo

#### Question 2
* **Question Prompt**
  * **English**: Your borehole serves your household. Neighbours ask for water too. Which action best reflects all three ethics?
  * **Sesotho sa Leboa / Sepedi**: Borehole ya gago e šomela lapa la gago. Baagišani le bona ba kgopela meetse. Ke tiro efe yeo e bonagatšago melao ka moka e meraro ya boitshwaro gabotse?

* **Options**
  * **Option 0**
    * **English**: Sell access to the highest bidder
    * **Sesotho sa Leboa / Sepedi**: Rekiša phihlelelo go motho yo a lefelago tšhelete e ntši kudu
  * **Option 1**
    * **English**: Keep all the borehole water for a larger irrigation area
    * **Sesotho sa Leboa / Sepedi**: Boloka meetse ka moka a borehole bakeng sa lefelo le legolwane la go nošetša
  * **Option 2**
    * **English**: Find out if sharing is allowed and if the borehole can serve all users. Only then agree how to share fairly and keep watching the water level.
    * **Sesotho sa Leboa / Sepedi**: Hwetša gore na go abelana go a dumelelwa le ge eba borehole e ka kgona go šomela bašomiši ka moka. Ke moka fela le kwane ka mokgwa wa go abelana ka toka le go tšwela pele le lekola bogodimo bja meetse.
  * **Option 3**
    * **English**: Cap the borehole to preserve groundwater only
    * **Sesotho sa Leboa / Sepedi**: Tswalela borehole go boloka meetse a ka fase ga mobu fela

* **Correct Index**: `2`

* **Rationale**
  * **English**: First find out what water use is allowed and whether the source can serve all users without taking too much. If sharing is allowed and there is enough water, agree how to share fairly. Monitoring helps you notice change; it does not give permission to take more water.
  * **Sesotho sa Leboa / Sepedi**: Pele hwetša gore ke tšhomišo efe ya meetse ye e dumeletšwego le ge eba mothopo o ka šomela bašomiši ka moka ntle le go tšea meetse a mantši kudu. Ge eba go abelana go dumeletšwe gomme go na le meetse a lekanego, kwanang ka mokgwa wa go abelana ka toka. Go lekola go go thuša go lemoga phetogo; ga go fe tumelelo ya go tšea meetse a mantši.

### Issues & Ambiguities Flagged for Field Review

1. **Dialect & Geographic Context (Bushbuckridge / Bohlabela)**:
   * Bushbuckridge is linguistically diverse (contact zone between Northern Sotho / Sepedi, Xitsonga/Shangaan, and siSwati). Standard Northern Sotho orthography is used here, but local agricultural extension workers should verify whether specific terms (such as *mafela* vs *poone* for maize/mielies, or *borehole* vs *sediba sa motšhene*) align with local spoken usage in the SEF partner communities.

2. **Permaculture Terminology Borrowing vs Translation**:
   * **"Permaculture"**: Left untranslated as a proper methodology name.
   * **"Swales"**: Kept as *di-swale* (noun class 8/10 loan adaptation). An indigenous descriptive term like *meedi* refers generally to furrows/ditches/channels, which could be misconstrued as draining trenches rather than water-harvesting contour swales. Community facilitators should verify if *di-swale* is already standard among ACT trainers.
   * **"Zones and sectors"**: Rendered as *ditsone tša gago le disektara tša gago*. If ACT uses established vernacular phrases, local instructors may prefer those.

3. **Idiomatic Expressions**:
   * **"Stops paying you / keeps paying you"** (Slide 4): Rendered as *e kgaotša go go putsa* (stops rewarding/paying you) rather than literal cash transaction (*go go lefa*), because the source refers to ecological and yield returns, not financial wage payment.
   * **"Rulebook"** (*puku ya melao*): Used literally ("book of laws/rules") to capture the idea of a missing written manual or formal regulation.

4. **Resource Monitoring & Regulatory Nuances (Borehole)**:
   * The wording in Question 2 and Slide 7 carefully preserves the conditionality: finding out if sharing is legally/customarily permitted (*tumelelo*), checking whether capacity suffices (*bokgoni bja mothopo*), and stating unequivocally that monitoring water levels (*go lekola bogodimo bja meetse*) only alerts users to depletion and **does not** bestow permission to extract more water.
