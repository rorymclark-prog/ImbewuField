### Lesson Body Paragraphs

#### Paragraph 1
* **English:**
  Permaculture rests on three ethics. Earth Care means treating soil, water, plants and animals as living systems to protect, not resources to use up. People Care means your family's needs come first, then your community's. Fair Share means taking only what you need and returning the surplus — seeds, food, water, knowledge — back into the system.
* **Sesotho:**
  Permaculture e itshetlehile hodima melao-motheo e meraro ya boitshwaro. Tlhokomelo ya Lefatshe e bolela ho tshwara mobu, metsi, dimela le diphoofolo jwaloka ditsamaiso tse phelang tse lokelang ho sireletswa, eseng disebediswa tse lokelang ho feletswa ruri. Tlhokomelo ya Batho e bolela hore ditlhoko tsa lelapa la hao di tla pele, e be tse latelang ke tsa setjhaba sa heno. Karolo e Lokileng e bolela ho nka feela seo o se hlokang le ho khutlisetsa masalla — dipeo, dijo, metsi, tsebo — morao ka hare ho tsamaiso.

#### Paragraph 2
* **English:**
  These aren't abstract ideas. A farmer who sells every egg and vegetable but keeps nothing back for the family table is skipping People Care. A community that fences off a shared spring is breaking Fair Share.
* **Sesotho:**
  Tsenatso hase mehopolo e sa tsebetseng feela fatshe mona. Molemi ya rekisang lehe le leng le le leng le moroho o mong le o mong empa a sa siye letho bakeng sa tafole ya lelapa o tlola Tlhokomelo ya Batho. Setjhaba se teratelang sediba se arolelanwang se tlola Karolo e Lokileng.

#### Paragraph 3
* **English:**
  Ethics matter because they help you decide when there's no rulebook — a neighbour asking to graze cattle after a drought, a flood damaging your swales. Build these three into how you think before you build anything on the ground.
* **Sesotho:**
  Melao-motheo ya boitshwaro e bohlokwa hobane e o thusa ho etsa qeto ha ho se na buka ya melao — moahelani ya kopang ho fula dikgomo kamora komello, kapa morwallo o senyang di-swale tsa hao. Hahanya tsena tse tharo tseleng eo o nahanang ka yona pele o haha kapa o lema ntho efe kapa efe fatshe mona.

### Quiz

#### Question 1
* **English Question:**
  A farmer sells all his surplus maize but keeps nothing for composting or seed saving. Which ethic is he most failing?
* **Sesotho Question:**
  Molemi o rekisa poone yohle ya hae e saletseng hodimo empa ha a boloke letho bakeng sa manyolo a mobu (compost) kapa poloko ya dipeo. Ke molao-motheo ofe wa boitshwaro oo a o hlolwang haholo?

* **Options:**
  * **Option 0:**
    * **English:** Earth Care only
    * **Sesotho:** Tlhokomelo ya Lefatshe feela
  * **Option 1:**
    * **English:** People Care only
    * **Sesotho:** Tlhokomelo ya Batho feela
  * **Option 2:**
    * **English:** Fair Share — he returns nothing to the system
    * **Sesotho:** Karolo e Lokileng — ha a kgutlisetse letho ka hara tsamaiso
  * **Option 3:**
    * **English:** All three equally
    * **Sesotho:** Tse tharo kaofela ka ho lekana

* **Correct Index:** `2` (Preserved)

* **Rationale:**
  * **English:**
    Fair Share means returning some of what you take — as seed, compost, or food for others. Selling everything and keeping nothing back breaks that cycle.
  * **Sesotho:**
    Karolo e Lokileng e bolela ho kgutlisa karolo e nngwe ya seo o se nkang — jwaloka peo, manyolo a compost, kapa dijo bakeng sa ba bang. Ho rekisa ntho e nngwe le e nngwe ntle le ho boloka letho morao ho kgaola modikwadikwe oo.

---

#### Question 2
* **English Question:**
  Your borehole serves your household. Neighbours ask for water too. Which action best reflects all three ethics?
* **Sesotho Question:**
  Borehole ya hao e sebeletsa lelapa la hao. Baahelani le bona ba kopa metsi. Ke ketso efe e bontshang hantle melao-motheo ena e meraro kaofela ya boitshwaro?

* **Options:**
  * **Option 0:**
    * **English:** Sell access to the highest bidder
    * **Sesotho:** Rekisetsa ya fanang ka theko e phahameng ka ho fetisisa tokelo ya ho sebedisa metsi
  * **Option 1:**
    * **English:** Keep all the borehole water for a larger irrigation area
    * **Sesotho:** Boloka metsi ohle a borehole bakeng sa sebaka se seholo sa ho nosetsa
  * **Option 2:**
    * **English:** Find out if sharing is allowed and if the borehole can serve all users. Only then agree how to share fairly and keep watching the water level.
    * **Sesotho:** Fumana pele hore na ho arolelana ho a dumellwa le hore na borehole e ka kgona ho sebeletsa basebedisi bohle. Ke hona feela moo le ka dumellanang ka moo le tla arolelana ka tekatekano le ho tswela pele ho shebella boemo ba metsi.
  * **Option 3:**
    * **English:** Cap the borehole to preserve groundwater only
    * **Sesotho:** Kwala borehole ho boloka feela metsi a ka tlas'a lefatshe

* **Correct Index:** `2` (Preserved)

* **Rationale:**
  * **English:**
    First find out what water use is allowed and whether the source can serve all users without taking too much. If sharing is allowed and there is enough water, agree how to share fairly. Monitoring helps you notice change; it does not give permission to take more water.
  * **Sesotho:**
    Pele fumana hore na ke tshebediso efe ya metsi e dumelletsweng le hore na mohlodi o ka kgona ho sebeletsa basebedisi bohle ntle le ho hula metsi a mangata haholo. Haeba ho arolelana ho dumelletswe mme ho na le metsi a lekaneng, dumellanang ka moo le ka arolelanang ka tekatekano. Ho beha leihlo ho o thusa ho lemoha phetoho; ha ho fane ka tumello ya ho nka metsi a mangata ho feta.

## 3. Issues & English/Source Ambiguity (Dintlha tse sa Hlakang Hantle)

1. **"Mielies" vs. "Maize":**
   * *Observation:* The lesson text and quiz use "maize", whereas Slide 6 uses the South African vernacular "mielies".
   * *Resolution in draft:* Both were translated into standard South African Sesotho as *"poone"*. If the curriculum intends to retain colloquial local English borrowing (*"dimilisi"*), this should be decided during community review.

2. **Technical Terms ("Swales", "Zones", "Sectors", "Borehole"):**
   * *Observation:* "Swales", "zones", and "sectors" are specialized permaculture civil/design terms. In Sesotho spoken in rural Free State / QwaQwa, "swales" has no single direct native equivalent other than descriptive terms like *dikanale tsa metsi tse thibelang khoholeho* (water-retaining contour swales). Similarly, "zones" and "sectors" are design concepts.
   * *Resolution in draft:* In accordance with the instruction not to add extra advice, descriptions, or unauthorized terminology, terms were kept as direct loan/adapted concepts (*di-swale*, *dibaka (zones)*, *makala (sectors)*, *borehole*). Review with local agricultural trainers is recommended to determine whether descriptive phrases are preferred in learner materials.

3. **"Keeps paying you" (Slide 4):**
   * *Observation:* "A system you protect keeps paying you" is an English idiomatic expression that can imply monetary yield or sustained ecological/livelihood returns.
   * *Resolution in draft:* Translated as *"Tsamaiso eo o e sireletsang e tswela pele ho o fa molemo"* ("yields benefit/advantage"), which preserves the meaning without introducing specific financial terms or rates.

4. **"Serving all users without taking too much" (Quiz 2 Rationale & Slide 7):**
   * *Observation:* The English phrase "without taking too much" is colloquial and lacks defined hydrologic criteria.
   * *Resolution in draft:* Strictly preserved as *"ntle le ho hula metsi a mangata haholo"* without injecting flow rates, drawdown thresholds, or regional groundwater policies.
