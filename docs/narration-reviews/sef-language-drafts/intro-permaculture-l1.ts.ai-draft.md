### Body Paragraphs

#### Paragraph 1
- **English**:
  > Permaculture rests on three ethics. Earth Care means treating soil, water, plants and animals as living systems to protect, not resources to use up. People Care means your family's needs come first, then your community's. Fair Share means taking only what you need and returning the surplus — seeds, food, water, knowledge — back into the system.
- **Xitsonga**:
  > Permaculture yi titshege hi milawu-nkulu ya mahanyelo yinharhu. Ku Hlayisa Misava swi vula ku teka misava, mati, swimilana na swiharhi tanihi maendlelo lama hanyaka lawa ma faneleke ku sireleriwa, ku nga ri swilo swo tirhisiwa ku fikela loko swi hela. Ku Hlayisa Vanhu swi vula leswaku swilaveko swa ndyangu wa wena swi rhanga emahlweni, kutani ku landzela swa vaaki va wena. Ku Avelana hi Vululami swi vula ku teka ntsena leswi u swi pfumalaka kutani u tlherisela leswi salaka — mbewu, swakudya, mati, vutivi — endzeni ka maendlelo ya ntumbuluko.

#### Paragraph 2
- **English**:
  > These aren't abstract ideas. A farmer who sells every egg and vegetable but keeps nothing back for the family table is skipping People Care. A community that fences off a shared spring is breaking Fair Share.
- **Xitsonga**:
  > Lawa a hi miehleketo ya le moyeni ntsena. Murimi loyi a xavisaka tandza na matsavu hinkwawo kambe a nga siyi nchumu ematafuleni ya ndyangu u tshika Ku Hlayisa Vanhu. Vaaki lava biyelaka xihlovo lexi avelaneriwaka va tlula Nawu wa ku Avelana hi Vululami.

#### Paragraph 3
- **English**:
  > Ethics matter because they help you decide when there's no rulebook — a neighbour asking to graze cattle after a drought, a flood damaging your swales. Build these three into how you think before you build anything on the ground.
- **Xitsonga**:
  > Milawu leyi ya mahanyelo i ya nkoka hikuva yi ku pfuna ku endla swiboho loko ku nga ri na buku ya milawu — muakelani loyi a kombelaka ku risa tihomu endzhaku ka dyandza, ndhambi leyi onhaka tiswales ta wena. Nghenisa swilo leswi swinharhu endleleni leyi u ehleketaka ha yona u nga si aka nchumu emisaveni.

# Issues & Linguistic / Contextual Flags

1. **Permaculture Core Terminology**:
   - *Ethics*: Translated as `milawu-nkulu ya mahanyelo` (fundamental codes of conduct/ethics) or `milawu ya mahanyelo`. In Xitsonga, `nawu` commonly means a law or rule, but `mahanyelo` grounds it in conduct, values, and ethics rather than state legislation.
   - *Earth Care / People Care / Fair Share*: Rendered as `Ku Hlayisa Misava` (Earth care/protection), `Ku Hlayisa Vanhu` (People care/nurturing), and `Ku Avelana hi Vululami` (sharing with equity/fairness). An alternative for *Fair Share* in rural dialogue is `Ku avelana hi ku ringana`, but `vululami` conveys justice and fairness better than pure mathematical equality.
   - *Living systems*: Rendered as `maendlelo lama hanyaka` or `fambiselo leri hanyaka`. The English "system" has no single direct non-technical equivalent in Xitsonga; `maendlelo` (workings/processes) and `fambiselo` (system/regime) were used depending on syntactic flow.

2. **Technical Agricultural Words Kept or Borrowed**:
   - *Swales*: Kept as loan word `tiswales` / `swales`. There is no traditional Xitsonga single-word equivalent for an on-contour water-harvesting ditch and berm. Facilitators should demonstrate this physically.
   - *Zones and Sectors*: Rendered as `ti-zone na ti-sector` with standard prefixes.
   - *Mielies vs. Maize*: In English, the source alternates between "maize" (quiz) and "mielies" (slide 6). In Xitsonga, `mavele` (or `swifake` for fresh cobs) is standard across Bushbuckridge.

3. **Land and Plot Terminology in Bushbuckridge Context**:
   - Words like "plot", "smallholding", and "farm" have distinct nuances in communal tenure settings under Traditional Authorities (amakhosi/tindhuna) in the Bushbuckridge / SEF area. We used `xitandi` (residential plot / smallholding stand), `purasi` (farm/smallholding), and `misava / tiko` (land) strictly according to the English source text, without introducing land rights or customary tenure terms not present in the original.

4. **Preservation of Caveats and Strict Water Nuance**:
   - In Slide 7 and Quiz 2, the exact conditions were preserved:
     - Checking whether sharing is allowed (`loko ku avelana ku pfumeleriwa`)
     - Checking source capacity (`loko mbhovo wu ta kota ku tirhela vatirhisi hinkwavo handle ko teka mati yo tala ngopfu`)
     - Monitoring caveat (`Ku langutisisa xiyimo xa mati ntsena a swi endli leswaku kutirhisiwa ka mati yo engeteleka ku sireleleka kumbe ku pfumeleriwa` / observation alone does not grant permission or make additional withdrawal safe).
   - No external legal regulations, extraction rates, or borehole testing durations were introduced.
