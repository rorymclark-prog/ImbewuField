## Source boundaries

- The [ARC climate monitoring service](https://www.arc.agric.za/arc-iscw/Pages/Climate-Monitoring-Services.aspx) reports wind speed and direction from a national weather-station network. It supports checking observations; it does not establish the draft's fixed seasonal directions as rules for every Highveld or KZN escarpment smallholding.
- The [South African Weather Service description of berg winds](https://testing.weathersa.co.za/home/weatherques) describes hot, dry winds blowing off the interior plateau, generally toward the coast, and says they usually occur in winter. It does not validate the exact Highveld north-westerly timing or the KZN seasonal directions in this lesson.
- [FAO frost guidance](https://www.fao.org/4/y7223e/y7223e0c.htm) says cold air can drain down slopes and accumulate in low spots during radiation-frost conditions, and emphasizes site-specific topography, obstacles, soil and crop factors. It supports conditional observation, not a guaranteed nursery position or a universal “valleys frost first and last” rule.
- [KZN Department of Agriculture guidance on tomato diseases](https://www.kzndard.gov.za/images/Documents/Horticulture/Veg_prod/tomato.pdf) says late blight can be destructive during prolonged cool, damp spells. [University of Minnesota Extension's late-blight guidance](https://extension.umn.edu/agriculture/specialty-crops/vegetable-farming/disease-management/late-blight) identifies *Phytophthora infestans* as a water mould and lists keeping leaves dry and allowing air between plants among several management measures. These sources do not support promising that changing a bed's position alone prevents or controls late blight.

## Quiz alignment

The current English quiz asks (1) where to first look for a frost-sensitive nursery, with the keyed option requiring a sunny, sheltered place outside an observed frost hollow after checking the site's cold-night pattern; and (2) which bed position may help late-blight-affected tomatoes dry, alongside local crop-health advice, with good airflow and morning sun keyed. Its rationales say no hillside position guarantees freedom from frost, and that bed movement alone is not a complete disease-control plan.
