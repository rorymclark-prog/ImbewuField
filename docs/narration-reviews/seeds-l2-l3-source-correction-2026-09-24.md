# Seeds L2–L3 source correction — 24 September 2026

Rory authorized publication of clearly labelled isiZulu drafts before fluent
review. These narrow English and isiZulu corrections were made while registering
the Seeds L2–L3 lesson drafts, so the two languages carry the same limits.

## L2: isolation depends on crop and variety

The old key point said tomatoes need “little isolation”. It could be read as a
general instruction for all tomato varieties or for seed intended for sharing.
The new key point says tomatoes mostly self-pollinate but can cross, maize is
wind-pollinated, and growers should check crop- and variety-specific isolation
guidance. No distance has been added. The existing lesson body, quiz answers,
slides and narration already preserve the possibility of tomato crossing.

Source: [Organic Seed Alliance, Tomato Seed Production Guide](https://seedalliance.org/publications/tomato-seed-production-guide/),
which distinguishes modern, heirloom and currant tomato pollination and varies
isolation advice by type and seed use; [Organic Seed Alliance, Seed Saving Guide](https://seedalliance.org/publications/seed-saving-guide/),
which explains that wind-pollinated crops generally need more isolation than
mostly self-pollinated crops and that a recommended distance is not absolute.

## L3: dry before sealing; longevity varies

The old lesson body said seed “can last for years” and recommended a little
rice or dried milk powder inside a sealed container. That omitted the critical
precondition that seed be thoroughly dry before sealing. The English and
isiZulu body now states the sequence explicitly and says longevity varies by
crop and conditions. The first quiz rationale also names the drying
precondition. The unverified rice suggestion and loosely specified milk powder
step were removed; neither appears in the registered narration. No new storage
temperature, moisture percentage or lifespan has been invented.

Sources: [Organic Seed Alliance, Seed Saving Guide](https://seedalliance.org/publications/seed-saving-guide/),
which says moisture-restricting containers require drier seed and describes
crop-dependent storage life; [University of Minnesota Extension, Saving
vegetable seeds](https://extension.umn.edu/garden-and-home/yard-and-garden/gardening-in-minnesota/saving-vegetable-seeds),
which places paper packets inside a sealed container and treats any powdered
milk desiccant as a separately wrapped material.
