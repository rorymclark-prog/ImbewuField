## Rights caution for the coordinated English correction

The official [Plant Breeders' Rights Act 12 of 2018, sections 7 and
10](https://www.gov.za/sites/default/files/gcis_document/201903/4234729-3-2019plantbreedersact12of2018.pdf)
sets rights over specified acts involving protected varieties, including
production or reproduction and sale or other marketing. Section 10 includes
exceptions for private, non-commercial purposes and a farmer exception subject
to prescribed farmer categories, plant categories and uses, with possible
royalty and labelling conditions. The [Department of Agriculture says the Act
and regulations took effect on 1 June
2025](https://www.gov.za/news/media-statements/agriculture-commencement-plant-breeders-rights-act-2018-and-regulations-23).

This supports a cautious lesson prompt, not a conclusion that the seed in any
example is protected or that all farmer exchange is forbidden. The corrected
English message to translate after qualified review is:

> Before exchanging seed, check whether the variety is protected and whether
> permission is needed.

Keep the quality checks separate and plain: identify the crop and variety,
record its source and collection date, and test germination before relying on
the seed for planting. Those checks do not determine whether exchange is
permitted. A fluent isiZulu reviewer should choose familiar local wording for
“protected variety” and “permission”; do not publish an unreviewed AI
translation of legal terminology.
