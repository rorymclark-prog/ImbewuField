## Comparison and review findings

| Surface | Current English | Existing isiZulu draft | Finding |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lesson title | “Nutrients Moving Through the Farm” | “Close the Nutrient Loop” | The ZU title promises closure that the corrected English does not claim. |
| Slide 14 | Some nutrients move from plants to animals; bought feed brings nutrients in; some nutrients may return in manure compost; food/products carry nutrients away. | Tells learners to watch nutrients go plants → animals → manure/compost → growing bed. | Omits feed inputs and product outputs and gives a complete return path. | Slide 15 | Animals return only some nutrients. Fresh manure may carry harmful germs; compost manure fully before use around food crops. | Says animals return nutrients and manure becomes crop compost, without a treatment safeguard. | Preserve “some” and the fresh-manure hygiene cue. Do not imply raw manure is safe for food crops or that composting makes manure sterile. |
| Quiz | The two questions concern guinea-fowl tick checks and goat worm control. | No corresponding nutrient-loop question in this narration segment. | No quiz edits or translations proposed. Keep the existing animal-health answers and advice intact. |

The cited FAO source in the English review explains that feed imports and
product exports affect farm nutrient flows, and that nutrients can be lost.
South Africa's fresh-produce hygiene code notes that manure can carry pathogens
and that suitability depends on effective treatment. These sources support a
partial-flow explanation and a treatment safeguard; they do not establish a
particular compost duration, guarantee sanitation, or prove anything about the
specific manure represented in the still.

## Suggested isiZulu wording — unreviewed AI draft only

Have a fluent isiZulu reviewer check that the distinction between animal
manure and compost is clear and natural, and that “fully compost” communicates
the intended process without implying sterility. Keep any locally preferred
term for feed, nutrients, manure and compost consistent across lesson, narration
and still captions.

**Lesson title:**

> Ukuhlanganisa Imfuyo: Izakhamzimba Zihamba Epulazini

**Slide 14 — replace the closed-loop narration:**

> Ezinye izakhamzimba zisuka ezitshalweni ziye ezilwaneni. Ukudla kwezilwane okuthengwayo kuletha izakhamzimba epulazini. Ezinye izakhamzimba zingabuyela endaweni yokutshala nge-compost eyenziwe ngomquba. Ukudla okuvunwayo neminye imikhiqizo kususa izakhamzimba epulazini.

**Slide 15 — replace the closure title and add the manure safeguard:**

> Ezinye izakhamzimba zingabuyela epulazini nge-compost eyenziwe ngomquba. Umquba omusha ungaba namagciwane ayingozi. Vumela umquba uboliswe ngokuphelele ngaphambi kokuwusebenzisa eduze kwezitshalo zokudla.
>
> Izinkukhu zidla ukudla okufanele okuvela epulazini nezinambuzane.
>
> Ukudla kwezilwane okuthengwayo kuletha izakhamzimba epulazini. Ukudla okuthengiswayo noma okuyiswa ekhaya, neminye imikhiqizo, kususa izakhamzimba epulazini. Bhala phansi ukudla kwezilwane okuthengwayo nokudla okuthengiswayo noma okuyiswa ekhaya.
>
> Izinsalela zokudla kuphela zingase zingazanelisi izidingo zezilwane.

The draft above uses the current English claims and introduces no new numbers,
species, feed rates or compost timing. Its terminology and grammar remain
unverified. The phrase about fully composting manure must be checked by a
fluent practitioner so it does not imply a guaranteed germ-free result.
