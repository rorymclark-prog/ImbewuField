## Quiz and media comparison

The current L2 quiz in `lib/course-modules.ts` has two questions. The first
asks about frost risk to a young planting and its rationale says to check actual
conditions and reliable local guidance rather than assume shelter removes the
risk. Its keyed option (“likely killed or badly damaged”) is stronger than the
lesson narration's “can suffer frost damage”; this is a current English wording
variance recorded here for editorial review, not permission to alter the quiz.
The second question asks why to include locally appropriate indigenous plants:
they **can** support local habitat, pollinators and other wildlife. Its rationale
explicitly rejects a universal percentage and says local suitability still
needs checking. The isiZulu draft has no quiz translation; preserve those
conditions if one is prepared.

The deployed L2 media check is
[`food-forest-l2-lesson-check.md`](../media/studies-animation-quality/food-forest-l2-lesson-check.md).
Slide 9 is an illustrative regional map; slide 10 is a generic Highveld versus
warm-region comparison with Frost, Rainfall and Humidity factors and no named
species. Neither is a farm survey or planting approval. The check records that
the imagery was inspected at full size and at 269 × 151, and that the module
player has no video for the slide 10 narration; its English audio title still
contains “Watch.” The course's audio manifest and MP3s are English-only. There
is no isiZulu Food Forest transcript, deck or audio registered, so this draft
does not yet have a corresponding learner media package.

## Primary-source anchors

- The [ARC overview of horticulture](https://www.arc.agric.za/arc-infruitec-nietvoorbij/Pages/Horticulture/Overview-of-Horticulture.aspx)
  says resource-limited as well as commercial growers should select crops and
  rootstocks adapted to their specific climate and soil; it notes that
  overseas-developed technology should be evaluated under South African
  conditions before final recommendations.
- The [ARC Climate-Smart Agriculture subtropical fruit manual](https://www.arc.agric.za/arc-iscw/CSA-Toolbox/Pages/assets/modules/6.pdf)
  describes subtropical crops as having relatively poor winter hardiness and
  says frost resistance varies by crop; rainfall and soil also affect area
  suitability. This supports a risk check, not the draft's guaranteed outcomes.
- [SANBI's Grassland Biome account](https://pza.sanbi.org/vegetation/grassland-biome)
  describes grassland as a distinct ecosystem with high biodiversity and rare
  plants. The [SANBI Grasslands Programme](https://www.sanbi.org/biodiversity/science-into-policy-action/mainstreaming-biodiversity/grasslands-programme/)
  records biodiversity pressures and the need to integrate conservation into
  production landscapes. These support protecting existing natural grassland,
  not treating all grassy ground as vacant planting space.
- The [DFFE NEMBA Alien and Invasive Species Lists](https://www.dffe.gov.za/sites/default/files/legislations/nemba_invasivespecieslist_g43726gon1003.pdf)
  are a reason to check current restrictions before planting. This review has
  not made a species-by-species legal determination and does not approve any
  name in the course examples.
- The existing [L2 English lesson check](../media/studies-animation-quality/food-forest-l2-lesson-check.md)
  links the ARC, SANBI and DFFE sources and records the remaining categorical
  English line on slide 10. Its source review also states that a regional
  example is not blanket approval.
