## Source safeguards retained

- Succession sowing is described as reducing risk, not guaranteeing harvest during continued adverse conditions.
- The 2–3 week sowing interval is a starting rhythm; overlap depends on the crop's actual timing.
- Three Sisters is attributed to Indigenous farming traditions in the Americas; crop competition and delayed nutrient release from decomposing residues are stated cautiously.
- Sweet-potato drought tolerance is qualified to after storage roots form; early water needs and possible harvest effects are retained. Amadumbe's wet-ground suitability stays conditional.
- Yellow leaves are not treated as proof of insects. Any product must be registered for both crop and pest, and its label, protection, and harvest-wait instructions must be followed; no improvised mixes or stronger doses are advised.
- The ten-day field photo is only an assignment checkpoint. Care begins at planting; the record can support the next decision with reliable local advice.
