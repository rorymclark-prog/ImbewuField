# Water Harvesting L1 — isiZulu alignment review packet

**REVIEW ONLY. The current isiZulu draft and its audio are stale and are not approved for learners or recording.** This packet inventories the existing isiZulu wording for Water Harvesting L1 and compares its apparent meanings with the corrected English lesson. English glosses and alignment findings are working notes, not verified translations. No fluent isiZulu, farmer, practitioner, quiz, or audio review is claimed.

## Scope and sources

- Water Harvesting L1 covers slides 3–9: `water-harvesting-l1`, “Swales and Berms: Slowing Water on the Slope,” in `lib/course-modules.ts`.
- Existing isiZulu narration: `docs/narration/water-harvesting.zu.md`, slides 2–9. Slide 2 is included because it states the course learning outcomes; the L1-specific narration starts at slide 3.
- Current English narration: `docs/narration/water-harvesting.en.md`, slides 2–9.
- Historical English source correction proposal: `docs/media/studies-animation-quality/water-l1-source-correction-proposal.md`.
- English lesson release check: `docs/media/studies-animation-quality/water-l1-lesson-check.md`.
- English lesson and quiz: `lib/course-modules.ts`, `water-harvesting-l1`.
- Audio inventory: `lib/course-audio.ts`, Water Harvesting slides 3–9. The English release coordinated narration, transcript, affected still captions, English audio, full audio, manifest and saved-pack migration.

The source-backed English correction was adopted in commit `73ce253c`. It teaches a level contour swale as one design; other swales may use a slight controlled grade to a safe outlet; the suitable approach depends on soil, slope, drainage and storm flow; and a trained local adviser should assess the line, overflow and receiving point before digging. It qualifies infiltration and tree moisture as conditional. The older absolute contour, infiltration and tree-moisture wording in this packet is present in the **stale isiZulu draft**, not in the corrected English lesson. The steep-land advice and existing site and neighbour cautions remain. A fluent reviewer should use the deployed English wording as the source, not the historical proposal.

## English source and quiz dependencies

The English correction changed the lesson body, key point, quiz 1 and narration in commit `73ce253c`. The English learner source now distinguishes contour and intentionally graded swales, qualifies infiltration and tree moisture, and requires local assessment. The isiZulu reviewer has a fixed English source for alignment; this packet still does not establish fluent-language or local practitioner approval.

The corrected English L1 quiz asks about a **planned level contour swale**, what to check before changing it, and a rationale that allows for intentionally graded swales. The existing isiZulu narration file has no L1 quiz question, options or rationale. Provide the adopted English question, all options and rationale together for translation and review. Preserve the correct answer index and the distinction between an unintended low point and an intentionally graded design. The steep-land quiz also needs coverage in any isiZulu quiz review if learners see it in isiZulu.

## Terminology and review handoff

Have a first-language South African isiZulu reviewer familiar with smallholder farming check:

- Whether the glosses above accurately represent each quote, especially `ikhontolo`, `ishonisa`, `ukuchichima`, `indawo yokuhambisa amanzi`, `i-berm`, `ama-terrace`, and borrowed words such as `i-swale`, `i-spillway` and `i-vetiver`.
- Which local spoken terms learners understand for level contour, a slight controlled grade, a safe outlet, receiving point, runoff, infiltration and site assessment. Do not coin technical language or assume a loanword is familiar.
- Whether any revised sentences are plain and natural when spoken aloud to a learner using a phone, while keeping the difference between observing a concept and receiving construction advice clear.
- Whether the safe-overflow explanation makes clear that a downstream swale or dam is a possible receiver only after site assessment.

A qualified local water or earthworks practitioner must separately review the technical meaning and safety of the final English and isiZulu teaching. Language review does not establish a safe design, site suitability or construction approval. Record each reviewer’s role, date, exact material reviewed, edits and limits; do not imply one reviewer approved work outside their scope.
