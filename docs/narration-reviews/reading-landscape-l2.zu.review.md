**REVIEW ONLY — the existing isiZulu draft is not approved for learners or recording.**

This packet compares the current L2 isiZulu narration with the documented English source-hold proposal. The proposed English correction is itself a review packet; its scope is pending authorization. - Evidence and source limits: `docs/media/studies-animation-quality/reading-landscape-l2-lesson-check.md`, “Source and teaching holds.”

The source hold identifies four claims needing review: a country-wide northern-sun rule; tying frost pooling to south-facing aspect instead of teaching cold-air drainage and observed low hollows; a specific 2 m shade-cloth size and “most of the day” outcome without site evidence; and a north-facing wall presented as assured frost protection. The proposal keeps the winter shadow observation, treats slope aspect as one factor, makes the wall only a possible aid, and asks learners to observe local frost. It preserves the existing plant names and adds no hardiness figure.

## English quiz and lesson alignment

The current English quiz in `lib/course-modules.ts` also contains the held claims. Quiz 1 (lines 232–240) treats a north-facing wall that radiates heat as the best position for a frost-tender pawpaw, and its rationale says the wall’s day heat is released at night when frost damage happens. The proposal replaces this with a sunny spot outside a known frost hollow after checking the site's frost pattern, while saying this may reduce risk rather than guarantee safety. Quiz 2 (lines 243–251) asks what happens when a farmer installs 2 m shade cloth and gives “shades the garden most of the day” as the correct outcome; the proposal instead asks what to check before fixing shade cloth and makes actual winter shadow observation the correct answer.

No corresponding isiZulu quiz questions or rationales appear in the narration file. Please confirm whether the app presents these quizzes to isiZulu learners, and whether an isiZulu assessment exists elsewhere. ## Reviewer handoff

Please ask a first-language isiZulu reviewer familiar with South African smallholder farming to:

- Confirm that the working meanings and claim strengths in this packet accurately describe the existing isiZulu lines.
- Check that any later correction uses ordinary spoken farming isiZulu and remains understandable to a learner using a phone alone.
- Review local terms for north/south-facing slopes, a low hollow, frost pocket, shade/shadow, and a wall that may add warmth. Confirm that borrowed terms such as “shade cloth” and “frost” are understood locally; do not coin technical terms.
- Preserve the existing pawpaw and young citrus references without adding species, temperature figures, or frost guarantees.
- Confirm the distinction between observing local conditions and predicting a universal outcome for a wall or shade structure.
- Record reviewer identity/role, date, specific edits, and what was actually reviewed. Language review alone is not agronomic or site-specific frost advice.

This packet changes no learner-facing isiZulu, quiz, narration, audio, still, animation or manifest. It does not authorize translation, recording or publication. Recheck against the final authorized English source before any later ZU edit.
