## Priority corrections

| Slide | Draft issue | Current English teaching to preserve |
|---|---|---|
| 3 — Learning outcomes | Adds a fixed 500 m² farm, a required pioneer sequence, and a guaranteed rationale for the design. | Learners name the seven commonly described layers, check plants against their site, local ecology and approved project list, and plan care without assuming a fixed harvest or canopy-closure date. Remove the area and promised sequence. |
| 5 — Seven planting layers | The draft asks learners to look from canopy down to roots and mentions climbers, but never names the full set. | The English narration and current slide 5 still name/key **Tall canopy, Smaller trees, Woody shrubs, Herbaceous plants, Ground cover, Root crops and Climbers**. The sequence is a planning vocabulary, not seven measured height bands. Keep each category represented; the current still is a labelled key, not a video. |
| 6 — The seven layers | Adds fixed height claims (`10m+`, `4–8m`, `1–3m`, and under `1m`) and says each layer has its own place for efficient light/moisture use. | The current lesson explicitly says heights and spacing depend on plants and site and that layers are planning categories, not fixed height bands. Remove the unsourced measurements and avoid presenting light/moisture use as guaranteed or non-competing. Climbers use suitable supports; root crops grow below the surface. |
| 7 — Highveld example | Presents the example as a productive combination and changes the wording around the existing proper-name example. | The English script labels it an **original Highveld example**, not a planting recommendation. It requires identity, frost tolerance, mature-size and local-restriction checks. Preserve the source's proper names and spellings exactly; do not add, rename or normalize plant names in translation. |
| 8 — Care as plants grow | States that the first two to three years are a fixed work phase and the canopy closes during years three to five. | The English lesson gives no establishment or canopy-closure calendar. It asks growers to check moisture, weeds, damage, competition and access, then prune, thin or adjust lower planting when observation calls for it. Remove the fixed years and any implication that the system becomes self-care. |

## Quiz and media comparison

The current L1 quiz in `lib/course-modules.ts` asks what should guide action when
weeds compete with young lower-layer plants. Its keyed response is to check the
affected plants and manage competition; the rationale rejects a fixed calendar.
The second question says shade and leaf litter **can** reduce surface water loss;
it rejects guaranteed groundwater access, guaranteed yield and stopping watering.
The isiZulu script has no quiz translation. Preserve these conditional meanings
when a quiz translation is prepared; do not add a year, yield or care-free result.

The current L1 slide 5 asset is the labelled static key described in
[`food-forest-l1-lesson-check.md`](../media/studies-animation-quality/food-forest-l1-lesson-check.md).
The lesson player has no video or Watch control, and the previously authored
seven-layer tour is held outside the player. The current audio manifest and
audio files are English-only; no isiZulu Food Forest transcript, deck or audio
is registered. The draft's “Watch” slide title should therefore not be treated
as evidence of a current isiZulu video. The English transcript at slides 4–8
matches the current narration and carries the planning-layer and site-dependent
wording above.

## Source anchors

- [FAO, “Multilayer Cropping”](https://www.fao.org/4/x3996e/x3996e37.htm) describes layered home-garden planting as a pattern built over time and explicitly directs growers to local extension advice when suggested crops may not suit their area. It supports a flexible framework, not the draft's fixed height bands or a universal species arrangement.
- The current [L1 English lesson check](../media/studies-animation-quality/food-forest-l1-lesson-check.md) records the seven labels, site-dependent spacing, local identity/restriction/frost checks, the current static still, and the media hold. Its FAO anchors support multi-layer planting and matching management to local conditions.
- No primary source or current English lesson supports the draft's stated layer heights or establishment/canopy years. Do not replace them with other numbers.
