# Food Forest Design L3 — isiZulu review packet

**Status: unpublished review draft.** The current isiZulu narration is stale against the corrected English lesson. Do not publish or record it until the wording below is reconciled and reviewed by a fluent isiZulu-speaking farmer or agricultural educator. The cardboard and mulch advice also needs practical review in a South African smallholder setting. No fluent-language, farmer, practitioner or learner approval is claimed.

## Priority corrections

- **Slide 3:** “Hlela ukulandelana kokutshala epulazini elingu-500m²” retains a fixed plot size that is absent from the current English lesson. Remove the 500 m² requirement.
- **Slide 16:** The old title says “Year One on a 500m² Plot.” Its narration prescribes a 20 cm wood-chip layer and pioneer spacing of 2 m. Neither figure appears in the current English lesson; remove both rather than translating them as recommendations.
- **Slide 16 and field action:** “I-cardboard imboza utshani obukhona … futhi yondle izidalwa zomhlabathi” can read as a certain grass-killing result and a promised soil-feeding outcome. The current English quiz says suitable cardboard **can help suppress** grass by blocking light and asks the learner to check for regrowth. Use equally qualified language; do not promise eradication.
- **Slide 18:** “Ungalokothi utshale emhlabathini owomile,” the claim that transplant shock and drought stress rarely allow recovery, and the fifth-year harvest claim exceed the current English guidance. English now asks growers to choose a suitable opportunity from soil moisture and expected weather, check root-zone moisture, retain backup watering, avoid waterlogged ground, and makes no fixed harvest-year promise.
- **Slides 8, 13–15, 20:** The draft still contains fixed establishment and canopy-closing years, an unsupported 30% indigenous-species target, mandatory planting order, and a promised reduction in outside inputs. Reconcile these with current English before translation approval: sequence follows site conditions, support plants are pruned when observation calls for it, and no percentage or fixed establishment/harvest outcome is taught.

## Mulch and field-use review

Check that the isiZulu wording preserves the current conditions: use plain cardboard only where appropriate and under suitable mulch; keep water able to enter the soil; leave trunks clear; observe grass regrowth; and adjust care to the site. Review whether “sheet-mulch,” “cardboard,” and “wood chips” are clear to the intended learners, without turning the example into a required material, depth, spacing, calendar or guaranteed result. The current lesson asks learners to check the site, available water and care before starting.
