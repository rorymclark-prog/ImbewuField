## Source holds for review

- Seven food-forest layers are planning roles, not fixed heights. Height and spacing depend on plant and site; plants compete for light, water and nutrients.
- The Highveld species arrangement is an example, not planting permission. Confirm each plant’s identity, frost tolerance, mature size and restrictions against the approved local list. Warm-region examples are not automatically suitable or edible; confirm identity and safe use.
- Slide 10's isiZulu draft qualifies the English sentence “Climate decides which species belong” as “climate helps determine which plants may suit a site,” consistent with the slide's site-by-site checks. The English/isiZulu wording alignment requires source-owner resolution.
- Indigenous planting is local and ecosystem-specific; this lesson sets no percentage. Healthy natural grassland should be protected.
- Plain cardboard under suitable mulch may suppress unwanted growth where appropriate; it does not guarantee grass removal. Keep water entering soil and trunks clear.
- Pruning/support plants and establishment depend on species, conditions and available care. Check root-zone moisture and retain backup watering; avoid waterlogged ground. No fixed harvest or fifth-year outcome is claimed.
