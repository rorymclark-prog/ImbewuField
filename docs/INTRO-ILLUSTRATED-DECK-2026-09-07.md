## Source corrections

Eight English blocks changed: **9, 13, 14, 15, 16, 18, 19, 20**. The corresponding isiZulu blocks are reconciled drafts, still requiring a first-language farmer's review. The lesson bodies and relevant quiz explanations were updated too.

- **9:** attribute the twelve-principle framework to David Holmgren; present the selected three as the course's starting point, not an unsupported ranking for all South African farms.
- **13:** diversity can spread risk; mixed crops are not immune to hail and no harvest is guaranteed. The animation brief requires damage on both plots.
- **14:** keep poultry off growing food; acknowledge fresh-manure contamination and a safe interval before the next harvest. No unsourced waiting-period number was invented.
- **15:** zones describe use and care, not a fixed weekly timetable for crops or animals. A small plot need not contain all zones; woodland and forage replace the misleading blanket placement of fruit trees in Zone 4.
- **16:** remove the claim that every herb requires daily picking. Close access supports frequent use and checking; distance is not the only cause of poor plant performance.
- **18–19:** remove unsupported province-wide weather directions and month claims. North-west wind is explicitly an example; learners observe their own site. A windbreak is not a firebreak.
- **20:** zones follow actual paths and land; they need not be circles.

## Sources used

- [Holmgren Design — What is permaculture?](https://holmgren.com.au/permaculture/what-is-permaculture/) and [David Holmgren principles, Permaculture Association](https://www.permaculture.org.uk/principles/david-holmgren-principles): framework and attribution.
- [Permaculture Association — Zoning](https://www.permaculture.org.uk/design-methods/zoning): zones organised by frequency of use and maintenance, with typical activities rather than fixed care schedules.
- [University of Minnesota Extension — Growing herbs](https://extension.umn.edu/garden-and-home/yard-and-garden/gardening-in-minnesota/growing-herbs): harvest as needed. Local seasonal advice was not transferred from Minnesota to South Africa.
- [University of Nevada Cooperative Extension — Using Chicken Manure Safely](https://psdocs.spes.vt.edu/consumer/3_using_chicken_manure.pdf): raw poultry manure may carry pathogens. No US certification interval was presented as South African law.

The hail and weather edits remove unsupported guarantees and geographic generalisations; they are not new quantified crop-loss or weather claims. This is a focused content correction, not a completed agronomic audit of all modules.
