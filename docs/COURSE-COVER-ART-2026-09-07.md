# Illustrated course covers — production record

Nine opening illustrations were generated for the remaining modules, using the
existing English Seeds cover as the visual reference. These are narrative scenes,
not verified technical diagrams. They make the openings consistent while the
teaching illustrations and demonstrations remain separate production work.

Reference: `public/course-decks/seeds-sovereignty/en/slide-01.jpg`.
Destination: `public/course-decks/<module>/en/cover.jpg`.
The generated originals were preserved; project copies were encoded as JPEG
with quality 86 and mozjpeg. No raster painting or compositing was used to alter
the generated scenes. All nine covers were visually inspected.

## Reproduction prompt

Use the Seeds cover reference to make a 16:9 ImbewuField home-study module cover.
Keep the warm cream background, dark green serif title, small rust-coloured
eyebrow and restrained ochre rule. Reserve roughly the left 42% for the title and
the right 58% for an illustrated rural KwaZulu-Natal farming scene. The setting
includes a modest homestead, local hills and adult Black South African growers.
Use the reference’s detailed, warm, naturalistic illustration rather than a
generic corporate stock photograph. Make the title clearly readable and keep
the composition calm. Show the scene described below. This is an opening
illustration; do not invent technical dimensions, engineering instructions or
agronomic labels. Eyebrow: “IMBEWUFIELD · HOME STUDY”. Footer:
“OBSERVE · PRACTISE · RECORD”, except Water’s “OBSERVE · PLAN · CARE”.

## Review boundary

The covers establish setting and visual continuity. A chicken enclosure in cover
art is not a housing specification; a garden scene is not proof of crop or species
suitability. Technical teaching needs its own source-matched drawing, review and
clear labels. No isiZulu cover was generated or represented as reviewed here.
