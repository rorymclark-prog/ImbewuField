# MEL feature audit and concept-note reference

Updated: 6 September 2026. MEL means monitoring, evaluation and learning.
This is a capability audit, not proof of project outcomes. See FEATURE-REGISTER.md
for deployment status. “Existing” refers to an app workflow, not a fully audited
end-to-end service or a finding verified by a funder.

| Traditional MEL tool | Existing foundation | What to add or verify |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Theory of change / results framework | Project milestones and progress views | Link inputs, activities, outputs and outcomes; record assumptions and external factors |
| Indicator register and MEL plan | Harvest, areas, attendance and progress figures | Per-indicator definition, baseline, target, unit, source, owner, frequency and disaggregation |
| Baseline, midline and endline | Assessment templates and published summaries | Matched participant comparisons, response rates, missing-data handling and comparison periods |
| Training evaluation | Registers, venue evidence and course assessments | Pre/post knowledge comparisons, later practice adoption and facilitator reflection |
| Field monitoring | Mentor assignments, basic visit records, site assessment and photos | Expanded visit evidence, agreed actions, due dates and follow-up completion |
| Qualitative evaluation | Feedback and assessment comments | Interview/focus-group guides, consent-aware change stories and outcome harvesting |
| Beneficiary accountability | Feedback and feature/bug submission | Response deadlines, closure feedback and accessible assisted submission |
| Learning and adaptation | App/project assessment stages; product feedback | Link finding to decision, owner, app release or programme change, then reassess in the next project |
| Data quality assessment | Access boundaries and publication workflows | Accuracy, completeness, timeliness, consistency checks, duplicate detection and a review trail |
| Evaluation planning | Programme reports and evidence | Evaluation questions, appropriate methods, timing, independence and limitations |
| Value and efficiency | Recorded sales, expenses, harvest and production area | Crop-specific retail replacement value; verified programme cost denominators; avoid double counting |

## How this should appear in the app

Use **Assessments & learning** as the common entry point. Show all instrument
categories, completion status, respondents, date, project/cohort/area, evidence,
findings and follow-up actions. Templates must be labelled as available tools;
never count their presence as a completed assessment. Funders see authorised,
organisation-published findings. Private respondent records remain restricted.
App feedback belongs here as learning that can improve the next programme; a
feature request is not itself an achieved project outcome.

## Funder metrics and valuation

Keep the headline concise: gardens, unique farmers/households, food grown,
planted area, milestone progress and evidence completeness for a stated period.
The current farmer count must not be called a livelihood improvement. Distinct
mentor, farm and national sample registers must not be added together.

A proposed **estimated retail replacement value** is the sum of each recorded
crop quantity multiplied by its dated local retail price in compatible units.
Store crop, quantity, market/location, date, price source and coverage. Show how
much produce has no price. Do not apply one generic price to mixed tonnes.

Show separately: actual sales revenue; produce kept, donated or sold; recorded
costs; and estimated replacement value. Replacement value is not cash income,
profit, proven household savings or SROI. Avoid counting sold produce twice in a
combined benefit figure. Targets and before/after comparisons need like-for-like
periods and an explicit baseline.

## Concept-note wording

ImbewuField brings garden planning, field records, training evidence, assessments
and role-appropriate reporting into one workspace. Implementing organisations
can review programme information and share authorised summaries with funders,
while mentors support assigned growers. Demonstration workspaces let users
explore example records, designs and reports without altering a live project.

The next development phase will strengthen results frameworks, indicator
tracking, data-quality review and the link between learning and programme
improvements. Offline capability will be assessed and tested on target devices;
the concept note should not yet promise that all records, photos, maps and AI
services work without connectivity.

## Reference framework

- IFRC monitoring and evaluation guide: https://www.ifrc.org/document/monitoring-and-evaluation-guide
- OECD evaluation criteria (relevance, coherence, effectiveness, efficiency, impact and sustainability): https://www.oecd.org/en/topics/sub-issues/development-co-operation-evaluation-and-effectiveness/evaluation-criteria.html
- FAO guidance on valuing food consumed from own production: https://www.fao.org/4/i0430e/i0430e04.pdf

These guide the proposed approach. They do not certify the application or imply
that every tool is appropriate for every project.
