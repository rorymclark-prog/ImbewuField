# Soil artwork production prompts

Built-in image generation was used, with the Seeds-style compost illustration as the style and
recurring-character reference. Final reusable artwork is under `public/course-art/soil-health/`.
The jar-layer SVG is a deterministic diagram rather than generated imagery.

Shared direction: landscape 16:9, warm hand-drawn educational art, cream paper texture, fine brown
outlines, earthy greens and browns, rural South African homestead. Keep the recurring African woman
in an olive apron and teal headscarf. One clear practical action at phone size; natural anatomy,
tool grip and scale. No text, logos, watermarks, invented numbers, magical growth or glowing nutrients.
Titles and captions are composed separately from the shared wordless image.

- `compost-building.jpg`: Show the woman kneeling and adding a handful of greens to a substantial
  square-footprint compost heap with a flat top, two adjoining straight sides and visible corner.
  Thick loose brown layers alternate with much thinner greens; natural softly sloped edges, not a
  compressed cube. Keep the wheelbarrow of dry browns parked behind, the separate materials piles,
  basket and upright watering can. Do not turn it into a round mound or shallow mulch mat.
- `compost-monitoring.jpg`: The same square layered heap, with the woman standing and checking a
  long compost thermometer inserted into the central body. Plain dial without invented numbers.
  Retain the separate materials tray, closed waste bucket and parked wheelbarrow. No smoke or heat glow.
- `soil-observation.jpg`: Crouching beside a small soil-inspection hole, she holds crumbly soil with
  fine roots in one hand and compacted clods in the other. Use similar brown colours; structure is
  the comparison. Include a trowel, plain cloth and blank notebook. No giant worms or colour-only diagnosis.
- `soil-life.jpg`: Close observational view of a hand lifting loose topsoil with fine roots, decomposing
  leaf fragments and a small naturally proportioned earthworm. This is a tactile scene, not a microscopic
  diagram or a claim that one organism proves soil health.
- `jar-test.jpg`: At an outdoor table she holds a securely closed jar of muddy water and fine soil
  with both hands, preparing to shake it. Show soil sample, water jug, plain dish-soap bottle and blank
  notebook. No gravel, invented measuring marks or implication that the contents are drinking water.
- `mulching.jpg`: She places loose dry grass between growing plants, leaving stems and crowns clear.
  Close three-quarter view of the bed, with a short bare section for comparison and parked wheelbarrow.
- `mature-compost.jpg`: She uses a small scoop to spread dark crumbly mature compost over a bed,
  with matching compost in the wheelbarrow. Keep plant crowns clear. No liquid fertilizer or instant growth.
- `cover-crops.jpg`: A harvested main-crop field is protected by low living cover. She walks along a
  separate edge path. Generic plant forms, not a species-identification plate; no calendar dates or frost guarantees.
- `worm-farm.jpg`: Under a shaded verandah she lifts a ventilated worm-bin lid and damp paper cover.
  Show moist bedding, a modest amount of raw peelings and a few small compost worms. Keep the collector
  tap closed, with finished crumbly castings in a separate tray. No leachate extraction or application.

The square-heap correction is Rory's explicit construction reference and applies to animation too.
The wattle-pod identification picture is pending a verified reference rather than a generated guess.
