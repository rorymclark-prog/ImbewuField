## Facilitation and practice setting

Use a notebook, calculator and the event cards below. Learners may point, sort or explain aloud. Read each card aloud before introducing a table. Do not ask anyone to reveal household income, borrowing, customer details or bank records to a group.

**Every amount below is invented solely for arithmetic practice.** These are neither local prices nor recommended budgets. “Day 1” through “Day 7” are an imaginary week. There is one practice farm, one owner and one cash tin. No bank account, tax, opening debt, opening customer balance, refunds or other cash movements are in this example. The opening R1,000 is counted money already in the tin, not a new sale. The cash result is complete for this deliberately bounded example; its production costs and profit are not complete.

Show each card's reference beside the written entry. All events and amounts are also available in [f1-practice.json](f1-practice.json). The JSON uses whole cents to keep arithmetic exact; learner-facing materials use rand and cents.

## F1.1 — Household and farm money

**Outcome:** Explain why money can enter or leave the farm without being a produce sale or an ordinary production cost.

**Opening picture:** A grower has a basket, a household shopping bag and a farm notebook on a table. Ask: “Which things belong in the farm record?” Accept different answers before discussing what each item actually represents. Do not use clothing, home quality or gender to imply financial competence.

**Narration draft:** “Follow what happened. A buyer paid for produce. The owner added some of their own money. The farm borrowed money. All three can put money in the tin, but they are different events. Write the reason beside each amount. When the owner takes money home, record that too. Do not hide it among packaging or transport. The farm and household both matter. Clear records help you see how they support each other.”

**Demonstration:** Put down the opening-balance card: R1,000. Add C01, the owner's R300 contribution. Count R1,300. Put C01 under “Owner added money”, not “Produce sold”. Remove C02, R120 paid for packaging: R1,180 remains. Add C03, R200 cash sale: R1,380 remains. Point back to the reasons, not only the final total.

**Guided sorting:** Give learners C05, C06, C08 and C10 below. Use the headings “Household withdrawal”, “Borrowing”, “Equipment purchase” and “Loan repayment and interest”. Ask which change cash. All four do. Ask whether any is another produce sale. None is. Do not calculate final accounting profit from these headings. Loan principal, interest and equipment need different treatment in full accounts; F6 and F7 develop that distinction.

**Independent check:** A grower sells produce for R90 and then takes R30 home. Ask separately: “What was the sale?” and “How much of that cash remains at the farm?” Answers: R90 sale; R60 remains from that receipt. R60 is not the sale value and is not a calculation of profit. Then ask where the R30 should be visible: in the household-withdrawal record.

**Feedback:** If the learner reports a R60 sale, place the buyer's R90 payment card beside the R30 withdrawal card. Keep both events visible and ask again. If the learner calls the contribution a sale, ask what produce the owner bought. In this card they bought none.

**Field task:** Make headings for farm sales, farm purchases, owner contributions and household withdrawals in a notebook. Use a private record or new practice cards. No personal amounts need to be shared in class.

## F1.2 — Cash, sales, amounts owed and profit

**Outcome:** Follow a credit sale into a later payment without counting it twice, and explain why closing cash is not enough to establish profit.

**Narration draft:** “A buyer can take produce today and pay later. Record the sale and what the buyer owes. When the payment arrives, match it to that sale. The payment clears the amount owed; it is not a second basket sold. Cash answers how much money is available. Profit needs the income and costs for the period. Some costs or stock information may still be missing. Say what you know and what you still need.”

**Demonstration in three views:** Make three cards: “Sales”, “Cash tin” and “Buyer owes”. Start after C03, with sales R200, cash R1,380 and nothing owed. Read C04: delivered produce worth R150, buyer pays later. Sales become R350; buyer owes R150; cash stays R1,380. Do not move a cash token. Later, read C07: buyer pays that same R150. Move the owed token to “Settled” and add cash once. Sales remain R350. Keep the invoice reference S02 on both cards so the connection is visible.

**Guided calculation:** Work through the event table in order. At the end, sales are R470, cash received from buyers is R350 and buyers still owe R120. Ask the learner to point to S03, which explains that difference. Do not call the R1,380 closing cash profit: it includes opening cash, the owner's contribution and borrowing, and this pack does not supply all production costs, stock or equipment-use information.

**Independent check:** A buyer receives produce for R240. They pay R100 now and agree to pay R140 later. Ask for sale value, payment received and amount still owed. Answers: R240, R100 and R140. The current app has paid-in-full/unpaid invoice status, not a partial-payment ledger. Keep this exercise in the workbook; do not mark the full invoice paid or create another sale to force the app to show the balance.

**Feedback:** If cash is given as R240, cover the unpaid R140 card and ask what money arrived. If sales are given as R340, place the payment beneath its existing sale instead of adding it as a new sale. Check with a new example, R180 sale and R60 received: R120 remains owed.

**App companion:** Read [Make and manage an invoice](https://imbewufield.vercel.app/student/guides/invoices). In the existing sample tour, identify the unpaid/paid-in-full choice and the route for linking an existing sale. Do not enter this entire workbook as crop sales: borrowing and household transfers are different events. The published guide currently teaches text and practice, not a narrated screen recording.

**Field task:** Using practice or redacted evidence, connect one sale to its payment. Preserve both dates when they differ. If the payment cannot be found, keep the status unresolved and name the evidence you need.

## F1.3 — Keep an evidence trail

**Outcome:** Reopen a record, locate its supporting reference, and correct a duplicate or wrong unit without inventing a missing value.

**Narration draft:** “Give each record a date and a clear reason. Keep the receipt, invoice or another useful reference. After saving, open the entry again and compare it with the source. Check the amount and the unit. A kilogram and a bunch are different. If evidence is missing, write what is unknown. Do not use zero to mean ‘I do not know’. Keep a note of a correction so you can explain it later.”

**Demonstration:** Place S02, “Delivered sale R150 on Day 3”, beside C07, “Payment R150 on Day 6 for S02”. Draw a line between their references. A duplicate card says “New produce sale R150 on Day 6” but shows the same payment reference. Ask what new delivery it proves. None. On the practice worksheet, cross out the duplicate, retain a short correction note and recalculate sales. Do not prescribe deleting real transactions without checking linked invoice behaviour.

**Guided inspection:** Give these independent cards: (a) a receipt says 2.5 kg, entry says 2.5 bunches; (b) source says R12.50, entry says R125; (c) transport receipt missing, entry says zero. Learners identify the unit, decimal and missing-evidence errors. Correct (a) and (b) from their sources. For (c), leave the cost unknown until evidence is obtained; a plausible amount is not evidence. These are new error-finding exercises, not additional events in the cash table below.

**Independent check:** Hand the learner one correct record and one altered copy. Ask them to find the changed date or unit, identify the source that resolves it, and explain their correction. Then ask them to reopen the saved practice entry. A save message is not enough for this exercise: compare the reopened amount and unit too. A pending sync message is not proof that another device has the entry.

**Feedback and transfer:** If a learner can repeat the rule but misses the changed record, simplify to two fields and then reintroduce the date. Success requires finding an error in a fresh example, not reciting “check the receipt”.

**Field task:** Build a small evidence folder or envelope, with references that match the notebook. Use sample records if private material cannot be redacted. Keep actual customer details out of classroom screenshots.
