# F4 - Budget and manage cash through the season

Status: three full English facilitator drafts with synthetic practice cards, an interactive cash timeline and checked answers. **Reserve only**: not public, translated, narrated or accredited. Bookkeeping and learner review remain open. This follows F3 in the [24-lesson finance pathway](../financial-course.md). See [sources and limits](f4-sources.md), the [four-page learner workbook](../../../../output/pdf/finance-f4-workbook.pdf), and the [interactive cash timeline](../../../../output/html/finance-f4-timeline.html).

## Prepare the session

Bring a calculator, paper, date cards and counters. Read one card aloud at a time. Learners may point, move cards, explain aloud or ask a partner to write. Use the timeline for demonstration, then hide its answers for independent practice. Do not require anyone to share private household accounts, customer names or bank details.

All dates, amounts, working hours and offers are **invented classroom inputs**. January-March 2027 is a practice horizon, not a recommended South African growing season. Activities A and B have no specified crop, area or yield. Do not infer one. This is a new exercise, not a continuation of F1-F3 balances. The opening cash tin has R120.00 and the bank R180.00, both assumed available, with no restrictions or fees in the supplied model. The separate opening amounts owed are R120.00 by a customer and R80.00 to a supplier. Neither is opening cash.

A negative projected balance means the proposed sequence cannot be paid from the specified available funds. It is a warning to revise the plan or establish an appropriate, evidenced funding arrangement. It is not cash that exists, an approved overdraft or permission to miss a bill. Later balances describe an **unfunded projection**, not evidence that the intervening transactions could happen. Exact shortfall amounts are model results, not recommended loans or contingency reserves.

## F4.1 - Build an enterprise and whole-farm budget

**Outcome:** Combine two activity budgets without counting shared bills twice, distinguish cash spending from unpaid resource use, and identify missing costs and capacity evidence.

**Opening scene:** Put two baskets on the table for Activities A and B. Place one equipment-hire card between them. Ask whether starting the second activity creates a second copy of that same bill. Ask what information would be needed before promising both activities can run together.

**Narration draft:** “A budget is a plan with reasons behind the numbers. Name the activity and the period. Show what you expect to sell and what you expect to use. Keep shared bills once in the farm total. Family work still matters when no wage is paid. Write down what you do not yet know. A positive answer on paper does not prove that the land, water, time or buyers are available.”

**Demonstration:** The source cards give expected sales of R600.00 for A and R500.00 for B. Direct inputs are R220.00 and R180.00. Share the R120.00 hire using recorded planned hours, two for A and four for B. Share the R90.00 trip using two and one equal-size crate spaces. A receives R40.00 hire and R60.00 trip; B receives R80.00 and R30.00. Combined shared allocations remain R210.00, not R420.00.

The budget's specified cash inputs total R610.00. Separately show four and six hours of unpaid family work at the exercise comparison value of R25.00/hour, giving R250.00. That rate is a hypothetical comparison, not a wage recommendation or assertion of outside earning opportunities. After these included costs the two activities show R240.00 in planned surplus. Land, owned-equipment use, administration, finance costs, tax and other missing costs prevent this from establishing full farm profit. Do not add the time comparison to the cash payments.

**Guided practice:** Build the A and B columns and reconcile their sum to the original shared bills. Explain why paying R250.00 for equipment in March is a cash movement but does not establish a R250.00 cost of equipment use for this activity period. The value of its use and accounting treatment need separate evidence and bookkeeping review. Household withdrawal is also a cash movement, not automatically a crop input.

**Capacity discussion:** A whole-farm plan also needs the actual availability and timing of land, water, labour, tools and markets. Two activities cannot each be assigned all of the same scarce resource at the same time. This pack supplies cost-sharing hours, not proof that a particular tool or worker is available on the required dates. Keep those checks unresolved until evidence exists. Do not invent a yield from the sales value.

**Independent attempt:** A new shared R60.00 bill is divided by recorded use of one unit and two units. Allocate it, then check the total. A learner says a R500.00 funding application makes the two activities profitable: ask them to identify approval, cash receipt and whether funding is a sale. Do not assume it is a grant or loan when the terms are absent.

**Feedback:** If the farm total includes both the shared bill and its allocations, physically remove the duplicate card. If unpaid family time becomes a cash payment, ask for payment evidence. If a missing cost becomes zero, replace the zero with an unresolved source request. Give a fresh allocation after feedback.

**App connection and field task:** The [expense companion](https://imbewufield.vercel.app/student/guides/expenses) helps find actual recorded costs. It does not create this forecast. Keep the proposed budget in the workbook, label actual evidence and estimates, and give each estimate a source date. Bring one redacted quote and one capacity question for local review.

## F4.2 - Put receipts and payments on their dates

**Outcome:** Calculate a dated cash projection, carry closing balances forward, and detect a shortfall hidden by a positive month-end balance.

**Narration draft:** “Start with money that is available at the beginning. Add a receipt when you expect the money to arrive. Subtract a payment when it must leave. Carry the balance to the next date. An invoice is not cash received. Check the dates between month ends too. A good total at the end cannot pay a bill that comes before the money arrives.”

**Demonstration:** Start at R300.00. Pay the planned R220.00 on 5 January: R80.00 remains. The next planned R180.00 payment on 10 January produces a projected minus R100.00. Stop the cards here and ask how that payment could be made. No borrowing facility or extra money has been supplied. Continue the calculation as a clearly labelled unfunded projection, not as an instruction to pay unavailable cash. The R120.00 hire on 20 January takes it to minus R220.00; the R600.00 receipt on 25 January brings it to R380.00.

The first shortage is 10 January and the deepest base-case shortage is R220.00 after the 20 January payment. January nevertheless closes at positive R380.00. A monthly closing-only check misses the earlier gap. If a receipt and payment share a real date, check which clears first; this exercise avoids same-date ambiguity. Combined available funds also do not prove the right payment account has accessible funds at the required moment.

**Guided practice:** Continue through February and March. Opening balance plus receipts minus payments equals closing balance. February opens with January's R380.00, not the original R300.00. March's R120.00 collection concerns a customer balance from before the exercise; it is a cash receipt now, not another current-season sale. Paying the old R80.00 supplier bill also needs cash now even though it is not one of these current activity inputs.

Set aside X01, the R50.00 transfer from bank to cash tin: both are already inside the combined cash scope, and the supplied transfer has no fee. It changes location, not the combined amount. Set aside X02, the unapproved R500.00 application. Keep the two issued invoices X03/X04 as links to expected buyer receipts; do not count each invoice and its eventual receipt as two inflows. Family-time comparison is not a payment either.

**Independent attempt:** In a separate example, open with R90.00; pay R130.00 on Day 2, receive R80.00 on Day 10 and pay R20.00 on Day 20. Find closing cash, lowest projected cash and the first date requiring a response. Then ask whether the closing answer is complete if an additional payment fee is known to apply but its amount is missing.

**Feedback:** For a learner who subtracts the net inflow, use counters: opening R10.00, receipt R5.00 and payment R3.00 leaves R12.00. For a learner who sees only the positive ending, replay one card at a time and stop at the first negative projection. For an invoice counted as cash, place its promised receipt on the later date. Ask for a new dated example after correction.

**App connection and field task:** The live records/chart series summarises recorded events and has its own payment and missing-data boundaries. It is not this dated forecast or an available-bank-balance guarantee. Keep invented examples and future expected receipts out of actual My Records. Use the [invoice companion](https://imbewufield.vercel.app/student/guides/invoices) to check an existing unpaid invoice and its source, without marking it paid to make the plan balance. Build a short redacted forecast with exact dates; reconcile its opening funds and list restrictions or timing uncertainty.

## F4.3 - Test changes and make a reasoned response

**Outcome:** Change timing, amounts and uncertainty separately, identify combined stress, and explain a response that is feasible before the first shortfall.

**Narration draft:** “Ask what changes if the buyer pays late, less produce sells, or an input costs more. Change the matching card and recalculate from that point onward. A delay changes when cash arrives. A lower sale changes how much arrives. Test them together too. Write down the evidence needed for your response, and check the plan again before committing.”

**Demonstration:** In LATE, move only A's R600.00 receipt from 25 January to 28 February. Do not make a new receipt or change the sale value. The March closing remains R430.00, but the deepest gap grows from R220.00 to R310.00, after the 5 February trip payment. Equal end totals do not imply equal ability to pay on time.

In HIGH, increase B's input payment from R180.00 to R240.00, with no other change. In COMBINED, apply this increase and the late A receipt together. Do not add the individual scenario balances together: each scenario starts from the same opening position and recomputes the changed events. The combined deepest gap is R370.00 and the closing balance is R370.00.

**Guided practice:** LOW reduces B's expected sales and receipt from R500.00 to R350.00; this is a lower-sales case, not a claim that a R500.00 debt has been settled in full. OUTSIDE moves B's same R500.00 receipt to 20 April, beyond the 31 March horizon. It remains an expected amount owed, not vanished money; exclude it from cash received inside the horizon. UNKNOWN removes B's input quote. Later exact balances and the deepest gap become unknown, not larger positive balances caused by treating a blank as free.

**Compare responses:** DEFER moves the proposed R250.00 equipment purchase to 10 April, assuming the purchase date can actually be changed by agreement. It improves the March closing figure to R680.00 but leaves January's R220.00 gap. It therefore does not solve that earlier problem. A response must work before the shortage: possible options to investigate include changing the scale and associated costs/revenue, agreeing timing with the buyer or supplier, or identifying genuinely available funds. Do not simply delete essential inputs, assume a buyer pays early, cut basic household needs, or treat an unapproved application as available cash. Borrowing requires its full terms and a repayment plan; this unit recommends no lender or loan size.

**Independent decision:** Give the learner LATE and DEFER without the answer table. Ask which date first fails, whether the proposed equipment deferral solves it, and what evidence would be needed for a different response. Then present a real unknown quote: accept “cannot calculate yet” with a precise source request. Do not reward a guessed zero for producing a tidy budget.

**Feedback and field task:** A good response names the affected date and amount, what can actually change, what must be agreed, and the consequences for later costs, production and receipts. After corrective feedback, use fresh dates or values. For a redacted local practice plan, keep base and adverse cases side by side, label assumptions and set a review date. Compare actual evidence with the plan when it arrives; retain the original forecast rather than rewriting it to pretend it predicted the outcome.

## Assessment and media

The learner must explain, on a new example: the chosen cash scope; an opening balance with evidence; a receipt versus invoice date; a carried-forward balance; an earlier gap despite a later positive total; an internal transfer; an unresolved amount; and a proposed response that addresses the relevant date. Corrective feedback is followed by a different example, not a copied answer. This is practical learning evidence, not an accounting credential or certification of farm viability.

The companion timeline uses authored numbers and a dated step graph, with manual steps, play/pause, scenario selection and reduced-motion support. It is an English reserve teaching aid, not a production app chart. Its warm colour palette and reviewed homestead notebook illustration follow the existing course style; financial text and amounts are never AI-generated imagery. Show the gap as unresolved throughout the projection. Narration, fluent isiZulu, phone lesson integration and learner trial are still outstanding.
