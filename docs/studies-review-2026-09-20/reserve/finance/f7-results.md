## Shared practice case

A small market stall buys finished goods for resale. It is a separate exercise within the farm-business pathway, not a valuation of growing crops, animals, home-grown harvests or an entire homestead. All amounts and dates are invented. The period is 1–31 January 2027. Values below are supplied carrying amounts for the exercise; they are not market quotations. No VAT or income-tax calculation is included. Equipment classification, depreciation and stock-loss treatment are supplied assumptions, not policies to copy into real accounts.

At opening: cash R1000; customers owe R200; resale stock R300; equipment R600. The business owes suppliers R300 and has no loan. Total assets R2100 less liabilities R300 leaves opening equity R1800. Equity is the owner's residual interest, not a second cash account. There are no other opening balances in this bounded model.

## F7.1 — Three views of one business

**Learner outcome.** Point to the record that answers each question: what cash moved during a period; what income and costs belong to that period; and what the business owns and owes at a date. Explain why one total cannot answer all three.

**Plain narration.** “Start by saying which business and which dates you mean. Our stall starts with money, stock, equipment and amounts owed. A cash summary follows money received and paid. A profit-and-loss view follows the period's income and costs. A balance sheet shows the position on a date: assets, liabilities and what remains for the owner. The views are connected, but their totals need not match. Borrowed money increases cash and debt. It does not become a sale. A customer can owe us for goods already delivered. That sale can be recorded before the money reaches us.”

**Demonstration.** Lay out three spaces: Cash, Period result, Position. Show R01. Move R200 from “customer owes” to “cash”; do not add a January sale. Show S01: add R900 to sales and the customer balance; move R350 of stock into goods cost. Cash stays R950 after P01. The running result is R550 before later costs and checks. Then R02 moves R600 from the customer balance into cash. Sales remain R900; R300 remains owed. This model assumes the delivery and accepted invoice establish that sale; an order or an unfulfilled promise alone would not be the same evidence.

Continue with L01: cash and debt both rise R500, while the result stays unchanged. Use the interactive aid one card at a time. Invite the learner to predict which of the three views changes before advancing. Early cards show a running result from the evidence processed so far, not a completed month's profit.

**Guided practice.** Sort P02, E01, L02 and H01. For each, say what cash leaves and whether the supplied case records a period cost. P02 settles a supplier balance. E01 changes cash into equipment. L02 has two parts: principal reduces debt; the R10 interest is a period cost. H01 reduces cash and the owner's equity; it is not a business expense here. Do not generalise this personal withdrawal into a rule about salaries in every business form.

**Worked January summaries, after all checks.** Cash receipts total R1300: R200 old balance + R600 current receipt + R500 borrowing. Payments total R1190. Opening R1000 + R1300 − R1190 = closing cash R1110, an increase of R110. February's R300 receipt is excluded. This is a cash summary; it is not a standards-compliant statement of cash flows with all classifications and disclosures.

Sales R900 less goods supplied R350, interest R10, transport R80, utilities R60, stock loss R50 and depreciation R60 gives **R290 result before tax in this model**. No tax amount is inferred. Closing assets are cash R1110 + customers owing R300 + stock R300 + equipment R840 + prepayment R120 = R2670. Liabilities are supplier/service balances R330 + loan R400 = R730. Equity is R1940. Check R2670 = R730 + R1940, and R1800 opening equity + R290 result − R150 personal withdrawal = R1940.

**Independent attempt.** Begin another case: cash R400 and resale stock R120, no debts. Deliver the entire stock for a R300 credit sale, then receive R200 of that sale. Ask for cash, the customer balance, remaining stock, result and total assets. Answer: R600 cash, R100 owed, no stock remaining, R180 result and R700 assets. The opening equity R520 plus the R180 result also equals R700. The R200 receipt must not be counted as another sale.

**Corrective feedback.** If a learner calls R110 January profit, ask which cash movements were borrowing, equipment or a household withdrawal and which period costs had no January payment. If they add a receipt to sales twice, physically join the invoice and receipt with the same reference, then repeat the fresh example.

**Field task and app connection.** Choose one redacted recorded sale and its evidence. Say which part is a sale, receipt or unpaid amount. Read the Sales and Invoice app companions before changing an entry. Charts presents recorded information; it does not create this complete position, stock valuation or loan ledger. Keep these practice figures outside actual My Records.

## F7.2 — Close the period without hiding gaps

**Learner outcome.** Separate event, document and payment dates. Explain why stock, equipment, unpaid costs and future services matter at the period boundary. Keep an unsupported value unknown.

**Plain narration.** “A document can arrive after the work. Payment can happen before or after the service. Keep those dates together. At the end of our month, ask what was delivered, used, owed and still available. A bank line alone cannot answer every question. Check the stock and equipment records too. When evidence is missing, write what you still need. Do not enter zero just to finish a total.”

**Demonstration.** Place A01 across the January–February boundary. Work was completed in January at the agreed R80 charge. The invoice is dated February and the amount is unpaid at January end. Under this model, January includes the R80 cost and liability, while January cash does not change. Retain both documents and dates. Now place F01: cash leaves in January, but the whole storage service is for February. January includes a R120 prepayment asset, not a January storage cost. A real contract may need different treatment; this case supplies the timing and rights explicitly.

Show the stock bridge: opening R300 + purchases R400 − goods supplied R350 = R350 before the closing check. C01 supports a R50 loss and R300 remaining. This is a reviewed reconciliation, not an instruction to label every unexplained difference as theft or spoilage. Ask for evidence before choosing a cause or accounting treatment. Show the equipment bridge: opening R600 + purchase R300 − supplied period depreciation R60 = R840. The charge spreads cost in this model but is not a new payment and is not a tax allowance.

**Guided practice.** Run the missing-stock case. Withhold C01 entirely. The book shows R350 before review, but the physical count, valuation and adjustment are unknown. Cash R1110, customer balances R300, equipment R840, prepayment R120, payables R330 and loan R400 still have supplied support. **Adjusted stock, complete period result, total assets and equity are unknown.** Do not reuse R300 from the complete case after its evidence has been removed. Ask the learner to identify the precise document that would allow a reliable update.

**Independent attempt.** New case: start with cash R200 and no other balance. Pay R90 for a service supplied wholly next month, with the right unused at this month's end. Cash becomes R110; the prepayment is R90; the period result is R0; total assets remain R200. If the service period is missing from the document, the R90 payment is known but that classification is not established. Request the agreement and service dates rather than assuming all of it is a current expense.

**Corrective feedback.** If the learner puts A01 into February only because of the invoice date, point to the January service evidence. If they call every purchase an expense, compare stock still held, equipment and a service already used. If they equate a balanced equation with accurate accounts, show that a wrong classification can still balance: evidence and period checks are needed too.

**Field task and app connection.** Assemble a small period-end folder: opening balances, sales and deliveries, receipts, supplier bills, payments, stock evidence, equipment schedule, debt schedule and unresolved questions. Keep original references and dates. An export from the app is one input to that folder. Do not invent stock, depreciation or loan entries in a crop-sale form to force a chart to match this exercise.

## F7.3 — Prepare records and ask the right tax questions

**Learner outcome.** Assemble an evidence pack and find the appropriate current official guidance. Distinguish a readable app invoice from a verified compliant tax invoice. State what remains unconfirmed without guessing a tax rate, deadline or registration outcome.

**Plain narration.** “Keep the story behind a number. Who supplied what? When did it happen? Which document supports it? Which money movement belongs with it? Keep records organised and safe. Tax and record duties depend on the business and its circumstances. Use current SARS guidance and suitable help. A saved invoice, tax-reference field or app export cannot confirm all those duties for you.”

**Demonstration.** Use an invented pack, not a learner's private identifiers. Put an opening balance list, source documents, account statements, stock check, equipment and debt schedules, and unresolved items behind a one-page index. Give each a reference and period. Reconcile the index against the summaries. Mark missing items visibly. Keep actuals separate from forecasts. Do not ask learners to send private financial documents to a public class or to an unverified adviser.

Open the current official SARS pages in the source notes. Show how to record the page title, URL, date checked and the exact question. For example: which business form and registrations apply; what records are required; which retention conditions apply; whether an invoice has the required particulars for the relevant supply; and whether electronic storage arrangements meet the applicable requirements. This is an evidence-finding exercise, not a conclusion that a particular learner must register or can discard records. Retain source documents while questions remain unresolved. Do not teach a universal destruction date from a short web table.

**Guided practice.** Discuss four cards:

1. “The app printed a VAT number, so this is a valid tax invoice.” **Not established.** A field or title alone does not establish registration, supply treatment or all required particulars. Inspect the document with the applicable current guidance.
2. “I did not need to submit a return, so no records are needed.” **Not established.** Do not infer all record duties from that fact. Ask about the applicable requirements and any continuing audit, dispute or other obligation.
3. “My export is a complete set of accounts and guarantees a loan.” **Not established.** Check it against the other evidence. An export does not establish completeness, creditworthiness or approval.
4. “A missing stock value should be zero.” **Incorrect.** Request the count, valuation and reconciliation. Do not disguise missing information as a measured result.

**Independent attempt.** Give an invoice and cash export with no stock check, unpaid-customer list or equipment schedule. Ask the learner to prepare an index of supplied and missing evidence, name one specific question for a bookkeeper, and record a current SARS source to consult for one compliance question. Accept an oral explanation or a card sort. A satisfactory answer separates known documents from missing ones and does not claim the export proves profit, complete accounts or tax compliance. The answer must be based on the supplied pack, not private records.

**Corrective feedback.** If the learner asks for “the tax number that makes everything correct,” return to the supply, business status, document and current rules. If they try to fill gaps with plausible figures, ask them to label the gap and identify who or what can resolve it. Repeat with a different missing document.

**Field task and app connection.** Reopen the appropriate saved invoice and inspect its details without sending it. Use the invoice companion and, when available, the export companion. Prepare a private evidence index and questions for appropriate local help. Keep a readable copy and recovery plan for needed records. Do not assume a device-local document has synchronised to another device, or that a course completion badge certifies the business's accounts.
