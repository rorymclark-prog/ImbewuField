# F2 — Keep and reconcile useful records

Production status: three full English teaching drafts, learner practice and checked worked answers. ## Facilitation and practice boundaries

Use the [learner worksheet](f2-worksheet.md) or [four-page printable workbook](../../../../output/pdf/finance-f2-workbook.pdf), a calculator, a notebook and counters representing kilograms and money. Read one event aloud at a time. Let learners point, sort cards, explain aloud or ask someone to write their answer. Introduce the second money account only after the learner can follow the cash tin. Keep real household finances, buyer details and payment credentials out of group exercises and screenshots.

All weights, prices, fees and amounts in this pack are invented for arithmetic practice. They are not local market evidence, recommended fees, budgets or yields. This is a separate four-day practice period for the fictional farm; do not carry forward or add F1's balances. The opening position is given: 4 kg of carrots already stored, R500.00 in a cash tin and R100.00 in a mobile-money wallet, with no buyer balance owed. No field area or production period is supplied, so no yield per area can be calculated.

The exercise tracks the same produce in comparable weighed condition. It excludes purchases, processing, refunds, unknown shrinkage, pending transfers, tax, loans and equipment. In real work, identify additional movements and changes in weighing conditions rather than forcing them into this example. No numerical loss allowance is recommended. Exact quantities are stored as whole grams and money as whole cents in [f2-practice.json](f2-practice.json); the teaching tables use kg and rand.

## F2.1 — Harvest, stock and destinations

**Outcome:** Trace one product from opening stock and harvest into its recorded destinations, then compare the calculated remainder with a separate count.

**Opening scene:** A grower places a weighed basket beside a notebook. Four destination cards read “Sold”, “Home”, “Gift” and “Loss”; an empty crate is labelled “Still here”. Ask: “If we picked it, did we sell it?” Let the learner name a different destination. Add “Stored from before” before doing the arithmetic so the harvest total is not mistaken for everything available.

**Narration draft:** “Start with what was already here. Add what you picked. Record what left and why. A sale, food for home and a gift are different uses. Keep the same unit. At the end, count or weigh what is still here. Compare that with the book. If they differ, look for the cause. A difference is a question to investigate, not proof that someone stole food.”

**Demonstration:** Put down four counters for opening stock, then add H01's 16 kg. Twenty kilograms are now available. Move six counters to Sold for delivery D01 and two to Home for HOME01. Twelve remain. Read the source reference as you write each row. The sold quantity belongs to sale S01; the home quantity is not another sale or a cash receipt.

**Guided task:** Work through Q04–Q08 in the stock table below. Keep each destination in its own column. The book ends with 9 kg. Now uncover the independently supplied count card: 9 kg. Ask which two separate pieces of information agree. Do not claim that agreement proves every record is right; two errors can cancel each other out. Keep the source checks as well as the total check.

**Independent task:** Use the worksheet's new 10 kg example. Ask the learner to show 3 kg sold, 2 kg used at home and 4 kg counted at closing. The record explains only 9 kg. One kilogram is unexplained. There is no loss note or second count yet, so “1 kg spoiled” is not a supported answer. Ask what could be checked: a repeat weighing, a missed gift or delivery record, the opening quantity and the units. Do not imply that every difference has the same cause.

**Feedback:** If the learner enters bunches as kilograms, hold up the written unit and ask what was actually measured. Weigh a comparable practice bundle or keep its count in a separate record. No conversion is supplied in this pack. If the learner records all remaining produce as home food, point to the crate that is still present. Remaining stock and food already used are different destinations.

**App companion:** Read [Record and check a harvest](https://imbewufield.vercel.app/student/guides/harvest). The current form records kilograms and the date of entry, not a backdated picking date. The chart's “kept” is picked minus logged sold quantities; it cannot identify home use, gifts, storage or loss separately. In this workbook, 24 kg picked minus 15 kg delivered is 9 kg; that happens to equal closing stock because the 4 kg opening stock and 4 kg other outflows offset. It is a coincidence, not a general stock formula or an expected app chart result. The current invoice link adds kilogram sale evidence when an invoice is paid in full, so unpaid and partly paid deliveries do not map directly to this workbook. Use its destination records to explain the movement; do not mark an invoice paid just to force the chart to match.

**Field task:** Choose one product and a short agreed period. Write the opening amount, unit and how it was measured. Keep source references for additions and destinations. Compare with a separate closing count and list anything unresolved. Do not invent a weight for an unweighed bundle.

## F2.2 — Sales, payments and expenses

**Outcome:** Keep delivery, sale and payment linked; track what a buyer still owes; separate an internal transfer from new farm income or spending.

**Opening scene:** Place a delivery note, an invoice and a payment slip on the table. Ask whether these are three sales. They may all describe different stages of one sale. Ask the learner to find the common sale reference before reading the amounts.

**Narration draft:** “Write the sale when the agreed produce is delivered in this exercise. Keep the payment beside that sale, with the date it arrived. If only some money arrives, show what is still owed. Moving your own money from the cash tin to the wallet does not create another sale. It changes where the money is kept. A fee is a separate payment if the evidence shows one. Keep the amount and cents from the source.”

This exercise's agreed delivery is the point used to record its sale. It is not a rule covering every contract, deposit, return or accounting policy. Those need their own terms and bookkeeping review.

**Demonstration:** Read S02: 5 kg delivered on Day 2 for R100.00. Nothing is paid that day. Put R100.00 in “Buyer B owes”. On Day 4, PAY02 shows R60.00 received into the wallet for S02. Move R60.00 out of the owed amount and into wallet receipts; R40.00 remains owed. Sale value stays R100.00 and delivered weight stays 5 kg. The payment has not produced another delivery.

**Guided transfer:** Before MOVE01 the cash tin holds R607.50 and the wallet R100.00. Move R100.00 from cash to the wallet. Cash becomes R507.50 and the wallet R200.00. Combined money remains R707.50. Keep both sides connected by MOVE01. This transfer is complete, with no fee attached in the supplied evidence. FEE01 is a separate Day 3 wallet charge of R2.50, not a guessed transfer cost. If a real transfer is pending, record and investigate that state; do not pretend the receiving account already has it.

**Guided reconciliation:** Complete all seven money rows. The farm receives R260.00 from buyers and pays R45.00 in documented costs. Combined money changes from R600.00 to R815.00. Sales total R300.00; R40.00 remains owed on S02. Neither R815.00 nor R215.00 is established accounting profit. The pack omits production costs and stock values needed for that conclusion.

**Independent task:** A new R90.00 sale has a R30.00 payment. Ask separately for the sale value, money received and amount owed: R90.00, R30.00 and R60.00. Then give the learner a new internal R20.00 transfer between accounts. The transfer alone changes neither total money nor sales. An independently documented R1.00 fee would reduce money by R1.00; it would not make the transfer itself a produce purchase. These are separate practice cases, not extra rows in the main tables.

**Feedback:** If the learner adds the transfer to income, move one physical token between the two account mats and ask how many tokens were created. If a part-payment settles the whole sale in their answer, place the R60.00 payment under the R100.00 sale and uncover the R40.00 gap. If cents disappear, compare PACK01's R12.50 with the written entry before recalculating.

**App companions:** Use [Record a cost and keep its receipt](https://imbewufield.vercel.app/student/guides/expenses) and [Make and manage an invoice](https://imbewufield.vercel.app/student/guides/invoices). The app currently offers unpaid or paid-in-full invoice status; it does not model the partial-payment or cash-tin/wallet ledgers in this exercise. Keep those in the workbook. Do not mark S02 paid in full, create another sale for its payment, or enter an own-account transfer as a crop sale to make the app resemble the worksheet.

**Field task:** Use one approved practice or redacted sale and its payment evidence. Record the separate dates and account, match the reference, calculate any balance and name missing evidence. Do not contact a real buyer as part of this classroom task.

## F2.3 — Reconcile the book and correct mistakes

**Outcome:** Compare independent evidence with records, locate the cause of a difference, and retain a clear correction trail without concealing an unresolved amount.

**Narration draft:** “Reconcile means compare and explain. Count the cash. Read the wallet statement. Match deliveries, sales, payments and receipts by their references. Check the dates and units as well as the totals. When you find a mistake, keep the original reference and write what changed and why. If you cannot explain a difference yet, say so. Do not add a guessed entry just to make the totals agree.”

**Demonstration:** The practice book omits FEE01 and reports wallet money of R340.00. The supplied closing wallet statement shows R337.50. Compare the rows, find the R2.50 debit on that statement and add its missing source reference. The book now agrees with the statement. This correction is supported by FEE01, not by a made-up “balancing fee”. If the statement did not show the reason, the difference would remain unresolved.

**Guided error hunt:** Present four altered copies, one at a time. Each begins from the clean main pack; do not stack their errors.

| Altered practice copy | What the learner should find | Evidence-led correction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| H02 is absent; book says only 1 kg remains | Missing 8 kg harvest | Restore H02 from the supplied Day 3 weighing record; closing stock becomes 9 kg |
| PAY02 appears twice; buyer payments total R320.00 | One R60.00 payment counted twice | Retain one payment for its one reference; payments return to R260.00 and S02 still has R40.00 owed |
| MOVE01 is called a new sale | Own money mistaken for revenue | Restore linked transfer treatment; there is no new buyer or delivery supporting a sale |
| PACK01 is written as R125.00 | Decimal error of R112.50 | Copy R12.50 from the source; do not alter the receipt to agree with the entry |

Write a correction note containing the record reference, old entry, corrected entry, source used, date of correction and person checking it. In the paper exercise use a single line through the error so it remains readable. Follow the actual app's supported correction controls for real records; do not prescribe deleting a linked sale or invoice as a routine fix.

**Independent task:** The worksheet gives an 8 kg record changed to “8 bunches”, a Day 2 delivery with a Day 4 payment, and a closing count that is still unexplained. The learner must keep the original measurement unit, preserve both event dates and leave the unknown cause unresolved. Being able to repeat the rule does not pass the task; they must find and explain each discrepancy on a fresh copy.

**Feedback and transfer:** If the learner changes a source document to fit the total, ask which record was the evidence and which was the copy. If they stop after totals agree, show two offsetting errors: an omitted R5.00 receipt and an omitted R5.00 payment. The net change is zero, but both records are still missing. Ask them to identify the missing events rather than congratulating a balanced total. These two example events are outside the main pack.

**App practice:** The published guides provide save/reopen steps for harvests, costs and invoices. Practise through the existing sample tour in the same tab. The sample farm's edited data lives in memory: a full reload restores its starting data, although the tab can remain in sample mode. Reopening within the same session is the task here; sample persistence does not demonstrate real-account sync. On a real account, read pending/offline messages and check the saved result without entering the same event twice. The harvest list currently has no Edit control; keep the exact correction needed rather than inventing an adjustment harvest.

**Field task:** Reconcile one small, agreed set of practice records. Mark each source as checked, record the differences, make only supported corrections and leave an unresolved list. Ask for bookkeeping help on treatment that you cannot establish. Do not show account numbers, payment access details or private customer information to the group.

## Assessment and worked feedback

The learner passes the practical task by (a) tracing the 28 kg available into all destinations; (b) matching three sales to payments and retaining S02's R40.00 balance; (c) keeping the internal transfer out of sales and costs; (d) checking cents; and (e) explaining a supported correction and an unresolved difference. Offer a different example after feedback. A guessed zero for missing information or a total that only matches by cancelling errors is not successful reconciliation.

Worksheet answers: stock task 10 − 3 − 2 − 4 = 1 kg unexplained; payment task R90.00 − R30.00 = R60.00 owed; own-account transfer creates no extra combined money; independently documented R1.00 fee reduces it by R1.00. For the altered H02 unit, retain 8 kg from the weighing record. Keep Day 2 as delivery and Day 4 as payment. No evidence supports relabelling the unexplained kilogram as theft, spoilage or a gift.
