# F6 - Equipment, borrowing and reserves

Status: three full English facilitator drafts with original synthetic practice cards and checked answers. **Reserve only**: not a published, narrated, translated or accredited finance unit. Bookkeeping and learner review remain open. See [sources and limits](f6-sources.md), the [learner workbook](../../../../output/pdf/finance-f6-workbook.pdf), and the [interactive money-and-equipment cards](../../../../output/html/finance-f6-decisions.html). This follows F5 in the [24-lesson finance pathway](../financial-course.md).

## Prepare the session

Bring three cards marked Buy, Hire and Share, counters, a calculator and two envelopes marked Daily work and Set aside. Read one source card at a time. Learners may point, sort, explain aloud or ask a partner to write. Keep private financial details out of group work. Use approved redacted documents only when people agree to discuss them.

Every rand amount, day, date, fee and schedule here is **invented for arithmetic practice**. None is a local quote, normal price, legal maximum, interest-rate recommendation or offer of credit. The tool is deliberately unspecified. No crop, yield, machine capacity or instruction to perform a farming operation is supplied. Each exercise starts separately; do not carry cash or debt from another case. Full profit remains unknown.

A negative cash projection is an unfunded shortage, not spendable money or an approved overdraft. Continuing the calculation shows a proposed plan's problem; it does not prove the intervening payments can be made. Real affordability needs the full household/farm commitments and income evidence, which this unit does not supply.

## F6.1 - Compare buying, hiring and sharing

**Outcome:** Compare the same service need, identify cash tied up before use, retain missing information, and explain why a low purchase contribution does not prove a tool will be available.

**Opening scene:** Show the reviewed picture of neighbours beside shared tools. Ask what a picture cannot tell us: ownership, availability, safe use, repair responsibility or price. Agree the job first, then compare ways of getting it done. Having a tool nearby does not prove it suits the job or that a learner can safely operate it.

**Narration draft:** “Start with the work you need done and the dates. Ask what each offer includes. Buying, hiring and sharing move money at different times. A deposit may come back later, but you still need it before use. Keep the value of a tool you still own separate from cash in your hand. Check who can use a shared tool, when it is available and who pays for damage or repairs.”

**Demonstration:** The practice need is the same suitable tool service on Days 2, 10 and 20 within a 30-day horizon. Opening available cash is R500.00. Suitability, safe operation and access for BUY and HIRE are assumed confirmed solely to isolate the money exercise. SHARE has an unconfirmed Day 10 booking. No other receipts are supplied.

BUY pays R900.00 on Day 1, R60.00 for operating inputs on Day 10 and R40.00 for maintenance on Day 20. Total payments are R1,000.00. The plan is already R400.00 short on Day 1 and ends R500.00 short. It cannot be funded from the stated R500.00. The buyer would retain an asset, but its remaining value and useful life are unknown. Do not call the whole purchase an established 30-day cost of using up the tool, or assume the retained asset is worth zero. A future sale of that asset is not cash until its price, costs and date are evidenced.

HIRE pays R210.00 for all three uses, R60.00 transport and R150.00 refundable security on Day 1. These are three parts of one offer, not three separate R210.00 service fees. The exercise says operating and maintenance service costs are included, no other charges apply, and the R150.00 deposit is returned in full on Day 30. Cash needed first is R420.00, leaving R80.00. After the refund, net cash paid is R270.00 and closing cash is R230.00. A R270.00 net figure does not mean the farmer could start with only R270.00. The refund returns the farmer's money; it is not a produce sale.

SHARE pays R300.00 towards ownership on Day 1, R30.00 operating contribution on Day 10 and R20.00 maintenance contribution on Day 20. The conditional cash plan uses R350.00 and closes at R150.00. The retained share value is unknown. The unconfirmed booking means this offer has not yet been shown to meet the same need. A favourable cash total cannot resolve that availability problem.

**Guided comparison:** Lay out all source cards by date, keeping the refundable deposit distinct. Check the included operator, transport, fuel or other operating inputs, cleaning, repair, storage, return terms, insurance and downtime arrangements in an actual offer. In this synthetic comparison the stated package assumptions are complete for the shown cash movements; they do not establish complete economic costs. None of the three totals proves full profitability or a lifetime winner.

**Independent attempt:** A different hire costs R80.00, plus a R50.00 security deposit returned fully later. Find the amount needed before hire and net cash paid after refund. Then remove the refund agreement: which conclusion is no longer established? For a shared tool, name the missing agreement if two members need the same slot.

**Feedback and field task:** If the deposit is ignored, place the payment before the first use and its refund after the last use. If an unknown asset value becomes zero, replace it with a precise evidence request. If sharing is called cheapest without availability, ask how the required Day 10 work happens. Obtain a dated redacted quote or a list of questions for a supplier/group. Record the job, dates, inclusions, ownership and access terms. Do not buy, borrow or sign anything as an automatic lesson activity.

**App connection:** Use the [expense guide](https://imbewufield.vercel.app/student/guides/expenses) for supported actual cost entries and receipt evidence. Keep the comparison, deposit schedule and asset/share record separately. The app's cost tag is not a complete asset register, depreciation calculation or tool-booking system. Do not enter every proposed offer as an actual expense or create an invented produce sale for a deposit refund.

## F6.2 - Read the whole borrowing commitment

**Outcome:** Reconcile money received, fees, principal and repayment dates; recognise an incomplete quote; and connect a proposed repayment to a dated cash plan without claiming that arithmetic alone proves affordability.

**Narration draft:** “The amount borrowed is not the amount you may have left to spend. Read the fees and when they are paid. Each instalment may contain repayment of the amount borrowed, interest and other charges. If a charge is already included, do not add it again. Keep the dates. A smaller-looking instalment is not enough information to choose a loan.”

**Source-reading demonstration:** Use the current [NCR quotation form](https://www.ncr.org.za/index.php/act/list-of-forms/category/123-english?download=480:english) to locate fields, not to manufacture a contract. Identify the advance, deposit if applicable, instalment count/frequency, interest information, fees, required and optional insurance, security and repayment arrangements. Ask a qualified local adviser about applicability and unclear terms. Do not infer a legal limit or a right from this lesson. No form is signed.

**Worked SEPARATE case:** In this original model, R600.00 principal is advanced on 1 January 2027 and credited in full. A R30.00 fee is paid separately that day, leaving R570.00 from the advance available for the intended purpose. The quoted schedule has three principal payments of R200.00. Interest components are R12.00, R8.00 and R4.00. Each instalment also includes R6.00 service and R4.00 required insurance. No optional insurance or other charges are assumed in this model. These are supplied charge amounts, not an interest calculation or a statement that such terms comply with law.

The payments due are R222.00 on 31 January, R218.00 on 28 February and R214.00 on 31 March. Their total is R654.00. With the separate R30.00 fee, total outflow is R684.00 against the R600.00 gross advance. Specified financing charges are R84.00: interest R24.00, service R18.00, insurance R12.00 and the initial fee R30.00. Repaying R600.00 principal is not an additional R600.00 interest expense or produce sale. Remaining principal is R400.00, R200.00 and zero after the three scheduled payments. That number excludes future charges; label it remaining principal, not a complete early-settlement quote.

**WITHHELD case:** Now keep the same principal, fee and repayment schedule, but the provider withholds the R30.00 fee once before paying out R570.00. Do not subtract it again from the R570.00 actually credited. The scheduled R654.00 outflow less R570.00 received still gives R84.00 financing charges. The apparent bank receipt differs, but these two cases have the same net initial funds and specified total charges. A fee added to the loan principal would be a different arrangement and needs its own supplied schedule; it is not silently substituted here.

**UNKNOWN case:** A different practice quote shows principal R600.00, a separate R30.00 fee, three R200.00 principal components and R10.00 interest per instalment, with no service fee. Required insurance applies but its amount is missing. Each full instalment, the full repayment total and total financing charges remain unknown. The displayed remaining principal assumes the specified principal payments are made; it does not confirm payment or full settlement when charges are unresolved. Do not set missing insurance to zero and advertise this as a cheaper loan. Obtain the required amount, its payment timing and a corrected complete schedule.

**Guided practice:** Separate the principal and charges in each dated row. Match the total to the document rather than adding an included service fee twice. Link each proposed due date to a cash forecast that includes other obligations. Check the actual purpose, disbursement timing, rate basis, variable-rate exposure where applicable, fees, insurance, collateral and consequences of default with suitable help. This practice pack has no complete business-income forecast, household commitments or lender assessment, so it cannot certify affordability or recommend borrowing.

**Independent attempt:** A separate payment consists of R100.00 principal, R10.00 interest and R2.00 service already included in the instalment. Calculate that instalment. A learner adds the service again: explain the error. Then give an offer with missing compulsory insurance and ask what prevents a complete comparison.

**Feedback and field task:** If loan receipts become sales revenue, link the money to its repayment obligation. If a fee is charged twice, trace whether it was withheld, paid separately or already included. If the learner chooses the unknown offer by its apparent total, restore the missing field. Collect a blank or approved redacted quote and list questions. Keep original terms and dates. Do not apply for credit, choose a lender, enter private identity details or transfer money as a lesson exercise.

**App connection:** The current My Records summaries and lender record export are not this repayment schedule, a credit assessment or an automatic affordability decision. Keep the complete schedule and supporting evidence separately. F4 supplies dated cash planning; F7 will address statement boundaries. Do not force a loan advance into a produce sale or imply the app distinguishes principal and finance charges automatically.

## F6.3 - Set money aside and test a shock

**Outcome:** Distinguish a reserve allocation from money received or spent, retain restricted/earmarked funds in the correct scope, and identify a later cash gap after an unexpected repair.

**Narration draft:** “Setting money aside gives it a job. It does not create more money. If you move it between two accounts you own, the total is unchanged unless there is a fee. When you pay someone else for a repair, money leaves. Keep the amount still set aside separate from money for daily work. A reserve target is a plan, not an account balance.”

**Demonstration:** Start this separate case with R800.00 total owned cash: R600.00 for daily work and R200.00 earmarked. Both are inside the same scope, available to the owner, with no transfer fees or legal restrictions supplied. Earmarking is a management allocation; real contractual restrictions must be checked separately. A target of R500.00 is an invented goal for the exercise, not a recommended reserve size.

On Day 5 allocate R100.00 from daily work to the reserve. Daily work becomes R500.00 and reserve R300.00; total remains R800.00. On Day 10 pay a R120.00 maintenance bill from the reserve: reserve R180.00 and total R680.00. On Day 15 an evidenced R80.00 operating receipt arrives: daily work R580.00, reserve R180.00, total R760.00. Its source belongs with the receipt; the amount alone does not establish profit. On Day 25 a specified R550.00 essential bill leaves daily work R30.00 and reserve R180.00, total R210.00. The reserve is R320.00 below the invented R500.00 target.

Keep D01, a separate R50.00 non-cash equipment-use estimate, out of these cash movements. It neither pays for replacement nor moves money into the bank. Keep the unapproved R300.00 funding application G01 out too. A proposal is not money received. This lesson does not prescribe depreciation or its tax treatment.

**SHOCK case:** Return to the original opening position and insert a R250.00 repair on Day 20. After Day 15 only R180.00 is reserved. Using that plus R70.00 from daily work is a proposed change of plan, not additional income. Total falls from R760.00 to R510.00, with reserve zero. The later R550.00 essential bill would then produce a R40.00 shortage. Stop and investigate a feasible response before that bill is due. Do not silently clip the negative answer to zero or treat an unapproved application as the missing R40.00.

**Guided discussion:** If the earmarked amount were legally restricted or unavailable, permission/access would be a separate constraint; this exercise assumes none. A response may require revising timing by agreement, obtaining reliable repair alternatives or reassessing the work and its consequences. Do not automatically cancel essential maintenance, divert protected funds or borrow. Recalculate the resulting dates and amounts from actual evidence. A reserve target must be grounded in the farmer's circumstances, not a universal percentage.

**Independent attempt:** Start a new case with R400.00 total and no reserve. Earmark R90.00. Then pay R20.00 from that reserve. State total cash after the allocation and after the external payment, including what remains reserved. Explain why a non-cash cost estimate cannot pay the next repair.

**Feedback and field task:** If allocation reduces total money, physically move counters between the two envelopes without removing any. For an external payment, remove its counters once. If the later shortage disappears, replay the repair before the essential bill. Prepare a redacted list of maintenance needs, their evidence, likely payment dates, funds genuinely available and unresolved costs. Keep the target, actual balance and obligations distinct. No private bank balance must be shared with the class.

**App connection:** Keep reserve allocation and replacement planning in the workbook unless a supported ledger represents them faithfully. Use the expense companion for actual recorded costs, with original receipt evidence. The app chart does not establish your unrestricted bank balance, asset value or depreciation provision.

## Assessment and media

Use fresh cases after feedback. The learner must explain up-front versus net cash, an unknown retained asset value, an unavailable shared booking, principal versus charges, one fee counted once, a missing compulsory charge, an internal allocation, and a later shortage after a shock. Practical reasoning may be spoken or demonstrated with cards; this is not a credit recommendation or accounting credential.

The interactive aid reveals source-linked cards with manual controls and play/pause. Its exact values come from the checked practice pack. The warm shared-tools image supplies context; generated text, bank screens and changing numerical imagery are excluded. It is a reserve teaching aid, not a production app forecast or loan calculator. No real records are saved. English narration, fluent isiZulu, local bookkeeping review, learner trial and full course integration remain open.
