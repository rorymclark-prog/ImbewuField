# F5 - Sell, invoice and collect payment

Status: three full English facilitator drafts with original synthetic document cases and checked answers. **Reserve only**: not narrated, translated, accredited or a substitute for a reviewed accounting system. Bookkeeping and learner review remain open. This follows F4 in the [24-lesson finance pathway](../financial-course.md). See [sources and limits](f5-sources.md) and the [four-page learner workbook](../../../../output/pdf/finance-f5-workbook.pdf). The separate [Record a sale once app companion](https://imbewufield.vercel.app/student/guides/sales) teaches the current interface.

## Prepare the session

Bring paper cards for orders, delivery, invoices and receipts; counters; a calculator; and two envelopes marked “customer owes” and “money received”. Let learners point, sort, explain aloud or ask a partner to write. Keep real customer names, account numbers and private receipts out of group discussion. The app tour uses sample records; these classroom amounts must not be entered in a learner's real farm.

Every amount and numbered day below is an **invented classroom input**. Each P case begins separately with no opening invoice, cash receipt, deposit or credit. The cases contain no tax, discounts, payment fees, refunds or costs unless an event explicitly states an adjustment. They therefore establish neither full profit nor a compliant tax invoice. A single invoice covers each completed delivery in these simplified cases. Real contract terms, tax treatment and disputed rights require appropriate review.

## F5.1 - Follow the order, delivery, invoice and receipt

**Outcome:** Explain what each document proves, preserve the quantity and unit, and distinguish a sale from cash collected against it.

**Opening scene:** Show one basket of produce and four blank cards. Ask what must be agreed before the basket leaves: the buyer, produce, quantity and unit, condition, agreed price, delivery and payment arrangements. An order records an agreement; it does not alone show that produce was delivered or money received. An invoice asks for payment and identifies the transaction. Delivery evidence records what was handed over. Payment evidence supports the amount and date actually received. A document's title alone does not prove that it is accurate.

**Narration draft:** “Keep the story of one sale together. First, agree what the buyer wants and how payment will work. Check what you deliver. Check the invoice against that delivery and the agreed price. When money arrives, match it to the same invoice. The payment does not create another basket of produce. Keep its date and reference so you can show what is still owed.”

**Demonstration:** Lay out P01: Day 1 order O01 for R120.00; Day 2 completed delivery and invoice D01-I01 for R120.00; Day 3 receipt R01-A for R40.00. Invoice value is R120.00, money received is R40.00, and the recorded balance is R80.00. On Day 4 the buyer asks for another copy. I01-COPY is the same invoice, not another R120.00 sale. On Day 5 receipt R01-B adds R80.00. Total collected is now R120.00 and the balance is zero. Do not add the order, invoice, copy and receipts together as revenue.

Use the app companion's four-stage payment example to reveal one stage at a time. Pause before each answer. The animation is an explanation using invented numbers, not a live account or a demonstration that the app supports part-payment allocation.

**Guided practice:** Invoice M01 has two lines: 2.5 kg at R12.00/kg and three bunches at R8.00/bunch. Calculate each line, then add money. The result is R54.00. Do not add the quantities as 5.5 kg. A bunch has no supplied mass. If a line lacks a price or unit, stop and resolve that source instead of choosing a convenient zero or conversion.

**Independent attempt:** Give the learner four new blank document cards. Read aloud: an agreement is made, produce is delivered, an invoice is issued, and a buyer pays only some of it later. Ask the learner to place each card in the correct sequence and identify the evidence still needed. Then supply the independent R150.00 invoice and R50.00 receipt. Ask for the recorded amount still owed and what would prove settlement.

**Feedback:** If invoice value becomes cash received, move the unpaid portion into the “customer owes” envelope. If a copy creates new income, place it behind the original invoice instead of beside a second basket. If mixed quantities become kilograms, return to the two labelled lines. Give a fresh example after feedback.

**App connection and field task:** Use [Record a sale once](https://imbewufield.vercel.app/student/guides/sales). Before any real entry, find the matching existing sale or invoice. Collect a redacted set of order, delivery, invoice and receipt references and explain any gap. This lesson does not require sending a customer message or sharing private documents.

## F5.2 - Choose the right invoice route and verify the saved result

**Outcome:** Choose new, past or paper entry appropriately; preserve a source reference; reopen or link an existing sale; and check the saved result without creating duplicate income.

**Narration draft:** “Look in your records before you add anything. If the invoice is already there, open it. If the sale is there but needs an invoice, use that sale. If you are bringing in an older or paper record, keep its original reference and dates. Read the save message. Open the saved document and check it. A second copy of a document must not become a second sale.”

**Demonstration on the actual app:** Start in the sample tour, confirm it is sample data, and open My Records → Sold. Show New sale & invoice and Past sale / paper invoice. Show View invoice for an already linked sale and Create invoice when an eligible existing sale needs one. Reopening is different from starting a blank form.

Choose Invoice type and, when asked, Existing sale record **before** entering buyer and line details. Changing either starts a fresh form in the current app. A new invoice begins a new document. Produce already sold and An invoice already written on paper ask whether the sale exists. Select the source sale when applicable; do not choose No merely to avoid matching it. An already invoiced sale should be reopened, not invoiced again. Preserve the original paper reference when the paper route asks for it.

Check buyer, product, quantity, unit, agreed price, growing area where relevant, dates and payment status. A suggested price is not evidence of the buyer's agreement. Keep bags and bunches as their actual units. The app can link paid kilogram lines into crop-sale evidence; it cannot infer the mass of other units. Existing-sale linking is limited by the supported source records. Do not split one past sale into invented new sales to make the form accept it.

Save, read the actual message, and reopen the same invoice from Saved or its sale link. Check its number, total, date and status. A device save with crop-sale synchronisation pending is not a reason to create another invoice. Documents are currently saved on the device within the account scope; crop-sale sync does not prove the complete invoice document is available on another device.

**Guided practice:** Sort these situations: (a) an invoice already saved; (b) an eligible sale already recorded with no invoice; (c) a completed sale not yet recorded; (d) an old paper invoice whose sale may already exist. Explain the lookup step and route for each. For (d), missing evidence about an existing sale stays unresolved until checked. “Paper” does not mean “unpaid”.

Use the prepared sample invoice to demonstrate unpaid → paid in full only when the exercise supplies full payment evidence. Keep the same invoice identity. A receipt date and invoice issue date answer different questions. If the app does not support the original record faithfully, retain it separately and identify the limitation instead of manufacturing a replacement history.

**Independent attempt:** Present a saved R150.00 invoice and the R50.00 receipt from F5.1. Ask whether to create a second R50.00 sale, mark the original paid in full, or keep the part payment in a separate checked balance record. Only the last fits these facts. Then ask what to inspect before downloading a requested copy of the existing invoice.

**Feedback and field task:** For duplicates, start at the existing reference and follow it to the document. For premature full payment, compare the invoice amount with all verified receipts. For altered dates, return to original evidence. In the sample workspace, reopen one prepared invoice and identify its amount, payment status and source. Print/PDF is a separate document check; downloading does not send it to a customer. Use the [invoice companion](https://imbewufield.vercel.app/student/guides/invoices) for the detailed steps.

## F5.3 - Reconcile deposits, part payments and unresolved balances

**Outcome:** Keep cash receipts, allocations, adjustments and questions distinct; explain a remaining balance; and know when the current app needs a separate supporting schedule.

**Narration draft:** “Write down every payment once, with its evidence. A deposit may arrive before produce is delivered. Keep it linked to that customer and order. Later, applying that same deposit to an invoice does not bring in more money. Keep questions and agreed corrections visible. If money arrives and you do not know who sent it, investigate before matching it to a sale.”

**Deposit demonstration P02:** Day 1 order O02 is R90.00. Day 2 advance DEP02 is R30.00; nothing has been delivered and no invoice issued in this exercise. Cash received is R30.00 and the customer's advance remains separately held in the record. This describes an outstanding obligation, not a promise that a separate physical bank account exists. It does not establish completed sales revenue.

Day 5 completed delivery and invoice D02-I02 is R90.00. First identify the original R30.00 receipt, then record ALLOC02 applying it to this invoice. After that allocation, recorded amount owed is R60.00, advance held is zero and total cash received remains R30.00. The intermediate table row before allocation deliberately leaves the deposit separate until matched. Day 6 receipt R02 of R60.00 settles the balance. Total cash collected is R90.00, not R120.00. Real deposits, cancellations, refund terms and tax timing require the actual agreement and local bookkeeping guidance; this pack invents none.

**Dispute demonstration P03:** Invoice D03-I03 is R200.00; R03-A confirms R120.00 received. Q03 records that the customer questions R40.00. Keep the issue and evidence visible. Merely entering that question does not turn the R80.00 recorded balance into zero or establish that the disputed amount is legally owed. Investigate the delivery, price and agreement. For this synthetic case only, ADJ03 is a separately documented, approved R20.00 reduction. Retain the original invoice and adjustment trail. The resulting recorded balance is R60.00. Receipt R03-B of R60.00 settles it. This is a bookkeeping exercise, not advice to demand a disputed amount or authority to create a tax credit note in the app.

**Unmatched receipt P04:** BANK04 proves R25.00 arrived, but no buyer or invoice has been identified. Cash received is R25.00; it stays unmatched. Do not select a convenient unpaid invoice or call it produce sales without evidence. Check the reference and appropriate source safely. If unresolved, retain that status for review.

**Overpayment P05:** Invoice D05-I05 is R50.00; R05 proves receipt of R60.00. Only R50.00 settles that invoice. Keep the extra R10.00 as an unresolved customer credit; it is not extra produce revenue or automatically a tip. Confirm an appropriate agreed treatment and its evidence. This exercise performs no refund, future-sale allocation or financial transfer.

**Guided practice:** Build a separate customer schedule with original invoice reference, dated receipt references, approved adjustments, allocations and the remaining recorded balance. Keep advance deposits, unknown receipts and customer credits visible. Do not combine unrelated customers' credits and debts into one misleading net figure. In these cases, original invoice less approved reductions minus allocated receipts gives the remaining balance, with any excess shown as customer credit. The model excludes fees, tax and other arrangements.

**Independent attempt:** Invoice R150.00, received R50.00. The buyer questions R30.00; no change has been approved. State the recorded balance and label the unresolved question. Then an evidenced R10.00 reduction is approved: recalculate. In a separate case, invoice R35.00 and receipt R40.00: identify the credit. Explain why the app's paid-in-full control cannot represent the first part-payment history or the credit ledger.

**Feedback and field task:** If allocation creates cash, reuse the same receipt card rather than introducing another coin. If a dispute automatically changes the invoice, ask for the approved adjustment source and keep the question separate. If overpayment increases sales, return to the delivered amount. Prepare a blank customer schedule and use an invented new case or redacted approved records. Identify the next evidence needed to resolve one item; do not expose customer details or contact anyone as an automatic course action.

## Assessment, media and release boundaries

A learner should independently explain: which event delivered produce, which brought cash, what a copy changes, the remaining balance after a part payment, why deposit allocation adds no new cash, how an approved adjustment differs from a question, how unmatched money remains unresolved, and why overpayment is not additional produce revenue. Give a fresh case after corrective feedback; assess reasoning and evidence, not memorised totals.

Use the reviewed lifelike market image for context. Use authored document labels and exact arithmetic for the payment animation, with manual steps and play/pause. Do not ask Flow to generate legible amounts, pretend bank screens or imaginary app controls. The companion is an English readable task guide, not a filmed workflow, assessment or partial-payment feature. The reserve unit still needs bookkeeping review, fluent isiZulu, narration, learner trial and integrated delivery. App routes must be rechecked when recording the final walkthrough.
