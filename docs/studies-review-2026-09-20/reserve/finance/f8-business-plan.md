# F8 - Plan, compare and improve the business

**Status:** Three English reserve teaching drafts. Bookkeeping review, learner trial, fluent isiZulu, narration and course integration remain open. This is practical business learning, not a qualification, lender assessment or advice to select a real investment.

Use `f8-practice.json`, the learner workbook and the visual comparison together. All money, dates, quantities and hours are invented classroom inputs. They are not local prices, yields, recommended workloads or farming forecasts. This case concerns a produce-selling activity and a basket-selling activity; it does not model production from land. Keep kilos and items separate. Both are alternatives using the same opening money, not two activities that can each spend it simultaneously. The actual case is an invented record set shown after the plan, not a prediction of what will happen.

Build on F3 cost boundaries, F4 cash timing, F5 payment evidence and F7 complete-account boundaries. Supply counters, source cards, a calculator and writing space. A learner may explain aloud or point while a facilitator records the reasoning. No real buyer details or bank documents are needed.

## F8.1 - Choose an enterprise from evidence

**Outcome:** Compare two options using demand evidence, the same period, available resources and dated cash needs; explain a missing assumption before choosing a next action.

**Narration draft:** “Start with a buyer and a task you can deliver. What does the buyer need? How much, in which unit, at what quality, and by when? Interest is not an order. A high sales total does not tell you what you will keep or whether you can pay the earlier bills. Put the alternatives beside each other. Keep the missing evidence visible.”

**Observe and sort:** Lay out M-A and M-B. Ask the learner to point to a documented statement, a planning assumption and an unanswered question. M-A is an enquiry about up to 40 kg at R12 per kg, subject to quality and confirmation. The forecast assumes the full quantity sells and the money arrives on 30 January. M-B expresses interest in baskets at R30 each, but confirms no quantity. The forecast's 20 items is an assumption, not a completed market survey or agreed order. Neither document proves a secure market. Ask about grade, acceptance, quantities, delivery costs, payment terms, repeat demand and alternative buyers. Keep a date and source beside each answer.

**Demonstration:** For January, Plan A forecasts sales of R480 and operating payments of R280. Plan B forecasts R600 and operating payments of R480. Their included cash surpluses are R200 and R120 respectively, assuming all forecast sales are paid within the period. These are not full profits: closing stock, equipment use, overheads and unpaid work are not completely supplied. Do not treat the omitted costs as zero. Do not compare R12 per kg with R30 per item as though the larger number identifies the better enterprise.

Each alternative starts with R300. A's quoted payments leave R120 on 5 January, R80 on 10 January and R20 on 20 January. The assumed R480 payment on 30 January brings its closing projection to R500. B's payments leave R20, then an unfunded -R120, then -R180; its assumed receipt brings the closing projection to R420. A positive closing forecast does not make those earlier B payments fundable. The negative amounts are projected gaps, not an assertion that a physical cash tin held negative notes or that an overdraft was approved.

The supplied work plan allows eight owner hours. A requires six; B requires ten in addition to its paid helper. B has a two-hour owner-time gap. Those are exercise capacities, not recommended work rates. A's apparent fit does not establish that a real farm has sufficient land, water, skills, labour or permissions. Check each real constraint separately using suitable local evidence.

**Guided practice:** Sort each source into “supports this fact”, “assumption to test” or “missing”. Show the first projected shortage on the dated cash line. Ask the learner to justify a next step, not announce a universal winning enterprise. A defensible answer is to investigate A further while confirming buyer terms, the complete costs and deliverability. Another is to revise B's scope and obtain evidence for labour and cash arrangements before any commitment. “B wins because R600 is bigger” fails. “A is guaranteed profitable” also fails.

**Independent attempt:** Hide the demonstration. Give the separate items case: planned ten items at R15 each, R60 payments and R80 opening cash. Ask for sales and closing cash, then ask whether these alone prove profit. Next remove the buyer's confirmed quantity from a fresh enquiry. The learner must name a market question rather than fill the blank with ten. Finally show kg beside items: ask which measures can be compared and which cannot be added directly.

**Feedback:** If sales become profit, put the excluded-cost card beside the surplus. If January's final cash hides the earlier gap, replay B one bill at a time. If the learner calls an enquiry an order, point to its conditional wording. Use a new example after correction; do not give a pass for repeating the model's answer.

**Field task and app connection:** Prepare a small market enquiry sheet with product, unit, quality, quantity, collection/delivery, date and payment terms. Record who supplied information and permission to keep it; use redacted copies in class. Use the mapping guide to identify the correct site and distinguish beds from a whole parcel. A place pin does not prove productive area or land rights. Keep these enterprise forecasts in the workbook; My Records is for supported actual entries.

## F8.2 - Turn the idea into a checkable plan

**Outcome:** Present a connected plan whose claims lead back to evidence, show timing and constraints, and distinguish an unapproved funding request from available cash.

**Narration draft:** “A useful plan lets another person follow your reasoning. Say what you intend to sell, why a buyer may want it, and what you need to deliver it. Show the work and payments in order. Keep estimates separate from records. If a number or permission is missing, name it and say how you will check it. A neat document cannot turn an application into money.”

**Demonstration pack:** Build eight short sections: purpose and chosen activity; market evidence; site and resource access; work and delivery sequence; cost assumptions; dated cash forecast; risks and responses; review actions. Give every significant figure a reference, date, unit, scope and status. Mark a quotation as a quotation, an enquiry as an enquiry and an actual payment as a record. Keep an original plan version and a date for revisions. Explain the business in plain language before presenting the numbers.

For A, connect the three quote cards to the cash line and its assumed buyer payment. Keep B as a genuine alternative, with its hours and earlier cash gap visible. Do not combine the two R300 opening balances. A funding application F-ASK for R180 has no approval, terms or disbursement date. It does not fill B's gap. Even an approval may have conditions and timing to check; this lesson gives no approval or borrowing recommendation. F6 supplies questions for reading actual finance terms.

**Test timing:** Move A's entire assumed R480 receipt to 5 February, retaining January's cost dates. January now has no buyer receipt and ends at R20. The projected sale remains R480; payment timing changed, not the quantity or price. The amount remains expected from the buyer at the window's end under this scenario. This is a January horizon, not a full next-season plan: later bills and obligations still need their own dated rows. A real forecast should cover the relevant operating cycle and commitments, rather than copying this short exercise horizon blindly.

A response must be specific and checked. For example, ask the buyer to confirm terms, then record any actual agreement and recalculate; obtain a genuine alternative transport quote; reconsider the proposed quantity against capacity. Do not assume a deposit, delay a supplier's bill without agreement, cut necessary care or insert hoped-for funding as certain income. Explain the effects on quality, workload, household needs and others before adopting a change.

**Guided practice:** Give one learner the proposed plan and another the evidence cards. For each claim ask “which record supports it?” Put missing demand confirmation, full cost treatment and site/resource evidence into an action register with an owner and review date. The model's proposed 10 February 2027 review is a classroom date, not a universal planning interval. Ask what could make the plan wrong and what observation would trigger reconsideration. Keep a funding pack's requested information separate from what the class currently knows.

**Independent attempt:** A learner submits a polished map, a screenshot of R420 closing projected cash and the R180 application. Explain why these do not prove land rights, earlier liquidity, profitability or funding approval. Ask for the dated B payments and the missing work-hour arrangement. Then give A's delayed receipt case without the answer and ask the learner to build January cash. They must leave the February receipt outside January.

**Feedback:** If a forecast is relabelled as actual, return to the source status. If an application pays the bill in the model, remove it and restore the gap. If a claim rests on the map's appearance, ask for the appropriate underlying agreement, measurement or record. A legitimate answer may be “not ready to commit; obtain this evidence”. It is not a failure to preserve uncertainty.

**Field task and app connection:** Build a redacted practice plan and a short evidence index. Use the live mapping, invoice and Charts guides for supported tasks. A record export or “Records for a lender” summary is supporting material, not a full application, a title document, complete financial statements or an eligibility decision. Check the current requirements with the relevant organisation when preparing a real pack. Keep private documents secure; this lesson sends nothing to a lender or buyer.

## F8.3 - Review the season without rewriting its history

**Outcome:** Reconcile an original forecast with actual records, separate quantity, price, collection and cost differences, preserve a missing value, and set a justified next action.

**Narration draft:** “Keep the plan you started with. Put the actual records beside it for the same period and units. What sold? What money arrived? What was paid? Which amounts are still owed? Explain each difference with evidence before saying why it happened. Then make the next plan. Changing the old plan to match the result hides what you learned.”

**Demonstration:** Actual A has 36 kg delivered at R11 per kg on 25 January, an invoice for R396. R300 arrives on 30 January, leaving R96 owed at 31 January. The remaining R96 arrives on 5 February; it is not January cash. Actual payments are purchase R180, packaging R50 and transport R80, totalling R310. An evidenced R20 owner contribution arrives on 15 January from outside this business cash scope. It is not a sale or loan. Starting with R300, the actual cash sequence is R120, R70, R90, R10 and R310. ACT-C1 reconciles the final R310 to the cash count and statement evidence. This complete model has no unfunded actual negative balance.

The closing forecast was R500; checked actual closing cash is R310, a R190 decrease against that forecast. Do not call this R190 a business loss. The sales-value difference is R84, and neither difference establishes full profit. Use the visual bridge in this stated order:

- Quantity effect: (36 - 40) kg at the planned R12 per kg = -R48.
- Price effect: 36 actual kg at (R11 - R12) per kg = -R36.
- Unpaid January balance: -R96 of the invoiced amount is not yet received.
- Purchase payment difference: R180 versus R180, no change.
- Packaging payment difference: R50 versus R40 reduces cash by R10.
- Transport payment difference: R80 versus R60 reduces cash by R20.
- Owner contribution adds R20 cash, not sales.

R500 - R48 - R36 - R96 - R10 - R20 + R20 = R310. The quantity and price effects sum to the R84 sales difference. This decomposition values the quantity change at planned price and the price change at actual quantity; different valid ordering allocates the interaction differently. It is arithmetic attribution, not proof of cause. The records do not say why fewer kilos sold or why the price changed. Do not invent crop failure, poor effort, buyer misconduct or weather as the explanation. Investigate using appropriate evidence.

**Guided practice:** Trace every bridge step back to its source. Ask why the invoice and its two receipts are not three sales, and why the owner contribution does not increase product revenue. Keep January and February windows visible. If a learner changes the plan quantity to 36, restore the original 40 before comparing. Ask for a reasoned next action such as confirming quality/demand and payment terms, checking current quotes, and fitting work to available hours. The actual R11 and 36 kg inform an enquiry; they do not guarantee next month's price or sales.

**Missing-evidence case:** Hide the amount on ACT-P2 and remove ACT-C1's closing reconciliation. Packaging was paid but its amount is unavailable. Full payments, closing cash, the cash difference and deepest gap now remain unknown. Sales of R396 and the January customer balance of R96 stay supported. Do not use the other case's R310 closing figure to reconstruct the missing amount. With a separate trustworthy closing reconciliation, a discrepancy could be investigated; this case deliberately does not supply one. Keep a request for the receipt and reconciliation open.

**Independent attempt:** Return to the separate items case. Plan: ten items at R15, R60 payments, R80 opening. Actual: eight items at R14, R90 received, R70 payments and R20 contributed by the owner; no other movements or opening customer balance. Find actual sales, customer balance, closing cash and the difference from plan. Decompose the sales change with the same order as the demonstration. Then remove the actual cost evidence and ask which answers remain supported. Do not label a missing amount zero.

**Feedback and assessment:** The learner must produce R112 sales, R22 still owed and R120 closing cash against the R170 plan, a -R50 cash difference. Sales quantity effect is -R30 and price effect -R8, totalling -R38. Closing bridge: R170 - R30 - R8 - R22 - R10 + R20 = R120. Correct explanations distinguish product sales, money received, owner funds and the period. If a concept is missed, give corrective feedback and a new small source set; do not change the old answer to count it as independent success.

**Field task and app connection:** Write a short review with the original assumption, actual evidence, explained difference, unresolved question, next action, responsible person and review date. Use the Charts guide to check each display's time window and cost scope. The app's single paid-in-full date does not reproduce this partial-payment history; keep the settlement schedule separately and do not mark a deposit as full payment. Do not enter invented classroom figures into a real farm's records.

## Connected capstone

The learner presents an original/revised plan pair with: one market evidence sheet; a resource and access check; an activity budget with exclusions; dated cash rows; a delivery/invoice/payment trail; a cost evidence index; an actual-versus-plan review; and a next-action register. Use only systems relevant to that learner. A farming plan needs appropriate land, resource and technical evidence beyond this market exercise. The map and app records can support the pack but cannot replace those checks.

Assess whether the learner can detect an unconfirmed buyer assumption, an unfunded early payment, double-counted income, unlike units, an unpaid invoice counted as cash, an owner contribution called sales, an out-of-period receipt, missing costs presented as zero and a forecast presented as actual. Record the evidence of performance, feedback and next practice. Do not claim accounting competence, business viability, accreditation or funding approval from this classroom pack.
