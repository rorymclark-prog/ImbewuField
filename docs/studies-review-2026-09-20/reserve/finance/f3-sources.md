## Primary references

1. [UF/IFAS FE1103, Knowing Your Product Costs: A Primer for Farmers and Food Entrepreneurs](https://ask.ifas.ufl.edu/publication/FE1103), cost concepts and product-costing steps. Direct/shared classification concerns attribution; fixed/variable classification concerns cost behaviour over a specified change and period. An appropriate allocation basis and the product's scale/time boundary must be stated. F3 applies these concepts to new practice inputs, not the publication's crop budgets, yields, wage assumptions or regional calendar. Its examples are not evidence of South African costs.
2. [FAO, Agricultural and Food Marketing Management, Chapter 8: Pricing Decisions](https://www.fao.org/4/W3240E/W3240E08.htm), cost-plus methods and break-even. Supports the different denominators for markup and margin, and contribution toward fixed costs. The simplified model requires stated price/cost and sales assumptions. **Do not copy Table 8.2:** its first price scenario states 75,000 units sold but uses 50,000 for variable expense. Recalculation from its stated inputs gives 250,000 rather than 500,000 surplus. These historical source figures are not used in the learner material. Authority does not replace arithmetic checking.
3. [FAO, Farm Management for Asia, Chapter 4](https://www.fao.org/4/W7365E/w7365e07.htm), sections 4.3.6-4.3.7. Supports separating cash movements from resource valuation and showing unpaid family time with an explicit comparison basis. F3's hypothetical time value is not a wage claim, a tax deduction, or an assertion that outside paid work is available. Do not transfer this source's historic Asian prices or labour assumptions.

The Penn State product-cost article found during initial research returned 403 on full fetch. It is not relied on as reviewed evidence here. No access restriction was bypassed.

## Exercise boundaries that were checked

- Each allocation pool is divided exactly once using recorded units for the same practice cycle; shares sum to the original bill. Direct costs and family-time values remain separate. R510.00 cash and R250.00 unpaid-time comparison do not become R760.00 cash paid.
- The marketable-quantity calculation and expected-sales recovery target use different, explicit denominators. The target is not inventory valuation, a complete cost-of-sales calculation, a recommendation to write off unsold stock, or a guaranteed market price.
- Break-even uses whole packs prepared **and** sold, constant supplied rates and unchanged included fixed costs within capacity. Unsold production, waste, discounts, bad debts and missing costs require a richer model. Mathematical break-even is tested against capacity; assumed demand remains unproven.
- Zero/negative contribution with positive fixed costs is infeasible under the stated model. Unknown cost returns unknown. The result is called an included-cost model surplus, not full profit.
- The buyer offers concern the same quantity/quality and are alternatives. Extra costs, common already-incurred costs, unpaid selling time and payment date are shown separately. No promised payment is called received money.
- No statutory thresholds, tax rates, lending recommendations, legal wage rates, named new species, yields or real local price claims are introduced.
