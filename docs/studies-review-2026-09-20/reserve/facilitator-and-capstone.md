# Facilitator guide and integrated farm capstone

Status: reserve delivery guide and learner capstone draft. This expands the design-review and teach-back sessions in Rory's outlines and addresses the training-quality, mentor-readiness and MEL assessment audits. It does not claim accreditation or authorise graduates to practise regulated professions.

## Lesson 1 — Make learning visible without excluding people

Start with the learner's experience and one practical question. Show the action clearly, explain the reason in short phrases, then ask the learner to try or explain it. Offer narration, readable text, pictures and spoken responses. Ask what language and practical support will help. A quiet learner may understand the work but need a different way to show it.

Use the approved images as teaching aids. Ask what the learner notices before giving the answer. Do not treat image recognition alone as plant identification or competence with a tool. Separate an illustrative animation from a real demonstration, and say when a step needs a trained practitioner.

**Teach-back:** explain one safe, already approved task to a partner, then ask them to show the sequence and reason. **Check:** is repeating the trainer's words enough to demonstrate a practical skill? **Answer:** no; observe the relevant action or reasoning and allow another attempt.

## Lesson 2 — Keep participation, evidence and competence distinct

Attendance records show who attended. A submitted photograph shows an image was submitted. Neither alone proves that the learner can carry out a task or that household food security improved. Use a short rubric with observable criteria linked to the lesson. Record what was seen, what support was used and what remains to practise.

Ask before taking or sharing learner photographs. Keep private feedback and personal records within the appropriate organisation and role. Use redacted examples for group discussion. A certificate should describe what was actually completed and assessed; do not turn a course title into an unsupported professional or internationally recognised qualification claim.

**Activity:** sort examples into participation, self-report, observation and assessed performance. **Check:** can an attendance tick establish improved nutrition? **Answer:** no; the claim needs a different measure and evidence.

## Lesson 3 — Review a whole farm plan as connected decisions

Begin with the site and household brief. Distinguish measured features, reported information, estimates and proposed changes. Explain the water route and safe overflow, soil care, planting choices, household food plan, animal needs if relevant, labour and money. Show implementation order and what must be checked before spending or construction.

Invite structured feedback: one supported strength, one missing assumption and one question that could change the plan. Revise the plan and keep the reason for the change. An attractive map does not replace feasible access, a water balance, legal checks or a maintenance plan.

**Check:** the plan includes a new tank, but no water-demand record or purchase cost. Is that part ready to implement? **Answer:** no; identify and resolve those assumptions first.

## Capstone task

Prepare a small, achievable phase of a real or supplied practice site. Include:

1. A site/household brief with consent and privacy respected.
2. A base map distinguishing actual and proposed features, with measurement sources.
3. A connected water, soil and planting plan with local species and permission checks identified.
4. An animal-care plan only where animals are part of the proposal; choosing not to add animals is valid.
5. A local calendar, labour/care responsibilities and a cost record or clearly marked unknowns.
6. One implementation step, a way to observe its result and a review date agreed by the learner.
7. A short explanation of what changed after feedback and why.

No new purchase, paid render or publication of private household information is required. Learners can present orally with drawings or supported notes. A practice site must be clearly labelled; simulated evidence must not be submitted as a real farm outcome.

## Assessment rubric

| Criterion | Evidence needed | If not yet demonstrated |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reads the site | Observations linked to map and source; unknowns visible | Revisit one uncertain feature with a mentor |
| Connects systems | Explains a water/soil/plant or animal interaction and a trade-off | Use one simpler system and explain it again |
| Checks feasibility | Care, access, resources, costs and required advice identified | Revise scope or gather missing information |
| Works responsibly | Species, water, animal welfare, privacy and permission boundaries recognised | Pause the affected action and complete the relevant check |
| Uses evidence | Observation or record supports a proposed decision | Collect a suitable baseline or label the claim as unverified |
| Learns and adjusts | Explains one justified change after feedback | Repeat review with a fresh scenario |

The assessor records achieved/not yet demonstrated for each criterion and the evidence behind it. A later retry is part of learning. Keep the capstone result separate from mentor attendance, employment compliance, funding eligibility or specialist certification.

## Delivery and media notes

Use small downloadable units and a printable/pictorial task sheet. Start each session by checking whether the learner can play audio, read the transcript or use an offline alternative. Do not require a live AI answer to finish a lesson. Flow can animate a farmer and mentor observing a bed or tracing a map with a finger; precise map labels and assessment decisions should remain deterministic and readable.

The source outlines describe one-, five- and ten-day formats and make certificate claims. Those formats inform pacing only; the app must not automatically advertise accreditation, international recognition or permission to design professionally without verified programme authority. The RVCC schedule's practical local adaptation is retained, including respecting grassland ecology. The FHR certificate bundle contributes only generic curriculum headings; learner identities are not used.

Publication gates: programme owner's assessment/certificate policy, privacy/access review, an actual facilitator/learner trial, fluent isiZulu review and accessible media verification. No new funder or learner records are created by this draft.
