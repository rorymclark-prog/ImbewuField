# D1 English narration candidate

**Status:** Candidate scripts only. These are not accepted, recorded or published. Human practitioner review, learner review and fluent isiZulu review remain open.

These scripts are short spoken companions for D1.1–D1.3. They follow the reserve lesson text and the fictional busy-yard case. They do not add measurements, species, engineering instructions or technical claims.

## D1.1 — Agree what the household needs

Start with the people and the work. Ask who uses this place, what is working, what is difficult, and what people want to protect. Include the people who carry, care, move and check things. Write the household's priorities in plain words. Keep a need separate from an idea: needing a reliable way to water an existing garden is different from choosing a new tank. Record resources as known, reported or still unknown. Read the brief back and invite corrections. A drawing full of features has not answered the brief unless it answers what the household needs.

## D1.2 — Observe, ask and keep the source

Keep each piece of evidence in its place. “I saw this” is an observation. “The resident described this” is a report. “I measured this” needs the method and unit. “I think this may happen” is an interpretation. “I want to put this here” is a proposal. These statements can all help, but they are not the same. Record when and how you observed, and note what still needs checking. In the busy-yard case, the route and carried containers are seen, while the account of water during heavy rain is reported. Do not turn one dry visit into a measured fact.

**Source mapping:** `d1-people-and-place.md`, D1.2 Teaching text, Worked example and Learner task; `CASEBOOK.md`, WALK-A and TALK-A; `SOURCES.md`, D1.2 source map.

## D1.3 — Make a base map you can trust

A base map shows the place before a proposed change. Keep the existing house, path, water point, growing area and other important features visible. Mark what is measured, estimated or still uncertain. Keep units with dimensions, and label a first sketch “not to scale” when that is true. Put ideas on a separate proposal layer or copy, so a new bed does not erase the path that is already there. Add a title, date or revision, legend and source notes. A neat export can improve presentation, but it cannot make an estimate into a verified measurement.
