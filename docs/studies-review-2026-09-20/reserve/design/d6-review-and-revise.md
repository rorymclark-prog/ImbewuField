# D6 — Explain, test and improve

Status: three English reserve drafts. The reserve integrated capstone rubric is the assessment starting point. These lessons do not award course credit or claim professional accreditation.

## D6.1 — Present the reasoning behind the plan

**Question to show.** A learner names every feature on a map. Have they explained why the design meets the household's needs?

**Teaching text.** Begin a design presentation with the brief. Show the existing situation and the evidence that mattered most. Then explain the chosen arrangement, an alternative you considered, the important trade-off and the first feasible phase. Keep unknowns visible; explaining a limitation is better than hiding it behind a confident picture.

Choose a format the learner can use: drawings, a printed map, spoken explanation, supported notes or the app. Assess the reasoning and relevant evidence rather than fluency with a device. Ask for consent before recording or sharing a person's presentation.

Invite specific feedback. A reviewer can name a supported strength, a missing assumption and a question that might change the plan. Listen before defending the drawing. Record whether the question exposes missing evidence, a misunderstanding in the presentation or a genuine conflict in the design.

**Worked example.** The busy-yard learner explains why access and care shaped the provisional choice. They show that water and measurements are still unresolved. A claim that the plan is ready to construct would not follow from that evidence, however attractive the presentation.

**Learner task.** Present your folder to a partner. Ask them to trace a decision from brief to evidence to proposal. Record their question and your answer or next check. Use the capstone criteria to identify anything not yet demonstrated.

**Check.** Does a confident presentation resolve an unknown water supply? **Answer:** no. **Reason:** speaking clearly can communicate evidence; it cannot create missing evidence.

**Evidence for feedback.** The learner explains the choice and its limits, responds to a substantive question and identifies a justified next action.

## D6.2 — Observe whether the design is helping

**Question to show.** A growing area looks green after rain. Does that prove the design has met the household's food and workload goals?

**Teaching text.** Decide what to observe from the goals in the brief. If access was a priority, ask its users whether the route works and observe it under relevant conditions. If care was a concern, record the tasks and difficulties. A harvest record can inform food production, but it does not alone prove improved household nutrition or income.

Keep a starting record so later observations have context. Record what was actually implemented, when, and any changes from the plan. Note the conditions and other events that may influence the result. Do not attribute every improvement or setback to the design without considering other explanations.

A short course cannot show a whole seasonal outcome. Agree a follow-up appropriate to the decision and the situation, with actual dates set during delivery. If no implementation has occurred, assess the plan and reasoning as such. Do not substitute a generated picture or imagined harvest for observed results.

**Worked example.** The busy-yard brief suggests following access usability and care demands. There is no claimed later harvest or success story in the supplied case. The learner prepares an observation sheet and explains what would count as useful evidence; they cannot fill it with invented results.

**Learner task.** Link each priority to an observation, a source or method, the person responsible and an agreed review point. State what the observation can and cannot demonstrate. Use practice data only if clearly labelled as supplied classroom data.

**Check.** Does a photograph establish that income increased? **Answer:** no. **Reason:** that claim needs appropriate financial evidence and context, not appearance alone.

**Evidence for feedback.** The monitoring plan matches the brief and distinguishes planned, reported and observed results. No outcome is claimed before evidence exists.

## D6.3 — Revise and hand over a usable plan

**Question to show.** New evidence changes a placement decision. Should the old plan disappear without a record of what changed?

**Teaching text.** Revision is part of design. Keep the previous version, identify the new evidence and explain which decision changes. Check the connected records as well as the drawing. Moving a feature may change access, quantities, costs, water needs or care. A revised map with an old budget can tell a misleading story.

Agree the revised scope with the people affected. Separate work that can proceed from work still needing evidence or advice. Give the person taking over the current plan, source notes, implementation sequence, care responsibilities and unresolved actions. Make the information available in a format they can use without your presence.

Use feedback to plan another attempt where a skill is not yet demonstrated. Finishing the course pages, attending sessions or exporting a map are different from demonstrating the whole design process. Record the actual evidence behind the assessment, and do not turn a course completion label into unsupported specialist authority.

**Worked example.** If later busy-yard evidence shows that the preferred option cannot preserve access, the learner returns to the alternatives. That is a conditional teaching example, not a reported event. The revision would update the plan, decision record, cost scope and care discussion together.

**Learner task.** Make a revision record with the question, new evidence, affected decision, change and connected records updated. Ask a partner to use the handover pack to explain the next action and what remains on hold. If they cannot, improve the pack and try again.

**Check.** Is changing only the picture enough when the proposal's material quantities change? **Answer:** no. **Reason:** linked quantities, cost assumptions and implementation information must also be checked.

**Evidence for feedback.** The learner explains a justified revision, preserves its reason and produces a usable handover. The facilitator records demonstrated, needs another attempt or not yet observed with evidence.

**App companion for D6.** Evidence, design and exports guides. Inspect the saved and exported revision, retain the previous record and share only with the household's agreement. A paper/oral handover remains valid.
