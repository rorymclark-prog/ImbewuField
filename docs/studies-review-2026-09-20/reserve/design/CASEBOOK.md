# Worked household case — the busy yard

All people, observations and events below are invented for classroom discussion. They are not a report of a real household or evidence of an intervention's success. There are no supplied dimensions, rainfall totals, water quantities, costs, yields or plant identities. Do not infer them from a future illustration.

## Stable starting facts

**BRIEF-A — household discussion.** The household wants vegetables for meals, a route that stays usable between the home and gate, and care work that can fit existing commitments. It does not want animals added in this first phase. The person who usually carries water asks to be included in siting decisions. Another household member needs a route without obstructions. The household agrees to explore options in its existing yard, but that agreement is not a verified boundary survey or permission for work beyond the yard.

**WALK-A — initial visit.** A used route crosses the yard between the home and gate. The existing growing patch lies beside that route. A water point is beside the home. Containers are carried from it to the patch. The eastern part of the yard is shaded during this visit. These observations say nothing yet about the full seasonal shade pattern, water pressure, available volume, water quality or production potential.

**TALK-A — reported history.** A resident says water has crossed the eastern part during heavy rain. No rain is falling on the first visit. Record the resident's experience as reported information, with consent; do not replace it with an invented slope arrow or present it as a measured flood level.

**MAP-A — first sketch.** It locates the home, gate, used route, water point, existing patch and the area mentioned in TALK-A relative to each other. It is labelled “not to scale; boundary, north and dimensions need checking”. No compass direction is derived from an unverified photograph. The supplied statement that part of the yard is eastern is a case description, not a direction the learner has measured.

**RESOURCES-A — current limits.** Available care time, reliable water supply, soil conditions, material quantities and costs are not yet recorded. No other person's labour or money is promised. No neighbour's land, water route or access is available merely because it appears on a picture.

## Worked decisions through the first two stages

### D1 — what is the task?

The brief prioritises food, usable access and manageable care. Adding a pond or animals is not a stated need. The facilitator asks the people doing the work what they can maintain and what must remain clear. The learner records missing resource information instead of assigning an imaginary budget.

On the first map the growing patch is an existing feature. An idea for a new patch belongs on a proposal overlay. The learner does not erase the used route because it interrupts the picture. The family report about rain is kept beside its source; it is not changed into an observed event.

### D2 — what do the facts mean?

The daily watering journey makes convenient access worth investigating. It does not prove that the closest possible location is suitable. The shade snapshot needs further seasonal evidence, and the reported water route needs examination before considering an intervention.

The learner writes: “Can we improve care of the existing patch while keeping the route usable?” This is a design question. “Build a swale through the eastern yard” would jump past the missing terrain, water, suitability and permission evidence.

For the next safe visit, the learner asks the household to show how people move through the space. They arrange further shade observations and ask who can help assess the reported water problem. They do not enter dangerous runoff to obtain a photograph. They keep all three options open: improve the existing patch, consider another suitable location, or defer expansion until resources are known.

## D3 — concept comparison, still conditional

Concept A investigates improving care of the existing patch. Concept B investigates a different location while retaining the access route. Both must meet BRIEF-A. A has not been shown to cost less; B has not been shown to produce more. Both still need the resource and site checks recorded in D1–D2. The provisional preference for investigating A is based on manageable care and existing access, not an invented performance advantage. Do not add new dimensions or resource quantities silently. When a later source card supplies information, identify what changed, why it is credible within the case and which earlier decision it affects.

A completed worked example must eventually show a scaled source pack, concept comparison, developed layout, dependencies and care, a cost/unknown record and a revision after feedback. This file currently supplies the brief, analysis and conditional concept comparison; it is not that completed example.
