## Sequence and learner choice

Keep the ten-module foundation as the shared route. Follow with propagation, seasonal planning and postharvest, then money and community practice. Animal modules are electives: a learner need not keep every species. Take the shared care module before the relevant animal module, and planned grazing before implementing a grazing design. Land/water responsibilities applies before relevant works. The capstone integrates only the systems actually chosen.

Depth is staged. This draft does not turn a short introduction into competence in grafting, veterinary care, dairy hygiene, hive work or engineered earthworks. Those need supervised practical units and qualified review. Advanced food preservation, seed production quality, irrigation maintenance, local ecological restoration and nutrition remain candidates for later specialist units, not falsely labelled complete here.

## Connection to the source reviews

- The course-gap audit supplies propagation, animal health, seasons, postharvest, finance and permission gaps.
- Farmer-money and production-metrics audits supply cash/profit, units, missing values and attribution hazards.
- Training, mentor and MEL audits supply practical evidence, accessibility, privacy, retry and certificate boundaries.
- Rory's course outlines supply observation → demonstration → practice → record → feedback. Their unsupported professional/certificate claims are not repeated.
- The SA and ASAP handbooks supply homestead integration and record exercises. Unsupported figures, old prices, legal claims and recipes are held out.
- Homestead Ecology and RVCC material supply local systems thinking. The universal succession-to-forest claim is corrected: existing grasslands and other natural habitats matter.
- The FHR file is a certificate bundle, not a manual. Only generic topic headings are used; no learner identities are reproduced.

## Production order

Start with propagation and money because they address clear gaps and can use current nursery, harvest and record imagery. Next prepare animal care, chickens and planned grazing with local practitioners. Rabbit, goat, sheep, cattle and bee demonstrations require species-specific review before filming. Finish calendar/postharvest/community/capstone with actual local exercise data and learner trials.

Use the approved warm rural South African image style. Flow is for credible actions and natural movement; deterministic animation is for exact mechanics, units, labels, timelines and calculations. Never use invented AI writing, instant growth, changing animal anatomy or cinematic recovery as teaching evidence. Keep one continuous action per short clip. Review the complete clip and first/last frames, not just its poster. Include visible download size, readable transcript, audio timing and an offline fallback.

All reserve releases require: source/claim review; local practitioner approval where specified; learner-readable text; fluent isiZulu review; narration matched to the exact script; still and full-motion inspection; practical assessment trial; phone/offline and quiz verification. This is a production checklist for the team, not an extra approval burden on the learner.
