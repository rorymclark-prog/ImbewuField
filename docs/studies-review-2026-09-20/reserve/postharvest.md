# From harvest to household or customer

Status: reserve English draft, three lessons. Food-safety practitioner review and product-specific storage/preservation procedures are required before release. This fills the course-gap audit's postharvest gap without treating older handbook recipes as validated processes.

## Lesson 1 — Plan the route before picking

Know where the harvest is going and what condition the household or customer needs. Prepare suitable clean containers, tools, shade and transport. Handle produce gently; crushing, dropping and overfilling can cause damage that appears later. Keep the harvest away from animals, manure and dirty surfaces. Different crops need different maturity and handling decisions; check the local crop guide.

**Demonstration:** trace a basket from plant to shaded sorting area and then to its destination. Identify avoidable handling and delay. **Check:** is picking more produce always better when there is no storage or buyer? **Answer:** no; match the harvest plan to safe use, demand and handling capacity.

## Lesson 2 — Protect food during sorting and storage

Separate damaged or unsuitable produce from the sale batch and record what happens to it. Use water suitable for the food-handling task. Clear-looking water is not proof that it is safe. Clean hands, containers and work surfaces matter. Keep incompatible dirty activities away from the clean packing area.

Use storage and cooling guidance suited to the crop. Shade can limit heating, but does not replace every crop's cooling requirement. Do not assume all produce belongs at the same temperature or in a sealed wet bag. Record the batch and harvest date so the oldest suitable stock can be identified and a problem traced.

**Activity:** arrange picture cards for harvest, sorting, packing, storage and transport. Mark one contamination risk and one damage risk at each relevant stage. **Check:** can a good appearance prove food is free of harmful microbes? **Answer:** no; the handling process and water quality also matter.

## Lesson 3 — Measure losses before changing the system

Record the amount harvested, used, sold, rejected and still stored, using matching units. Note the reason for rejection where known. A shorter journey may help, but packaging, heat, delay and buyer demand still affect the outcome. Compare actual results before paying for new equipment.

Preserving food is a separate technical skill. Drying, fermenting, bottling and canning need a validated process for the particular food and equipment. This module does not turn an old recipe or an attractive animation into a safety guarantee. Learn the approved method with a qualified trainer before making shelf-stable food for sale.

**Field task:** follow one real or demonstration batch, identify a preventable loss and propose a change with a way to check it. **Pass criteria:** appropriate handling route, clean/dirty separation, traceable batch, complete destination record and recognition of the limits of the demonstrated storage method. No fixed loss percentage is a pass target.

**Media:** Flow can show careful picking, a basket placed in shade or sorting on a clean surface. Keep produce count and condition consistent. Use diagrams for contamination pathways and the batch record. Detailed preservation, time/temperature and sanitation procedures need verified real demonstrations and independently checked overlays.

**Sources:** [FAO small-scale harvest handling](https://www.fao.org/4/ae075e/ae075e03.htm) and [cooling and shade](https://www.fao.org/4/ae075e/ae075e13.htm); [WHO Five Keys to Safer Food](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789241594639) for hygiene principles. Rory's manuals supply the household surplus/use context; their figures and recipes are not adopted without a current validated source. These sources do not establish compliance of a particular South African food business.

Before release, select the actual demonstration crop, water standard, cleaning procedure, storage method and local food-sale requirements with the appropriate practitioner. Complete fluent isiZulu, audio and full-motion review.
