# A local growing calendar and household food plan

Status: reserve English teaching draft. Three lessons, a field task and assessment; crop-specific calendars and isiZulu/media review remain outstanding. This develops the year-round production workshop and recorded commitments in Rory's five-day outline.

## Lesson 1 — Start with the household and place

List what the household actually uses, when it runs short and what space, water and care are available. Record rain, heat, cold, shade and frost observations. Ask experienced local growers how conditions vary across the area. Keep their advice with its place and date. A South African province contains different sites; one national sowing calendar cannot describe them all.

**Activity:** place household food-gap cards beside a blank month strip. Add actual observations without filling unknown months from imagination. **Check:** another village grows a crop in a certain month; must your sowing date match? **Answer:** no; check your site, crop and variety, water and harvest needs.

## Lesson 2 — Work back from the harvest

Choose a suitable crop from the checked course list. Use its local growing guidance and expected development period to work backwards from the desired harvest. Label this as a plan, not a completed harvest. Allow for nursery work where needed. Consider succession sowings where the crop and season suit them, adjusting from experience rather than copying one interval for every crop.

Plan labour, water and inputs alongside sowing. A date is not workable if the household cannot care for the crop then. Keep room to change the plan when weather or household circumstances change.

**Activity:** make one crop plan with a source for the proposed sowing window. Name the assumption most likely to change it. **Check:** is the app's projected harvest already food in the household? **Answer:** no; it remains an estimate until the crop is harvested and its destination recorded.

## Lesson 3 — Compare the plan with what happened

Record actual sowing, emergence, transplanting where relevant, losses and harvests. Note weather and management changes. Compare the same crop and unit when judging results. Food kept at home, food sold and food lost answer different questions. Do not claim improved nutrition from crop area alone.

At the next planning meeting, identify one useful change and one unresolved question. Keep a backup for a likely food gap. Revisit commitments rather than blaming the learner for weather they could not control.

**Field task:** a local calendar, one sourced crop plan and one later observation. **Pass criteria:** local conditions named, forecast distinguished from result, resources considered and a justified adjustment explained. Spoken responses and pictorial calendars are valid evidence.

**Media:** a warm homestead harvest image followed by a deterministic calendar. Reveal actual observations and planned dates in different styles. Flow can show a farmer inspecting a bed; it must not turn a seedling into a harvest in seconds without an explicit illustrative time-change label.

**Sources:** Rory's course outlines and ASAP handbook supply the calendar, observation and follow-up structure. [Maryland succession planting](https://www.extension.umd.edu/resource/vegetable-succession-and-companion-planting) supports staggered crops but its calendar is not imported. [FAO Farm Business School](https://www.fao.org/4/i2136e/i2136e00.htm) supports planning against resources and reviewing results. Audits on crop-plan truth, production metrics and site survey supply the distinction between forecast, harvest and measured household outcome.

Release requires locally checked crop/variety examples, dated sources, a learner trial, fluent isiZulu and audio review. No new species or yield targets are introduced by this draft.
