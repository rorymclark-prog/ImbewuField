# Homestead animals: a reserve learning pathway

Status: English teaching drafts, not public modules. This pathway expands the current Small Livestock introduction into six species modules plus a shared care module. It is not a ration, veterinary treatment schedule or breeding prescription. Each module needs a South African animal-health/husbandry review, local examples, fluent isiZulu review and practical assessment before publication.

Rory's manuals provide the integrated-farm approach. Their unsupported feed-saving percentages, stocking densities, annual honey yields and automatic pest-control claims are deliberately excluded. Keep real costs, daily care and animal welfare visible alongside useful products.

## Shared module — Can this household care for animals?

**Lesson 1: Start with the daily responsibility.** An animal needs care when the garden is busy, when someone is ill and when there is no sale. Before bringing animals home, name the person who will check water, feed, shelter, behaviour and security each day. Name a backup person too. Count the work and costs before counting the hoped-for eggs, milk, meat or honey.

Draw the proposed animal area beside the garden map. Mark feed storage, water, shade, shelter, handling access and where new or unwell animals can be kept apart according to local advice. Keep manure and dirty runoff away from drinking water and food handling.

**Activity:** walk the route a carer will use. Find one task that would fail if the main carer were absent. Change the plan before acquiring animals. **Check:** a neighbour offers free animals today, but no feed or housing is ready. **Answer:** prepare first. Free acquisition does not remove daily costs or welfare needs.

**Lesson 2: Observe and get help early.** Learn what normal eating, drinking, movement and behaviour look like in the chosen species. Record changes. An animal that stops eating, struggles to breathe, collapses or shows unusual disease signs needs prompt qualified help. Do not turn a photograph or a chatbot answer into a diagnosis. Keep the local animal-health contact accessible offline.

Ask the state veterinarian about current disease controls before buying, moving or mixing stock. Keep a record of origin and movements. New animals that look healthy may still bring disease. Agree separation, cleaning and a health plan with the adviser; do not invent a universal quarantine period from this draft.

**Activity:** practise describing an observation: animal identity, what changed, when it began and what has already been done. Use a simulated case. **Check:** should healthy-looking new animals automatically join the resident group? **Answer:** no; follow the agreed introduction and biosecurity plan.

**Lesson 3: Connect animals to farm records.** Record animals entering and leaving, births, deaths, feed, care costs and useful products. Keep household food separate from sales and unpaid sales separate from payments. If animals use crop residues or garden space, record that resource use too. Manure is a useful material to manage, not proof that the farm has become a closed nutrient cycle.

**Field task:** produce a care rota, an annotated housing/water plan, a dated source of local advice and one example animal record. A learner without animals can assess a demonstration unit. **Pass evidence:** identifies daily needs, a backup carer, a disease-reporting route and a realistic reason to proceed or postpone. Buying an animal is not an assessment requirement.

## Chicken module — Feed, shelter and eggs

**Lesson 1: A flock needs more than a place to scratch.** Decide whether the purpose is eggs, meat, breeding or a combination. That changes the care and feeding plan. Foraging can contribute, but scraps and insects alone do not guarantee an adequate diet. Match feed to the birds and their stage of production with local guidance. Protect stored feed from damp, pests and contamination.

**Demonstration:** inspect a feed label, feeder and drinker. Identify the intended bird group and show how every bird can reach clean water. **Check:** can a green-looking run prove that layers receive enough nutrition? **Answer:** no; feed quality and the birds' needs must be assessed.

**Lesson 2: Keep the house usable in real weather.** Plan a dry, ventilated, secure shelter with appropriate resting and laying places. Check it during heat and rain, not only on a pleasant day. A moveable pen is useful only if birds remain protected and the ground can recover. Move or rest an area before scratching and wet conditions damage it; there is no universal area-per-bird rule in this lesson.

**Activity:** map the clean and dirty routes for feed, eggs, litter and visitors. Identify a muddy or crowded point and propose a practical correction. **Check:** is moving the pen a substitute for checking the birds' health? **Answer:** no; land care and health checks answer different questions.

**Lesson 3: Protect flock health and food quality.** Keep new and unwell birds apart under the local health plan. Limit unnecessary traffic and clean shared equipment appropriately. Indigenous birds can still need vaccination and other disease prevention. Do not prescribe a vaccination schedule from an old manual. Record eggs collected, broken or rejected, used at home and sold, plus feed and other costs.

**Field task:** explain a daily inspection and reconcile an egg record. **Pass evidence:** adequate feed/water planning, shelter checks, disease-contact route and honest records. **Media:** Flow can show filling a clean drinker or collecting eggs with stable hands and egg counts; a diagram should show clean/dirty routes. No clip should imply that one handful of scraps supplies a whole flock.

## Rabbit module — Prepare the system before breeding

**Lesson 1: Housing and diet come before numbers.** Plan a secure, cleanable enclosure that allows suitable movement, resting and normal behaviour. Ask a rabbit-experienced adviser to check the design, flooring, social grouping, ventilation and protection from heat and predators. A cage sketch is not a welfare approval. Provide clean water and a suitable fibre-containing diet for the animals' life stage; do not copy chicken feed or assume garden scraps are balanced.

**Activity:** inspect a proposed unit without acquiring rabbits. Check access for cleaning and observation, and identify how water failure or hot weather would be noticed. **Check:** does rapid reproduction make rabbit keeping automatically profitable? **Answer:** no; housing, diet, welfare, mortality, care and actual demand still determine the result.

**Lesson 2: Biosecurity protects domestic and wild animals.** Rabbit haemorrhagic disease has been detected in South Africa. Prevent contact with outside rabbits and hares, including indirect contact through equipment and people. Ask the state veterinarian for current local precautions and vaccine advice. The statement about vaccine availability in a 2022 notice must not be presented as today's availability.

Unexpected deaths need veterinary investigation. Do not move, sell or distribute affected animals or carcasses while seeking instructions. Do not teach carcass disposal or disinfection doses without current local veterinary guidance.

**Scenario:** rabbits die suddenly after new stock arrives. **Check:** should the remaining animals be sold quickly? **Answer:** no; avoid spreading a possible disease and contact the veterinarian promptly.

**Lesson 3: Breeding is a planned responsibility.** Keep individual identity, parentage where known, dates, health observations and outcomes. Plan homes or a lawful market and capacity for young animals before breeding. Ask an experienced practitioner to supervise handling and reproductive management. Do not teach breeding intervals or litter forecasts as fixed promises.

**Field task:** submit an enclosure assessment, a prevention plan and a blank record that can trace an animal and its care. **Pass evidence:** identifies the missing resources before breeding and knows how to report unexpected deaths. **Media:** a reviewed still may show a suitable unit; Flow may show a calm animal moving naturally in it. Reject cramped housing, unstable anatomy, rough restraint or instant multiplication.

## Goat module — Browse, fencing and observation

**Lesson 1: A goat does not make unwanted plants safe.** Goats can browse woody vegetation as part of their diet, but access to vegetation is not a complete feeding plan. Identify forage and poisonous-plant risks with local expertise. Protect young trees and sensitive areas. Check fences and gates from the animal's point of view, including escape and entanglement risks.

**Activity:** map permitted browsing and areas to exclude. Record forage available now and the planned source during shortages. **Check:** can every unidentified shrub be treated as goat feed? **Answer:** no; identify it and verify suitability first.

**Lesson 2: Nutrition and health plans are specific.** Feed and mineral products are not interchangeable between species. Use a goat-appropriate plan; do not add supplements by guesswork. Sheep and goats can share important parasites. Adding chickens behind goats is not a proven replacement for parasite checks and an animal-health plan.

**Demonstration:** read the intended-species section of a real feed label and a blank treatment record. Have an animal-health practitioner teach any hands-on health assessment. **Check:** after rotating camps, may the farmer stop all parasite monitoring? **Answer:** no; rotation does not establish that animals are parasite-free.

**Lesson 3: Record the whole enterprise.** Decide whether the herd is for milk, meat, breeding or vegetation management. Record the resources and work for that purpose. Breeding adds needs for mothers and young animals. Milk or meat intended for food requires a separate hygiene and lawful-sale process; a productive animal does not by itself produce a safe final food.

**Field task:** present a browse/exclusion map, seasonal feed plan and daily observation record. **Pass evidence:** protects young plantings, distinguishes feed needs and identifies when to seek qualified help. **Media:** Flow can show browsing on verified safe material behind a stable fence. Use a diagram for nutrient movements; do not show goats clearing every shrub as a restoration guarantee.

## Sheep module — Flock needs and pasture condition

**Lesson 1: Read both the flock and the ground.** Grazing management must consider what animals can actually eat, their condition and the recovering vegetation. A green field may still provide inadequate feed. Ask for local forage and flock assessment before choosing stock numbers. Record the condition and needs of different groups rather than treating all animals as identical.

**Activity:** compare observations from two camp visits at similar viewpoints. Separate grass cover from the animals' actual feed supply. **Check:** does a photograph of green grass establish carrying capacity? **Answer:** no; quantity, quality, season and animal demand need assessment.

**Lesson 2: Keep feed and minerals appropriate.** Sheep are particularly sensitive to excess copper. Do not offer cattle or goat supplements simply because they are available. Have the whole diet checked; adding several supplements can change the total intake. No dose is prescribed here. Keep feed labels with records so the adviser can see what was offered.

**Scenario:** a mixed flock has one shared mineral feeder intended for another species. **Check:** what comes next? **Answer:** check and correct the feeding arrangement with qualified advice; do not assume every animal chooses the right amount.

**Lesson 3: Plan care for the actual type of sheep.** Wool, hair, breeding and growing animals can need different management. Arrange appropriate shearing, foot care, birthing support and disease prevention with competent practitioners. Do not improvise invasive procedures from an animation. Keep records of losses and care, as well as animals sold.

**Field task:** explain one flock group's needs and a grazing/health observation record. **Pass evidence:** recognises species-specific feed, identifies care not yet available and monitors both stock and ground. **Media:** Flow can show calm flock movement through an open gate; fence, feet and animal count must remain coherent. Skilled procedures need real expert demonstration.

## Cattle module — Land, water and a workable daily system

**Lesson 1: Match the herd to resources.** Cattle require a reliable feed and water plan through the dry period, not only during the green season. Assess forage with local expertise and include bought feed, access, handling facilities and labour in the budget. Keep a plan for drought or failed water supply before expanding the herd.

**Activity:** trace the route from each camp to water and safe handling access. Record an actual shortage contingency rather than a promised future borehole. **Check:** does a full tank today establish year-round supply? **Answer:** no; supply, demand, maintenance and dry periods must be considered.

**Lesson 2: Movement and handling need planning.** Obtain current advice about identification, movements and disease controls before buying, selling or transporting cattle. Rules may change during outbreaks. Use trained handlers and suitable facilities. Learners observe from a safe position; they do not practise restraint or emergency treatment from a video alone.

**Scenario:** a buyer wants immediate collection during a disease-control period. **Check:** should a sale agreement override movement requirements? **Answer:** no; check the current veterinary requirements before movement.

**Lesson 3: Dairy adds another system.** A milking enterprise needs appropriate feeding, udder care, clean equipment, suitable water, hygienic handling and prompt temperature control. Arrange training for the actual system and verify food requirements before sale. Milk that looks normal is not proof of microbiological safety. Do not copy old cooling times or temperatures into a universal instruction without a validated process.

**Field task:** compare a beef or dairy proposal with available feed, water, skills and buyers. **Pass evidence:** identifies what remains unknown, names competent help and includes costs beyond the animal purchase. **Media:** a Flow scene can show cattle walking calmly toward a maintained water point. Milk hygiene, treatment and restraint require expert-verified footage and precise diagrams.

## Bee module — Pollination with responsible hive management

**Lesson 1: Start with the site and people.** Before installing a hive, learn with an experienced local beekeeper. Assess neighbours, paths, public access, livestock, forage and water. Plan safe access for the beekeeper and what happens if people are stung. Do not place hives beside a busy footpath because the garden picture looks attractive.

**Activity:** mark people and animal routes on a proposed apiary map. List questions for the mentor. **Check:** is an empty corner automatically a suitable hive site? **Answer:** no; flight activity, neighbours and safe management must be considered.

**Lesson 2: Keep records and follow the local controls.** Confirm current beekeeper registration, apiary records and movement requirements through the Department and the mentor. Do not move colonies between regions using an old slide as permission. Learn disease recognition and colony assessment under supervision. Natural resilience is not immunity to every disease or parasite.

**Demonstration:** use empty equipment and a sample inspection record before a supervised hive visit. No learner must open a live hive to pass an introductory theory lesson. **Check:** can a borrowed colony be moved without checking the relevant controls? **Answer:** no; verify requirements first.

**Lesson 3: Pollination and honey are different outcomes.** Bees may contribute to pollination, but effects depend on crop, flowering, other pollinators and conditions. Honey harvest varies with forage, weather, colony health and management. Record observations and actual harvests; do not budget from a guaranteed annual yield. Leave colony feeding decisions to sound husbandry advice rather than taking every visible store.

**Field task:** present a site assessment, mentor contact route and sample colony/harvest record. **Pass evidence:** recognises people-related risks, current controls and uncertain output. **Media:** warm flower-and-bee close-ups can use Flow if flower structure and insect movement remain plausible. Hive opening, smoke use and extraction should use experienced beekeepers and verified footage, not invented procedures.

## Sources and publication requirements

Sources accessed 20 September 2026. Primary South African sources define local scope; specialist veterinary references support the limited species distinctions. No foreign dose, seasonal calendar, stocking rate or legal requirement is transplanted.

- Rory's South African homestead handbook and prior course outlines: integrated farm planning, animals as responsibilities and practical demonstration. Numerical output/feed claims need evidence and are excluded.
- [ARC poultry teaching scope](https://www.arc.agric.za/arc-api/Pages/PoultryNutrition.aspx) and [ARC poultry training, biosecurity section](https://arc.agric.za/arc-iscw/csa-toolbox/pages/assets/modules/11.pdf): housing, stage-specific feeding, records and prevention. The draft does not copy vaccination or culling instructions.
- [South African RHD notice, 17 November 2022](https://www.gov.za/news/media-statements/agriculture-land-reform-and-rural-development-outbreak-rabbit-hemorrhagic): historical detection, contact precautions and reporting. Vaccine availability in that notice is explicitly not a current claim.
- [Rabbit housing and nutrition review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28781038/) and [Merck rabbit nutrition](https://www.merckvetmanual.com/exotic-and-laboratory-animals/rabbits/nutrition-of-rabbits): welfare and life-stage diet review; no production forecast or exact ration adopted.
- [UMN multi-species grazing](https://extension.umn.edu/agriculture/animals-and-livestock/livestock-operations/multi-species-grazing) and [ACSRPC parasite planning](https://www.wormx.info/growworms): species and parasite distinctions, without importing a universal rest interval.
- [Texas A&M diagnostic laboratory, copper toxicity](https://tvmdl.tamu.edu/case-studies/copper-toxicity-in-show-animals/): particular sheep sensitivity and cumulative supplements. No treatment advice or dose copied.
- [ARC rangeland management](https://www.arc.agric.za/arc-api/Pages/Animal%20Recording%20and%20Improvement/Rangeland-Management-.aspx): local condition, capacity and fodder planning. [ARC dairy](https://www.arc.agric.za/arc-iscw/CSA-Toolbox/Pages/CSA-Dairy-Production.aspx): context-specific feeding, facilities, sanitation and welfare.
- [Department Animal Health](https://www.nda.gov.za/index.php/core-business/agricultural-production/animal-production/animal-health) and [FMD reporting/support](https://fmd.nda.gov.za/): routes for current local advice. Do not teach old outbreak maps or movement periods as current everywhere.
- [Department beekeeper forms](https://www.nda.gov.za/index.php/publication/393-inspection-services-forms), [honeybee control measures](https://www.nda.gov.za/images/Branches/AgricProducHealthFoodSafety/PlantProductionHealth/PlantHealth/Legislation-and-Regulations/Regulations/Control-Measures-Honey-Bees/Consolidated%20Control%20Measures%20relating%20to%20honey-bees.pdf) and [ARC pollination](https://www.arc.agric.za/arc-ppri/Pages/Insect%20Ecology/Pollination-Research.aspx): registration/records and crop-specific pollination context. Exact current movement provisions require verification before publication.

Before each module is released: local species practitioner signs off the actual housing, feed and health demonstrations; dynamic controls are rechecked and dated; isiZulu and audio are reviewed together; all animations are watched for welfare, anatomy, continuity and implied claims; the practical rubric is tried with learners. These drafts do not confer competence in veterinary care, hive work, dairy hygiene, animal handling or slaughter.
