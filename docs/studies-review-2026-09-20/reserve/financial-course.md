# Farm Finance: from a first record to a business decision

Status: expanded course specification requested by Rory on 20 September 2026. Eight units and 24 lesson plans below; not 24 completed teaching scripts or published lessons. Keep the five-lesson `farm-money.md` introduction as the accessible starting point. This deeper pathway follows it and connects to the app companions. Do not silently change the existing 25-module inventory to imply these lessons are already produced.

## Learning approach

Follow one clearly labelled practice farm through a season: mapped growing areas, planned work, purchases, harvest destinations, sales, invoices, payments and review. Use a second enterprise to test whether the learner can transfer the method. The facilitator supplies redacted local evidence or a reviewed synthetic practice dataset. Practice figures are exercise inputs, never local prices, yields or income promises. Use rand and cents consistently and retain the original product units.

Each lesson needs a plain-language explanation, a demonstrated example, a guided exercise, an independent attempt, an answer with reasoning, and a short field task. Let learners answer aloud, point, sort cards or use a calculator. Progressive difficulty comes from decisions and evidence, not harder vocabulary. Include a printable workbook and an app companion; neither assumes uninterrupted signal.

## Depth and worked exercises

F1 begins with the learner's existing record habits and a boundary between household and farm. Keep household food value visible without calling it cash income. Work from an opening position; a list of this month's receipts and payments is not automatically the bank balance. The independent check includes money borrowed, money owed by a buyer, equipment bought, produce eaten at home and a cash sale.

F2 uses linked documents and distinct dates. Reconcile cash, bank and mobile-money evidence without adding the same transfer between accounts as a second income. Reconcile quantities only within comparable units. Show an unknown quantity or cost explicitly, and retain a correction trail. The learner must explain why changing a kilogram entry to a bunch is not a harmless label edit.

F3 builds a cost sheet with quantities, units, source dates and cost attribution. A simplified exercise may use contribution per unit = selling price per unit minus variable cost per unit. Break-even units = included fixed costs divided by positive contribution per unit, only under the stated constant-price/cost assumptions. If contribution is zero or negative, this formula does not yield a viable break-even quantity. State exclusions, relevant period, capacity and whole-unit rounding. Do not label a partial margin as net profit. Include unsold produce, transport, packaging, paid labour and separately visible family labour in discussion. Review these calculations with a bookkeeper before recording.

F4 extends the budget into dated receipts and payments. Opening cash plus receipts minus payments gives closing cash within the defined cash scope. Carry the closing amount into the next period. Show the difference between making a sale and receiving payment. Test timing as well as totals; a promising season-end result does not pay an earlier bill. Do not treat an application for funding as confirmed cash.

F5 uses an unpaid invoice, a paid invoice, an old paper document and a sale already in the book. A payment settles an amount owed; it does not create new produce. Teach a separate customer balance schedule for deposits, instalments and adjustments. The present app has only paid-in-full/unpaid status, so it cannot faithfully demonstrate a partial-payment ledger. Never mark an invoice paid in full after only a deposit. Do not issue invented refunds or credit notes as an app exercise; include those concepts with verified bookkeeping guidance and real supported tools.

F6 compares a purchase, hire and shared equipment arrangement with the costs each includes. A loan receipt changes cash and an obligation; it is not sales revenue. Repayments need a schedule separating principal from finance costs. Read lender-provided fees, interest basis, due dates, security and default terms with qualified help. No recommended lender, invented interest rate, debt ratio or guaranteed affordability rule. Replacement reserves and maintenance are planning decisions; tax depreciation and accounting treatment require current qualified guidance.

F7 explicitly separates management summaries from complete accounts. Use opening and closing stock, receivables, payables, equipment and debt to show what a cash-only book omits. A balance sheet describes assets, liabilities and equity at a date; it is not a forecast of next month's cash. Teach how to find current SARS guidance, gather source records and ask the right questions. Do not hard-code registration thresholds, tax rates, retention periods or claim that filling a VAT/tax-number field makes an invoice compliant.

F8 ends with a small, evidence-backed farm business plan. The learner presents the market need, available land and capacity, production assumptions, enterprise costs, cash timing, delivery arrangements, risks and a review date. Separate actual records from estimates and scenario outputs. A polished map or lender summary does not prove title, profitability, creditworthiness or funding approval.

## Capstone and assessment

Produce a connected practice pack: a mapped growing area, budget, dated cash forecast, harvest-and-destination record, expenses with references, invoice, payment reconciliation and a short season review. Compare a second enterprise without inventing a common unit. Include one unresolved missing value and explain what evidence would resolve it.

The assessment must detect duplicate income, an unpaid invoice counted as cash, missing costs represented as zero, a unit mismatch, an incorrect date, a partial payment marked paid in full, and a forecast presented as actual. Give a new example after corrective feedback. Successful completion demonstrates the assessed skills; it is not an accounting qualification or certification of a real business.
