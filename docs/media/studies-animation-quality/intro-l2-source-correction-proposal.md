# Introduction L2 — source correction proposal

**Status: review proposal only.** This packet does not change learner content, narration, audio, or media. The three holds below need a coordinated English edit across `lib/course-modules.ts` and `docs/narration/intro-permaculture.en.md`; update the slide 13 still only after its visual is separately reviewed. No human or practitioner approval is implied.

## 1. Avoid ranking three principles for all South African smallholders

Holmgren’s own account describes the twelve principles as his design framework, developed in *Essence of Permaculture*. His source says to use the principles together and that methods vary by place and situation. It does not support a universal “three matter most” ranking.

**Suggested narrow wording**

- Body/narration opening: “David Holmgren set out twelve design principles in *Essence of Permaculture*. Bill Mollison and David Holmgren co-originated the permaculture concept.”
- Replace “Three matter most for South African smallholders” / “Three of them do most of the work on a South African smallholding” with: “Three useful starting points for this lesson are…”
- Keep the ensuing three examples, but frame them as lesson choices rather than a ranking for all farmers.

Sources: [Holmgren, About Permaculture Principles](https://permacultureprinciples.com/about/); [Holmgren, The 12 Principles](https://permacultureprinciples.com/permaculture-principles/); [Holmgren, *Essence of Permaculture* (PDF)](https://www.permacultureprinciples.com/wp-content/uploads/2013/02/Essence_of_Pc_EN.pdf).

## 2. Observation is not a swale design or safety assessment

Watching water over a wet season is a useful first observation, but it does not establish that a swale is suitable or safe to build. ARC’s South African soil-erosion guidance describes contour swales and controlled overflow routes; it is general guidance, not a site-specific design standard. Oregon State Extension’s swale guidance also documents that suitability depends on site conditions. Its US criteria and measurements must not be copied as South African specifications.

**Suggested narrow wording**

- Body/narration: “Observe where water flows and pools over a wet season. This helps you understand the site; it does not by itself show that a swale is suitable or safe to build. Before digging, check the soil, slope, drainage and a safe overflow route that will not cause erosion or send damaging water to neighbours. Ask a trained local adviser to assess the site.”
- Keep the quiz’s current correct option about observing water during a wet season, but change its rationale to: “A wet season shows more than one storm, but observation is only a first step. Check the soil, slope, drainage and safe overflow route with a trained local adviser before digging.”
- Replace the quiz distractor “Hire a civil engineer to survey the contours” with a clearly unsuitable universal shortcut such as “Assume the same swale design fits every site.” The current distractor could imply that professional assessment is unnecessary.

Sources: [Agricultural Research Council, “Simple solutions to combat soil erosion” (South Africa)](https://www.arc.agric.za/Agricultural%20Sector%20News/Simple%20solutions%20to%20combat%20soil%20erosion.pdf); [Oregon State University Extension, *Water-Quality Swales*](https://extension.oregonstate.edu/catalog/pub/em-9209-water-quality-swales-low-impact-development-fact-sheet). ARC’s article is general practice guidance, not approval for a particular farm design.

## 3. Remove the guaranteed hail-protection claim

University of Minnesota Extension explains that hail injury and yield impact vary with damage severity and crop growth stage. It does not test the lesson’s specific comparison between monoculture maize and a mixed planting. The claim that a mixed planting “rarely” suffers a total loss, or is “insurance,” therefore goes beyond the cited evidence.

**Suggested narrow wording**

- Body: replace the maize/mixed-planting sentence with: “Use and value diversity. Hail injury to maize depends on the storm and the crop’s growth stage.”
- Narration: “Use and value diversity. Hail injury to maize depends on the storm and the crop’s growth stage. This principle does not promise that a crop will survive every event.”
- Replace the slide 13 image’s deterministic contrast (maize destroyed, mixed plot unharmed) with a neutral still that does not promise survival or relative loss rates.
- Remove or qualify the key point “Diversity protects against a single event — hail, drought, or pest outbreak”; it makes a broader outcome claim than the source establishes.

Source: [University of Minnesota Extension, “Wind and hail damage on corn”](https://extension.umn.edu/agriculture/crop-production/corn/wind-and-hail-damage).

**Practitioner judgement still needed:** the cited source addresses maize, not the performance of a specified local mixed-crop design. Any claim about which South African crop mix reduces a household’s risk, and any replacement illustration comparing outcomes, needs local agronomic/practitioner review. The neutral wording above avoids making that comparison.
