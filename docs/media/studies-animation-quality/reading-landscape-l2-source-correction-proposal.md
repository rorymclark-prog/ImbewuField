# Reading the Landscape L2 — proposed English source correction

**Historical proposal, superseded by the [L2 lesson check](reading-landscape-l2-lesson-check.md).**
The released wording omits the proposed wall-warmth suggestion because FAO
notes that buildings can block cold-air drainage and worsen frost in some
layouts. This packet preserves the source-audit reasoning, not the current
learner text.

## Exact lesson proposal

Replace L2 body with:

> In much of South Africa, especially in winter, the sun is to the north. Its
> path changes with the season and your location. North-facing slopes often
> receive more sun and can be warmer and drier. South-facing slopes are often
> cooler and moister. Frost can collect in low hollows where cold air settles.
> Watch your own site before choosing where to plant tender crops or place
> buildings.
>
> Winter sun is lower and farther north than summer sun. A wall or shade cloth
> can shade a bed longer in winter than in summer. Before placing anything
> permanent, stand in the spot at 8am, midday and 4pm on a winter's day and
> watch where the shade falls.
>
> Pawpaw and young citrus are sensitive to frost. A north-facing wall may add
> some warmth, but it cannot make a frost pocket safe. Keep tender plants out
> of known low frost pockets and observe local frost before planting.

Replace key points with:

- North-facing slopes often get more direct sun; south-facing slopes are often
  cooler and moister.
- Winter sun is lower and farther north; check local shade before building.
- Cold air can collect in low hollows; aspect is only one site factor.
- Check local frost before placing tender pawpaw or young citrus.

Replace quiz 1, keeping `correct: 2`:

> **Question:** Where should a farmer first look for a frost-tender young
> pawpaw on a Highveld smallholding?
>
> A. The lowest point where cold air collects.
>
> B. A cold, shaded hollow.
>
> C. A sunny spot outside a known frost hollow, after checking the site's frost
> pattern.
>
> D. A position chosen without checking the site.
>
> **Rationale:** Cold air can collect in low places. A sunnier site outside a
> known frost pocket may reduce risk, but local frost observations must guide

Replace quiz 2, keeping `correct: 0`:

> **Question:** A farmer plans shade cloth on the north side of her garden.
> What should she check before fixing it in place?
>
> A. Where its shadow falls on the bed in winter.
>
> B. Whether it redirects frost away.

## Source basis and coordinated work

- [Department of Basic Education geography material](https://www.education.gov.za/Portals/0/CD/Computer/2017%20FebMarch%20Exam%20Papers/Geography%20P1%20Feb-March%202017%20Memo%20Eng.pdf)
  identifies the December solstice sun over the Tropic of Capricorn, which
  crosses northern South Africa. “Northern sky” needs a seasonal/site limit.
- [FAO frost guidance](https://www.fao.org/4/y7223e/y7223e0c.htm) explains
  cold-air drainage into low spots and treats aspect, soil and local topography
  as separate site factors. It does not guarantee protection from a wall.
- [KZN agricultural guidance](https://www.kzndard.gov.za/images/Documents/Horticulture/Veg_prod/Fruit%20and%20%20Nut%20Production%20in%20KZN.pdf)
  identifies papaya as frost-sensitive and citrus as vulnerable to severe
  frost. No new species or hardiness figure is proposed.
