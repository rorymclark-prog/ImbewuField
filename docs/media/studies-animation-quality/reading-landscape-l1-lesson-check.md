## Teaching hold

The [existing fact-check packet](../../studies-review-2026-09-20/CORE-FACT-CHECK-FOLLOWUP.md)
already identifies the unsupported two-hectares-in-a-morning promise, “No
surveyor needed,” and the quiz rationale that treats an A-frame as sufficient
for swale earthworks. [FAO's A-frame guidance](https://www.fao.org/fishery/static/FAO_Training/FAO_Training/General/x6707e/x6707e06.htm)
describes a low-cost contouring tool, its calibration and its accuracy limits;
the current six-second clip is an illustration, not those steps.

Two further absolute claims need coordinated review. Slide 4 and the lesson
body say all water leaving the property is a lost resource. [FAO's water
management guidance](https://www.fao.org/4/y4690e/y4690e09.htm) describes safe
escape routes for excess water to prevent damage; retaining all runoff is not
a sound universal objective. Slide 7 and quiz present “slow it high, sink it
mid, control it low” and “as high as possible” as placement rules. [FAO's
contour-farming study guide](https://www.fao.org/fileadmin/templates/nr/images/resources/pdf_documents/FARMESA_SWC1.pdf)
warns that poorly laid contours can increase erosion and that low-infiltration
soils can accumulate dangerous water. Keep the memorable observation prompt,
but make construction and overflow site-specific. Do not add a new numeric
threshold without a local source.
