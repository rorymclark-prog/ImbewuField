## Source and teaching holds

1. The body and slide 8 narration state that the sun tracks across the northern
   sky throughout South Africa. Northern Limpopo reaches the Tropic of
   Capricorn; [South Africa's Department of Basic Education](https://www.education.gov.za/Portals/0/CD/Computer/2017%20FebMarch%20Exam%20Papers/Geography%20P1%20Feb-March%202017%20Memo%20Eng.pdf)
   identifies the December solstice sun over that tropic. Qualify the northern
   sun pattern by season and site instead of teaching a country-wide absolute.
   The useful winter observation task can remain.
2. The lesson joins “south-facing” and “hollow” as if aspect is what makes
   frost pool. [FAO's frost-protection guidance](https://www.fao.org/4/y7223e/y7223e0c.htm)
   identifies cold-air drainage into low spots as the main mechanism during
   radiation frosts, with aspect one of several site factors. Teach low hollows
   and locally observed frost first; retain aspect as a shade/warmth factor.
3. Slide 10 narration and quiz claim a **2 m** north-side shade cloth blocks
   winter sun for “most of the day.” No distance, slope, site latitude or
   actual shadow observation is supplied, so that exact outcome is an
   unsupported inference. Keep the instruction to check morning, midday and
   afternoon winter shadows before fixing a structure; remove the size and
   duration promise from protected text and assessment.
4. Slide 11, body and first quiz imply a north-facing wall will radiate enough
   heat at night to save pawpaw or young citrus. A sun-facing site can be
   helpful, but [FAO's guidance](https://www.fao.org/4/y7223e/y7223e0c.htm)
   emphasizes local cold-air drainage, aspect and soil, and does not guarantee
   a wall's protection. Qualify the wall as a possible aid, keep frost history
   and site selection primary, and remove “difference between life and death”
   and the quiz rationale's certainty. The existing species names are not to
   be changed or extended.
