# Windbreak placement and airflow

Checked 21 September 2026. This replaces the old slide-19 diagram and its overly absolute claim that standing between wind and crops is all a windbreak does. The northwest wind is explicitly an example, not a regional weather forecast. The adjacent quiz's hypothetical farm remains valid.

- [South African ARC: Windbreaks for Wind Protection, R J du Preez, 2010](https://www.arc.agric.za/arc-iscw/CSA-Toolbox/CSA%20Fruit%20Production/Pamphlets/Windbreaks%20for%20wind%20protection%20-%20final.pdf), pages 1–4: wind protection depends on placement, permeability and continuity. A belt filters wind; a solid barrier can create troublesome turbulence. Position it across the damaging wind and maintain its lower cover. Competition for water, nutrients and light is also a design consideration. Reviewed in the preceding source pass; the subsequent fetch timed out. No orchard-specific numeric recommendation is transferred into this introductory example.
- [USDA NRCS: Field Windbreak](https://www.nrcs.usda.gov/conservation-basics/land/forests/agroforestry-systems/field-windbreak): trees and shrubs across troublesome winds shelter the leeward side. Density, branches and foliage matter. This supports the placement principle, not South African species or spacing advice.
- [University of Nebraska–Lincoln, Nebraska Forest Service: How Windbreaks Work](https://nfs.unl.edu/how-windbreaks-work/), characteristics, density and orientation: some air passes through openings, while other air moves above or around the belt. Its structure affects wind-speed reduction; changing wind direction changes shelter. Numeric density, distance and climate claims are deliberately omitted.

## What the animation shows

Generated aerial teaching artwork; no real farm or measured planting layout is represented. North points upward. Arrows travel from upper-left (northwest) toward lower-right (southeast), across the illustrated tree-and-shrub belt toward the crops. The arrows represent **some** through-flow only; narration explicitly describes air moving above and around the belt too. The top-down scene cannot demonstrate vertical airflow.

Arrow spacing, length, travel time and colour are explanatory graphics. They are not measurements of air speed, shelter distance, porosity or an agronomic prescription. The plants do not grow or morph during the clip. No species names, yields, dimensions, planting calendar or quantified protection claim are added. Design and maintenance still need site-specific judgement, including shade and root competition.
