## Teaching and source boundary

The existing slide 16 narration says that, where appropriate, plain cardboard
under suitable mulch can suppress unwanted growth. The source-supported visual
relationship is mulch **above** cardboard on soil. UC Agriculture and Natural
Resources describes cardboard laid down and covered with wood mulch:
<https://ucanr.edu/site/uc-master-gardener-program-contra-costa-county/article/how-sheet-mulch>.
The clip does not prove weed suppression, show a completed bed, set a mulch
depth, or replace the lesson's safeguards to leave trunks clear and let water
enter the soil. No lesson body, quiz, rationale or species name changed.

The close-up prompt asked for one continuous fixed-camera shot of a small plain
cardboard patch on soil, one pair of gloved hands moving nearby wood chips onto
it, physical fall and settling of chips, and a final visible cardboard edge.
It excluded instant growth, new tools, buried plants, captions and claimed
outcomes. It did not use a reference image, so it should be presented as an
illustrative action, not footage of the original garden or local practice.
