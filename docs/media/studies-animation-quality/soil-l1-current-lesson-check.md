## Source and action boundary

The lesson tells learners to combine soil structure, roots, cover, moisture,
plant growth and water entry; colour or worm count alone cannot establish a
cause. [USDA NRCS soil-health assessment](https://www.nrcs.usda.gov/conservation-basics/soil/soil-health/soil-health-assessment)
also evaluates physical, biological and chemical indicators together and
compares patterns across fields and time. Slide 6's jar is expressly a rough
learning exercise rather than a treatment decision; [Montana State's jar
method](https://landresources.montana.edu/soilfertility/html/SoilTextureJarTest.html)
supports settling in stages. The current lesson body and quiz are cautious;
no source-backed L1 correction requires changing them now.

The module-opening English line “make safe compost” may sound like a sanitation
guarantee, whereas later compost teaching correctly says an ordinary heap's
hot centre does not prove every part has been treated. A matched wording review
is recommended before any protected narration edit; this is a consistency
concern, not evidence that L1's jar or soil advice is false.
