## Source basis and coordinated work

- [FAO frost guidance](https://www.fao.org/4/y7223e/y7223e0c.htm) explains
  cold-air drainage and site-specific effects of slope, aspect, soil and
  obstacles. It says radiation frosts are associated with calm conditions or
  light wind and cold-air drainage, while advection frost can make exposed
  hillside positions colder. Where local climate data are not available, it
  recommends observing through at least one frost season; 10–20 clear, cold
  nights can provide enough site data to assess frost risk. This is a source
  recommendation, not a new lesson threshold. It does not promise that every
  valley frosts first and last.
- [SAWS weather questions](https://www.weathersa.co.za/home/weatherques)
  defines frost as ice crystals forming at or below freezing and distinguishes
  it from dew and mist. It also explains black frost: plant damage can occur
  without visible frost when there is too little moisture to form visible ice.
- [ARC climate monitoring](https://www.arc.agric.za/arc-iscw/Pages/Climate-Monitoring-Services.aspx)
  provides wind speed, direction, gust and wind-rose data; it does not support
  one damaging wind direction for every Highveld or KZN escarpment farm.
- [KZN DARD tomato guidance](https://www.kzndard.gov.za/images/Documents/Horticulture/Veg_prod/tomato.pdf)
  identifies prolonged cool, damp spells as favourable to late blight and
  discusses site selection and cultural practices as part of disease
  avoidance. The [ARC summer vegetables production guideline](https://www.arc.agric.za/arc-vopi/Leaflets%20Library/Production%20Guideline%20for%20Summer%20Vegetables.pdf)
  also says late blight can be destructive under prolonged wet, cool
  conditions.
  [University of Minnesota Extension](https://extension.umn.edu/agriculture/specialty-crops/vegetable-farming/disease-management/late-blight)
  gives the specific advice to keep leaves dry, allow air between plants and
  water in the morning so foliage can dry in the sun. This supports the
  limited “may help leaves dry” wording; it is not a South African source or
  a complete late-blight control plan. Bed relocation alone is not a complete
  control.
