# 00-introduction (blind back-translation of the isiXhosa)
Introduction.
It is rare that the very rich small farmers are the ones who have very big land. They are the ones whose land works for them: land that holds water, trees that feed and shelter, and animals that make the soil rich.
That is what permaculture offers the South African farmer. Our rain is not certain, droughts keep getting longer, and seed, fertiliser and bought poisons are expensive. Permaculture answers with design: we watch how nature works and plan the farm so water, soil, plants and animals support each other, mostly using what is already on the land.
Bill Mollison, one of the founders of permaculture, said simply: "The great change we want to make comes from moving from using to producing, even if on the smallest scale, in our gardens."
By the end of this introduction you will be able to: explain why permaculture suits a small farm in SA; find the chapter of work in front of you; know where to look up a new word.
Who is this guide prepared for: small farmers and home gardeners in SA, with the mentors, students and field workers who work with them. You need a piece of land, however small, and the courage to begin.
Where this guide comes from: Rory Clark compiled it. It began as the book The Permaculture Gardening Handbook for a training course of the RVCC project, run by UNDP and the Lesotho government in 2020 and 2021. Since then it has been reviewed and adjusted in South Africa. Some parts are taken, with permission, from Introduction to Permaculture and Homestead Gardening (2014) by the African Conservation Trust.
How to use: 1 Start with ideas. Ch 1 explains permaculture and its twelve principles. Ch 2 and 3 show how to plan your farm by going around sun, wind, water and fire. 2 Then go to work. Ch 4 to 11 cover plants, animals, trees, water, soil, nature, insects and home. Each stands alone and ends with a task "Try this". 3 Try one thing at a time. Watch it work before you add the next. 4 Use the Glossary. Technical words like swale and mulch are explained when they first appear, and all are explained together in the glossary at the end. 5 Find the back notes. Notes on the law and on keeping people and animals safe are collected in one place at the back.
This guide also exists in English, isiZulu, Sesotho, Tshivenda and Xitsonga, as drafts still being reviewed by people who speak them well. And this isiXhosa language is also a machine-made draft. If something is unclear, compare with English.
Key points: Permaculture builds a farm that feeds in hard times, by design not by bought things. Start with chapters 1 to 3, then go to the chapter you need. New words are in the glossary; legal and safety notes are at the end. Start where you stand. Chapter 1 begins with the ideas that hold everything together.
