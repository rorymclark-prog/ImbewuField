# Phase A blind back-translation (from isiXhosa only) - ch 13 notes and references
(Reference entries are English titles/authors/URLs; only the prose, headings and key points are Xhosa.)

Intro: This guide shows you how to farm. Here at the back are two things that should be at the back: a note on safety and law, and the sources of the facts in all chapters.
Safety and law: This guide shows how to do the work; it doesn't replace the law or the knowledge of people who know your area, and laws vary by place and change over time. In South Africa ask the right people first before you build a dam or pond, or do any work in river, stream, spring or wetland (Dept of Water and Sanitation, National Water Act); before you plough land that "jumps a lot" [otsiba - unclear, probably meant steep] (Dept of Agriculture, CARA); before you burn a firebreak or make a veld fire (local Fire Protection Association, National Veld and Forest Fire Act); before you plant, sell or move a plant or fish you don't know, because many are declared invasive, and before you cut an indigenous tree, because many trees are protected by law (NEMBA lists 2020, National Forests Act); before you keep bees, which must be registered; and before you install solar power, which needs a trained electrician. Report sudden death of chickens or pigs to the state vet. Handle manure, greywater, sprays, fire, gas, batteries, deep water and machines carefully, keep children away from them, and if a person is badly hurt, stung or poisoned, go to clinic or phone 10177 (112 from cell). In Lesotho, Eswatini, Zimbabwe, ask your country's authorities, local agricultural advisor or traditional council.

Key points: Laws on water, land, fire, invasive plants, bees and animal diseases apply on every farm; check before taking big steps. Every fact in this guide has a source listed here. The guide is a map, not the land. Walk your land, keep your notes, add your own lines to this list.
