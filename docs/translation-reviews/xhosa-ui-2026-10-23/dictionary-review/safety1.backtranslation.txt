48 | biomeWaterSavanna | Strong summer rain storms arrive quickly. Dig trenches beside/along the slope (water-holding swales) and small ponds before rain starts in October or September, so water enters the soil instead of running off. Make tanks big: at least 50 litres for each square metre of roof that pours water into them. Dig long narrow dams — about three times longer than wide — on gentle slopes.
49 | biomeWaterGrassland | Summer brings short storms with heavy rain. On each slope, dig swales that fall slightly following the land (about 1m drop for each 1,000m crossed) so they spread water instead of it rushing. Winter is dry, so storing water is important. As a rule: 1 square metre of roof collects about 1 litre of water for each 1mm of rain.
50 | biomeWaterFynbos | Rain falls only in winter, so do digging earthworks at the end of autumn (March to May), before the rain comes. Small earth walls following contours and shallow pits covered with mulch work well. Size your water tanks carefully — summer (December to February) is dry. Household water already used (greywater) is fine for food-growing areas, but never use it on natural Fynbos — it has too many nutrients for it.
51 | biomeWaterSucculentKaroo | On west-facing slopes, shade net can catch fog water. Build a small raised rim around each plant to catch all raindrops where they fall. Reusing household greywater is suitable here. Each 100mm of rain matters — even a small roof tank is valuable.
52 | biomeWaterDesert | Shade net can catch fog water in the air. Design swales so they safely catch sudden floods. Store water underground, where it stays cool and does not evaporate. Plant trees as windbreaks — they reduce evaporation and give shade before you plant crops.
53 | biomeWaterAlbanyThicket | Rain falls in winter and summer. Use shallow swales and earth walls following contours. A Spekboom-based food forest can withstand drought and uses water wisely. Small tanks that can irrigate by gravity-fed drip work well. On clay soil, take care that plants do not stand in water.
54 | biomeWaterIocb | Rain is heavy, so controlling floods is more important than harvesting water. Use shallow swales to slow water on steep slopes. Raised beds prevent plants from staying in water. 55 | biomeSoilGrassland | Grazing animals often compact this soil to be hard. Loosen it below the surface before digging swales or ponds. Plant oats or vetch as a winter cover crop to protect and feed the soil. The soil is often slightly acidic (pH 5.5–6.5) — add agricultural lime if it is more acidic than that. Add organic matter to the soil with compost and biochar.
56 | biomeSoilSucculentKaroo | Cover the soil with stones rather than organic mulch — it is cheap and works well here. Add compost only in the food-growing area. 57 | biomeSoilNamaKaroo | A hard cement-like layer (calcrete) often lies below the soil surface and may need to be broken with a special plough (chisel plough) so roots grow downward. 58 | biomeSoilDesert | Any organic matter here is valuable — use goat or camel dung. Plant shade trees first, before any food plants. In low-lying areas, plant only species that can withstand salt. Raised sand beds with a wick (a strip of absorbent material pulls water up) work well for vegetables.

Check dates and units as well as totals. When you find an error, keep the original reference and write what changed and why. If you cannot yet explain the difference, say so. Do not add an estimated record so that the amounts agree.
