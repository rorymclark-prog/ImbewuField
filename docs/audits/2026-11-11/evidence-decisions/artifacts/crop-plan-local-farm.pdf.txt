Before using stored food
The storage row applies only when these sourced conditions are met. Check the food before using it.



   Sweet potato - sourced storage window: 3 months
   Only mature, undamaged roots; cured 30-32°C/85-90% RH for 4-7 days, then stored with minimal
   handling as cool as achievable (source optimum 13°C) at ~85-90% RH. The sweet potato weevil (Cylas
   spp.) is the main storage threat.
   Source guide



   Groundnuts (peanuts) - sourced storage window: 6 months
   Unshelled (in-shell) pods dried to in-shell moisture ~10% or less, stored in gunny bags, mud bins, baskets
   or a ventilated dry room, protected from pests and damp.
   Source guide

How this plan was put together
From the plan suggested in November 2026. Anything changed by hand since is not described here.



   WHAT TO WATCH
   Rain-fed plan: a crop is only sown where every month it grows gets rain of at least half the estimated evaporation (the rainfall test
   of the FAO growing-period method, using this site's monthly rainfall; evaporation estimated from temperature by Thornthwaite's
   method, which can under-estimate it in dry, windy places). Average rain is not every year's rain - water in a dry spell if you can.
   Maize was not placed for you: the app can measure mapped area, but it cannot draw or check a wind-pollinated maize block. Add a
   staple plot on the map, or add maize by hand once you have checked the real row layout.
   Butternut, Pumpkin, Watermelon want more room to sprawl than a veg bed can give - grow them in a dedicated plot, along your
   property edges, or in a food forest area instead. Turn on "Grow big vines in a veg bed anyway" if you'd rather use one of your beds
   for them.
   Hot-area sowing months for Groundnuts (peanuts) are not yet from a published local table - check those dates with your extension
   officer before relying on them.
   Dry beans (sugar beans), Swiss chard (spinach), Kale, Cabbage, Onions, Potato, Garlic, Peas, Broad beans (fava beans),
   Broccoli, True spinach (English spinach), Cauliflower were left out: none of their sowing windows has enough rain through the
   whole crop without irrigation.



  GROUND WITH NO NEW SOWING
  1 growing area has a stretch with no new sowing: Bed 6 (Feb, May-Aug, Nov-Dec). Well-documented crops have a sowing window
  for those stretches, but the months around them are already fully planted, or crop rotation rules out the families that would fit.
  Clearing a nearby month, or letting the ground rest, are both fine choices.
  6 growing areas have a stretch with no new sowing: Bed 1 (May-Aug, Nov), Bed 2 (May-Aug, Nov), Bed 3 (May-Aug, Nov), Bed 4
  (May-Aug), Bed 5 (May-Aug, Nov-Dec), Hügel 1 (May-Aug, Nov-Dec). No crop the catalog can plan properly - one with a checked
  growing time, spacing and yield - has a sowing window that reaches those stretches. Ask locally what else does; this plan is not
  proof that nothing can grow then.

HOW THIS PLAN WAS MADE
Sowing calendar from satellite climate records for this site: Hot area, no frost - summer rain.
This plan follows a repeating whole-year cycle. It was chosen by comparing plans starting in each of the 12 months and keeping the
one with the fewest months without a fresh harvest - not just what fits best from Nov.
These sowing months follow the KZN DARD hot-area calendar. Outside KwaZulu-Natal, confirm them with your local extension
officer.
Broad automatic choices use crops with verified yield, duration and field spacing so their productivity comparison has a common
evidence basis.
How many people you feed is not used to guess how much to plant. The planner tries the exact crops you chose; your mapped
space, the sowing windows and crop rotation decide what fits.
Some sowing months were skipped for this site's climate - too hot, or not enough rain through the crop: Maize (mielies) (not Dec);
Green beans (not Mar, Apr, May, Jun, Jul, Aug); Butternut (not Mar, Apr, May, Jul, Aug); Pumpkin (not Mar, Apr, May, Jul, Aug);
Carrots (not Feb, Mar, Apr, May, Jun, Jul, Aug); Beetroot (not Feb, Mar, Apr, May, Jun, Jul, Aug); Tomatoes (not Jan, Feb, Mar, Jul,
Dec); Peppers (not Jan, Feb, Mar, Jul, Oct, Nov, Dec); Chilli (not Jan, Feb, Mar, Jul, Oct, Nov, Dec); Sweet potato (not Aug, Nov,
Dec, Jan, Feb, Mar); Lettuce (not Feb, Mar, Apr, May, Jun, Jul); Groundnuts (peanuts) (not Nov); Cucumber (not Mar, Apr, May, Jul,

What this forecast assumes
Projection, not a harvest promise. Vegetables and staples repeat only saved recurring sowings; dated and existing crops happen
once.

Vegetable and staple crop-cycle kilograms are counted in the year of first picking, not divided into monthly quantities. Carry-in
harvests need actual records.

Vegetable and staple estimates hold bed area, sowings and crop benchmarks constant. Revise the plan if growing trees change
shade, water or available bed space.

Tree ages run from planting on this site. Separate older and younger plants into age groups. Research first-crop ages are
references, not guaranteed dates.

Tree kg are your farm or nursery assumptions per plant per year. Values hold until the next age you enter; no growth multiplier is
invented. A range covers changes within the year. Review the schedule as plants age.

Check that the variety, local climate, pollination, water and care match your yield assumptions. Ages alone do not establish site
suitability or survival.

Known subtotals leave out missing yields. Eggs, meat, milk, fish and honey are not included in these crop kg totals. Picking
months and food-gap checks still need local confirmation.

   Source direct-sown or tray seed before the named sowing month; the source does not provide a universal procurement lead time. Ready-grown seedlings are listed at the start of the field-readiness

   are approximate field positions from mapped area and published spacing, not guaranteed order quantities or loss allowances; supplier and crop-specific guidance may change what to purchase.

   Sweetcorn - check the sowing method
   Published field rate: 12-15 kg/ha. The buying range uses the mapped area; no germination allowance is added.
   Grown and eaten as a fresh vegetable, unlike field maize - the dossier's own food-group is fruiting vegetable, not staple grain, so it is kept there rather than lumped with maize despite the shared
   species. Tender to frost like maize.




   Gem squash - check the sowing method
   Published field rate: 2-3 kg/ha. The buying range uses the mapped area; no germination allowance is added.
   Cucurbitaceae family, same as pumpkin, butternut, cucumber and watermelon - avoid repeating this family in the same bed. Frost-tender vine crop; no Western Cape sowing calendar was found
   even though it is a known WC crop, so treat that as a local-confirmation item.




   Parsley - check the sowing method
   Published field rate: 3 kg/ha. The buying range uses the mapped area; no germination allowance is added.
   A biennial herb grown as an annual for its leaves. Apiaceae family, same as carrots - avoid repeating that family in the same bed too often.




   Spanspek (sweet melon) - check the sowing method
   Published field rate: 3 kg/ha. The buying range uses the mapped area; no germination allowance is added.
   Cucurbitaceae melon vine. A January sowing is left off this catalog's planner because the resulting harvest would run into frost - still workable with local frost protection, just not auto-scheduled here.
