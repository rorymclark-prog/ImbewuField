Before using stored food
The storage row applies only when these sourced conditions are met. Check the food before using it.



   Garlic - sourced storage window: 3 months
   Well-cured bulbs (dried several weeks, dark/dry/ventilated), then kept unrefrigerated in a cool, dry, dark,
   well-ventilated place (e.g. mesh bags).
   Source guide



   Onions - sourced storage window: 2 months
   Field-dried (cured, necks fully dry) bulbs held at ambient temperature in well-ventilated trays/racks.
   Source guide



   Butternut - sourced storage window: 2 months
   Mature hard-rind fruit, cured, clean, dry, undamaged, kept ventilated at 10-15°C/50-70% RH.
   Source guide



   Maize (mielies) - sourced storage window: 6 months
   Shelled grain dried to 14% moisture, kept in a clean sealed container or silo with active
   weevil/larger-grain-borer control.
   Source guide



   Sweet potato - sourced storage window: 3 months
   Only mature, undamaged roots; cured 30-32°C/85-90% RH for 4-7 days, then stored with minimal
   handling as cool as achievable (source optimum 13°C) at ~85-90% RH. The sweet potato weevil (Cylas
   spp.) is the main storage threat.
   Source guide



   Groundnuts (peanuts) - sourced storage window: 6 months
   Unshelled (in-shell) pods dried to in-shell moisture ~10% or less, stored in gunny bags, mud bins, baskets
   or a ventilated dry room, protected from pests and damp.
   Source guide

 WHAT TO WATCH
 Check Bed 1 before sowing there: something is already growing in that bed and this whole-year plan may overlap it in its first
 months.
 Rain-fed plan: a crop is only sown where every month it grows gets rain of at least half the estimated evaporation (the rainfall test
 of the FAO growing-period method, using this site's monthly rainfall; evaporation estimated from temperature by Thornthwaite's
 method, which can under-estimate it in dry, windy places). Average rain is not every year's rain - water in a dry spell if you can.
 Pumpkin, Watermelon want more room to sprawl than a veg bed can give - grow them in a dedicated plot, along your property
 edges, or in a food forest area instead. Turn on "Grow big vines in a veg bed anyway" if you'd rather use one of your beds for them.
 Hot-area sowing months for Groundnuts (peanuts), Maize (mielies) are not yet from a published local table - check those dates with
 your extension officer before relying on them.
 Dry beans (sugar beans), Swiss chard (spinach), Kale, Cabbage, Onions, Potato, Garlic, Peas, Broad beans (fava beans),
 Broccoli, True spinach (English spinach), Cauliflower were left out: none of their sowing windows has enough rain through the
 whole crop without irrigation.



CHOICES THIS PLAN MADE
Butternut gets Plot 1 to itself - a staple plot is exactly the dedicated sprawling room it wants.

Sweet potato (sow Sep). No earlier crop is recorded on these plots, so rotation could not steer this first choice - record what grew
there before, if you know it.



GROUND WITH NO NEW SOWING

(Apr-Sep), Plot 2 (Mar, May-Sep), Plot 3 (Mar, May-Oct). Well-documented crops have a sowing window for those stretches, but the
months around them are already fully planted, or crop rotation rules out the families that would fit. Clearing a nearby month, or letting
the ground rest, are both fine choices.
3 growing areas have a stretch with no new sowing: Bed 5 (May), Plot 1 (Mar-Aug), Plot 4 (Apr-Aug). No crop the catalog can plan
properly - one with a checked growing time, spacing and yield - has a sowing window that reaches those stretches. Ask locally what
else does; this plan is not proof that nothing can grow then.

individual-tree yields or default production for a different density, cultivar, climate or management. Intermediate ages are
