# isiXhosa glossary — Permaculture Manual

> **Machine-drafted glossary for fluent-speaker review.** Every isiXhosa term here was drafted by
> a machine and has not yet been checked by a fluent isiXhosa speaker with farming knowledge. Do
> not treat it as final. Terms marked **(check)** are the least certain and should be reviewed
> first.

## How to use this glossary

- Use the isiXhosa term in this table every time the English term appears in an `xh` chapter, so
  all chapters agree with each other and with the app.
- Register: plain, rural, spoken isiXhosa, the way an extension officer would say it in the
  village. Short phrases are better than coined "academic" words. Standard isiXhosa spelling
  (*iinkukhu*, *imisele*, *ukhuseleko*), not isiZulu spelling: isiXhosa has *-fumana* where isiZulu
  has *-thola*, *ifoto* where isiZulu has *isithombe*, *iimbotyi* where isiZulu has *ubhontshisi*,
  *umbona* where isiZulu has *ummbila*, and *umgquba* where isiZulu has *i-compost*.
- **Loan words** keep the English stem with a hyphenated isiXhosa prefix: *i-mulch*,
  *ii-swale*, *i-food forest*, *kwi-GPS*, *nge-WhatsApp*. A singular loan takes *i-* (plural *ii-*),
  as the app already writes them (*ii-sector*, *i-berm*, *i-permaculture*). Concords follow the
  isiXhosa noun class of the prefix (class 5 *i-*, class 10 *ii-*): *i-mulch igubungela umhlaba*,
  *ii-swale zibamba amanzi*.
- **Explain on first use**: where the notes say so, the first time the term appears in a chapter
  give the isiXhosa phrase and the English in brackets, e.g. *umsele ogcina amanzi emvula (swale)*.
  After that the short form is enough.
- **The 66 technical words** in the "Word" column of `content/manual/en/12-glossary.md` stay in
  **English** in every language of the manual (Rory's decision, 26 Sep 2026). See the section
  "Kept in English" near the end: it overrides the isiXhosa terms in the numbered tables for those
  words.
- Keep Latin plant names in italics, unchanged. Numbers and units stay as in English.
- If a chapter needs a term that is not listed, add it here and mark it **(check)**.

**"In app" sources** (the term is already used in the app, so the manual matches it):

  manual writes the loan **i-zone** / **ii-zone** because *zone* is one of the 66 kept-in-English words.
- **compost**: *umgquba* in xh.ts, course and the UI brief. *Ubulongwe* is used for manure only.
- **soil / land**: *umhlaba* covers soil, land and Earth. xh.ts and course use it for all three. The
  manual relies on the sentence, and adds a gloss for "the Earth" in the first ethic.
- **earthworm / parasite**: course writes *iintshulube* for both soil worms and goat worms. The manual
  keeps *iintshulube* for earthworms and uses *iinqolowa* for parasites. (check)
- **spring (season)**: *entwasahlobo* (course) rather than *entlakohlaza*.
- **months**: xh.ts uses the traditional names *eyoMdumba*, *eyoKwindla*, *uCanzibe*, *eyeThupha*,
  *eyoMsintsi*, *eyoMnga*. See "Second edition" for the full list used in the chapters.
- **bed**: *ibhedi* alone can mean a sleeping bed; the manual writes *ibhedi yegadi* where it could be
  misread.

## Kept in English (isiXhosa form)

Rory's decision (26 Sep 2026): the 66 technical words in the "Word" column of
`content/manual/en/12-glossary.md` stay in **English** in every language of the manual. This
section overrides the isiXhosa terms in the numbered tables above for these words.

How to write them:

- The English root never changes. The isiXhosa prefix is joined with a hyphen: *i-swale* /
  *ii-swale*, *i-mulch*, *i-greywater*, *ii-predator*. Other forms follow the same rule:
  *nge-mulch*, *kwi-worm farm*, *ye-compost*, *zi-annual*, *yi-nitrogen fixer*, *e-topsoil*.
- Singular loan nouns take *i-* (class 5), plurals *ii-* (class 10). Verbs, relatives and object
  markers in the same sentence take the matching concords (*i-greywater isetyenziswa*, *ii-swale
  zibamba amanzi*).
- First use in a chapter: bold, prefix included, then a short isiXhosa explanation in brackets, e.g.
  `**i-biomass** (izinto zezityalo ezikhulayo, ezinjengamagqabi namasebe)`. Where the sentence itself
  defines the word (as the English does), the definition does the job. Later uses: plain.
- Headings, list items, callouts and tables are unchanged in number; only words change.
- *Indigenous* is the one exception, as in isiZulu: kept as *-omdabu* (*izityalo zomdabu*).
- Permaculture is not re-explained after chapters 0 and 1.
