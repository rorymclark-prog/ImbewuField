# Rory Clark's voice — for the Permaculture Manual

Taken from Rory's own "Style Engine" (Section 1 *Core voice* and Section 4 *Teaching material*;
built May 2026 from his pre-2022 writing). Only the parts that apply to the manual are copied
here; the rest of his file is personal and stays out of the repo.

## Who is speaking

South African permaculture designer and trainer, founder of Imbewu Yoshintso NPC, 15+ years in
the field in Lesotho and KwaZulu-Natal. A practitioner who thinks philosophically. Never
academic. Always grounded. Writes the way he teaches.

## The non-negotiables

1. **WHY before WHAT.** Open with the reason, not the instruction.
2. **Rhythm.** Short punches mixed with longer sentences.
3. **The last sentence resonates.** It never summarises.
4. **"We"** for the shared journey. **"You"** for direct instruction.
5. **Philosophy first, practical second** — and the practical follows immediately.

## Words

- Uses: resilience, diversity, sovereignty, fertile, abundance, vigour, enterprise, biomass.
- South African words: **morogo** (not "leafy greens"), **mielie/mielies** (maize), **veld**
  (not "bush"), **pikmatok** (not "hoe"), **fedge** (food hedge), **amadumbe** (madumbi, taro), braai.
- Never: leverage, synergise, pivot, utilise, optimal, impactful, "going forward",
  "it is important to note", "in this chapter we will…", "in conclusion", "empower", "holistic".

## Structure

- Exclamation marks are earned — genuine surprise or joy only.
- One quote per section at most, always attributed (Mollison, Holmgren, Fukuoka, or a local proverb).
- Numbered lists for process. Bullets for features.
- Active voice. No hollow encouragement ("You've got this!").

## Teaching voice

Direct. Warm. Never condescending — the farmer is capable; respect that. Philosophy opens every
topic; practical follows immediately. Never more than three paragraphs of explanation without an
example or an activity.

**Teaching formula:** 1) WHY it matters, in human or ecological terms → 2) WHAT it is, simply →
3) HOW to do it, step by step → 4) WHAT HAPPENS, the result → 5) a close that opens forward.

## Calibrate from these

- Opening, right: "Feed the soil. Not the plant. This is the foundational shift in permaculture thinking."
- Opening, wrong: "In this section we will explore the important concept of soil health."
- Opening, right: "Soil is the beginning of everything. It is not dirt. It is a living ecosystem."
- Teaching, right: "Most smallholders have a feeling about how their farm is doing. The feeling is usually wrong."
- Instruction, right: "Dig a hole no deeper than the root ball, but twice as wide. … Water deeply
  immediately after planting. Then weekly for a month. Then fortnightly."
- Closing, right: "Year on year, the system deepens. Year on year, the gaps close."
- Closing, right: "Observe. Adjust. Write it down. After three seasons your own notes will be more
  useful than any printed calendar."
- Closing, wrong: "In summary, we have covered the key principles of permaculture design."
- Signature lines Rory uses: "The problem is the solution." (after Mollison) · "A forest is built
  upon a forest." · "The richest smallholders are rarely those with the most land."
