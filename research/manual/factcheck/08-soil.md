## Could not verify / open questions

- **NEMBA status of the green-manure and mulch-bank species.** I could not open the 2020 NEMBA lists (dffe.gov.za and laws.ewt.org blocked). To my knowledge sunn hemp (*Crotalaria juncea*), cowpea, lablab, velvet bean (*Mucuna pruriens*), buckwheat, oats, vetch, *Lupinus angustifolius*, fodder radish and pigeon pea (*Cajanus cajan*) are not listed, but a reviewer should confirm against the 2020 lists. Note: at least one ornamental *Crotalaria* (*C. agatiflora*) is listed (https://www.sanbi.org/resources/infobases/invasive-alien-plant-alert/crotalaria-agatiflora/), so only sunn hemp is named.
- **Comfrey** (*Symphytum*) was deliberately not recommended: its NEMBA status could not be confirmed.
- **Napier grass** (*Pennisetum purpureum*) was not recommended: a search summary suggested it is NEMBA-listed (Category 2), but this could not be confirmed.
- **Dynamic accumulators.** The statement that there is little scientific evidence for special mineral accumulation is from general knowledge of the literature; the search budget ran out before I could cite a review.
- **Heap minimum size (1 m³), cold-heap time (6–12 months), hot-heap time (2–3 months) and "2–3 volumes browns to 1 green"** are standard extension rules of thumb, but I could not fetch a specific extension page to cite each one.
- **Red wiggler upper temperature.** Sources differ (some give 30 °C as the stress point, others 35 °C as lethal; a southern African study summary suggests *E. fetida* tolerates wider ranges). The text uses "about 30 to 35 °C".
- **Starting worm quantity** (250–500 g, "several hundred to about a thousand") is common practice rather than a sourced figure.
- **pH(KCl) vs pH(water) offset** is given as "about 0.5 to 1 unit"; the cited Canadian study gives an average of 0.7 units, and it varies by soil.
- **Summer/winter sowing months** for green manures are general guidance for the summer-rainfall interior and the Western Cape; local frost dates should override them.
