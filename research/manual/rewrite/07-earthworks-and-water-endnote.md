# 07-earthworks-and-water — legal, permit and "check with" points removed

One line each; the coordinator merges these into the single end-of-book paragraph.

- South Africa's National Water Act (Act 36 of 1998), Schedule 1, lets you store and use runoff from your own roof without a licence, and allows reasonable domestic use, small non-commercial gardening and watering of animals (not feedlots).
- Storing water in a dam or pond is a water use (s21(b)); the 2016 General Authorisation for taking and storing water covers small storage, and more than 10 000 m³ on one property must be registered with the Department of Water and Sanitation (DWS). Conditions differ between catchments.
- Any work in a watercourse (river, stream, spring, wetland or drainage line, even one that flows only after rain), such as a dam wall, sand dam or diversion, is a water use under s21(c) and (i) and needs a General Authorisation (low-risk work, after a risk assessment) or a water use licence before work starts.
- Digging out or filling in more than 10 m³ of soil, sand or rock in a watercourse can also need environmental authorisation under NEMA (EIA Listing Notice 1, activity 19).
- A dam with a wall higher than 5 m that holds more than 50 000 m³ is a "dam with a safety risk" and must be registered with the DWS Dam Safety Office; for some dams a professional designer is a legal requirement.
- Get experienced advice before large machine earthworks (dams, long machine-dug swales).
- Before stocking a pond with fish, find out which species you may keep; some alien fish are NEMBA-listed invasive species that need a permit, and pond fish must never be released into rivers.
- Municipal by-laws may control greywater use in towns.
- Treat roof water for drinking as your clinic or local health officer advises (the chapter now says only "boil it").
- In Lesotho, Eswatini and Zimbabwe the water rules differ; ask the national water authority (this includes the Bethel sand-dam example in Lesotho).
- Before digging, find out where water pipes, electric cables and septic lines run (removed with the trench-safety box).
- Amadumbe (taro) is not a NEMBA-listed invader but is documented spreading into SA wetlands (the chapter keeps only "grow it away from streams and wetlands").
- Wattles (*Acacia mearnsii* and others) are NEMBA-listed invaders (the chapter keeps only "use dry stems without seed pods").
