# Endnote items — 10-natural-pest-control

- Report a new or unusual pest outbreak (for example fall armyworm or Oriental fruit fly) to the provincial Department of Agriculture or an extension officer; early reports help everyone.
- Report very large red-billed quelea flocks to the provincial Department of Agriculture; bird control with poison is a matter for the authorities, not the farmer.
- Check the local rules before trapping or killing any wild animal, including porcupines and mole-rats.
- Several golden mole species are threatened and protected; they must not be killed.
- Use only pest-control products registered in South Africa for the crop and pest (for example Bt products for fall armyworm), and follow the label.
- Mosquito fern (*Azolla filiculoides*) is a listed invasive plant under NEMBA; do not introduce it to dams or ponds.
- Before planting push-pull companion plants (for example *Desmodium* or *Brachiaria* grass), check that they are allowed in your area under the NEMBA Alien and Invasive Species Lists.
- *Coreopsis lanceolata* is a NEMBA Category 1a invader, so the older handbook's "calliopsis" was dropped; goldenrod, tansy and sweet Annie were dropped because their status could not be confirmed.
- Syringa (*Melia azedarach*) is a NEMBA-listed invader.
- Nicotine is no longer approved as a pesticide in many countries (banned in the EU since 2010).
- Slug pellets containing metaldehyde are restricted or banned for outdoor use in some countries (for example the UK).
- Ask an extension officer or mentor to identify a pest you do not know (a clear photo helps), and for help making fruit-fly bait traps.
- If anyone swallows a garden spray, go to a clinic at once and take the bottle.
