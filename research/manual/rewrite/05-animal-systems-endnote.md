# Endnote points removed from 05-animal-systems

- South African law forbids feeding pigs swill (food waste from kitchens, hotels, restaurants and the like) unless it has been boiled for at least one hour; this is part of African swine fever control.
- Newcastle disease is a controlled disease under the Animal Diseases Act (Act 35 of 1984): report sudden sickness or deaths in a flock to the nearest state veterinarian.
- Newcastle vaccination schedules: follow the advice of the local animal health technician or state veterinarian as well as the vaccine label.
- The mallard (ancestor of most domestic duck breeds) is a listed invasive species (NEMBA Category 2); ask the provincial conservation authority whether a permit is needed for the ducks you keep.
- Every beekeeper, even with a single hobby hive, must register with the national Department of Agriculture under the Agricultural Pests Act (Act 36 of 1983) and its control measures for honeybees; registration is free and must be renewed when it expires.
- Bees may not be moved between the Cape bee region and the rest of South Africa, and Cape bees may not be kept outside their region, without written permission from the Department of Agriculture.
- Many municipalities have bylaws on keeping bees in towns.
- American foulbrood must be reported to the Department of Agriculture; infected hives usually have to be destroyed by burning.
- If a colony is starving in a drought, ask an experienced beekeeper for advice.
- Anyone who has had a bad reaction to a bee sting should ask a doctor about carrying emergency medicine.
