# Endnote points removed — 11-home-and-appropriate-technology

Legal / authority points:
- Any solar installation bigger than a plug-in lighting kit must be installed by a qualified electrician, who issues a Certificate of Compliance.
- A solar system connected to the municipal or Eskom grid must be approved by and registered with the municipality or Eskom before it is switched on.
- Solar geysers should be installed by a qualified plumber following the SA installation standard for solar water heaters (SANS 10106).

Safety warnings removed from the chapter (essential lines kept in the text are listed in the report):
- Batteries store a lot of energy: keep them dry, ventilated and away from children; lead-acid batteries give off a flammable gas while charging (no smoking or sparks); buy lithium batteries only with a built-in battery management system.
- Solar geyser water can scald: fit a mixing (tempering) valve.
- Parabolic solar cookers focus sunlight to a very hot point: never look into the reflector, wear sunglasses, keep children, dry grass and paper away, use oven gloves.
- Keep food in a heat-retention bag above 60 °C (germs multiply fast between about 5 and 60 °C).
- Rocket stoves: stand on a firm, level base away from children, grass and anything that burns; never use petrol to light a fire.
- Paraffin: keep it in a clearly labelled container with a child-proof cap, never in a cooldrink or milk bottle; swallowing paraffin is the most common cause of accidental poisoning of young children in South Africa.
- Methane explodes at 5 to 15% in air; methane rises and collects under roofs.
- Slurry: wear gloves and wash hands after handling it.
- Aflatoxin: do not feed mouldy grain to animals either.
