# Field stories for the manual — questions for Rory

The second edition is written in your voice, but it has no invented stories. These are the places where one real story from Lesotho or KZN would bring a section alive. Answer any you like, in a sentence or a voice note: what happened, where (roughly), what you learnt. Anything you don't answer simply stays out.

## Chapter 0: Introduction

- Opening (WHY): ask Rory for one real moment from the RVCC project in Lesotho or from KZN that shows why he does this work (a garden that fed a family through a drought, a farmer who stopped buying fertiliser).

## Chapter 1: What is permaculture?

- Section "Why permaculture matters in Southern Africa": ask Rory for a real Lesotho or KZN example of a family or field losing soil (a gully that grew, a field abandoned) — or of one turned around.
- Section "The design principles of permaculture", principle 1 "Observe and interact": ask Rory for a time a rain walk or a season of watching changed a design (where a swale, tank or bed ended up going).
- Section "The design principles of permaculture", principle 9 "Use small and slow solutions": ask Rory for a farmer or project that started too big and scaled back — or started with one kitchen garden and grew.
- Section "The three ethics": ask Rory for a real case of "fair share" in a village (seed shared, surplus sold or given, a work party).

## Chapter 2: Planning your farm or homestead

- Section "Step 1: Site assessment": ask Rory for a time a family or group drew their base map together (the "planning together" photo), and what they saw on paper that they had missed on the ground.
- Section "Step 2: Zone planning": ask Rory for a homestead where moving the garden, tap or chickens closer to the house changed how well it was looked after.
- Section "Step 3: Slope planning": ask Rory for a Lesotho or KZN site where a tank or dam placed uphill fed a garden by gravity, or where greywater was sent down to trees.

## Chapter 3: Sector planning

- Section "Drawing a sector map": ask Rory for a time a sector map changed a design on a Lesotho or KZN project (e.g. a windbreak or garden moved once the wind or fire direction was mapped).
- Section "Wind": ask Rory for a harmful wind he has seen strip or dry out a project garden, and what the windbreak did afterwards.
- Section "Fire": ask Rory for a veld fire that reached (or was stopped short of) a homestead or project garden, and what held it: a firebreak, a green buffer, a watered garden.
- Section "Frost": ask Rory for a frost pocket or black-frost night in the Lesotho highlands or the KZN midlands, and what survived it (covers, sun traps, a horseshoe hedge).
- Section "Climate change": ask Rory for a drought year on a project and which practices (mulch, perennials, stored water, saved seed) carried households through.

## Chapter 4: Vegetable and staple crops

- Section "Growing food in Southern African climates": ask Rory for a time a frost pocket (or a wall that trapped cold air) killed a crop in Lesotho, and what was changed.
- Section "1. The kitchen garden (Zone 1)": ask Rory for a kitchen or keyhole garden from the RVCC project (no names) that fed a family through the year.
- Section "3. Small-scale commercial vegetable farming (Zone 3)": ask Rory for a market gardener he has worked with who found buyers first and sold direct.
- Section "4. The staple garden (Zone 3)": ask Rory for a mielie–bean–pumpkin polyculture he has seen outyield a mielie monoculture, or a field that was rested and came back.

## Chapter 5: Animal systems

- Section "Place animals where they work for you": ask Rory for a real case from Lesotho or KZN where an animal's habit was given a job (e.g. hens put onto a harvested bed, and what the bed looked like after).
- Section "Animal tractors": ask Rory for a chicken or pig tractor he built or saw on a project: what it was made of, how often it was moved, what went wrong at first.
- Section "Chickens": ask Rory for a Newcastle disease outbreak he saw in a village flock, and what changed once the households vaccinated.
- Section "Pigs": ask Rory for a time pigs cleared a patch of rough or weedy ground, and what was planted after.
- Section "Ducks": ask Rory for a time ducks (or Muscovies) cleared snails or slugs from a garden.
- Section "Bees": ask Rory for a hive-siting lesson from the field (e.g. a hive placed too near a path or a tethered animal, or one raised on tyres).

## Chapter 6: Tree systems

- Section "Food forests": ask Rory for a real food forest he planted or visited in KZN or Lesotho (for example the Durban food forest in the photo) and what it looked like before and after five years.
- Section "Food forests", Coppicing: ask Rory for a real case of a coppiced woodlot or wild-olive stand giving a household firewood.
- Section "Planting a food forest": ask Rory for a time pigeon pea or thorn-tree pioneers, chopped and dropped, visibly lifted a young fruit tree.
- Section "Windbreaks": ask Rory for a windbreak on a Lesotho or Highveld garden that saved crops in a windy spring (or a garden lost for lack of one).
- Section "Planting a tree": ask Rory for a planting day where trees in wide holes with deep weekly watering outlived trees planted the quick way.

## Chapter 7: Earthworks and water

- Section "Using greywater for irrigation": ask Rory for a real greywater system he set up (e.g. the drum and grease trap feeding a swale) and what grew on it.

## Chapter 8: Soil: feed the soil, and it will feed you

- Opening / "The layers of the soil": ask Rory for a time he dug a soil pit with farmers in Lesotho or KZN and what the profile showed them (e.g. a few centimetres of topsoil left on an eroded field).
- Section "Way 3: Mulch": ask Rory for a bed or field where mulch changed things through a dry spell (how the soil looked and felt under the mulch compared with bare ground next to it).
- Section "Way 4: Compost": ask Rory for a compost heap built at a training day — who built it, how hot it got, what the farmers did with the compost.
- Section "Way 5: Earthworms and worm farming": ask Rory for a worm farm he saw working in a village or school (the old-bath farm in the handbook photo, if it has a story).
- Section "Way 2: Animal manure": ask Rory for how farmers he worked with collected and used kraal manure.

## Chapter 9: Creating a balanced, productive ecology

- Section "Food chains and the ecological pyramid": ask Rory for a time a garden stopped spraying and the ladybirds or birds came back (or a time spraying made an outbreak worse).
- Section "Succession": ask Rory for a real Lesotho or KZN field where pioneers (weeds, cowpea, pigeon pea) healed bare soil, and how long it took.
- Section "Habitat": ask Rory about the tyre pond in the figure (09-wildlife-pond) — what moved in and how soon — and any owl box that was occupied.
- Section "Habitat": ask Rory for how a community he worked with talked about owls, if he has one to share.

## Chapter 10: Natural Pest Control

- Section "Why avoid chemical poisons": ask Rory for a garden or farmer (no names) that stopped spraying and saw the predators come back, and how long it took.
- Section "Common pests in Southern Africa" (Fall armyworm): ask Rory for what fall armyworm did to mielies in the Lesotho or KZN gardens after 2017, and what worked against it.
- Section "Common pests in Southern Africa" (Mole-rats and porcupines): ask Rory for a time wire baskets or a buried-mesh fence saved a crop from mole-rats or porcupines.
- Section "Encourage predators": ask Rory for a pond, rock pile or flower strip that brought frogs, lizards or wasps into a project garden.
- Section "Home-made pest sprays": ask Rory which home-made sprays he has seen work (or fail) in Lesotho or KZN gardens, and whether tobacco sprays were common.
