## A-D1-S0 · 09:00–09:30 · Opening circle (30 min)

**By the end, learners can:** name the facilitators, find their group, and show where they stand
on six AI questions (start quiz).

**Poster:** A-P01 · **Slides:** A-D1-01 → A-D1-02
**Materials:** register, POPIA consent forms + the phone and data note (day-0 §5), pens, green
stickers, name tags, masking tape, 3 A4 sheets marked **A**, **B**, **C** taped in three corners.

| Time | Step | Teacher talk? |
|---|---|---|
| 09:00 | Register at the door; consent read aloud and signed | 3 min |
| 09:12 | Greeting, call and response | 2 min |
| 09:17 | Start quiz — stand in a corner | 2 min |
| 09:27 | Today's promise | 1 min |

### Say / ask / do

1. **DO (co-facilitator, at the door):** each learner signs the register (AM column), gets a
   name tag, and — with the lead facilitator in groups of 5–6 as they arrive — hears the **POPIA
   consent** and the **phone and data note** read aloud in the local language. Tick the photo
   choice. Green sticker on the name tag for "No photos".
   Mark on the register (small **P** next to the name) who has a smartphone with them. This
   is for group-making, not for judging.
2. **SAY:** *"Sanibonani. Welcome. For two days we learn about AI on the phone. You don't need
   a phone to learn this. Those with phones will share."*
3. **DO:** agree the call and response (facilitation-toolkit §5). Suggest: you call
   *"Buza!"* (ask), they answer *"Hlola!"* (check) — or agree your own in the local language.
   It is the course in two words: ask, then check.
4. **DO: Start quiz.** Point to the corners A, B, C. Read each question from the README table
   and the three answers, pointing at each corner as you say it. Learners walk to a corner.
   Co-facilitator counts bodies and writes totals on the back of the session sheet.
   **SAY after each:** *"Thank you. No answers today — we find out together."* Don't correct.
5. **SAY (09:27):** the day promise (top of this file).

### If things go wrong
- **Many arrive late:** start on time with whoever is there. Late learners sign in at tea, and
  hear the consent then from the co-facilitator. They skip the quiz (note it).
- **Someone can't walk to corners:** they hold up 1, 2 or 3 fingers; a neighbour reports it.

### Language & inclusion notes
- Read consent slowly. Stop after "we will not sell them" and ask: *"Any question?"*
- A thumbprint or mark is a valid signature.

## A-D1-S1 · 09:30–10:30 · What AI is and isn't — The Guessing Machine (60 min)

**By the end, learners can:** explain AI as a fast guesser trained on lots of writing and
pictures; name three things AI is not; name three places they already meet AI on a phone.

**Poster:** A-P01, A-P02, A-P03 · **Slides:** A-D1-02 → A-D1-07 · **Clip:** A-ANI-01 (optional)
**Materials:** the cardboard **AI sign** on a string · flipchart + markers · group role cards
(6 sets) · **Where AI hides** picture cards (prompt-cards.md K-02, 1 set per group) · group
phones · 6 group envelopes.
**Room:** chairs in a horseshoe facing the poster wall, space in the middle for standing.

| Time | Step | Teacher talk? |
|---|---|---|
| 09:30 | Make groups of 5, roles | 3 min |
| 09:37 | Our class and phone agreement | 2 min |
| 09:45 | Role-play: The Guessing Machine | 5 min |
| 10:00 | AI is / AI isn't — stand or sit | 3 min |
| 10:10 | Where AI hides — hunt on the phone | 2 min |
| 10:22 | Language time, key messages, take home | 2 min |

### Say / ask / do

**Groups (09:30, 7 min)**
1. **DO:** form 6 groups of 4–5 (facilitation-toolkit §4). Mix young and old, men and women,
   readers and non-readers. **Every group needs at least one learner with a working smartphone**
   (use the P marks from the register). If a group has none, give it a spare kit phone.
2. **DO:** name groups after plants — Bean, Pumpkin, Maize, Banana, Marula, Spinach. Hand out
   role cards. **SAY:** *"The Materials person is also the **Phone Keeper** in this course: they
   look after the group phone, keep it charging at breaks, and make sure everyone gets a turn."*
3. **SAY:** *"If it's your own phone, you are in charge of it. Nobody touches your phone
   without asking. You can say no."*

**Class and phone agreement (09:37, 8 min) — A-P01**
4. **GROUPS:** *"Each group: agree one rule that helps us learn together. 2 minutes."*
   Reporters say it; Recorder of the Bean group writes (or draws) on the flipchart beside A-P01.
5. **DO:** point to the four phone rules already on A-P01 and read them:
   ① *Phones silent, not away.* ② *Share the screen, share the turn.* ③ *No photos of people
   without asking.* ④ *Private stays private — no ID, PIN or bank details typed today.*
   **ASK:** *"Can we all live with these?"* Thumbs up. Add anything the groups said.

**The Guessing Machine role-play (09:45, 15 min) — A-P02**
6. **SAY (as yourself):** *"Before we talk about AI, let's play. Finish my sentence — shout it!"*
   Say three sentence starts, one at a time. Co-facilitator tallies the answers on flipchart:
   - *"In the morning I drink…"* *(tea, coffee, water, amahewu…)*
   - *"Pap and…"* *(meat, chakalaka, amasi, cabbage…)*
   - *"When it rains, I…"* *(plant, stay inside, collect water…)*
7. **DO:** circle the **most common** answer for each. **SAY:** *"Now I have listened to all of
   you. I have learned your patterns."*
8. **DO:** put on the **AI sign**. Change your voice — flat, fast, very sure. **SAY (as the
   machine):** *"I am the Guessing Machine. I have heard everybody. Ask me to finish any
   sentence."* Invite 2 learners to say *"In the morning I drink…"* — machine answers the most
   common word, every time, even when the learner drinks something else.
   **ASK the room:** *"Did the machine know what* she *drinks?"* *(No — it said what most people say.)*
9. **DO (still the machine):** a learner reads (or you prompt): *"My goat is coughing. I should
   give it…"* Machine answers at once, very sure: *"Give it 10 ml of medicine from the shop,
   three times a day. It will be fine."*
   **ASK the room:** *"Is that safe? What did the machine not know?"* *(Which goat, how old,
   how sick, which medicine, the dose, whether it's a disease that spreads — it didn't ask
   anything.)*
10. **DO (still the machine):** *"When do I plant maize?"* Machine: *"Plant in April or May."*
    **ASK:** *"Is that right here?"* *(No — here we plant maize in spring and early summer,
    about October to December; April–May is spring in the north of the world. The machine
    learned mostly from writing from other countries.)*
11. **DO:** take off the sign, step to one side. **SAY:** *"Now I'm me again. That was a
    guessing machine. Real AI works a bit like that — but it learned from millions of books,
    web pages and pictures, so its guesses are very good. Very good is not always right."*
12. **DO:** point to **A-P02** callouts: ① *It learned from lots of words and pictures* ② *It
    guesses what comes next* ③ *It sounds sure — even when wrong* ④ *It knows only what you
    tell or show it.*

**AI is / AI isn't (10:00, 10 min)**
13. **SAY:** *"Stand up if you think it's true. Stay sitting if you think it's false."*
    Read one at a time; after each, ask one standing and one sitting person *"why?"*, then give
    the answer in one line.

| # | Statement | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | AI is a person inside the phone. | **False.** It is a computer program. Nobody is "in there". |
| 2 | AI learned from a huge amount of writing and pictures. | **True.** |
| 3 | AI is always right. | **False.** It makes mistakes, and sometimes makes things up. |
| 4 | AI can sound very sure and still be wrong. | **True.** That is why we check. |
| 5 | AI knows my garden. | **False.** Only what you tell or show it. |
| 6 | AI can help me write a message or a list. | **True.** You check it and it's yours. |
| 7 | AI understands isiZulu as well as English. | **False, often.** It is usually weaker in our languages. Try both. |
| 8 | What I type into an AI app may be kept by the company. | **True.** Don't type private things. |

**Where AI hides (10:10, 12 min) — A-P03**
14. **SAY:** *"You've probably used AI already this week. Let's find it."*
15. **GROUPS (10 min):** groups with a phone: *"Find three places AI is on your phone. Show
    each other."* Every group also gets the **Where AI hides** picture cards: put each card on
    one of two piles — *"We have used this"* / *"We have not used this."*
    Places to look (co-facilitator walks round and helps):
    - The **keyboard** guessing your next word as you type ("Good m…" → *morning*) — the
      Guessing Machine, live, on your own phone. Everyone should try this.
    - The **microphone** on the keyboard (voice typing).
    - **WhatsApp**: some phones show **Meta AI** (a chat with an AI). Not every phone has it.
    - The **camera** or Google app: search with a photo (**Google Lens**).
    - **Translate** buttons.
    - Videos chosen for you on Facebook, TikTok, YouTube — AI picks what you see next.
    - "Suspected spam" warnings on calls and messages.
    - **ImbewuField** (app ON): **Lima** — the help strip at the bottom of the app — plus
      **Lima Vision**, **Tree ID**, the till slip reader in **Finances**, and **Reports**.
16. **DO:** Reporters each name one place their group found. Tick it on A-P03's flipchart.
    **ASK:** *"Which one surprised you?"*

**Language time and close (10:22, 8 min)**
17. **GROUPS (3 min):** *"In your own language, tell each other: what is AI? Use your own words."*
    Listen in. Good answers sound like *"a machine that guesses from what it has read"*.
18. **SAY key messages** (below). **Take home:** *"Tonight, type a message on your phone and
    watch the keyboard guess your next word. Show someone at home. That's AI."*

### Debrief (the three questions)
1. *What did the Guessing Machine do?* *(Gave the most common answer, very sure, without asking.)*
2. *Why is that a problem for a farmer?* *(Your farm isn't "most common". Wrong advice can cost
   a crop, an animal, money.)*
3. *What will you do differently when you use AI?* *(Tell it about my farm; check the answer.)*

### Key messages
1. **AI is a very fast guesser** that learned from huge amounts of writing and pictures.
2. **It is not a person and it is not always right** — it can sound sure and be wrong.
3. **It only knows your farm if you tell it or show it.**
4. You already use AI: the keyboard, the camera, WhatsApp, videos.

### If things go wrong
- **Learners think the facilitator really is the AI / is joking at them:** step out of role
  clearly (sign off, stand in a different place). Name it: *"That was a game."*
- **"AI is the devil / will take our jobs":** don't argue. Put it on the Question Tree and say
  *"Good question — tomorrow afternoon we talk about what AI can't do, and about fairness."*
- **A learner is an AI enthusiast and takes over:** give them a job — helper for a group
  without phone experience. Ask them to show, not tell.
- **No group has a phone:** everything in this session works with the picture cards.
- ✂ **Cut if short:** statements 6 and 8 in the stand/sit game (they come back on Day 2).

### Language & inclusion notes
- Use the English word **"AI"** (say "ey-ai") alongside a local phrase — agree it in the
  room. For example in isiZulu a descriptive phrase like *"umshini oqagelayo"* (the guessing
  machine) works as a teaching name; check with the first-language facilitator and the room.
- Sentence starts must be in the local language — the game works in any language.
- Elders with no phone: pair them with a youth who *shows* the keyboard guessing on their phone.

## A-D1-S2 · 10:50–12:30 · Phone ready + the Question Hand (100 min)

**By the end, learners can:** switch on three data-saving settings; ask a clear question using
the Question Hand; ask again to get a shorter, better or local-language answer.

**Poster:** A-P04, A-P05 · **Slides:** A-D1-08 → A-D1-14
**Materials (per group of 5):** group phone(s) + power bank · hotspot password card · 1 set of
farm prompt cards P-01…P-20 (prompt-cards.md) — each group gets 4 · 1 set of AI answer cards
B-01…B-20 (facilitator-as-AI only, kept by the facilitator) · 1 flipchart sheet + 2 markers ·
5 **Question Hand** take-home cards (K-01).
**Room:** group stations; facilitator's "AI desk" (a chair with the AI sign) at the side.

| Time | Step | Teacher talk? |
|---|---|---|
| 10:50 | Hook: what does data cost you? | 2 min |
| 10:55 | Phone ready: save your data | 5 min |
| 11:15 | The Question Hand | 8 min |
| 11:30 | Practice round 1: ask a farm question [APP] / [PAPER] | 3 min |
| 12:00 | Practice round 2: ask again | 3 min |
| 12:15 | Debrief | 3 min |
| 12:25 | Key messages, take home | 1 min |

### Say / ask / do

**Hook (10:50, 5 min)**
1. **ASK:** *"Hands up if you ran out of data last month."* *"What did you spend on data or
   airtime last week? Tell your neighbour."* *(Answers vary; R20–R100 a week is common — don't
   judge.)* **SAY:** *"AI can eat data — especially photos and videos. Before we use it, we make
   the phone careful with your money."*

**Phone ready (10:55, 20 min) — A-P04**
2. **DO:** hotspot on. Phone Keepers connect the group phone to the course Wi-Fi (password card).
   **SAY:** *"On Wi-Fi you're not using your own data. At home, these settings save your money."*
3. **GROUPS (15 min):** with the poster steps, each learner who has a phone does the taps
   **themselves**; the rest of the group watches one screen and says the next step aloud.
   Facilitators walk round — **talk people through; don't take their phones.**
   ① **WhatsApp → Settings → Storage and data → Media auto-download:** "When using mobile data"
   → untick everything. *(Photos and videos wait until you tap them.)*
   ② **Photo upload quality:** Standard (smaller photos, less data).
   ③ **Android Settings → Data saver** on (the name differs by phone — search "data saver" in
   Settings).
   ④ **Play Store → auto-update apps over Wi-Fi only.**
   ⑤ Habits: close videos you're not watching; a voice note uses less data than a video;
   one clear question uses less than ten.
4. **SAY:** *"Menus change and every phone is a bit different. If you can't find it, use the
   search in Settings, or come to the Help Desk at lunch."*

**The Question Hand (11:15, 15 min) — A-P05**
5. **DO:** go to the AI desk, put on the AI sign. A learner (or the co-facilitator) asks the
   machine just one word: *"Spinach?"* Machine answers from card **B-00** (prompt-cards.md): a
   long, general answer about spinach being healthy, grown worldwide, rich in iron, and a recipe.
   **ASK the room:** *"Did that help a farmer with a problem?"* *(No — it didn't know what we wanted.)*
6. **DO:** sign off. Hold up your hand. **SAY:** *"A good question has five fingers."* Count them
   off, and point to A-P05:
   - **Thumb — WHO I am:** *"I am a small farmer near Bergville. I grow for my family."*
   - **Pointer — WHAT I have:** *"I have one bed of spinach, 3 m by 1 m. The leaves have
     small holes. No money for sprays."*
   - **Middle — WHAT I want:** *"I want to stop the holes getting worse."*
   - **Ring — HOW to answer:** *"Give me 3 short steps. Simple words. In isiZulu."*
   - **Little finger — ASK AGAIN:** *"Make it shorter." "Why?" "What else do you need to
     know?"*
   Point at the finger each time; on the poster the hand is drawn with a numbered callout per finger.
7. **DO:** put the AI sign back on. The learner asks the full five-finger question. Machine
   answers from card **B-01** (short, useful, 3 steps). **ASK:** *"Which answer helps more?
   Why?"* *(The second — it knew who, what, and how to answer.)*
8. **GROUPS (3 min):** everyone holds up a hand and says the five fingers to a neighbour in
   their own language.

**Practice round 1 (11:30, 30 min)**

Each group takes **4 farm prompt cards** (P-cards). Each card has a weak question on the front
("Tomatoes dying?") and a picture of the situation.

#### [APP] Ask Lima (30 min)
1. **GROUPS (5 min):** for card 1, the Recorder writes (or the group says aloud) the
   five-finger question. Leader checks all five fingers are there.
2. **DO:** Phone Keeper opens ImbewuField. **SAY:** *"At the bottom of the app is the help
   strip — that's Lima, the app's AI helper. Tap the question button to ask."* Show on your own
   phone first (1 minute).
3. **GROUPS (20 min):** one learner types or speaks the question into Lima; a *different*
   learner does each card, so four people have a turn. After each answer the Reporter reads
   it aloud to the group in the local language. Recorder writes the **3 main points** on the
   flipchart, and one thing the group is **not sure about** (with a "?").
4. **SAY at 11:55:** *"5 minutes. Finish your card."*
5. **Groups with no working app:** go to the [PAPER] path below — same questions.

#### [PAPER] Ask a chat AI or the facilitator-as-AI (30 min)
1. **GROUPS (5 min):** same as [APP] step 1 — write or say the five-finger question.
2. **With a phone:** use an AI chat the learners already have — **Meta AI in WhatsApp** if it
   shows on that phone, or another AI chat app a learner already uses. Don't install new apps
   today (data, storage, privacy).
3. **Without a phone (or no AI on the phone):** the group's Reporter walks to the **AI desk**
   and asks the co-facilitator wearing the AI sign. The "AI" reads the matching **B-card**
   aloud, fast and sure. **If the question has fewer than five fingers, the AI answers from
   B-00-style — long and general** (make it up in the same style). Groups learn fast.
4. **GROUPS (20 min):** as in [APP] step 3: take turns, Reporter reads aloud, Recorder writes
   3 main points and one "?" point.

**Practice round 2 — ask again (12:00, 15 min)**
9. **SAY:** *"The little finger: ask again. The first answer is a first draft."* Each group
   picks one of its answers and asks again, three ways (Recorder ticks each):
   - *"Make it shorter — 3 steps only."*
   - *"Say it in isiZulu"* (or the group's language). **Reporter checks: is the language good?
     Does it sound right?** Rate it: good / OK / strange.
   - *"What else do you need to know about my farm to answer better?"*
   No-phone groups: the AI desk answers ask-again questions (co-facilitator improvises: shorter;
   in the local language with a few odd words to spot; asks two questions back — e.g. *"How big
   is your bed? What month is it?"*).

### Debrief (12:15, 10 min)
1. *What happened when you used all five fingers?* *(Shorter, more useful answers.)*
2. *What changed when you asked again? What about in isiZulu?* *(Shorter; the language was
   OK / strange / mixed English.)*
3. *What will you ask at home?* Reporters say one question each.
Write the "?" points on the Question Tree — **we check them tomorrow**.

### Key messages
1. **Save your data first:** auto-download off, standard photos, data saver on.
2. **Ask with the whole hand:** who I am · what I have · what I want · how to answer · ask again.
3. **The first answer is a draft.** Ask again — shorter, clearer, in your language.
4. Write down what you're not sure about — tomorrow we learn to check.

**Take home (12:28):** each learner gets a **Question Hand card** (K-01).

### If things go wrong
- **Hotspot overloaded:** only one phone per group online; others switch Wi-Fi off and do the
  question-writing on paper.
- **AI answers in a very long block of text:** perfect teaching moment — the ring finger
  ("give me 3 short steps") fixes it. Show it.
- **A tool refuses or says "I can't help":** say so honestly; try another tool or the AI desk.
- **Someone types private information:** use the agreed stop signal; quietly help them delete
  the message. Remind the room of agreement rule ④ without naming anyone.
- **A group finishes early:** they swap cards with another group and compare answers to the
  same question — different tools, different answers. (That's tomorrow's lesson; good.)
- ✂ **Cut if short:** Play Store setting (④) — do it at the Help Desk; drop round 2's third
  ask-again. Never cut the practice rounds.

### Language & inclusion notes
- Learners can **speak** their question (keyboard mic) instead of typing — let them; voice is
  taught properly in S3.
- Non-readers: the Reporter reads each answer aloud; the non-reader decides if it answers the
  question. They often spot the vague answers fastest.
- Code-switching (English and isiZulu in one question) is fine. AI tools often cope with it.
- The Question Hand works in any language: teach finger names in the local language too.

**Teacher talk:** 25 of 100 min (25%).

## A-D1-S3 · 13:15–14:30 · Ask with your voice and a photo (75 min)

**By the end, learners can:** use voice typing and check what the phone wrote; take a clear
photo of a plant; use a photo to ask AI and **check** the answer with a scale or a person.

**Poster:** A-P06 · **Slides:** A-D1-15 → A-D1-19 · **Clip:** A-ANI-02 (good photo / bad photo)
**Materials (per group of 5):** group phone + power bank · a leaf or plant (bring one from the
plant spot) · 1 A4 **Photo check sheet** (prompt-cards.md K-03) · pencil.
**Stations (shared):** (1) **Plant station** — 3–5 plants or a bed; (2) **Weigh station** —
kitchen scale + a bag of produce (1–3 kg), a 2 ℓ bottle of water for size; (3) **Tree station**
— one tree within 50 m. **No-phone kit:** printed plant photo cards (K-04) + B-cards.

| Time | Step | Teacher talk? |
|---|---|---|
| 13:15 | Energiser: Broken Telephone | 1 min |
| 13:20 | Voice typing in pairs | 3 min |
| 13:35 | A good photo | 5 min |
| 13:42 | Photo stations [APP] / [PAPER] | 6 min |
| 14:22 | Debrief | 3 min |

### Say / ask / do

**Energiser — Broken Telephone (13:15, 5 min)**
1. **DO:** whisper a farm sentence into one ear in the circle: *"Plant the beans after the first
   good rain, two fingers deep."* It passes ear to ear round the circle. The last person says it
   aloud. **ASK:** *"What changed?"* **SAY:** *"Voice typing can mishear too. So we always read
   what the phone wrote."*

**Voice typing (13:20, 15 min)**
2. **DO (2 min):** on your phone, open WhatsApp (a chat to yourself or a notes app), tap the
   **microphone on the keyboard**, speak a question, show what it wrote.
   **SAY:** *"The mic on the keyboard turns your voice into writing. Some phones can do our
   languages, some can't yet. Let's test."*
3. **GROUPS in pairs (12 min):** each learner with a phone speaks one farm question:
   first in **English**, then in their **first language**. Partner reads what the phone wrote.
   Fix mistakes by hand. Recorder tallies: *English worked? Our language worked?*
   **Where voice typing is set to English only:** show how to add a language in the keyboard
   settings (Help Desk if it takes long). Don't promise every language is there.
   **No-phone pairs:** one says a question, the other writes it down word for word, then the
   speaker checks it — same skill: *what I said = what was written?*
4. **ASK (1 min):** *"Did the phone understand our language?"* Collect a quick show of hands.
   Note it on the flipchart — **we use this tomorrow afternoon** (fairness and language).

**A good photo (13:35, 7 min) — A-P06**
5. **DO:** show A-ANI-02 on a tablet, or hold up two printed photos: one dark and blurry, one
   clear. **ASK:** *"Which one can a person — or AI — understand?"*
6. **DO:** point to the five callouts on A-P06:
   ① **Light:** sun behind you or bright shade — not the sun in the lens.
   ② **Close:** fill the screen with the problem.
   ③ **Still and sharp:** tap the screen on the plant to focus; hold with two hands.
   ④ **One thing:** one plant, one leaf, one problem.
   ⑤ **Show the size:** a hand, a 2 ℓ bottle or a matchbox next to it.
   Plus: photograph **both sides** of a sick leaf, and the **whole plant** too.
7. **SAY:** *"And ask first before a person is in your photo."*

**Photo stations (13:42, 40 min)**
**DO:** 2 groups per station, **12 minutes** each, then rotate clockwise when you call
*"Buza!"* (4 minutes for moving between rotations). Each group fills the **Photo check sheet**
(K-03): *What did AI say? How sure was it? How did we check? Was it right?*

#### [APP] Lima Vision and Tree ID (40 min)
- **Plant station — Lima Vision, "What's growing":** Phone Keeper opens **Lima Vision**, chooses
  *What's growing*, photographs a bed. Lima Vision gives a crop name, a rough kg estimate, weeks
  to harvest and a **confidence** label (high / medium / low).
  **Check:** ask the group's best gardener — *is that the crop? Does the time to harvest match
  what you know?* Record: agree / not sure / disagree.
- **Weigh station — Lima Vision, "Weigh my harvest":** photograph the bag of produce with the
  2 ℓ bottle beside it for size. Lima Vision gives an estimated kg. **Then weigh it on the scale.**
  Write both numbers. **ASK the group:** *"How far off was it? Would you sell by the photo or
  by the scale?"* *(By the scale — the photo is a guess.)*
- **Tree station — Tree ID:** photograph the tree (whole tree, then leaves close up; take a trunk
  photo if Tree ID asks for one). Tree ID gives a name, a confidence number, and notes.
  **Check:** ask an elder in the group what the tree is called locally and what it is used for.
  Do they agree? If Tree ID says "protected" or "invasive", write it down with a "?" — it is a
  lead to **check with the extension officer or the local municipality/conservation office**, not a
  final answer.
- **Rule for all three:** *low confidence = treat it as a guess. High confidence = still check.*

#### [PAPER] Lens, chat photo, or photo cards (40 min)
- **Plant station:** photograph a leaf with **Google Lens** (in the Google app or camera, where
  the phone has it), or send the photo to an AI chat the learner already has (e.g. Meta AI in
  WhatsApp, where it shows) with a Question Hand question: *"I'm a small farmer in KZN. What is
  this plant and is anything wrong with it? 3 short points."*
  **Check:** the group's best gardener and the elders: agree / not sure / disagree.
- **Weigh station:** send the photo of the produce + 2 ℓ bottle to the AI chat and ask *"About
  how many kg is this?"*. If no AI photo tool: every learner **guesses** the weight, and the AI
  desk (co-facilitator) answers confidently from card **B-21**: *"About 5 kg."* Then weigh on the
  scale. Compare the AI guess, the group's guesses and the scale.
- **Tree station:** Lens or chat photo on the tree, then the elder's knowledge (same as [APP]).
- **No phone at all:** use the **printed plant photo cards** (K-04). The group asks the AI desk
  using the Question Hand; the AI desk answers from the matching B-card (some are wrong on
  purpose, see the answer key). Group checks against the elders and the Course 1 posters.

### Debrief (14:22, 8 min)
1. *What did the AI get right? What did it get wrong?* Each station's last group reports.
2. *How did you check?* *(Scale. Elders. Our own eyes. Asking the best gardener.)*
3. *When would you trust a photo answer alone?* *(Only for low-risk things — and still check.)*

### Key messages
1. **Speak your question — then read what the phone wrote.**
2. **A clear photo gets a better answer:** light, close, sharp, one thing, show the size.
3. **A photo answer is a guess.** Weigh it, look again, ask a person.
4. **Ask before a person is in your photo.**

### If things go wrong
- **No signal outside:** take photos outside, walk back to the Wi-Fi, ask there.
- **Tool shows only English results for the plant:** ask the elders for the local name;
  that's a strength, not a failure.
- **AI names a plant confidently and the elders disagree:** don't settle it by authority. Write
  both answers and a "?" — it's tomorrow's first checking example.
- **Scale not there:** use the 2 ℓ bottle of water (about 2 kg) as the thing to photograph and
  "weigh".
- **Bees, sun, heat at the tree:** keep the station in shade; water available.
- ✂ **Cut if short:** make the Tree station optional (2 stations × 2 rotations); keep the Weigh
  station — it's the clearest checking lesson of the day.

### Language & inclusion notes
- Elders are the **checking experts** at the Plant and Tree stations. Say so aloud.
- Learners with shaky hands: rest the phone on a bottle or a knee; use a timer photo.
- A learner who doesn't want to be in any photo: they are the photographer, never the subject.

**Teacher talk:** 18 of 75 min (24%).

## A-D1-S4 · 14:45–15:40 · My real farm questions (55 min)

**By the end, learners can:** ask their own real farm question with the Question Hand, record
the answer on an **AI answer card**, and mark what they need to check.

**Poster:** A-P05 · **Slides:** A-D1-20
**Materials (per learner):** 2 **My AI answer cards** (prompt-cards.md K-05) · pencil ·
3 coloured dots or crayons (green, orange, red). **Per group:** group phone. **Room:** a
"**To check tomorrow**" sheet of flipchart on the wall.

| Time | Step | Teacher talk? |
|---|---|---|
| 14:45 | My question, in pairs | 2 min |
| 14:50 | Build it with the Question Hand | 2 min |
| 15:00 | Ask it (phone or AI desk) | 2 min |
| 15:25 | How risky if it's wrong? Colour it | 3 min |
| 15:35 | Wrap | 1 min |

### Say / ask / do
1. **SAY:** *"You were asked to bring one real question from your own garden or farm. Tell your
   partner."* Learners without one: pick a P-card that matches their garden, or ask about
   something they want to grow next season.
2. **DO (14:50, 10 min):** hand out AI answer cards (K-05). Each learner fills (or draws, or
   tells the Recorder) the five fingers for their question. Walk round: check the thumb and
   pointer (who, what I have) — these are the fingers people forget.
3. **GROUPS (15:00, 25 min):** the group phone goes round; each learner asks their own question
   — by voice or typing — [APP] in **Lima**, [PAPER] in an AI chat they already use, or at the
   **AI desk** (co-facilitator answers in the machine voice; for questions with no B-card,
   answer plausibly and add one **deliberate small mistake** the learner can find tomorrow —
   note it privately on your list). Each learner writes (or the Recorder writes for them) the
   **3 main points** of the answer on their card.
   **SAY at 15:20:** *"5 minutes. Everybody must have asked."*
4. **DO (15:25, 10 min):** **SAY:** *"If this answer is wrong, how bad is it?"* Learners colour a
   dot on their card:
   - **Green** — not much harm (an idea, a recipe, a name)
   - **Orange** — could cost me a crop or money (planting time, spacing, a pest)
   - **Red** — could hurt a person, an animal, or my money for real (sprays, medicine, animal
     health, loans, grants)
   **SAY:** *"Don't worry if you're not sure of the colour — tomorrow we learn this properly."*
   Learners pin their cards on the **To check tomorrow** sheet (or keep them — their choice; some
   questions are personal).
5. **SAY (15:35):** *"Tomorrow morning we check these answers. Some will be good. Some will
   surprise you."*

### Debrief
Folded into the closing circle.

### Key messages
1. **Your farm, your question** — AI is most useful for the questions *you* really have.
2. **Before you act, ask: how bad is it if this is wrong?**

### If things go wrong
- **Learner's question is private (health, money, family):** they keep their card in their
  pocket and ask on their own phone, or with a facilitator privately at break. Never pin it.
- **Group phone battery dies:** swap to the power bank; others use the AI desk meanwhile.
- **A learner asks a medical or animal-health emergency question:** stop the AI part. Help them
  contact the clinic or vet today (Who to Ask sheet). Say: *"This is a red one — a person, now."*
- ✂ **Cut if short:** colouring moves to tomorrow's S1 (it's done there anyway).

### Language & inclusion notes
- Many learners will ask in their first language. Let them. Compare with the English answer if
  time allows.
- Non-writers draw their question (a plant with holes, a goat, a tank) — the Recorder writes a
  line under it.

**Teacher talk:** 10 of 55 min (18%).
