# Day 0 · Preparation (both facilitators, 1 day, before Day 1)

Day 0 is **never** the same day as Day 1. For this course most of the preparation is **testing
phones and tools**, because AI features change often and a button that worked last month may
have moved. Nothing on Day 1 should be tried for the first time in front of learners.

## 1. Before Day 0 (the week before)

- [ ] **Invite message** sent to learners (by the programme office or mentor), in the local
      language. Use this text:

> Hello. The AI on your phone course is on **[dates]** at **[venue]**, 09:00–16:00.
> If you have a smartphone, please bring it **fully charged**, with your charger.
> If you don't have a phone, please come — you will share and learn the same things.
> Please bring **one real question about your garden or farm** (for example about a pest,
> a plant, water, or selling). Write it or remember it.
> You do not need airtime or data. We will give data at the course.

- [ ] Ask the programme office: how many learners have **smartphones**, how many have
      **WhatsApp**, which **languages**. Plan groups: each group of 5 needs at least 1 phone.
- [ ] Ask the programme office: is the **ImbewuField app** used on this programme? Are AI
      features switched on? If yes, which learners already have accounts? (Decide app ON / OFF.)
- [ ] Buy data (hotspot or bundles, see `resources.md`). Buy it **with enough days of validity**.
- [ ] Order posters (14 × A1) and print packs (see §4).
- [ ] Find the **local contacts** for the "ask a person" cards: extension officer, mentor,
      nearest clinic, nearest vet / animal health technician or state vet office, nearest
      agricultural co-op or agro-dealer, police station, SASSA office, the programme office.
      Write them on the **Who to Ask** sheet (one per learner, prompt-cards.md, card K-07).
      Check each number by calling it. Do not guess numbers.

## 2. Kit check (08:30–09:30)

Count every item in `resources.md` against the list. Tick. Anything missing: buy today or
write a substitute on the list. Specifically check:

- [ ] 2 spare phones: charged, factory-reset or on a course account, **no personal data**,
      screen lock set to a known course PIN (only facilitators know it), WhatsApp set up on a
      **course number** if possible.
- [ ] Power banks charged (at least 1 per group). Multi-plug and extension lead.
- [ ] Hotspot router charged; SIM with data; password written on 6 small cards.
- [ ] Kitchen scale (for the "weigh to check" activity) and a bag of produce (about 1–3 kg,
      bought or from a garden: carrots, potatoes, spinach bunch, tomatoes).
- [ ] The cardboard **AI sign** (A3 card on a string, a drawing of a phone with a question
      mark — no words needed) for the facilitator who plays the AI.
- [ ] Flipchart and 2 markers for the Guessing Machine tally (Day 1 S1); the 3 sentence starts agreed in the local language.
- [ ] Printed cards (prompt-cards.md): count and sort into 6 group envelopes.

## 3. Phone and data test (09:30–11:00)

Do this on **the cheapest phone you have** (entry-level Android), not your own good phone.

1. **Hotspot:** switch on the router, connect 6 phones at once. Open a web page on each.
   Send a photo on WhatsApp from one. If slow with 6, plan to connect **one phone per group
   only**. Check how much data 30 minutes of testing used (router or bundle balance) and write
   it down — this is your real cost per hour.
2. **Data-saving settings** — practise the steps you will teach in A-D1-S2 on an Android phone
   *and* whatever other phones you have (menus differ by brand and version; write the path for
   each brand on a sticky note in the kit):
   - WhatsApp → Settings → Storage and data → **Media auto-download**: set "When using mobile
     data" to no media (untick all).
   - WhatsApp → Settings → Storage and data → **Photo upload quality**: Standard.
   - WhatsApp → Settings → Storage and data → **Use less data for calls** (if the option shows).
   - Android → Settings → Network / Connections → **Data saver** on.
   - Android → Settings → Apps / Play Store → **auto-update apps over Wi-Fi only**.
3. **WhatsApp safety settings** (for A-D2-S2), same phones:
   - Settings → Account → **Two-step verification** → turn on (practise with the spare phone).
   - Settings → Privacy → **Groups**: "My contacts" (who can add me to groups).
   - Settings → Privacy → **Profile photo**, **About**, **Last seen**: "My contacts".
   - Settings → Privacy → Calls → **Silence unknown callers** (if the option shows).
   - How to **block and report** a chat.
   Write down which of these did **not** show on which phone. Menus change: teach what you see.
4. **Spare phones:** set up and signed in to what the course needs, with no personal chats.

## 4. Tool test (11:00–12:30)

For each tool, on the cheap phone, ask the same 3 test questions **in English and in the
learners' first language** (isiZulu, Sesotho, siSwati… as needed):

- *"I am a small farmer in KwaZulu-Natal. I have a 3 m × 1 m bed of spinach with small holes
  in the leaves. What can I do without buying poison? Give me 3 short steps."*
- *"When can I plant tomatoes in [your area]? We get frost in winter."* (Check the answer
  against the **Calendar** in the app or your own planting calendar — note if it gets the
  southern hemisphere wrong.)
- *"How many millilitres of [any pesticide] do I mix in 20 litres?"* (Note what it says. Many
  tools will give a number. This is your **red-light** example for Day 2: the answer must come
  from the label.)

Write down on the **Tool test sheet** (below): does it work, how long it takes, does it answer in
the local language, is the local-language answer good (ask the first-language facilitator),
did it make a mistake you can use in class.

| Tool | Works on cheap phone? | Local language OK? | A mistake to use in class |
|---|---|---|---|
| WhatsApp — Meta AI (if it shows) | | | |
| A general AI chat app, if any learners use one | | | |
| Google Lens (photo of a leaf) | | | |
| Keyboard voice typing (the mic on the keyboard) | | | |
| ImbewuField **Lima** (app ON only) | | | |
| **Lima Vision** — What's growing / Weigh my harvest | | | |
| **Tree ID** (a tree at the venue) | | | |
| **Finances** — scan a till slip (use a real slip) | | | |
| **Reports** — generate one for a sample garden | | | |

Keep 2–3 of the **real mistakes** you found. Real mistakes from today's tools teach better
than the printed ones. If you find none, use the printed answer cards (prompt-cards.md, B-cards).

**Know the honest limits before learners ask:**
- Meta AI in WhatsApp does not show on every phone or account. That's normal; use the other path.
- Voice typing may not support every South African language on every phone. Test it; don't promise.
- Lima Vision gives a **rough** estimate with a confidence label (high / medium / low).
- Tree ID gives a confidence number and may ask for a photo of the trunk base.
- The till slip reader reads the **total, a short description and the shop name** only, not
  every line — learners check those three things against the paper slip.

## 6. Rehearse the role-plays (14:00–15:00)

1. **The Guessing Machine** (A-D1-S1). One of you is the AI with the sign on. Practise:
   the sentence starts, circling the most common answer on the tally, answering a goat question
   confidently and wrongly. Practise stepping out of role: take off the sign, say
   *"Now I'm me again."* This matters — learners must see the difference.
2. **The fake voice note** (A-D2-S2). Record (on the spare phone) a short voice note in the
   local language: *"Ma, it's me, my phone fell in the water, this is my friend's number, please
   send R200 airtime quickly, I'll explain later."* Use a facilitator's voice, **never** a real
   learner's or family member's voice. Delete it after the course.
3. **Facilitator-as-AI for the no-phone groups** (all days). Practise answering from the
   printed AI answer cards in a flat, confident "machine" voice, including the mistakes.

## 7. Venue set-up (15:00–15:45)

- **Poster wall:** Day 1 posters A-P01 to A-P06 up at seated eye level, left to right. Day 2
  posters rolled and ready. Blank flipchart sheet beside each poster.
- **Question Tree** on a wall or tree (facilitation-toolkit.md §11).
- **Charging corner:** multi-plug, labelled with group plant names (Bean, Pumpkin, Maize,
  Banana, Marula, Spinach). Masking tape and a marker to label chargers with owners' names.
- **Phone Help Desk:** a table and 2 chairs near the charging corner.
- **6 group stations** 3 m apart, each with a table or mat, chairs, the group envelope of cards.
- **Weigh station** (Day 1 S3): kitchen scale on a flat table, produce in a bag.
- **Plant spot** (Day 1 S3): 3–5 plants or a small bed and one tree within walking distance
  that groups can photograph. If none, bring potted plants, a cabbage, a leaf with pest damage.
- Test the hotspot from the far corner of the venue.

## 8. Walk through Day 1 (15:45–16:30)

- Read `day-1.md` aloud together, session by session. Decide who leads, who co-facilitates.
- Decide the **call and response** for attention in the local language.
- Decide **app ON or OFF** and tell each other clearly which twin (`[APP]` / `[PAPER]`) you run.
- Plan groups: list learners with phones; spread phones so every group has one.
- Agree the **"stop" signal** you will use if someone starts sharing private information on a
  phone in front of the room (hand up, then a quiet word — never a public telling-off).

## If things go wrong on Day 0

| Problem | Do this |
|---|---|
| No signal at the venue | Move the course, or run the whole course on the no-phone path with printed cards, and do phone practice in a 1-hour "signal walk" to the nearest spot with signal |
| Hotspot too slow for 6 phones | One phone per group online; others use voice typing and camera offline |
| An AI tool won't work at all | Drop it. Use the next tool or the printed answer cards. Don't apologise to learners — say "tools change; the habits stay" |
| App not installed / AI features switched off | Run `[PAPER]` everywhere. Tell the programme office after the course |
| No produce or scale | Use a 2 ℓ bottle filled with water (it weighs about 2 kg) — photograph it and compare with the estimate |
