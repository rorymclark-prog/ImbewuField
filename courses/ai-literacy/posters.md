# Posters · Course 5 · AI Literacy

14 posters, A1 portrait unless stated. Layout, type, colour and art rules: `shared/poster-standards.md`.
Max 25 words per poster (headline + labels + question). Every label sits in a text box for
translation (isiZulu first). The art is text-free: **phone screens in the art show shapes,
bubbles and icons, never readable words** — words go in the typeset callouts.

Phones in the art: plain, generic, entry-level Android-style handsets (no brand logos). Screens
show simple flat shapes: chat bubbles, a camera frame, a keyboard, a microphone, a padlock.

## A-P01 · Our class and phone agreement
- Size: A1 portrait
- Used in: A-D1-S0, A-D1-S1
- Art: A-P01-ART

**Headline:** Learn together, phones too
**Art brief (text-free):** A circle of eight seated learners of mixed ages and genders under a
tree — a gogo with a headscarf, a young man in a cap, a young woman, an older man with a
walking stick. Two pairs lean over one shared phone together, smiling. A third phone lies
face-down on a knee (silent). One young woman holds up a hand to a person with a phone camera,
palm out, polite ("ask first"). A small padlock floats above one phone. Four numbered callout
circles: ① on the face-down phone, ② on a shared screen, ③ on the raised hand, ④ on the padlock.
Lower third of the poster left empty (paper colour) for the group's own rules written on the day.
**Labels on the poster (≤ 25 words, each ≤ 4 words):**
1. Phones silent, not away
2. Share the turn
3. Ask before photos
4. Private stays private
**Ask the room:** *What else helps us learn?*
**Layout:** Headline top band; art 45%; labels as numbered callouts; a 20% blank panel with
ruled lines for the class's own rules (marker on paper, or a sheet taped on the banner).
**Teach it — back-of-poster card (A5):**
```
A-P01  Our class and phone agreement            (≈ 8 min)
1. GROUPS: agree one rule that helps us learn together. 2 min.
2. Reporters say it; Recorder writes/draws it in the blank panel.
3. POINT ①: phones silent, not away — we use them in class.
4. POINT ②: one phone, many people — everyone gets a turn.
5. POINT ③: ask before you photograph a person (POPIA consent).
6. POINT ④: no ID, PIN or bank details typed today.
7. ASK: Can we all live with these? Thumbs up.
KEY: We share phones, we share turns, we protect each other.
```

## A-P02 · AI is a guessing machine
- Size: A1 portrait
- Used in: A-D1-S1, A-D2-S1
- Art: A-P02-ART

**Headline:** AI is a fast guesser
**Art brief (text-free):** Centre: a friendly, simple machine made of a phone shape with a big
thought bubble. Left side feeding into it: a tall stream of many small paper pages, books and
photographs flying in (the training). Right side coming out: three speech bubbles; the top one
has a big tick shape, the middle one a tick, the bottom one a cross — but all three bubbles look
equally bold and confident. Below the machine, a small homestead garden (beds, a goat, a water
tank) sits behind a closed gate — the machine can't see into it. A farmer (middle-aged woman,
gumboots) stands at the gate holding a phone, about to "show" the garden. Callouts: ① on the
stream of pages, ② on the machine's thought bubble, ③ on the confident bubble with the cross,
④ on the closed gate.
**Labels on the poster (≤ 25 words, each ≤ 4 words):**
1. Learned from lots
2. Guesses what's next
3. Sure, but wrong
4. Knows what you show
**Ask the room:** *What doesn't it know about your farm?*
**Layout:** Headline top; art 60% flowing left to right; labels under the art in the callout band.
**Teach it — back-of-poster card (A5):**
```
A-P02  AI is a guessing machine                  (≈ 20 min with the role-play)
1. PLAY: "Finish my sentence" — 3 starts, room shouts, tally the answers.
2. Put on the AI sign. Answer with the most common word, every time.
3. Ask the machine about a coughing goat — answer fast, sure, and unsafe.
4. Ask it when to plant maize — answer "April" (northern seasons).
5. Take the sign off: "Now I'm me again."
6. POINT ①→④ and say each label.
7. ASK: What doesn't it know about your farm?
KEY: AI is a very fast guesser. Very good is not always right.
```

## A-P05 · The Question Hand
- Size: A1 portrait
- Used in: A-D1-S2, A-D1-S4, A-D2-S0, A-D2-S3

**Headline:** Ask with your whole hand
**Art brief (text-free):** One big open right hand, palm facing the viewer, brown skin, a farm-
worker's hand (a little soil on the fingertips). Beside each finger a small picture: thumb — a
simple farmer figure pointing at herself (who I am); pointer — a bed with plants and a water
drum (what I have); middle — a target with a seedling in the centre (what I want); ring — three
short stacked bars like steps, and a speech bubble (how to answer); little finger — a circular
arrow (ask again). Callouts ①–⑤ on the fingers from thumb to little finger.
**Labels on the poster (≤ 25 words, each ≤ 4 words):**
1. Who I am
2. What I have
3. What I want
4. How to answer
5. Ask again
**Ask the room:** *Which finger do people forget?*
**Layout:** Headline top; the hand fills 60%; labels as callouts beside each finger (large, 64 pt).
**Teach it — back-of-poster card (A5):**
```
A-P05  The Question Hand                         (≈ 15 min + practice)
1. AI sign on. A learner asks one word: "Spinach?" Read B-00 — long, useless.
2. Sign off. Hold up your hand: "A good question has five fingers."
3. POINT ①–⑤ with an example: farmer near Bergville · spinach bed with holes ·
   stop it · 3 short steps in isiZulu · ask again.
4. AI sign on. Ask the full question. Read B-01 — short, useful.
5. ASK: Which answer helps more? Why?
6. PAIRS: say the five fingers in your own language.
KEY: Tell it who you are and what you have. The first answer is a draft.
```
