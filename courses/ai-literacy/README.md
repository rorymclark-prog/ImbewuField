# Course 5 · AI Literacy — using AI safely and usefully on a phone

**For:** farmers with smartphones (often entry-level Android on prepaid data), youth (18–35),
community mentors and facilitators on ACT·SEF-type programmes. Mixed literacy. Mixed languages.
Many learners first met AI inside WhatsApp or a chat app — some without knowing it was AI.
**Class size:** 20–30 learners (25 is ideal), in groups of 5. **Two facilitators.**
**Hours:** 2 days, 09:00–16:00, plus a Day 0 preparation day.
**Alternative:** 4 half-days (see *Four half-days* below).
**Venue:** a hall, school, church or lapa with shade, chairs, a wall or fence for posters,
a small garden or a few plants and a tree within 50 m, and — if at all possible — a plug point
or a charging station. The course runs without electricity if phones and power banks arrive charged.

## The one idea behind the course

> **AI is a fast guesser. Use it to think, not to decide. Check before you trust.**

Everything else hangs on that sentence. Learners leave able to *use* AI (ask well, use voice and
photos, get a first draft) **and** to *protect* themselves (check answers, keep private things
private, spot scams and fakes).

## Nobody is left out: phones, no phones, and data

- **Phones are shared.** Each group of 5 has at least **one working smartphone** (the "group
  phone"); pairs share where there are more. The kit carries **2 spare phones** for groups with none.
- **The no-phone path is a full path, not a lesser one.** Every activity can be done with
  printed **AI answer cards** (`prompt-cards.md`) and a **role-play in which a facilitator
  plays the AI** (wearing the cardboard "AI" sign). Learners without phones practise the same
  skills: asking well, checking, spotting danger and scams.
- **Data costs real money.** The course brings its own Wi-Fi hotspot or data bundles
  (`resources.md`). No learner is asked to use their own airtime or data. Session A-D1-S2 teaches
  data-saving settings before anyone uses AI.
- **Learner phones stay the learner's.** Facilitators never take a learner's phone to fix it
  without asking. Learners do every tap themselves; a facilitator talks them through it.

## What learners leave with

By the end of Day 2 every learner can:

1. **Explain** in their own words what AI is (a pattern-guesser trained on lots of text and
   pictures) and three things it is not (not a person, not always right, not knowing your farm).
2. **Name** three places they already meet AI on a phone.
3. **Ask** a clear question using the **Question Hand** (Who I am · What I have · What I want ·
   How to answer · Ask again) — in English or their own language.
4. **Use** voice typing and a clear photo to ask a question.
5. **Check** an AI answer with the **Check Ladder**, and **find** at least one mistake in a
   printed AI answer.
6. **Sort** questions by the **traffic light**, and **name** the red-light topics that must go
   to a person: poisons and sprays, medicine, animal health, money, loans, legal and grants.
7. **Choose** what never to share (ID number, bank details, PINs and codes, SASSA details,
   children's faces, exact location) and **set** two-step verification in WhatsApp.
8. **Spot** a scam message or fake voice note with **Stop · Check · Ask**, and **plan** a
   family code word.
9. **Use** AI to draft a work message, price list or CV — then **correct and own** it.
10. **Make** a personal AI plan: what I will use AI for, what I will always check with a person.

They take home: their own **Question Hand card**, a **Check Ladder card**, a **Scam Stop card**,
their personal **AI plan**, a corrected piece of work (message, price list or CV), and a
certificate of attendance.

## App on / app off

The course is **tool-neutral**. It teaches habits that work with any AI tool.

- **App track ON** (programme uses ImbewuField): sessions marked `[APP]` use **Lima** (the
  in-app assistant), **Lima Vision** ("What's growing" and "Weigh my harvest"), **Tree ID**,
  **scan a till slip** in **Records → Spent**, and AI-written **Reports**. Slides for these carry
  `Track: app`.
- **App track OFF:** use the `[PAPER]` twin — same time, same outcome — with tools learners
  already have (WhatsApp, Meta AI where it shows up, Google Lens, voice typing on the keyboard)
  or the no-phone role-play with printed answer cards.
- Switch the whole programme with one decision on Day 0: *"Do all groups have the ImbewuField app
  installed, logged in, and AI features switched on for this programme?"* If any answer is no,
  run `[PAPER]` for everyone. Programme admins can switch AI features off; check on Day 0.

**Be honest with learners about tools:**
- AI features change quickly. A button may move or disappear between courses. Test on Day 0.
- Some AI features cost data; some need a newer phone; some do not work in every language.
- We never tell learners one product is "the best". We name tools they already have.
- We never promise that AI will make them money or replace their mentor.

## Four half-days (ALT)

For groups who cannot leave home or work for full days. 09:00–12:30 each, one week apart or on
four mornings in a row. Each half-day starts with register + "what did you sleep on?" (20 min)
and ends with a 10-minute closing circle.

| Half-day | Sessions | Take-home task before next time |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | A-D1-S0, A-D1-S1, A-D1-S2 | Ask one real question at home with the Question Hand; bring the answer |
| 2 | A-D1-S3, A-D1-S4 | Take one clear photo of a plant problem; write down 3 questions |
| 3 | A-D2-S1, A-D2-S2 | Switch on two-step verification; agree a family code word |
| 4 | A-D2-S3, A-D2-S4, certificates | Share the AI plan with one family member |

The gaps between half-days are useful: learners try things at home and bring back real problems.
Keep the same groups of 5 for all four half-days.

## Checking what learners can do

**Start and end quiz (spoken, no reading).** Learners stand in the A, B or C corner of the room.
Count bodies per corner. Record class totals, not names. Run it in A-D1-S0 and A-D2-S4.

| # | Question (said aloud) | A | B | C |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | AI is… | a person hiding in the phone | a very fast guesser | always right |
| 2 | AI tells you how much poison to spray. You… | spray it | check the label or ask a person | spray double to be sure |
| 3 | A message says "You won R5 000, send your ID". You… | send it | stop, check, ask | share it with friends |
| 4 | Does AI know your soil? | Yes, always | Only what you tell or show it | It knows every farm |
| 5 | A voice note from "your son" asks for airtime. You… | send airtime | call him back on his old number | reply with your PIN |
| 6 | AI wrote your price list. You… | send it as is | check every price and own it | delete your records |

**AI skills checklist** (co-facilitator ticks during practicals; ✔ = did it alone, ◐ = with
help, ✘ = not yet). Same rules as the Course 1 checklist in `shared/forms.md` §4: this records
what learners can **do**; it is not a certificate of competence.

| Learner | Asks with the Question Hand | Uses voice or photo to ask | Finds a mistake in an AI answer | Names 3 red-light topics |
|---|---|---|---|---|

| Learner | Sets WhatsApp two-step verification (or explains it — no phone) | Spots a scam with Stop · Check · Ask | Corrects an AI draft |
|---|---|---|---|

Learners without a phone are marked on the no-phone version of each task (role-play and cards).
Never mark someone down for not owning a phone.

## Facilitators

- **Two facilitators.** At least one is confident on Android phones and WhatsApp settings; at
  least one speaks the learners' first language well. Both must have done Day 0 together.
- The facilitator does **not** need to be an AI expert. They need to model the habit:
  *"I don't know — let's check."* Saying this aloud in front of learners is part of the lesson.
- Co-facilitator plays the AI in role-plays, keeps the teacher-talk tally (session sheet,
  `shared/forms.md` §3), and runs the **Phone Help Desk** (a chair and a table where anyone can
  bring a phone problem at tea and lunch).

## Delivery rhythm

- Day 0 is never the same day as Day 1.
- At most three courses back to back, then a week at home (same as all courses).
- This course is light on digging but heavy on talking and small screens. Plan eye breaks:
  every 20 minutes of screen work, 2 minutes looking at something far away.

## How it connects to the other courses

- **Course 1 (Grow Food, Grow Strong):** the farm questions in this course come from Course 1
  topics (water, soil, seedlings, pests, seed, money). Learners who have done Course 1 check AI
  answers against what they learned there.
- **Course 2 (Walking With Farmers):** mentors are the "person to ask" on the Check Ladder.
  Mentors should take this course so they can support farmers who use AI — and so they check
  AI-cleaned visit notes and AI-written reports before signing them.
- **Course 3 (Teach the Teachers):** facilitators micro-teach A-D1-S1 (The Guessing Machine)
  and A-D2-S2 (scams) as practice sessions.
- **Course 4 (Farmer Money):** A-D2-S3 (till slip, price list) builds on its record-keeping;
  the scam session protects savings groups and stokvels.
