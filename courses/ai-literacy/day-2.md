## A-D2-S1 · 09:30–10:30 · The Check Ladder and the traffic light (60 min)

**By the end, learners can:** use the five steps of the Check Ladder on an AI answer; sort a
question as green, orange or red; find at least one mistake in a printed AI answer; name who
to ask for a red question.

**Poster:** A-P07, A-P08 · **Slides:** A-D2-02 → A-D2-06
**Materials (per group of 5):** 4 AI answer cards with mistakes (B-cards, see the S1 set in the
prompt-cards.md answer key) · 1 set of check-it cards C-01…C-06 (the ladder in the hand) ·
1 set of traffic-light topic cards T-01…T-12 · 3 A4 sheets:
one green, one orange, one red (or white sheets with a coloured circle and the words — works in
black and white) · 2 pens (red if you have) · 5 **Check Ladder** cards (K-08) and 5 **Who to Ask**
sheets (K-07) to hand out at the end · group phone.
**Also:** yesterday's AI answer cards on the To check tomorrow sheet; the 3 you chose last night.

| Time | Step | Teacher talk? |
|---|---|---|
| 09:30 | Hook: the machine plants cabbage | 2 min |
| 09:35 | The Check Ladder | 4 min |
| 09:43 | Green · Orange · Red — sort the topics | 3 min |
| 09:53 | Find the mistakes (B-cards) | 2 min |
| 10:10 | Report and answer key | 4 min |
| 10:18 | Check my own card from yesterday | 1 min |
| 10:26 | Key messages | 1 min |

### Say / ask / do

**Hook (09:30, 5 min)**
1. **DO:** AI sign on. Co-facilitator asks: *"I'm a farmer in KZN. How far apart do I plant
   cabbage seedlings?"* Machine, fast and sure: *"Plant cabbage 5 cm apart in rows. This gives
   the best yield."*
   **ASK the room:** *"Would you do it?"* *(No! Cabbage needs about half a metre — Course 1,
   F-P15.)* *"How did you know it was wrong?"* *(We learned it. We've grown cabbage. It makes no
   sense — they'd crowd.)*
2. **SAY (sign off):** *"You checked it without thinking — because you know cabbage. When we
   don't know, we need a ladder."*

**The Check Ladder (09:35, 8 min) — A-P07**
3. **ASK:** *"In the village, when you hear a rumour — 'the clinic is closed Friday' — how do
   you find out if it's true?"* *(Ask someone else. Go and look. Phone the clinic. See if it
   makes sense.)* **SAY:** *"We already know how to check. Same with AI."*
4. **DO:** point up the ladder on A-P07, bottom to top. Each rung, ask one learner to say it in
   their language:
   ① **Does it make sense here?** — on *my* farm, *this* season, sun in the north, my soil, my
     money.
   ② **Ask again, a different way.** Does the answer change? If it changes a lot, be careful.
   ③ **Look at a second source** — the product label, the planting calendar (or **Calendar** in
     the app), a Course 1 poster, a leaflet, the seed packet.
   ④ **Ask a person who knows** — mentor, extension officer, lead farmer, agro-dealer, vet or
     animal health technician, clinic nurse.
   ⑤ **Try it small first** — one bed, one row, one plant. Watch. Then do more.
5. **SAY:** *"You don't always climb every rung. How high you climb depends on how bad a
   mistake would be. That's the traffic light."*

**Green · Orange · Red (09:43, 10 min) — A-P08**
6. **DO:** point to A-P08:
   - **Green — little harm if wrong:** ideas, recipes, a name, help with writing. *Try it small.*
   - **Orange — could cost a crop or money:** planting dates, spacing, what pest it is, prices,
     what to buy. *Check a second source (rung ③).*
   - **Red — could hurt a person, an animal, the soil and water, or your money for real:**
     poison and spray doses, medicine, animal medicine and vaccines, loans, grants, SASSA,
     contracts, land, legal papers. *Always a person. Never AI alone.*
7. **GROUPS (6 min):** lay out the three coloured sheets. Sort the 12 topic cards (T-01…T-12)
   onto them. Leader makes sure everyone places at least two.
8. **DO (2 min):** walk round; ask any group with a red card on green *"what's the worst that
   could happen?"* Answer key is in prompt-cards.md (T-cards).

**Find the mistakes (09:53, 17 min)**
9. **GROUPS (15 min):** each group gets **4 AI answer cards** (B-cards) — each is a farm question
   and an answer "the AI gave". Some are good; most have one or two mistakes. For each card:
   - Climb the ladder: *Does it make sense here?* Use the posters on the wall (Course 1 posters
     if they are up), the group's own knowledge, a seed packet, and — if the group has a phone —
     **ask the same question to a second AI or ask it again differently** (rung ②).
   - Pass the check-it cards (C-01…C-06) round: each person holds one rung and asks its
     question of the card.
   - **Circle** (or put a finger on) any mistake. Recorder writes or draws what's wrong.
   - Give the card a **traffic-light colour**, and say **who you would ask** (rung ④).
10. **SAY at 10:05:** *"5 minutes."*

**Report and answer key (10:10, 8 min)**
11. **DO:** each Reporter shares **one** mistake the group found and who they'd ask. Read the
    answer from the key (prompt-cards.md) for each card reported. Clap for each mistake found.
    **ASK:** *"Which mistakes were hardest to spot? Why?"* *(The ones that sounded sure and
    used numbers; the ones with the northern-hemisphere months; the ones that were mostly right.)*

**Check my own card (10:18, 8 min)**
12. **SAY:** *"Now yesterday's answers — your own questions."* Learners take their AI answer
    card from the wall (or pocket). In pairs: *Does it make sense here? What colour is it now?
    Who will you ask?* Write the person's name on the card.
13. **DO:** take the **3 cards you chose last night** (with the owner's permission). Check one
    together as a room in 2 minutes — ideally a red one. Model saying: *"I don't know — I would
    ask the extension officer. Let's write down her number."*

**Key messages and hand-out (10:26, 4 min)** — give each learner a Check Ladder card (K-08)
and a Who to Ask sheet (K-07), filled in with the local numbers you checked on Day 0.

### Debrief (the three questions)
1. *What mistakes did the AI make?* *(Wrong months — northern seasons; wrong spacing; made-up
   dose; told us to buy things we don't need; mostly right with one dangerous bit.)*
2. *Why does AI make these mistakes?* *(It guesses from lots of writing, much of it from other
   countries; it doesn't know our farm; it sounds sure anyway.)*
3. *What will you check first at home?* Take one per group.

### Key messages
1. **Check before you trust** — climb the ladder: makes sense? · ask again · second source ·
   ask a person · try small.
2. **Red questions go to a person** — sprays and poisons, medicine, animal health, money,
   loans, grants, legal. Always.
3. **The label is the law** for any spray or chemical — not the AI, not the neighbour.
4. **Mostly right is not right.** One wrong number can cost a crop, an animal or a life.

### If things go wrong
- **A group finds no mistakes:** sit with them for one card; ask rung ① questions: *"Which
  month is that here? What season? How much would that cost?"*
- **A learner defends a wrong AI answer ("but the computer said"):** thank them; ask the room
  *"How could we check?"* — never "you're wrong". If it's red, you decide on safety: "We don't
  use that until the extension officer confirms."
- **A learner's own card is a red emergency** (sick child, dying animals): stop, help them
  contact the clinic or vet now.
- ✂ **Cut if short:** check-my-own-card becomes a take-home task; never cut Find the mistakes.

### Language & inclusion notes
- The B-cards are written in plain English with a line for the local-language version; read
  them aloud for non-readers. The Reporter can read; a non-reader often spots a wrong month or
  spacing faster than a reader.
- Rung ④ is where elders and experienced farmers are the experts. Name them as "people who
  know".

## A-D2-S2 · 10:50–12:30 · Keep yourself safe: privacy, scams and fakes (100 min)

**By the end, learners can:** choose what never to share on a phone or with AI; switch on
WhatsApp two-step verification and group privacy (or explain the steps); spot a scam message
using Stop · Check · Ask; plan a family code word against fake voice notes.

**Poster:** A-P09, A-P10, A-P11 · **Slides:** A-D2-07 → A-D2-14 · **Clip:** A-ANI-03 (optional)
**Materials (per group of 5):** 1 set of share-or-not cards H-01…H-16 · 3 sorting sheets:
"Fine to share", "Only face to face with someone I trust", "Never share with strangers or AI"
· 1 set of scam cards S-01…S-10 · 3 sorting sheets: "Scam", "Real", "Not sure — check" · group
phone · 5 **Scam Stop** cards (K-09) to hand out.
**Facilitator:** the fake voice note recorded on Day 0 on the spare phone (never a learner's
voice); a small speaker or the phone at full volume; the **paper phone** (an A3 drawing of a
phone with the WhatsApp settings screens as flip pages) for no-phone learners.

| Time | Step | Teacher talk? |
|---|---|---|
| 10:50 | Hook: what's in your wallet? | 2 min |
| 10:55 | Share or not — sort | 5 min |
| 11:10 | Lock your WhatsApp | 5 min |
| 11:30 | The fake voice note | 2 min |
| 11:35 | Stop · Check · Ask | 4 min |
| 11:40 | Scam cards — sort | 3 min |
| 12:00 | Fake voices, fake videos; family code word | 4 min |
| 12:15 | Debrief | 2 min |
| 12:25 | Key messages, hand-out | 1 min |

### Say / ask / do

**Part A — Keep it private (10:50–11:30)**

**Hook (10:50, 5 min)**
1. **ASK:** *"Think of your wallet or purse. What's in it that you would never give to a
   stranger?"* *(ID, bank card, SASSA card, PIN, money, photos of the children.)*
   **SAY:** *"Your phone is a wallet now. Anything you type to a stranger — or into an AI app —
   may be kept, read, or used. So we decide what goes in."*

**Share or not (10:55, 15 min) — A-P09**
2. **GROUPS (10 min):** sort the 16 H-cards onto the three sheets. Leader: everyone places
   cards; disagreements are good — talk them through.
3. **DO (5 min):** go through the "never" pile with the room (answer key in prompt-cards.md).
   Point to A-P09's six callouts — **never share with strangers or AI:**
   ① ID number and ID photo ② bank card, account and PIN ③ passwords and the **codes sent by
   SMS or WhatsApp** (one-time PINs / OTPs) ④ SASSA card and details ⑤ children's faces and
   names ⑥ your exact location (home pin, where you are right now) with strangers.
4. **SAY (POPIA, 2 min — plain):** *"South Africa has a law called **POPIA** — it protects your
   personal information. An organisation must tell you why it wants your information, use it
   only for that, and keep it safe. You can ask what they have about you. If they misuse it, you
   can complain to the **Information Regulator**. That's why we asked your consent on Day 1.
   But the law can't help if you give your PIN to a scammer. So — you are the first guard."*
5. **SAY (AI and privacy, 1 min):** *"When you type into an AI app, the company may keep what
   you type and may use it to improve the tool. Some let you switch that off in settings. So:
   ask about spinach, not about your bank account. Take the ID number off a document before you
   ask AI to help with it."*

**Lock your WhatsApp (11:10, 20 min)**
6. **SAY:** *"Scammers love WhatsApp. Three settings make it much harder for them."*
7. **GROUPS (15 min):** learners with phones do it **themselves** (facilitators talk them
   through; never take the phone). No-phone learners follow on the **paper phone** and explain the
   steps back to the group.
   ① **Two-step verification:** Settings → Account → Two-step verification → Turn on → choose a
     6-digit PIN **you will remember** and **nobody else knows** (not your birthday, not
     123456). Add an email if you have one (it helps if you forget the PIN).
   ② **Who can add me to groups:** Settings → Privacy → Groups → *My contacts*.
   ③ **Profile photo, About, Last seen:** Settings → Privacy → *My contacts*.
   ④ If it shows: Settings → Privacy → Calls → **Silence unknown callers**.
   ⑤ Practise **Block** and **Report** on a chat (open the chat → tap the name → scroll down).
8. **SAY (the golden rule, repeat twice):** *"WhatsApp, your bank and SASSA will never ask you
   for the code they SMS you. If anyone asks for that code — even a friend — it's a scam. Their
   phone may be stolen."*
9. **DO:** anyone who didn't finish → Help Desk at lunch.

**Part B — Scams and fakes (11:30–12:30)**

**The fake voice note (11:30, 5 min)**
10. **DO:** play the voice note recorded on Day 0 (*"Ma, it's me, my phone fell in the water,
    this is my friend's number, please send R200 airtime quickly…"*).
    **ASK:** *"What would you do?"* Let 3–4 people answer. *(Send it! / Call him / Ask
    something only he knows.)*
    **SAY:** *"This was a facilitator's voice. Today, a computer can copy a voice from a short
    voice note or video. The voice you trust can be faked."*

**Stop · Check · Ask (11:35, 5 min) — A-P10**
11. **DO:** point to A-P10:
    - **STOP.** Don't click, don't pay, don't send the code, don't reply fast. *Scammers rush
      you.*
    - **CHECK.** Call back on the number **you already have**, or the official number (on your
      card, the back of your bank card, the office wall) — never the number in the message.
      Look for the warning signs.
    - **ASK.** A person you trust: family, mentor, bank at the branch, SASSA office, police.
12. **SAY the warning signs** (count on fingers; they're on the scam cards):
    *Hurry · Secret · Send money, airtime or a code · Too good to be true · New number or
    stranger · A link to click · Pay a fee to get a job, grant or loan.*

**Scam cards (11:40, 20 min)**
13. **GROUPS (15 min):** sort the 10 scam cards (S-01…S-10) onto *Scam / Real / Not sure —
    check*. For each "Scam": Recorder writes or draws the **warning sign**. For each: *what
    would you do?*
14. **DO (5 min):** answer key (prompt-cards.md). Spend time on the ones groups disagree on —
    especially **S-06** (a real message that looks scary) and **S-09** (the "grant for farmers"
    with a fee).

**Fake voices, fake videos (12:00, 15 min) — A-P11**
15. **SAY:** *"AI can make a video of a famous person — a minister, a pastor, a TV presenter —
    saying things they never said: 'Invest R500 and get R5 000'. It can copy a voice. It can
    make a photo of a thing that never happened."*
16. **ASK:** *"How can you tell?"* Collect, then point to A-P11: ① *Too good or too scary to be
    true?* ② *Asks for money, a code, or to keep it secret?* ③ *Is it on the real news or the
    person's real page?* ④ *Call back on the number you know.* ⑤ *Use a family code word.*
    **SAY:** *"Sometimes the video looks perfect. Don't try to be a detective of pixels. Check the
    message, not the picture."*
17. **GROUPS (8 min) — family code word role-play:** in pairs, one plays a family member in
    trouble on a "voice note", the other asks the **code word question**. **SAY:** *"Agree a
    code word or a question only your family knows — the name of your first cow, gogo's
    favourite song. Agree it face to face, not on WhatsApp. Never write it on your AI plan
    or post it."*
    Swap roles once.

**Debrief (12:15, 10 min)**
1. *Which scam cards fooled you, or nearly?* *(The ones with a real-looking name; the grant.)*
2. *Why do scams work?* *(They rush you, scare you, promise money, pretend to be family or
   government.)*
3. *What will you do tonight?* *(Two-step verification; tell my family about the code word;
   tell gogo "SASSA never phones for your PIN".)*

**Key messages (12:25)** — hand out Scam Stop cards (K-09).
1. **Never share:** ID, bank and PIN, codes (OTPs), SASSA details, children's faces, where you are.
2. **Lock your WhatsApp:** two-step verification and "My contacts".
3. **Stop · Check · Ask.** Hurry + money or code = scam until proven real.
4. **Voices and videos can be faked.** Call back on the number you know. Use a family code word.

### If things go wrong
- **A learner says they've already been scammed:** thank them for their courage; never blame.
  If recent: help them at lunch to phone their bank (number on the back of the card) and open a
  case at SAPS. Their story (if they agree to share it) teaches better than any card.
- **A learner forgets their new WhatsApp PIN:** it's okay — WhatsApp will ask for it now and
  then; if they added an email they can reset. Help at the Help Desk; write nothing down for them.
- **Someone accidentally shows private messages on their screen to the group:** stop signal;
  they lock the screen; move on. No photos of screens.
- **Learners are frightened ("I'll never use my phone again"):** the point is *careful*, not
  scared. Remind them of the three settings and the three words. Most messages are real.
- ✂ **Cut if short:** the Report/Block practice (⑤) and the pair swap in the code-word role-play.
  Never cut the two-step verification practice or the scam cards.

### Language & inclusion notes
- Scam cards are in plain English with a local-language line. **Real scams arrive in English and
  in our languages** — say so. Scammers use local languages to seem trustworthy.
- Elders are the most targeted for SASSA scams; youth for job and loan scams. Pair them: each
  explains their scam to the other.
- Don't use real names of banks or real people in role-plays.

## A-D2-S3 · 13:15–14:30 · AI at work — AI drafts, you own it (75 min)

**By the end, learners can:** use AI to draft a work message, price list, CV or funding
request; find and correct mistakes in it; check what an AI tool read or wrote in their records;
name things AI can't do.

**Poster:** A-P12, A-P13 (and F-P20 Money in, money out; F-P05 Read your land, if up)
**Slides:** A-D2-15 → A-D2-20
**Materials (per group of 5):** 1 set of AI draft sheets D-01…D-05 (each with mistakes) · red
pens · group phone · 1 real **till slip** (buy seeds, a bag of compost or a tool before the
course and keep the slip — photocopies also work) · the group's **price notes** (current local
prices of 3 things they sell — they know these; write them on the flipchart first).

| Time | Step | Teacher talk? |
|---|---|---|
| 13:15 | Energiser: Human or Machine? | 2 min |
| 13:20 | AI drafts, you own it | 6 min |
| 13:28 | Work practical [APP] / [PAPER] | 4 min |
| 14:08 | What AI can't do | 6 min |
| 14:23 | Debrief | 2 min |

### Say / ask / do

**Energiser — Human or Machine? (13:15, 5 min)**
1. **DO:** read four short WhatsApp-style messages aloud (prompt-cards.md, D-00). Learners
   stand up for "a machine wrote it", sit for "a person wrote it". Reveal: two were AI, two
   were people. **SAY:** *"It's hard to tell. That's why, when AI writes for you, you must make it
   yours."*

**AI drafts, you own it (13:20, 8 min) — A-P12**
2. **DO:** point to A-P12. The four checks before you send anything AI wrote:
   ① **Facts true?** — every price, date, name, number, amount.
   ② **Sounds like me?** — my words, my language; not too fancy.
   ③ **Nothing private?** — no ID number, bank details, other people's details.
   ④ **Would I sign it?** — I can explain every line to a person.
3. **SAY:** *"If AI helps you write a funding request, a CV or a report, it's still your name
   at the bottom. AI can make up numbers and achievements that sound good. Never send something
   that isn't true. If a funder or employer asks whether you had help, be honest."*

**Work practical (13:28, 40 min)**

**GROUPS choose one task** (or the facilitator assigns so all four are covered):
- **A** — a WhatsApp message selling spinach and eggs to neighbours this week
- **B** — a price list for the group's produce (use the price notes)
- **C** — a one-page CV for a young person applying for a garden assistant job
- **D** — a short funding request (half a page) for a water tank for a community garden

#### [PAPER] Draft with a chat AI, or fix a printed draft (40 min)
1. **With a phone (20 min):** use the Question Hand to ask an AI chat the learners already use
   (e.g. Meta AI in WhatsApp, where it shows) to write the draft. Example: *"I am a youth in a
   community garden near Newcastle. I sell spinach bunches at R10 and trays of eggs at [price].
   Write a short, friendly WhatsApp message to my neighbours. In English and isiZulu. Under 50
   words."* **Use made-up names for any person in a CV — never real ID numbers or addresses.**
2. **No phone / no AI:** use the matching **printed draft** D-01…D-04 — "the AI wrote this".
3. **Fix it (15 min):** Recorder reads the draft aloud. The group checks it against the **four
   checks** (A-P12) and marks with a red pen: every wrong fact, every "doesn't sound like us",
   anything private, anything untrue. Then they **rewrite** the parts that were wrong.
4. **Share (5 min):** two groups read their corrected version aloud and say one mistake they
   found. Printed drafts: check against the answer key.

#### [APP] Till slip, Reports and Lima (40 min)
1. **Scan a till slip (15 min):** Phone Keeper opens **Records** → the **Spent** tab → **scan a till
   slip**, and photographs the group's slip (flat, good light, the whole slip). The app reads
   **three things**: the **total**, a **short description** and the **shop name** (and shows how
   sure it is). **Check all three against the paper slip, line by line with a finger.** Is the
   total the *grand total*, not a subtotal or the change? Is the description right? Fix anything
   wrong **before saving**. **SAY:** *"The slip is the proof. The app is the helper. If they
   disagree, the slip wins."*
2. **AI-written report (15 min):** open **Reports** for the group's garden (or a sample garden
   set up on Day 0) and generate a report. Reporter reads it aloud. The group underlines — on
   paper or by noting — **anything not true, not measured, or too positive** ("harvest doubled"
   when nobody weighed; a crop you don't grow). **SAY:** *"Before a report goes to a mentor,
   programme or funder, a person reads and corrects it. It carries your name."* (Where the app
   allows editing or adding notes, make the correction; otherwise write the correction on paper
   and tell the mentor.)
3. **Lima draft (10 min):** ask **Lima** for task A or B (message or price list) with the
   Question Hand; check with the four checks; correct.
4. **Groups with no app access:** use the [PAPER] path, and check the printed till slip exercise
   (prompt-cards.md D-05) — "the app read R1 250; the slip says R125.00".

**What AI can't do (14:08, 15 min) — A-P13**
5. **SAY:** *"Hands up if AI can do it. Hands down if it can't."* Read, then give the one-line
   answer:

| Can AI… | Answer |
|---|---|
| …see your soil? | Only a photo, if you show it. It can't feel, smell, squeeze or taste it. |
| …replace a soil test or a weighing scale? | No. Measure. |
| …know if it rained at your place this week? | No — unless you tell it. |
| …know today's price at your market? | Usually not. Ask at the market. |
| …know what your gogo knows about this land? | No. Ask her. Write it down. |
| …replace your mentor or extension officer? | No. It can help you prepare questions for them. |
| …sign your contract or vouch for you? | No. People do. |

6. **GROUPS (6 min):** each group agrees **one thing only people can do** on a farm or in the
   community, and the Reporter says it. Recorder writes or draws it beside A-P13.
   *(Observe the land through the seasons; share seed; care for a sick animal; trust; teach a
   child; be at the funeral; a stokvel.)*
7. **DO:** point to F-P05 if it's up: *"Reading your land is done with your feet and eyes."*

### Debrief (14:23, 7 min)
1. *What mistakes did the AI draft or the app make?* *(Wrong price; invented facts; too
   fancy; read the subtotal; report said we harvested more than we did.)*
2. *Why must a person check?* *(Our name is on it; money and trust are at stake.)*
3. *What will you use AI to draft this month?*

### Key messages
1. **AI drafts, you own it.** Check: facts · sounds like me · nothing private · would I sign it.
2. **The paper slip, the scale and the real count win** over any AI reading.
3. **Never send something untrue** — not in a CV, not in a funding request, not in a report.
4. **AI can't see your soil, feel the rain, or replace your mentor and your elders.**

### If things go wrong
- **AI writes a fancy CV full of invented experience:** perfect lesson — cross it all out, write
  what the person really did. Real is enough.
- **Till slip can't be read:** it's crumpled or faded. Try again flat, in shade, whole slip in
  view — or type it by hand. Say: *"The tool helps; you don't need it."*
- **No report can be generated (no data in the app):** use the printed draft D-04 (its
  "results so far" paragraph reads like an AI-written report).
- **Group argues about prices:** good — prices are local knowledge AI doesn't have.
- ✂ **Cut if short:** the Lima draft in [APP]; reduce "What AI can't do" to 4 questions.

### Language & inclusion notes
- Ask for drafts in **both English and the local language**, then compare. The local-language
  version often needs the most fixing; first-language speakers are the editors.
- Non-writers are the **fact checkers**: they know the prices and quantities.

**Teacher talk:** 20 of 75 min (27%).

## A-D2-S4 · 14:45–15:30 · Fair, honest, green AI · my AI plan · evaluation (45 min)

**By the end, learners can:** explain why AI is weaker in our languages and may assume the wrong
kind of farmer; name one cost AI has for the earth; make a personal AI plan.

**Poster:** A-P14 (and A-P13) · **Slides:** A-D2-21 → A-D2-22
**Materials:** 1 **My AI plan** sheet per learner (K-06), pencils · A/B/C corner sheets · the
evaluation (forms.md §6 — adapted, below) · yesterday's flipchart tally of voice typing and
isiZulu answers.

| Time | Step | Teacher talk? |
|---|---|---|
| 14:45 | Fair and green AI | 4 min |
| 14:55 | My AI plan | 2 min |
| 15:10 | End quiz — corners | 2 min |
| 15:18 | Evaluation | 2 min |

### Say / ask / do

**Fair and green AI (14:45, 10 min)**
1. **DO:** show yesterday's tally. **ASK:** *"Did the phone understand isiZulu as well as
   English? Were the isiZulu answers as good?"* *(Usually no.)*
2. **SAY:** *"AI learned mostly from writing in English and a few big languages, from richer
   countries. So:"*
   - *"It's weaker in our languages — always double-check an answer in isiZulu or Sesotho."*
   - *"It may picture a farmer as a man with a tractor and 100 hectares, or plant by northern
     seasons. You are the farmer it forgot — tell it who you are (the thumb!)."*
   - *"It can repeat unfair ideas it read about people — women, Black people, poor people,
     young people. If an answer feels unfair, it may be. Say so, and ask a person."*
3. **ASK:** *"Where does the AI's thinking happen?"* *(In big computer buildings far away.)*
   **SAY:** *"Those buildings use a lot of electricity and water to run and cool the computers.
   So: don't use AI for what you already know. One good question beats ten lazy ones. It
   saves your data too."*

**My AI plan (14:55, 15 min) — A-P14**
4. **DO:** hand out K-06. Learners fill it — words or drawings — alone (8 min):
   - **I will use AI for:** (3 things — e.g. planting ideas, writing a selling message, naming
     a pest)
   - **I will always ask a person about:** (3 things — e.g. sprays, my goats' health, loans)
   - **My data rule:** (e.g. Wi-Fi for photos; auto-download off)
   - **My safety step this week:** (two-step verification; tell my family the code-word idea)
   - **One person I will teach:** (name)
   **Never write the family code word on the plan.**
5. **GROUPS in pairs (7 min):** tell your partner your plan. Partner asks: *"What will you do
   first?"*

**End quiz (15:10, 8 min)**
6. **DO:** same six questions as Day 1 (README), same A/B/C corners. Co-facilitator counts.
   **SAY after each:** the answer, briefly — and cheer the change from Day 1.

**Evaluation (15:18, 12 min)**
7. **DO:** read the six evaluation statements from forms.md §6, replacing "for my garden" with
   "for my farm, work and family" and statement 6 with *"I used a phone or the role-play with my
   own hands (not just watched)."* Learners show 1–5 fingers; co-facilitator records class totals.
8. **DO:** the two sticker lists (forms.md §6), with these topics written on the flipchart:
   *What AI is · Asking with the Question Hand · Voice and photos · Checking answers · Red
   questions · Privacy · Scams and fakes · AI for work · Data saving.*
9. **GROUPS (3 min):** the three open questions (most useful / least useful / make it better);
   Recorders draw or write.

### Key messages
1. **AI is weaker in our languages** and may assume the wrong farmer — tell it who you are,
   and double-check.
2. **AI costs power, water and data** — use it when it helps, not for everything.
3. **Your AI plan:** use it to think, ask a person to decide.

### If things go wrong
- **Running late:** do the quiz and the plan; evaluation finger-votes can happen in the closing
  circle (5 min). Never cut the certificates.
- **"Is AI racist?":** answer honestly: *"It can repeat unfair things it read. It's not a
  person, so it doesn't 'mean' it — but the harm is real. Check, and say so."*
- ✂ **Cut if short:** the sticker lists (keep the finger scores and one open question).

### Language & inclusion notes
- The plan can be all drawings. A thumbprint on it is fine.
- Learners keep their plan — it's theirs. Take a photo only with consent.

**Teacher talk:** 10 of 45 min (22%).
