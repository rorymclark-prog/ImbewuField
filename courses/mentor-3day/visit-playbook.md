# The visit playbook — Walking With Farmers

The mentor's field book for the whole season. Print it (A5, spiral-bound) and keep it in the
bag; the phone copy works offline. It works **with or without the app**: every form here has
an app twin (Mentor workspace → Record visit), marked *App:*.

1. The Visit Hand
2. The individual visit (60 min and 45 min)

9. Planning sheets: caseload · week · season

## 1. The Visit Hand

Five fingers. Five steps. The same on every visit, every contact.

| Finger | Step | What you do | Ask |
|---|---|---|---|
| Thumb | **1 Look back** | Greet properly (family, health). Then the last agreed action. Look at the evidence. | "Last time you planned to… Show me how it went." |
| Pointing | **2 Walk & look** | The farmer leads the walk. Start with what's working. Look with the stage checklist (§5). Count and measure. | "Show me your garden. What are you proud of? What helped?" |
| Middle | **3 Listen & ask** | Find the farmer's own priority. Say back what you heard. Climb the question ladder. No advice yet. | "What would help you most right now? What have you tried? What else?" |
| Ring | **4 Try it together** | Coach **one** skill: I do · We do · You do, then talk. Decide the result. | "Shall we try it together, right here?" |
| Little | **5 Agree & record** | One action: what · who · by when · how we'll know. Write it at the farm; read it back; leave the card. | "So what will you do, by when? What will I see when I come?" |

**Why this order?** Looking back first shows the farmer that *their* plan matters and it closes
the loop (evidence of change). Walking before talking means you talk about what is real.
Asking before coaching means you coach what the farmer wants. Agreeing last means every
contact ends with a next step.

**Where it comes from:** adult experiential learning (do → reflect → understand → try again);
farmer-to-farmer extension and farmer field schools (learning in the field, by doing);
coaching and motivational interviewing (open questions, reflective listening, the person
decides); appreciative inquiry and positive deviance (start from what works, find who is
already succeeding with the same resources); goal-setting (specific, owned, dated actions,
with feedback).

**Rules of thumb**
- The farmer talks more than half the time.
- One skill per visit. One action per visit (two at most).
- Numbers beat words: "3 of 5 beds mulched", "2.5 kg spinach this week".
- An action is only **closed** with a **date** and **evidence of what changed**.
- No promises you cannot keep. No gifts that change how you help.

## 2. The individual visit

### 2a. The 60-minute visit, minute by minute

| Min | Finger | What happens | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Before | — | Read the last visit record (app: *Latest visit*; paper: last form). Note the last action, the skill result, the stage. Pack: forms/phone, spacing string, tape, the farmer's card copy. Phone/SMS the day before: "Is tomorrow at 10 still good?" | 10 min at home/office |
| 0–5 | Thumb | Arrive on time. Greet at the gate; wait to be invited in. Family, health, news. | Sit if offered. Phone away. |
| 5–10 | Thumb | "Last time you planned to ___. Show me." Look at the evidence together. Praise the effort, even if not done. If not done: "What got in the way?" | Close the action: date + what you saw |
| 10–25 | Pointing | Walk the garden — the farmer leads. Start: "What are you proud of?" Then use the stage checklist (§5). Measure one thing (spacing, mulch depth, tank level, bed count). Photos only with consent. | Write numbers as you go |
| 25–35 | Middle | Sit in the shade. "What would help you most right now?" Say back. Climb the ladder. Agree the **one** thing to work on today. | Support area: garden & water · soil & seedlings · harvest & food access · records · learning |
| 35–50 | Ring | Try it together: I do (2–3 min) · We do (5 min) · You do (5 min, silent) · Talk (2 min). Result: *Demonstrated* or *Needs further support*. | If app ON and the farmer is on the app track, this can be the app exercise of the month (app-track.md) |
| 50–57 | Little | Action agreement in the farmer's words. Fill the form/app at the farm. Read it back. Fill the farmer's card. Set the next contact date and type. | Agreed action · Responsible person · Follow-up date |
| 57–60 | — | Thank them. Leave on time. | Update traffic light that evening |

### 2b. The 45-minute visit (when time is short)
Thumb 5 · Pointing 10 · Middle 5 · Ring 15 · Little 10. **Never cut the ring finger or the little
finger** — the skill and the agreement are what change things.

### 2c. The first visit (baseline) — 75–90 minutes, once
Add to the 60-minute visit:
- Consent: explain what you record and who sees it (POPIA). App: onboarding consent; paper:
  forms.md §2. A mark or thumbprint is valid.
- Track: A (app) · P (paper) · A+P (paper + a helper enters it). Tick on the caseload sheet.
- Baseline: bed count, garden size (paced or measured), water source and storage, what is
  growing, who works in the garden, the Course 1 skills checklist (forms.md §4), the farmer's
  90-day plan from Course 1 (F-P21). Photos of the whole garden from the same spot (mark the
  spot — you'll take the "after" photo from there in month 6).
- Ask: "At the end of the season, what would make you proud?" Write it in their words.

## 3. The cluster visit (2–3 hours, 8–12 farmers, host garden)

**Why:** a cluster visit reaches 8–12 farmers in the time of three individual visits, and
farmers learn fastest from a neighbour with the same soil, rain and money. The host grows in
confidence. It is the backbone of the season for green and amber farmers.

**Cluster:** 8–12 farmers who live within walking distance (about 30 min) of each other.
**Host:** rotates monthly. Choose someone who does *one* thing well — not always the best garden.
Ask the host a week before; agree what they will show.

### 3a. A 2½-hour cluster visit, minute by minute (09:00–11:30)

| Time | Part | What happens | Mentor's job |
|---|---|---|---|
| 08:30 | Set-up | Arrive early. Hang M-P11 (or the stage poster) on the fence. Water, cups. Check the host's stations. | Brief the host again: "Show, don't lecture." |
| 09:00 | **1 Welcome** (15) | Register (forms.md §1). New people: consent. Energiser. Thumbs round: each farmer says last month's action — done / not yet. | Tally done / not yet (evidence of change) |
| 09:15 | **2 Host's tour** (25) | The host walks everyone round: what she did, what worked, what failed. | Ask the host open questions: "What helped this bed do so well?" |
| 09:40 | **3 Try it together** (50) | 2–3 stations, farmers in groups of 3–4, 15 min each, rotate. Each station one skill, I do · We do · You do. Stations can be led by the host and a green farmer. | Float. Keep hands busy. Tick the skill results. |
| 10:30 | Tea/seed swap (10) | Water, seed swap table, sharing seedlings. | Individual word with any red/amber farmer present |
| 10:40 | **4 Share circle** (25) | Each farmer: one success, one question. **Farmers answer each other first**; mentor answers last. | Write questions you can't answer — follow up on the phone |
| 11:05 | **5 Agree** (15) | Each farmer says one action aloud (Recorder writes). Next host and date agreed. | Write actions on the cluster record |
| 11:20 | **6 Close** (10) | Thank the host (clap). Tools back. | Stay 15 min for private conversations |

For a **2-hour** version: tour 15, try-it 40 (2 stations), circle 20. For a **3-hour** version add
a group work task for the host (e.g. build a compost heap or trench bed together — the
"garden party" idea, where the group helps one farmer with a big job, rotating).

### 3b. Stations by stage (pick 2–3)
| Stage | Stations |
|---|---|
| Month 1 Baseline | Draw your garden map · A-frame refresher · start the record book / set up the app profile |
| Month 2 Establishment | Spacing string & planting check · seedling tray · mulch depth |
| Month 3 Growth | Walk-and-spot pests (the ladder, F-P16) · liquid manure · a natural spray |
| Month 4 Harvest | Weigh and record a harvest · the family plate · choose seed plants to save |
| Month 5 Selling | Price a bunch (costs + local price) · write an invoice (app or duplicate book) · money book check |
| Month 6 Review | Seed drying & jar · before/after photos · plan next season |

| Station 2 | | | |

## 4. The farmer field day (half day, 20–60 people, once or twice a season)

**When:** at first harvest (month 4) and/or at the season review (month 6).
**Who:** all programme farmers in the area, families, neighbours, the ward councillor, the local
Department of Agriculture extension office, the school, local buyers (spaza, crèche kitchen,
church), other NGOs. **Farmers present; the mentor organises.**

### 4a. Minute by minute (09:30–13:00)

| Time | Part | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 08:00 | Set-up | Stations marked with posters; chairs and shade for elders; water; toilets; register table; first aid |
| 09:30 | Welcome (20) | Register; consent announcement; prayer/song if local custom; host community welcome |
| 09:50 | Farmer stations (100) | 4–5 stations run **by farmers**, visitors rotate in groups every 20 min: e.g. crowded bed vs spaced bed; compost & liquid manure; water harvesting on the site; seed jars & seed swap; records & money (app or book) |
| 11:30 | Harvest table (30) | Farmers display produce; weigh-in; buyers talk to farmers; recipes/family plate |
| 12:00 | Voices (30) | 3–4 farmers (mixed ages and genders) say what changed; guests respond briefly |
| 12:30 | Close (30) | Thanks; seed swap; food shared if budget allows; next steps |

## 5. What to look at, stage by stage

Use on **step 2 (Walk & look)**. Don't check everything — pick the 3–5 that fit the stage and
the farmer's priority. The Course 1 skills (forms.md §4) are marked ★.

### Month 1 · Baseline and set-up
- Garden boundary, size, beds counted; the farmer's map (paper, or app Map) ★
- Water: source, storage (drums, tank), roof catchment possible? ★
- Soil: hand test done? compost started? ★
- The 90-day plan (F-P21): what's first?
- Records: book or app set up; first entry made ★

### Month 2 · Establishment
- **Planting check** — is the planting plan followed? ★
- **Spacing check** (F-P15) — lay the spacing string along a row:

  | Crop (examples) | Roughly between plants | Check |
  |---|---|---|
  | Spinach / Swiss chard | 30 cm | |
  | Cabbage | 40–50 cm | |
  | Onion | 10 cm | |
  | Beetroot / carrot (thinned) | 5–10 cm | |
  | Tomato | 50 cm | |
  | Maize | 30 cm in rows 75–90 cm apart | |

  Spacing varies by variety and bed type — use the Course 1 planting plan and the seed packet.
  Record: "row of 10 cabbages at ~20 cm — too close; agreed to thin/transplant".
- Seedlings: sturdy, green, hardened before transplanting ★
- Mulch on every bed (depth: a hand's width of loose grass is a good start) ★
- Beds shaped to hold water (trench bed, keyhole) ★; A-frame used for any slope ★

### Month 3 · Growth
- Plant health; pests found early; the ladder followed (healthy soil and mixed plants first) ★
- Natural spray made correctly and safely (kept from children, eyes) ★
- Liquid manure / worm farm going ★
- Water: tank levels, bottle drip, greywater used safely (no oily/bleach water on food)
- Succession: seedlings ready for the next planting?
- Records: diary / Field Journal entries this month

### Month 4 · Harvest
- Harvesting right (outer leaves of spinach; picking at the right size)
- **Weighing and recording** harvests (kg, not bunches) ★
- The family plate: is the garden feeding the household? Who eats first? ★
- Seed plants chosen and marked (the best, not the leftovers) ★

### Month 5 · Selling surplus
- What is surplus after the family eats? Who buys? (neighbours, spaza, school/crèche, church)
- Price: costs known (money out), local price checked ★
- Money book or app Records (Sold / Spent) used; slips kept ★
- Invoices for any buyer who pays later (duplicate book or Invoice builder)
- Credit sales written down with a date to pay

### Month 6 · Review and next season
- Seed saved, dried, labelled, jarred; germination test ★
- Before/after photos from the baseline spot
- Records summary (paper month-end summaries, or app Reports)
- Compare with the baseline and the "what would make you proud" answer
- Next season plan: rotation, what to grow more/less of (F-P15)

### Skill result — how to decide
| Result | Means |
|---|---|
| **Demonstrated during this visit** | The farmer did it alone, correctly, while you watched |
| **Needs further support** | Did it with help, or part of it, or not yet — plan the next step |
| **Not assessed** | You didn't watch the skill on this visit |

## 6. The traffic light, caseload arithmetic, a mentor's week, routes

### 6a. The traffic light (re-sort on the first working day of each month)

| Colour | Who | Contact |
|---|---|---|
| **R — Red: needs me soon** | Not started / failing; missed 2 contacts; crisis (theft, illness, drought loss); new; asked for help | Individual visit **every 2 weeks** + phone between |
| **A — Amber: on the way** | Growing; some skills *need further support*; patchy records | Individual visit **monthly** + cluster visit |
| **G — Green: going well** | Skills demonstrated; records kept; could host or help | Cluster visit **monthly** + phone check-in; invite to host/present |

- Nobody goes a calendar month without a contact.
- The colour is **private** — a planning tool, not a label.
- Move farmers **up** as well as down. Celebrate a farmer moving to green (privately, in words).
- A farmer who says "no more visits" is recorded as *withdrawn by choice* with the date —
  respect it; invite them to cluster visits anyway.

### 6b. Caseload arithmetic (for any number of farmers)

```
Individual visits needed per month  = (Red × 2) + (Amber × 1)
Cluster visits per month            = number of clusters
Phone check-ins per month           = Green (+ Red between visits)

Individual visits you can do        = field days × visits per day
   visits per day: 4–5 if farmers are close (one village)
                   3   if spread out (rural, walking)
                   2   on a day with a cluster visit
```

**Worked example — 40 farmers, 4 clusters of 10, 1 mentor, 5-day week:**

| Item | Number |
|---|---|
| Red 8 × 2 | 16 visits |
| Amber 16 × 1 | 16 visits |
| **Needed** | **32 individual visits + 4 cluster visits + ~16 phone check-ins** |
| Available: 4 field days × 4 weeks, with 1 cluster day a week (2 visits) + 3 visit days (3 visits) | 11 a week → **44 a month** |
| Spare | ~12 visits a month for new red cases, repeat visits, a field day, bad weather |

If needed > available: make clusters bigger, move amber farmers to "cluster + phone" for one
month, ask a lead farmer to co-host, or **tell your supervisor** (with this sum). Don't quietly
do shorter visits.

### 6c. A mentor's week (40 farmers, 4 clusters: A, B, C, D)

Week 1 of the month shown. The cluster-visit day moves: week 1 = A, week 2 = B, week 3 = C,
week 4 = D. Each weekday is "the cluster's day", so travel stays short.

| Day | Morning | Afternoon | End of day |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Mon · Cluster A** | 08:30 set-up · **09:00–11:30 cluster visit A** (host rotates) | 12:30 visit A-red · 14:00 visit A-amber | Record/sync; update lights (15 min) |
| **Tue · Cluster B** | 08:30 visit B-red · 10:00 visit B-amber | 12:30 visit B-amber | Record/sync |
| **Wed · Cluster C** | 08:30 visit C-red · 10:00 visit C-red | 12:30 visit C-amber | Record/sync |
| **Thu · Cluster D** | 08:30 visit D-red · 10:00 visit D-amber | 12:30 visit D-amber | Record/sync |
| **Fri · Office & phone** | 08:30–10:30 phone check-ins (8–10 green farmers × 10 min) | 11:00 buddy/peer meeting or supervisor · 13:00 records, next week's plan, SMS confirmations | Rest |

- Visits start 08:30, 10:00, 12:30, 14:00: 60 min each + travel/walk + buffer.
- Friday is protected: records, phones, planning and your own learning. If a field day falls
  on a Saturday, take a weekday off in lieu (programme policy — check).
- Avoid SASSA grant pay days, funeral Saturdays, and market days for cluster visits — farmers
  won't come. Ask each cluster which days work for women with children.

### 6d. Route planning
1. Mark every farmer on a map: the app Map/People & gardens (garden pins) or a hand-drawn
   village sketch with house numbers or landmarks ("blue gate after the church").
2. Draw each cluster as a circle. Start the day at the **farthest** farmer and work back towards
   home or the taxi route.
3. Put reds first in the day (energy and time for them); ambers after.
4. Walk where you can; share programme transport by cluster; note taxi fares in your claim.
5. Keep a "wet-weather route" (roads passable in rain) and a list of farmers you can phone
   instead if a road is closed.
6. Always tell someone your route for the day (supervisor or buddy) — see §11.

## 7. The season plan (6 months, month by month)

Example: **Course 1 ends in August, summer-rainfall area** (KZN, Mpumalanga, Free State).
Shift the months to your area's planting calendar (app Calendar, the Department of Agriculture
local office, or the farmers themselves). Frost areas: wait to plant tender crops until the
local last-frost date has passed.

| Month | Stage | Focus of contacts | Cluster visit theme | App exercise (app-track ON) / Paper twin |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1 · Sep** | **Baseline & set-up** | First visit to everyone (75–90 min); consent; track A/P; baseline photos; 90-day plan | Draw your map; A-frame; record book start | Profile & consent; Map garden / paper garden map; first record-book page |
| **2 · Oct** | **Establishment** | Planting and spacing check; seedlings; mulch; water storage | Spacing & planting; seedling trays | Garden Survey → Crop Plan / paper bed plan |
| **3 · Nov** | **Growth** | Pests early; liquid manure; water through heat; succession sowing | Pest walk; natural spray; liquid manure | Field Journal / garden diary |
| **4 · Dec** | **Harvest** | Weighing, recording, family plate; mark seed plants. **Field day** (first harvest) — before the holidays | Weigh & record; family plate | Log harvest (Records → Picked; Lima Vision optional) / harvest page |
| **5 · Jan** | **Selling surplus** | Prices, costs, buyers, invoices, money book | Price a bunch; invoice; money book | Records → Sold / Spent + scan a till slip; Invoice builder / money book, slip envelope, duplicate invoice book |
| **6 · Feb** | **Review & next season** | Seed saving; before/after photos; records summary; plan next season; exit or continue | Seed jars; before/after; next plan. **Field day** (season review) | Read a report (Reports) / month-end summaries |

- **December holidays:** many households travel; plan the field day early December; phone
  check-ins over the break; agree who waters.
- **Month 6 outcome record** for each farmer: baseline vs now (beds, skills ✔, records kept,
  actions closed with evidence, the farmer's own words on what changed). This is the
  evidence the programme and funder can trust — it is not a claim of income.

## 10. Looking after mentors

- **Buddy observation:** once a month a buddy mentor comes on one visit with the observer
  checklist (day-2.md) and gives two stars and a wish. Swap the next month.
- **Monthly peer meeting (2 h):** all mentors in an area. (1) Wins — one each; (2) a case
  clinic on one hard situation (M-P15 four steps); (3) numbers — lights, visits, actions closed
  with evidence; (4) one skill refresh from the "want more help" list (Day 3 evaluation);
  (5) plan next month's cluster themes together.
- **Supervisor check-in:** monthly, on the numbers *and* on how the mentor is coping.
- **Self-care:** you will hear hard stories. Talk to your supervisor or buddy (not the village).
  Take your days off. You are a mentor, not a rescuer.

## 11. Safety and ethics

### The mentor's promise (M-P02)
1. **I listen first.**
2. **I promise only what I can do.** No promises of inputs, fencing, tanks, money, jobs or
   stipends. Write requests under *Support requested* and pass them on — and say so.
3. **I keep your story private.** Collect only what the programme needs; store forms safely;
   share with named people only (POPIA). Never discuss one farmer with another.
4. **I take no gifts that change how I help.** Tea and water: yes, with thanks. Money,
   livestock, produce to take home, favours: kindly no. Never ask for anything. Never charge.
5. **I keep you safe** — and I report harm.

### Boundaries
- Visit in daylight, at agreed times. Tell your buddy/supervisor your route.
- Meet in the garden or yard, in view of others — not alone indoors with one person.
- Don't lend or borrow money with farmers. Don't sell them things.
- Don't take sides in family or community disputes; refer.
- Relationships: no romantic or sexual relationships with farmers you mentor. Report any
  pressure or harassment to your supervisor.
- Programme checks (e.g. attendance for a stipend scheme) are done honestly; you don't
  change records for anyone.

### Gender and inclusion
- Women often do the garden work but men may speak for the household: speak with the person
  who does the work. Ask "Who works in the garden? Who decides what is sold?"
- Arrange visit times that suit caregivers. Offer women-only clusters if women prefer.
- Youth: pair with elders; recognise their phone skills (A+P track).
- Disability: adapt the walk (farmer sits and directs) and tasks.

### Safeguarding (children and adults at risk)
- **Children:** no photos of children (unless the guardian consents in writing and the programme
  allows); never be alone with a child; no physical contact beyond a greeting.
- **If you see or hear of harm** (abuse, neglect, violence): stay calm, listen, don't
  investigate or promise secrecy, don't confront anyone. Tell your supervisor the **same day**
  and follow your organisation's safeguarding policy. Record only "referred to ___ on ___".
- **Danger now:** SAPS **10111**. Childline **116** (free). GBV Command Centre **0800 428 428**
  (24 h). (Check these numbers are current and add your local clinic, social worker and police
  station to the back of this book.)
- Hunger or illness: refer to the clinic, local social development office or SASSA office
  (grant applications). Don't give money from your pocket.

### Photos and data
- Consent before every photo; respect the green sticker / "no photos" tick.
- Photos of gardens, not faces, unless the farmer agreed.
- App: records sync to the programme; don't keep copies on your personal WhatsApp longer than
  needed. Paper: forms in a closed folder, handed in weekly.

## 12. Hard situations

Four steps for every hard moment (M-P15): **Stay calm · Listen · What can *they* do? · Who else
can help?**

| Situation | What to do | What not to do |
|---|---|---|
| **Drought / no water** | Protect the most important bed and seedlings first; thick mulch; water early/late at the roots; greywater (no bleach/oil); shade with branches or shade cloth; bottle drip; delay sowing until rain; record the loss; support request to programme. Move to red. | Promise a tank or water delivery; blame the farmer |
| **Theft** | Listen; offer to help report to SAPS for a case number (their choice); neighbour watch in the cluster; less attractive crops near the road; tools stored with a neighbour; replant plan | Accuse anyone; name suspects in the record |
| **Conflict** (group garden, family, neighbours) | Hear each side separately; meet together with the group; agree a written rule (water roster, bed allocation); involve the group leader, traditional leader or ward committee if needed | Take sides; carry messages between people |
| **Drop-out** | One visit or call with no blame: "We miss you. What's happening?" Offer a smaller goal (one bed, one keyhole); a neighbour buddy; invite to the cluster visit; accept a "no" and record *withdrawn by choice* | Chase repeatedly; shame in front of the group |
| **Dependency** ("we wait for inputs") | Be honest (promise 2); start with what they have: saved seed, seed swap at cluster, kraal manure, grass mulch, 2 ℓ bottles; visit a green farmer who started with nothing; celebrate the first small step | Bring inputs from your own pocket; threaten |
| **Pests/disease beyond you** | Photo; ask Lima (app ON) and check the answer with your supervisor or the local extension office; ask the cluster; don't guess a chemical | Recommend poisons |
| **Farmer money taken / GBV** | Listen and believe; share helpline numbers privately; supervisor same day; safeguarding route | Confront the partner; record details in the visit form |
| **You feel unsafe** | Leave politely; phone your buddy/supervisor; report | Stay "to finish the form" |

**Record the hard situation in general words** ("water loss — drought"; "referred to
supervisor"), never private details. Private information does not go on the visit form.
