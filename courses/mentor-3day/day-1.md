## M-D1-S0 · 08:30–09:00 · Opening circle and consent (30 min)

**By the end, learners can:** name everyone in their group and agree how we work together.

**Poster:** F-P01 (as a model — leave space for our own) · **Slides:** M-D1-01 → M-D1-02
**Materials:** register (forms.md §1), POPIA consent forms (§2), name tags, green stickers,
flipchart for the class agreement, 5 role cards per group (toolkit §4).

| Time | Step | Teacher talk? |
|---|---|---|
| 08:30 | Register at the door as people arrive; consent form read aloud once the circle sits | 3 min |
| 08:38 | Welcome, call and response agreed | 2 min |
| 08:40 | *Mirror* energiser in pairs (toolkit §6) — "the listening game" | 1 min |
| 08:45 | Name round: "My name, where I work, how many farmers I walk with (or will)" | 1 min |
| 08:52 | Class agreement: 5 rules from the room, written on flipchart | 1 min |
| 08:57 | Form groups of 5; hand out role cards; today's promise | 2 min |

### Say / ask / do
1. DO: Register at the door. Consent form: read it aloud in the local language; learners tick
   and sign. Green sticker on name tag for "no photos".
2. SAY: "Welcome. You already help farmers. This course is about helping **well**."
3. DO: Agree the call and response in the local language (e.g. "*Imbewu!*" — "*Iyamila!*").
4. DO: *Mirror* — pairs face each other; one moves slowly, the other copies. Swap after 1 min.
   ASK: "What did you have to do to copy well?" *(watch closely, go slowly, pay attention)*.
   SAY: "That is listening with your eyes. We will need it all day."
5. ASK round: name, where you work, how many farmers. Write the numbers on the flipchart
   — add them up. SAY: "Together this room walks with ___ farmers." (Keep the number: Day 3 uses it.)
6. ASK: "What rules will help us learn together?" Take 5. Point to F-P01 only as an example.
   Expect: phones on silent, respect, start on time, everyone speaks, no mocking language.
   ADD if missing: "What a farmer tells us about their home stays in this room."
7. GROUPS: Make 5 mixed groups (extension officers with lead farmers; women and men; young and
   older). Name them after plants. Hand out role cards. Leader, Timekeeper, Materials,
   Recorder, Reporter.

### Key messages
- We learn by doing, together, in the same groups for 3 days.
- What farmers tell us is private.

### If things go wrong
- Many latecomers: start anyway; they sign at tea.
- Someone refuses consent: fine — green sticker, no photos, no questions asked.

### Language & inclusion notes
- Extension officers and lead farmers may feel a status gap. Mix them on purpose and say so:
  "Here, everyone is a learner. The lead farmer may know the village better than the officer."

**✂ 09:00 start:** cut the name-round numbers (step 5) and do the class agreement in 3 min.

## M-D1-S1 · 09:00–10:15 · Walk beside (75 min)

**By the end, learners can:** explain the difference between a mentor and an inspector, and
name the five promises a mentor keeps.

**Posters:** M-P01, M-P02 · **Slides:** M-D1-03 → M-D1-06
**Materials (per group of 5):** flipchart sheet, 2 markers, 10 sticky notes. For the room:
a hat and a clipboard (inspector props), a hoe (mentor prop).
**Room:** circle, then group stations.

| Time | Step | Teacher talk? |
|---|---|---|
| 09:00 | Hook — two-minute role-play: the inspector visit vs the mentor visit | 4 min |
| 09:06 | Pairs: "The best helper I ever had" story (3 min each way) | 1 min |
| 09:15 | Groups: what did your best helper *do*? — list on sticky notes | 1 min |
| 09:30 | Gallery: groups sort sticky notes onto M-P01 (walk beside / in front / behind) | 3 min |
| 09:45 | The mentor's promise (M-P02): groups test 5 promises with 5 "what would you do?" cards | 6 min |
| 10:05 | Three-question debrief + key messages | 5 min |

### Say / ask / do
1. DO (facilitators, 2 min each): **Visit A — the inspector.** Co-facilitator is the farmer,
   sitting. Lead walks in with a clipboard and hat, does not greet properly, says "Why is this
   bed dry? You did not do what we taught. I will write this down. Next time I want to see
   mulch." Walks off. **Visit B — the mentor.** Greets, asks after the family, "Show me your
   garden — what are you proud of?", listens, kneels at the dry bed, "What do you think is
   happening here?", agrees one thing, "When shall I come back to see?"
2. ASK: "Which visit would make you want to try again?" *(B)* "Why?" *(respect, listened,
   asked, farmer decides, came back)*. "Which one do we often do?" *(honest answers welcome — A
   is common when we are rushed and counting visits)*.
3. PAIRS: "Tell your partner about the best helper you ever had — a teacher, a gogo, a
   coach, a boss. What did they do that helped you?" 3 min each. Timekeeper calls swap.
4. GROUPS: each person writes or draws 2 things their helper **did** on sticky notes (one idea
   per note). Recorder can write for others.
5. DO: Reveal M-P01. SAY: "Three ways to walk with someone: **in front** (I lead, you follow),
   **behind** (I push, I check), **beside** (we walk together, you choose the path)."
   GROUPS: put each note on the flipchart under *in front / beside / behind*.
   Most will land under *beside*. ASK: "When is *in front* right?" *(teaching a new skill,
   danger, safety)*. "When is *behind* right?" *(almost never — maybe a deadline for a
   programme form)*.
6. DO: Reveal M-P02 **The mentor's promise**. Read the 5 promises with the room:
   1. **I listen first.** 2. **I promise only what I can do** (no promises of inputs, money,
   jobs, stipends). 3. **I keep your story private** (POPIA; only what the programme needs).
   4. **I take no gifts that change how I help** (a cup of tea is hospitality; a chicken is
   not). 5. **I keep you safe** (and I report harm to a child or adult).
7. GROUPS: each group gets one "What would you do?" card (below). 8 minutes. Reporter answers.
8. DEBRIEF — three questions (toolkit §7), then key messages.

**"What would you do?" cards**
| Card | Situation | Expected good answer |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Farmer: "If you bring us a JoJo tank, we will work hard." | Thank them; say honestly you can't promise a tank; ask what water they can catch now (roof, drums); write the request in *Support requested* for the programme — no promise |
| 2 | Farmer gives you a live chicken at the end of the visit | Thank them warmly; refuse kindly ("my job does not allow it; your hens are your savings"); accept water or tea |
| 3 | A neighbour asks you what the farmer's husband said on your last visit | "What people tell me stays with them. Ask them yourself." |
| 4 | A child at the homestead has bruises and says she is not allowed at school | Stay calm; do not investigate; tell your supervisor the same day; follow the programme safeguarding route (visit-playbook.md §11). Child in danger now → SAPS 10111 / Childline 116 |
| 5 | The farmer wants you to tell the programme they planted 5 beds; you saw 2 | Record what you saw; talk with the farmer about why; the record must be true. Help them plan the next 3 |

### Debrief (the three questions)
1. What did you see in the two visits? 2. Why does *beside* work better with adults? *(adults
learn when they choose, when it links to their own life, when they are respected)* 3. What
will you do differently on your next visit?

### Key messages
- **Walk beside:** the farmer chooses the path; you bring questions, skills and company.
- Adults learn when **they** decide it matters.
- **Promise only what you can do.** Broken promises end trust.
- What you see is what you write. True records protect the farmer and you.

### If things go wrong
- Groups argue about gifts ("our culture is to give"): honour it. The line is *what changes how
  I help*. A cup of tea: yes. Something with money value: kindly no.
- An officer says "our job *is* inspection (compliance)": agree some programme checks exist
  (e.g. SEF participant attendance). Say: "Do the check honestly and quickly, then **switch hats**
  to mentor."

### Language & inclusion notes
- Let pairs tell stories in their own language. Recorders may draw.
- Safeguarding can be upsetting. Say at the start: "You may step out if you need to."

**✂ cut if short:** the *behind* discussion in step 5. **✂ 09:00 start:** do only cards 1, 2, 4.

## M-D1-S2 · 10:35–12:30 · Listen and ask (115 min)

**By the end, learners can:** show active listening, turn a closed question into an open one,
and start a visit with what is working.

**Posters:** M-P03, M-P04 · **Slides:** M-D1-07 → M-D1-10 · **Clip:** M-ANI-01 (optional)
**Materials (per group of 5):** 1 set of 10 question cards (closed questions, below), 3
observer strips (listening checklist), pencils. For the room: a ball or rolled sock.
**Room:** group stations; triads spread out so they can hear each other.

| Time | Step | Teacher talk? |
|---|---|---|
| 10:35 | Hook — "the bad listener" game in pairs | 3 min |
| 10:45 | M-P03 — listen with ears, eyes and heart: what it looks like | 5 min |
| 10:52 | **Listening triads**, 3 rounds × 8 min (speaker · listener · observer) | 3 min |
| 11:20 | Debrief triads | 4 min |
| 11:28 | M-P04 — the question ladder; demo with one closed question | 5 min |
| 11:35 | Groups: climb the ladder with 10 closed question cards | 2 min |
| 11:55 | **Start with what's working** — fishbowl: two visit openings | 5 min |
| 12:15 | Three-question debrief, key messages, take home | 3 min |

### Say / ask / do
1. PAIRS: A tells B "what I did last weekend". B must **not listen**: look away, check phone,
   interrupt. 1 minute. Then swap. ASK: "How did it feel?" *(angry, small, stopped talking)*.
   SAY: "Farmers feel this when we are in a hurry."
2. DO: Reveal M-P03. Point to the three parts:
   - **Ears** — let them finish; count to 3 before you speak; say back what you heard
     ("So the water runs out in the second week…").
   - **Eyes** — look at them and at what they show you; sit or squat at their level; phone away.
   - **Heart** — notice feelings ("You sound tired of this.") without fixing them straight away.
   SAY: "Say it back, then ask. Don't jump to advice."
3. GROUPS → TRIADS (a group of 5 makes a triad + a pair; the pair joins with the facilitator
   as observer, or does a 4-person round with 2 observers).
   Roles: **Speaker** talks about a real problem in their own garden or work (not private
   family matters). **Listener** only listens, says back, and asks one open question.
   **Observer** ticks the listening strip:

   | Observer strip | ✔ |
   |---|---|
   | Listener let the speaker finish | |
   | Said back what they heard | |
   | Asked an open question | |
   | Gave no advice | |
   | Body: facing, level, phone away | |

   Round = 5 min talk + 3 min observer feedback (*two stars and a wish*, toolkit §8). Rotate 3×.
4. DEBRIEF: ASK "Which was hardest?" *(giving no advice!)*. "What did the speaker feel?"
   *(heard, found own answer)*. SAY: "When you stop giving advice, the farmer starts thinking."
5. DO: Reveal M-P04 **the question ladder**. Four rungs, bottom to top:
   1. **Closed** — yes/no: "Did you water?"
   2. **Open** — "How do you water the beds?"
   3. **Deeper** — "What happens to the water after you pour it?"
   4. **Farmer's own plan** — "What could you try? … What else?"
   DEMO: take "Are your seedlings healthy?" and climb it with the room shouting the next rung.
   Open questions start with **What, How, Where, When, Who, Tell me about…** "Why" can
   sound like blame — soften it: "What made you choose…?"
6. GROUPS: each group gets 10 closed-question cards. Recorder writes/draws the climbed ladder
   for each on the flipchart. Then each person practises asking one aloud in the local language.

   **Closed question cards:** Did you make compost? · Is the tank full? · Did you plant
   at the right spacing? · Are you keeping records? · Do you have pests? · Did you save seed? ·
   Did you sell anything? · Is your family eating from the garden? · Are you using the
   A-frame? · Will you come to the cluster visit?

7. FISHBOWL — **start with what's working**. Two volunteers in the centre, farmer and mentor.
   Round 1: mentor opens with "What problems do you have?" (3 min). Round 2: mentor opens with
   "What is going well in your garden? Show me what you're proud of." then "What helped that
   work?" then "Where else could that work?" (3 min). The circle watches.
   ASK: "What changed in the farmer?" *(energy, pride, she explains more, she finds her own
   solution)*. SAY: "This is called a **strength-based** start. We build from what the farmer
   already does well. Then the problem is easier to face."
8. DEBRIEF and take home.

### Debrief (the three questions)
1. What did you notice when you only listened? 2. Why do open questions help adults learn?
*(they think, they own the answer, you learn what they know)* 3. Take home: "Tonight, ask
someone at home three open questions and only say back what you hear."

### Key messages
- **Listen first.** Say it back. Count to 3 before advice.
- **Climb the ladder:** closed → open → deeper → farmer's own plan. Then ask "What else?"
- **Start with what's working.** Pride opens the door to change.
- The best answer is the one the farmer finds.

### If things go wrong
- Speakers bring very private problems (debt, marriage): SAY gently, "Choose a garden or work
  problem for practice." Remind everyone of the privacy rule.
- Everyone gives advice in the triads: stop the room, ask observers "What did you tick?"
  Then re-run one round with the rule "no advice at all".
- Only English-speakers talk: run the triads in home languages. Open questions translate well.

### Language & inclusion notes
- In isiZulu, "*Ungangitshela kabanzi…*" (tell me more about…) is a useful open opener; ask the
  room for equivalents in Sesotho/siSwati and write them on the flipchart beside M-P04.
- Some cultures avoid direct eye contact with elders. "Eyes" means attention, not staring —
  looking at what the farmer shows you is enough.

**✂ cut if short:** the fishbowl round 1 (do only the strength-based opening). Never cut the triads.
**✂ 09:00 start:** triads 3 × 7 min.

## M-D1-S3 · 13:15–14:30 · Coach a skill: I do · We do · You do (75 min)

**By the end, learners can:** coach a Course 1 skill in three steps and judge whether a farmer
has *demonstrated* it or *needs further support*.

**Posters:** M-P05 (+ F-P06 A-frame, F-P14 seedlings, F-P15 spacing) · **Slides:** M-D1-11 → M-D1-13
**Clips:** M-ANI-02 (I do · We do · You do, optional) · M-ANI-03 (A-frame, reused from the app)
**Materials (per group of 5):** one skill kit each — Group 1 & 2: A-frame kit (3 poles, string,
stone); Group 3: spacing string (knotted every 30 cm and 50 cm) + 4 pegs; Group 4: seedling tray,
mix, seed; Group 5: record book + a sample week of harvests. Skill coaching sheet (below) ×5.
**Room:** outside, the role-play garden and flat ground.

| Time | Step | Teacher talk? |
|---|---|---|
| 13:15 | Energiser *Compost Heap* (toolkit §6) | 1 min |
| 13:20 | Hook — facilitator "teaches" the A-frame by talking only; ask one learner to build it | 4 min |
| 13:27 | M-P05 — I do · We do · You do + the adult learning cycle | 6 min |
| 13:35 | Groups: plan a 12-minute coaching of your skill | 2 min |
| 13:45 | Coaching rounds: 2 rounds × 15 min (coach · farmer · observers) | 2 min |
| 14:17 | Three-question debrief, result: demonstrated / needs further support | 5 min |

### Say / ask / do
1. DO: Energiser.
2. DO: Talk for 90 seconds about how to build an A-frame — no touching, no pictures. Then ASK a
   volunteer to build one from what you said. They struggle. ASK the room: "Why was that hard?"
   *(only heard it, too fast, didn't see or try)*.
3. DO: Reveal M-P05. Three pictures:
   - **I do** — the mentor shows it once, slowly, saying each step (2–3 min). Farmer watches.
   - **We do** — farmer and mentor do it together; farmer's hands, mentor helps (5 min).
   - **You do** — farmer does it alone; mentor **watches and says nothing** until the end (5 min).
   Then **Talk**: "What went well? What was hard? How will you remember it?" (2 min).
   SAY: "Adults learn by doing, then thinking about it, then trying again. On a visit you have
   time for **one** skill. Choose the one the farmer chose."
4. GROUPS: each group plans a 12-minute coaching of their skill with the coaching sheet.
   Leader picks the first coach.
5. ROUNDS (2 × 15 min): one learner is the **mentor-coach**, one is the **farmer** (acts as a
   farmer who has not done it before), three **observers** fill the sheet. 12 min coaching +
   3 min two stars and a wish. Rotate so 2 different people coach. (The others coach on Day 2–3.)

   **Skill coaching sheet**
   | Did the coach… | ✔ |
   |---|---|
   | Ask what the farmer already knows first | |
   | Show it once, slowly (I do) | |
   | Do it together, farmer's hands (We do) | |
   | Stay quiet while the farmer did it alone (You do) | |
   | Ask the farmer to say the steps back | |
   | Decide: **Demonstrated** / **Needs further support** — and say why | |

6. DEBRIEF. Then SAY: "In the visit record, the skill result is only **Demonstrated during
   this visit** if the farmer did it alone, correctly. If you helped, it is **Needs further
   support** — and that is not a failure. It is the next visit's plan."
   Link: the farmer skills checklist from Course 1 (forms.md §4: ✔ ◐ ✘) uses the same idea.

### Debrief (the three questions)
1. What did the coach do? 2. Why is "You do" silent? *(the farmer must find the steps
themselves; if we talk, we do it for them)* 3. Which skill will your farmers need first this
season? *(usually spacing, seedlings, compost, mulch)*

### Key messages
- **I do · We do · You do** — then talk.
- The farmer's hands do the work; the mentor's hands stay in the pockets during *You do*.
- **Demonstrated** = alone and correct. Anything else = needs further support, and a plan.
- One skill per visit is enough.

### If things go wrong
- Coaches keep talking during *You do*: observers hold up a hand. Re-run the last 3 minutes.
- Not enough kits: groups 1–2 share one A-frame kit by rotating (carousel) while the other does
  the record-book skill. Never demo-only.
- Rain: record-book and seedling-tray skills indoors; A-frame with a tabletop model.

### Language & inclusion notes
- Mentors with limited mobility coach the seedling tray or record book seated.
- Encourage coaching in the farmer's language — practise exactly that.

**✂ cut if short:** round 2 (keep one round, rotate roles on Day 2). **✂ 09:00 start:** hook 4 min.

## M-D1-S4 · 14:45–15:40 · The action agreement and evidence of change (55 min)

**By the end, learners can:** write an action agreement (what · who · by when · how we'll know)
and say what evidence closes it.

**Posters:** M-P06, M-P16 · **Slides:** M-D1-14 → M-D1-16
**Materials (per group of 5):** 5 blank action-agreement cards (A6, below), 2 weak agreements
printed, pencils. Room: one pile of before/after photo pairs (print from M-IMG-06) or drawn pairs.

| Time | Step | Teacher talk? |
|---|---|---|
| 14:45 | Hook — "I'll try to mulch" vs "I will mulch the 2 front beds with grass by Friday" | 3 min |
| 14:50 | M-P06 — four questions of an action agreement | 4 min |
| 14:57 | Groups: fix 2 weak agreements | 1 min |
| 15:07 | Pairs: make a real agreement from this morning's triad problem | 1 min |
| 15:17 | M-P16 — "show me what changed": evidence game with photo pairs | 4 min |
| 15:32 | Debrief + key messages | 2 min |

### Say / ask / do
1. ASK: "Which of these will happen? A: 'I'll try to mulch.' B: 'I will mulch the two front
   beds with dry grass by Friday; my son will cut the grass; my mentor will see it on the 14th.'"
   *(B)* "Why?" *(clear, small, a person, a date, someone checks)*.
2. DO: Reveal M-P06. Four questions, one per corner:
   - **What?** One small, doable action (the farmer's words). Small wins build confidence.
   - **Who?** A named person — the farmer, a family member, the mentor, the group.
   - **By when?** A date — before the next contact.
   - **How will we know?** What we will **see** (evidence): mulch on the beds, 3 entries in the
     book, a photo, a tray of seedlings.
   SAY: "In the app these are *Agreed action*, *Responsible person* and *Follow-up date*. On
   paper they are the bottom box of the visit form. The farmer keeps a copy — the card."
3. GROUPS fix two weak agreements: (a) "Farmer must improve the soil." (b) "Mentor will bring
   seeds." For (b) ASK: "Is that a promise we can keep? Who owns the garden?" *(turn it into
   the farmer's action: "Thandi will save seed from 3 best bean plants by March; mentor will
   check the jar on the next visit.")*
4. PAIRS: take the problem from the morning triad. The listener asks the four questions; the
   speaker answers; write it on a card. Swap.
5. DO: Reveal M-P16. Hand each group 4 photo/drawing pairs (before/after). ASK: "Which pair
   *proves* the action was done?" Discuss: a photo of a bed *with* mulch, taken on the date;
   the book with entries; the farmer showing you. SAY the rule: **"An action is only closed
   with a date and evidence of what changed."** In the app, *Completed on* and *Completion
   evidence* must be filled. On paper, write the date and what you saw.
6. ASK: "What if the action was *not* done?" *(no blame: "What got in the way? What would make
   it possible? Keep it, change it, or drop it?" — a new agreement)*.

### Debrief (the three questions)
1. What makes an agreement strong? 2. Why do we close with evidence, not a tick? *(it proves
change to the farmer, the programme and the funder; it shows the farmer their own progress)*
3. Take home: write one action agreement for yourself for tomorrow.

### Key messages
- **What · Who · By when · How we'll know.**
- One small action the farmer chose beats five the mentor chose.
- **Show me what changed:** close with a date and evidence.
- Not done is information, not failure. Ask what got in the way.

### If things go wrong
- Learners write the mentor as "who" for everything: ASK "Whose garden is it?"
- Dates far away: "Before the next contact — usually 2 to 4 weeks."

### Language & inclusion notes
- The card can be drawn: a picture of the action, a stick person, a calendar date circled.

**✂ cut if short:** the photo-pair game — show 2 pairs on the poster only.

**Action-agreement card (A6, leave with the farmer; print 2-up carbon or write twice)**
```
WHAT will we do? ______________________
WHO? ____________   BY WHEN? __ / __
HOW WILL WE KNOW? (what we will see) __________
Mentor: ________  Next contact: __ / __  Phone: ______
```
