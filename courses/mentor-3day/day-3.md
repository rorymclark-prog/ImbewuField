## M-D3-S1 · 09:00–10:15 · Traffic light and caseload (75 min)

**By the end, learners can:** sort their farmers into red, amber and green, calculate whether
their caseload fits their month, and draw a week plan.

**Posters:** M-P09, M-P10 · **Slides:** M-D3-01 → M-D3-05
**Materials (per group of 5):** 40 farmer cards (A7, pre-printed "case cards" — below), 3
coloured cards (red, amber/ochre, green — with R/A/G written too for black-and-white),
1 blank week grid (A3), a cluster map (A3, 4 villages drawn), calculator or phone.
Each learner: their own farmer list (brought from Day 2 closing) and a caseload sheet
(visit-playbook.md §9).

| Time | Step | Teacher talk? |
|---|---|---|
| 09:00 | Hook — "40 farmers, 20 working days. Can you visit everyone every week?" | 3 min |
| 09:05 | M-P09 — the traffic light: what makes a farmer red, amber, green | 5 min |
| 09:12 | Groups sort 40 case cards | 1 min |
| 09:27 | Caseload arithmetic on the board with the room | 6 min |
| 09:40 | M-P10 — a mentor's week; groups draw a week for their 40 farmers | 4 min |
| 10:00 | Gallery + three-question debrief; own caseload sheet started | 3 min |

### Say / ask / do
1. ASK: "Say you walk with 40 farmers. One visit takes an hour, plus travel. How many can you
   see in a day?" *(3–5)* "In a month of 20 working days?" "Can you see all 40 every week?"
   *(no: 40 × 4 weeks = 160 visits)* SAY: "So we choose. We choose fairly, with a rule."
2. DO: Reveal M-P09 **the traffic light**.
   - **Red — needs me soon** (visit every 2 weeks): garden not started or failing; missed 2
     contacts; no water; a crisis (theft, illness, drought loss); new to farming; asked for help.
   - **Amber — on the way** (individual visit monthly): garden growing; some skills *need
     further support*; records started but patchy.
   - **Green — going well** (cluster visit monthly + a phone check-in): skills demonstrated;
     records kept; could host or help a neighbour.
   SAY: "The light changes. Re-sort every month, on the same day. A green farmer can turn red
   after a hailstorm. And **no one is invisible**: green farmers still get a contact every month."
   SAY: "Red is not a punishment and green is not a prize. Don't tell farmers their colour.
   It is a planning tool for you."
3. GROUPS: sort the 40 case cards into R/A/G (10 min). Expect about **8 red, 16 amber, 16
   green** — any split is fine if the group can say why.
4. ARITHMETIC (with the room, on flipchart):
   ```
   Needed each month
     Red:   8 farmers × 2 visits      = 16 individual visits
     Amber: 16 farmers × 1 visit      = 16 individual visits
     Green: 16 farmers → cluster visit + phone check-in (0 individual)
     Clusters: 4 cluster visits a month (one per cluster, all 40 invited)
     Phone: 16 green check-ins × 10 min ≈ 3 hours
   Available each month (4 weeks)
     4 field days a week × 4 weeks  = 16 field days
     1 office/phone day a week      = 4 days (records, phone, meetings)
     Each field day: 2 individual visits + (once a week) a cluster visit,
                     or 4 individual visits on a day with no cluster visit
   ```
   Week pattern: 1 cluster day (cluster visit + 2 visits) + 3 visit days (3 visits each) = **11
   individual visits a week = 44 a month** — 32 needed, leaving ~12 spare for new red cases,
   repeat visits and field days. SAY: "Always leave space. Something always goes wrong."
   ASK: "What if your farmers are far apart?" *(3 visits a day; more phone; bigger clusters;
   pay transport by cluster)*. "What if you have 60?" *(more green farmers in clusters; lead
   farmers as helpers; tell your supervisor — don't pretend)*.
5. DO: Reveal M-P10 **a mentor's week** (visit-playbook.md §6 has the full timetable). GROUPS:
   on the A3 week grid, draw a week: which cluster on which day; which farmers; the route (use
   the cluster map: start at the farthest point and work back towards home or transport).
6. GALLERY and debrief. Then each learner starts their **own caseload sheet** with names or
   numbers of their real farmers (or Course 1 graduates they will be given): track (A/P/A+P),
   cluster, first colour guess.

**Case cards (sample of 12 of the 40 — full set in resources.md print list)**
| # | Case | Likely colour |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Planted all beds; mulched; records every week; neighbours ask her advice | Green |
| 2 | Missed the last 2 visits; phone off | Red |
| 3 | Beds planted; seedlings leggy; records started | Amber |
| 4 | House burgled, tools stolen last week | Red |
| 5 | Youth, keen, has not started — waiting for "the programme's seed" | Red |
| 6 | Garden growing; spacing too close; keen to learn | Amber |
| 7 | Elder, strong garden, no records (can't write) | Amber (paper track with grandchild, A+P) |
| 8 | Hosted last cluster visit, selling spinach to the spaza | Green |
| 9 | Tank empty, no rain for 5 weeks | Red |
| 10 | All skills demonstrated; wants to learn invoicing | Green (app/paper money exercise) |
| 11 | Garden fine; told you her husband took the harvest money | Amber — and a private conversation; see M-P15 |
| 12 | New to the area, joined late, no Course 1 | Red |

### Debrief (the three questions)
1. How did your group decide red, amber or green? 2. Why re-sort every month? 3. What is your
first step with your own list?

### Key messages
- **Red every 2 weeks · Amber monthly · Green cluster + phone.** Nobody goes a month without contact.
- **Do the sum** before the month starts. Leave spare time.
- Plan by **cluster and route**, not by name order.
- The colour is a planning tool, not a label for the farmer.

### If things go wrong
- Groups make everyone red: ASK "Can you visit 40 farmers every 2 weeks? Who could a cluster
  visit serve?"
- Maths anxiety: the Timekeeper uses the phone calculator; the room does the sum together.

### Language & inclusion notes
- Colours: always write R/A/G too (colour-blind learners, black-and-white copies).

**✂ cut if short:** the gallery (groups pin the grids up; look at lunch). **✂ 09:00 start:** sort for 10 min only.

## M-D3-S2 · 10:35–12:30 · Group visits: cluster visit, field day and phone check-in (115 min)

**By the end, learners can:** plan a cluster visit, plan a farmer field day, and make a 10-minute
phone check-in.

**Posters:** M-P11, M-P12, M-P13 · **Slides:** M-D3-06 → M-D3-11 · **Clip:** M-ANI-04 (NEW community network)
**Materials (per group of 5):** cluster-visit planner (visit-playbook.md §3), field-day planner
(§4), 2 phones (for the phone role-play, calls not needed — sit back to back), the season map M-P13.

| Time | Step | Teacher talk? |
|---|---|---|
| 10:35 | Hook — ask Day 2 cluster-visit trainees: "What worked?" | 2 min |
| 10:40 | M-P11 — the cluster visit: 6 parts, 2–3 hours, rotating host | 5 min |
| 10:47 | Groups plan a cluster visit for a stage of the season (M-P13) | 2 min |
| 11:07 | Each group runs a 10-min "slice" of their cluster visit with another group as farmers | 3 min |
| 11:32 | Farmer field day: when and how (M-P13 month 4 or 6) | 6 min |
| 11:42 | M-P12 — phone check-in: 5 questions in 10 minutes; back-to-back role-play in pairs | 5 min |
| 12:05 | Season map M-P13: where each kind of contact falls in 6 months | 5 min |
| 12:20 | Three-question debrief | 2 min |

### Say / ask / do
1. ASK the Day 2 cluster teams what worked. Link to M-P11.
2. DO: Reveal M-P11 **the cluster visit** (2–3 h, 8–12 farmers, host garden):
   1. **Welcome & register** (15 min) — consent for newcomers; last month's actions (thumbs).
   2. **Host's tour** (20–30 min) — host shows what works; others ask.
   3. **Try it together** (40–60 min) — 2–3 skill stations, farmers in 3s–4s.
   4. **Share circle** (20–30 min) — one success, one question; farmers answer first.
   5. **Agree** (10–15 min) — each farmer one action aloud; next host and date.
   6. **Individual moments** (15–30 min) — the mentor speaks one-to-one with any red or amber
      farmers present, while others eat/share seed.
   SAY: "Rotate the host every month. Choose hosts who do something well — not always the best
   garden. Hosting builds pride and leaders."
3. GROUPS plan a cluster visit for a stage: Group 1 month 1 (baseline — start the book, draw
   the map), Group 2 month 2 (spacing and planting), Group 3 month 3 (pests, mulch, liquid
   manure), Group 4 month 4 (harvest, weighing, the family plate), Group 5 month 5 (selling,
   pricing, invoices). Use the planner.
4. SLICES: each group runs the "Try it together" part for 10 min on another group (who act as
   farmers). Then 2-min two stars and a wish. Two rounds so every group runs and receives.
5. DO: Field day (see visit-playbook.md §4). SAY: "A field day is bigger — 20 to 60 people,
   once or twice a season: at first harvest (month 4) and at the season review (month 6).
   Farmers show farmers. Invite the ward councillor, the local extension office, the school,
   buyers. The mentor organises; farmers present."
   ASK: "What could farmers show at a field day?" *(a bed at the right spacing vs a crowded
   one; a compost heap; seed jars; a money book; a harvest weigh-in)*.
6. DO: Reveal M-P12 **the phone check-in** (10 minutes, voice or WhatsApp voice notes):
   1. **Greet** — "How are you and the family?"
   2. **Look back** — "Last time you planned to… how did it go?"
   3. **Show me** — "Can you send me a photo of the bed?" (WhatsApp, if they can and agree) or
      "Describe it to me."
   4. **What's next** — "What will you do this week?"
   5. **Date** — "I'll call again on… / see you at the cluster visit on…"
   PAIRS: sit back to back (so you cannot see faces — like a phone). 4 min each way.
   SAY: "Write it: in the app, record it as a visit with the note *phone check-in*; on paper,
   the phone log (visit-playbook.md §8). Airtime/data: use the programme's allowance; keep calls short;
   voice notes are cheaper than calls for many people."
7. DO: M-P13 **season map**. Show 6 months × contact types. Groups add their cluster visit to
   the right month.

### Debrief (the three questions)
1. What makes a cluster visit work? 2. Why do farmers teach farmers well? *(trust, same
conditions, seeing is believing)* 3. Which contact will you use most in month 1?

### Key messages
- **Cluster visit** monthly per cluster: host teaches, farmers try, everyone agrees an action.
- **Field day** once or twice a season: farmers show farmers and the community.
- **Phone check-in:** 5 questions, 10 minutes — keeps green farmers connected.
- Every contact ends with an action and a date.

### If things go wrong
- Groups plan lectures for the cluster visit: ASK "Where are the farmers' hands in your plan?"
- Phone role-play feels silly: that's fine — laughter helps. Keep it to 4 minutes.

### Language & inclusion notes
- Phone check-ins: many elders prefer a call to text; many youth prefer voice notes. Ask.
- Field days: plan for elders (chairs, shade), children (supervision), and toilets.

**✂ cut if short:** the second slice round; field day to 5 min (they have the playbook).
**✂ 09:00 start:** season map 3 min, slices 1 round.

### [APP] Coach the app records and money exercises · 13:15–14:30 (75 min)

**Teacher talk:** 20 min. **Materials:** each mentor's phone; 1 practice account per triad (or
the app's sample tour); 3 printed till slips (a real spaza receipt, a seed receipt, a hardware
slip — personal details removed); a sample harvest (a bunch of spinach + a kitchen scale).

| Time | Step | Teacher talk? |
|---|---|---|
| 13:15 | Energiser | 1 min |
| 13:20 | Hook: "She sold spinach for a month. Did she make money? How would she know?" | 3 min |
| 13:25 | Tour M-P14 app column: the month-by-month sequence (profile & consent → Map → Garden Survey/Crop Plan → Field Journal → harvest → Records Sold / Spent → till slip → Invoice builder → Reports) | 6 min |
| 13:35 | Round 1 (15 min): **Log a harvest** — weigh the spinach; record kilograms | 3 min |
| 13:50 | Round 2 (15 min): **Records → Sold / Spent + scan a till slip** | 3 min |
| 14:05 | Round 3 (15 min): **Invoice builder** — build and share an invoice for 5 bunches to a spaza | 2 min |
| 14:20 | Debrief + key messages | 2 min |

Each round: the triad rotates roles. The **coach** uses the script from app-track.md §4 for
that exercise (I do · We do · You do, on the farmer's own phone; the coach never takes the
phone during *You do*). The **observer** ticks the exercise's **check**.
Key cautions to teach:
- **Weigh before you record kilograms.** A bunch count is not a weight.
- **Check before you add:** look for the entry first, so nothing is counted twice.
- **Lima's reading of a till slip is a suggestion.** Compare it with the slip before saving.
- **An invoice is not a payment.** Mark it paid only when the money has arrived.
- **Another copy is not another sale:** reopen the saved invoice to share it again.
- Keep real customer and banking details private in group practice.

### [PAPER] Coach the paper records and money exercises · 13:15–14:30 (75 min)

**Teacher talk:** 20 min. **Materials:** per triad: a record book (A5 exercise book ruled as in
app-track.md §5), a money book, a duplicate invoice book (carbon, from a stationer), a
receipt/slip envelope; the same 3 till slips; the spinach and kitchen scale.

| Time | Step | Teacher talk? |
|---|---|---|
| 13:15 | Energiser | 1 min |
| 13:20 | Hook: "She sold spinach for a month. Did she make money? How would she know?" | 3 min |
| 13:25 | Tour M-P14 paper column: the month-by-month sequence (consent → garden map on paper → bed plan → garden diary → harvest page → money book → slip envelope → duplicate invoice book → month-end summary) | 6 min |
| 13:35 | Round 1 (15 min): **Harvest page** — weigh the spinach; write date, crop, kg, where it went | 3 min |
| 13:50 | Round 2 (15 min): **Money book** — money in / money out from the 3 slips; staple slips in the envelope | 3 min |
| 14:05 | Round 3 (15 min): **Duplicate invoice book** — write an invoice for 5 bunches; tear the top copy for the buyer | 2 min |
| 14:20 | Debrief + key messages | 2 min |

Same cautions: weigh first; one entry per event; check the slip; an invoice is not a
payment; the copy stays in the book. The paper month-end summary (app-track.md §5) does what
Reports does in the app.

### Debrief (both tracks)
1. What did the "farmer" do with their own hands? 2. Why coach records at all? *(the farmer
sees what the garden gives the family, and what it costs; buyers trust a written invoice;
the programme can show change)* 3. Which exercise will your farmers need first? *(harvest
record — month 4 at the latest)*

### Key messages
- Same exercise, same outcome — **app or paper**. Paper is never second best.
- The farmer's hands on the phone or pen. The coach watches.
- Weigh first, check before you add, an invoice is not a payment.
- One exercise per visit, 10–15 minutes, with a check.

### If things go wrong
- No signal: the app saves offline — show that. Or switch this triad to [PAPER].
- A mentor is not confident with the app: they coach on paper this round and observe the app.
- Learners raise loans or stokvels: park it for Course 4 (Farmer Money), or answer briefly.

### Language & inclusion notes
- Numbers can be pictures: a farmer who can't write can keep tally marks and draw the crop;
  a family helper writes the words (A+P track).
- Money is private. In practice, always use sample numbers, never a learner's own finances.

**✂ cut if short:** round 3 on a later cluster visit (not ideal). **✂ 09:00 start:** rounds 13 min.

## M-D3-S4 · 14:45–15:25 · When it gets hard + my season plan (40 min)

**By the end, learners can:** choose a response to a hard situation and draw their own first
month's plan.

**Posters:** M-P15, M-P02 · **Slides:** M-D3-16 → M-D3-17
**Materials (per group of 5):** 1 hard-situation card; each learner a season-plan sheet
(visit-playbook.md §9) and their caseload sheet.

| Time | Step | Teacher talk? |
|---|---|---|
| 14:45 | M-P15 — four steps for hard moments: stay calm · listen · what can *they* do · who else can help | 4 min |
| 14:50 | Case clinic: each group one card, 8 min; report 1 min each | 3 min |
| 15:05 | My season plan: fill month 1 and your first week (own caseload) | 1 min |
| 15:20 | Pairs: tell your buddy your first week; swap numbers — your buddy observes you once in month 1 | 2 min |

### Say / ask / do
1. DO: Reveal M-P15. Four steps: **Stay calm** (breathe, sit) · **Listen** (say back, no blame)
   · **What can they do?** (their options, their choice) · **Who else can help?** (neighbours,
   the group, programme office, local services — refer; don't carry it alone).
2. GROUPS: one card each. Recorder writes the four steps for the card. Reporter: 1 minute.

   | Card | Situation | Look for |
   |---|---|---|
   | **Drought** | No rain for 6 weeks, tank empty, seedlings dying; the farmer wants to give up | Protect what matters most (seedlings, one bed); mulch thickly; greywater; shade cloth or branches; water early/late; replant after rain; pause selling plans; report to programme (support request) |
   | **Theft** | Harvest and tools stolen at night | Listen; report to SAPS for a case number if they want; fence/lock/neighbour watch through the cluster; plant less-attractive crops at the gate; tool store at a neighbour's; no blame |
   | **Conflict** | Two members of a group garden fighting over water turns | Meet both separately first; then together with the group; agree a written water roster; involve the group leader or ward committee if needed; mentor stays neutral |
   | **Drop-out** | Farmer missed 3 contacts, garden weedy | Phone/visit once more, with no blame: "We miss you. What's happening?" Illness, work, grief, caregiving; offer a smaller goal (one bed); a neighbour buddy; respect a "no" and record it |
   | **Dependency** | "We'll plant when the programme brings seed, fertiliser and fencing" | Appreciate; be honest (M-P02: no promises); start with what they have (saved seed, kraal manure, seed swap at the cluster); visit a green farmer who started with nothing; one small action |
   | *Spare* **Safety** | Farmer says her partner takes her money and hits her | Listen, believe, don't push; don't confront the partner; share the GBV Command Centre number (0800 428 428) privately; tell your supervisor; follow the programme's safeguarding route; no details in the record beyond "referred" |

3. SEASON PLAN: learners fill month 1 of the season-plan sheet with their real farmers:
   which cluster first, first cluster-visit host, red farmers' visit dates, phone day.
4. PAIRS: buddy up — ideally a mentor from a nearby area. Swap numbers. Agree one observed
   visit in month 1 (the buddy uses the observer checklist).

### Key messages
- **Stay calm · Listen · What can they do? · Who else can help?**
- You are not alone and not the rescuer. Refer and record.
- Safety first: yours, the farmer's, children's.
- Leave today with month 1 planned and a buddy.

### If things go wrong
- A card touches a learner personally (GBV, theft): let them step out; talk privately.
- Groups want to "fix" everything with inputs: point to M-P02, promise 2.

**✂ cut if short:** card discussions without reports; season plan finished at home and sent
on WhatsApp by Friday.
