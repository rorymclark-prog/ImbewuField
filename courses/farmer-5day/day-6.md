## F-D6-S1 · 09:30–10:30 · What each animal gives, and what it needs (60 min)

**By the end, learners can:** for any animal on their land, say what it eats, what it produces,
and what it needs from them — including water, shelter, fencing and daily care.

**Poster:** F-P22 What each animal gives and needs · **Slides:** F-D6-02 → 05
**Materials:** flipchart per group, markers, 6 animal cards (chicken, duck, guinea fowl, goat,
rabbit, bees), prestik
**Room:** inside or under shade, groups of 5

| Time | Step | Teacher talk |
|---|---|---|
| 09:30 | Who already keeps animals? Hands up, no names | 1 |
| 09:35 | The three questions, on the poster | 5 |
| 09:50 | Groups: one animal card each, fill the three columns | 1 |
| 10:10 | Report back — one animal per Reporter | 1 |
| 10:20 | The catch: what leaves the farm, what comes in | 5 |

### Say / ask / do
1. **ASK:** "Who here keeps chickens? Ducks? Goats? Bees?" Hands only. **SAY:** "Good. Today
   we are not starting animals — most of you have them. We are putting them to work."
2. **DO:** Reveal **F-P22**. Three columns, and every animal gets all three:
   - **① What it eats here** — what your land can actually feed it.
   - **② What it gives** — eggs, meat, manure, honey, pest control, money.
   - **③ What it needs from you** — water, suitable feed, shelter, fencing, daily care.
3. **SAY:** "Column three is the one people forget. An animal is not a machine you switch on.
   Scraps alone may not be enough to feed it."
4. **GROUPS:** One animal card each. Fill all three columns on the flipchart — draw or write.
   20 minutes. **Recorder** draws, **Reporter** presents.
5. **DO:** Reporters present. Stick the sheets in a row on the wall.
6. **SAY — the catch:** "Bought feed brings food into your farm. Eggs and meat that you sell
   carry it away. Keep track of both. If you buy more feed than the animal gives back, the
   animal is costing you — and you should know that, not guess it."
7. **ASK:** "Where do the nutrients go when you sell a chicken?" *(off the farm)* "And when you
   compost its manure?" *(back into the soil)*

### Debrief
1. Which column was hardest to fill?
2. Name one animal on your land whose column three you are not fully meeting.

### Key messages
- Every animal: what it eats here, what it gives, what it needs from you.
- Scraps alone may not meet an animal's needs.
- Feed bought in and food sold out both move nutrients. Keep track of both.

### If things go wrong
- **Nobody keeps animals:** use the cards anyway and plan for the animal they most want.
- **An argument about goats:** park it on the flipchart; S4 ends with each learner's own plan.

### Language & inclusion
Animal names differ between households — take the group's word, write it next to the English.
A learner who cannot read the card can describe the animal aloud; the Recorder draws it.

## F-D6-S2 · 10:50–12:30 · Build a chicken tractor and move it (100 min)

**By the end, learners can:** build a moveable floorless pen, place it on a bed that is ready
for it, and say when it must be moved.

**Poster:** F-P23 Move the pen before the ground goes bare · **Slides:** F-D6-06 → 09
**Materials (per group):** 4 poles or scrap timber, wire or cable ties, 3 m of wire mesh, 1
pair of pliers, 1 spade. Class: the harvested bed from Day 4, a bucket of water, shade cloth
**Room:** outside, at the beds

| Time | Step | Teacher talk |
|---|---|---|
| 10:50 | Why a pen that moves | 4 |
| 11:00 | Demonstrate the frame, then groups build | 2 |
| 11:45 | Place the pens on the cleared bed | 2 |
| 12:05 | When do you move it? Read the ground | 5 |
| 12:20 | Mark the next three positions | 1 |

### Say / ask / do
1. **SAY:** "A chicken tractor is a pen with no floor. The birds scratch through what is left
   after harvest, eat some insects and weed seeds, and leave manure behind. Then you move it."
2. **DO:** Reveal **F-P23**. Point: ① the pen on a cleared bed ② the scratched patch it leaves
   ③ the arrow to the next position ④ shade and water inside, always.
3. **DO:** Build one frame in front of everyone, naming each step. Then **GROUPS** build their
   own. 45 minutes. Rotate so everyone ties at least one corner.
4. **DO (11:45):** Place the pens on the **cleared** bed — the one harvested on Day 4.
   **SAY, and make them repeat it:** "After harvest. Never around young seedlings."
5. **ASK (12:05):** "How do you know when to move it?" Take answers, then point at the poster:
   - the ground is going **bare**
   - it is turning **muddy**
   - manure is **building up**
   **SAY:** "There is no number of days. There is no number of chickens that is right for every
   plot. You watch the ground and you move them."
6. **SAY:** "Fresh manure can carry germs. It goes to the compost heap, not straight onto food
   crops. Ask an extension adviser how to handle manure safely before the next crop goes in."
7. **DO (12:20):** Each group marks the next three positions of its pen with pegs.
8. **ASK:** "What about ducks?" **SAY:** "Ducks scratch less — good under established trees.
   But they trample, they eat plants, and they make wet ground muddy. Watch them the same way."

### Debrief
1. What tells you it is time to move the pen?
2. Why not put chickens in with your seedlings?

### Key messages
- Move the pen before the ground goes bare, muddy, or thick with manure.
- After harvest only — never around young seedlings or a crop being picked for food.
- Fresh manure goes to the heap. Compost it fully before it goes near food.

### If things go wrong
- **No mesh:** build the frame and mark the footprint; show the movement with pegs and string.
- **No cleared bed:** use any bare patch and explain the difference.
- **Very hot:** shade cloth over one end of every pen before any bird goes in. Non-negotiable.

### Language & inclusion
Building is the lesson — make sure the people who usually watch are the ones tying corners.
A learner who cannot lift can cut ties, hold the frame, or mark the next positions.

## F-D6-S3 ⚑ · 13:15–14:30 · Keeping the flock alive (75 min)

> ⚑ **VETERINARY REVIEW REQUIRED BEFORE THIS SESSION IS TAUGHT OR PRINTED.**
> The signs, the reporting route and any vaccination advice below must be confirmed with the
> local state veterinarian or animal health technician, and the vaccine schedule filled in by
> them. Vaccine type, timing and where to get it differ by province and by programme — that is
> why this card has a blank for it rather than a number.

**By the end, learners can:** describe what a sick bird looks like, say what they will do the
same day, and name who they will call.

**Poster:** F-P24 A sick bird — look, separate, report · **Slides:** F-D6-10 → 13
**Materials:** F-P24, the contact card for the local state vet / animal health technician
(**filled in before the course** — Day 0 task), a flipchart
**Room:** inside, seated in a circle — this session is talk and decision, not practical

| Time | Step | Teacher talk |
|---|---|---|
| 13:15 | The story: the whole village's chickens | 4 |
| 13:25 | ⚑ What to look for | 8 |
| 13:45 | ⚑ What you do the same day | 6 |
| 14:00 | Who you call — everyone writes the number down | 2 |
| 14:10 | Everyday flock care: water, shelter, new birds | 5 |
| 14:25 | Key message | 2 |

### Say / ask / do
1. **ASK:** "Has anyone here lost most of their chickens in one week?" Let people speak. Most
   groups will have somebody. **Do not rush this.**
2. **SAY:** "That is what we are dealing with today. Some poultry diseases can move through a
   whole village's birds very fast. The five days before this taught you to grow food. This
   session is about not losing it in a week."
3. **DO (13:25):** Reveal **F-P24**. ⚑ Walk through the signs the vet has confirmed on the card.
   Have the class say each one back while pointing at the picture.
4. **ASK:** "If you saw that this afternoon, what would you do?" Take every answer onto the
   flipchart before correcting anything.
5. **DO (13:45):** ⚑ Give the three actions, in order, as confirmed by the vet:
   **① Separate** the sick birds from the rest, today.
   **② Do not move, sell or give away** birds from a flock that is sick.
   **③ Report it** — same day.
6. **SAY:** "Reporting is not getting yourself in trouble. It is how the next village keeps
   its birds."
7. **DO (14:00):** Everyone writes the state vet / animal health technician number on their
   take-home card. Walk round and check every card. **Nobody leaves without the number.**
8. **DO (14:10):** ⚑ Vaccination — the facilitator reads what the vet has written in this space:
   > _________________________________________________________________
   > _________________________________________________________________
   **SAY only what is written there.** If it is blank, say so plainly: "I do not have that for
   your area yet — this number will tell you," and point at the contact card.
9. **SAY — everyday care, every day:** "Clean water they can always reach. Shelter from sun,
   rain and dogs. Do not mix new birds straight into your flock. Look at your birds every day —
   you will see a sick one before anybody else does."
10. **ASK:** "What did the app say ducks were immune to?" *(Nothing — that was the wrong answer
    in the quiz.)* **SAY:** "No bird on this land is immune to anything. Watch them all."

### Debrief
1. What are you looking for tomorrow morning?
2. Who do you call, and where is that number now?

### Key messages
- A sick flock can become a dead flock in days. Look at your birds every day.
- Separate, do not move or sell, report the same day.
- The number is on your card. Use it — that is what it is for.

### If things go wrong
- **No vet contact obtained before the course:** teach the looking and the separating, say
  plainly that you do not yet have the number, and get it to the group before the course ends.
  Do not invent a number or a vaccine schedule.
- **Somebody's flock is sick right now:** take it seriously, take it outside the session, and
  use the contact card with them directly.

### Language & inclusion
This session is spoken, not read — a learner who cannot read must still leave with the number,
so write it on their card for them and have them repeat it back. Use the group's own word for
each sign and write it beside the English on the flipchart.

## F-D6-S4 · 14:45–15:40 · Bees: a safe site, and the law · my animal plan (55 min)

**By the end, learners can:** choose a safe hive site, say who to learn from before getting a
hive, and say what the law requires of them.

**Poster:** F-P25 A safe place for a hive · **Slides:** F-D6-14 → 17
**Materials:** F-P25, each learner's garden map from Day 1, crayons
**Room:** outside, then seated for the plan

| Time | Step | Teacher talk |
|---|---|---|
| 14:45 | What bees do for the garden — and what they do not guarantee | 4 |
| 14:55 | A safe site: walk the ground and point | 4 |
| 15:10 | The law: registration, and moving bees | 4 |
| 15:20 | My animal plan, on my own map | 1 |
| 15:35 | Two learners show their plan | 1 |

### Say / ask / do
1. **SAY:** "Bees and other insects carry pollen between flowers. That helps many fruit and
   vegetable crops. It does not guarantee a bigger harvest — weather, water, plant health and
   other pollinators all matter too."
2. **DO:** Reveal **F-P25**. Walk the training site and point at real places: ① away from busy
   paths ② away from the house and anywhere children play ③ morning sun helps, but **a safe
   place comes first** ④ flowering plants across the seasons ⑤ away from spraying.
3. **SAY:** "Learn from an experienced local beekeeper before you get a hive. Not from me, and
   not from a video."
4. **SAY (15:10) — the law:** "The national honey-bee control measures require registration for
   defined beekeeping activities — managed hives for bee products, queen rearing, commercial
   pollination, and removing, destroying or moving a colony. If you are not sure whether it
   applies to you, ask the Department."
5. **SAY:** "There are also rules about **moving** bees between areas. Check the current rules
   with the Department and a local beekeeper before you move any hive. Do not load a hive onto
   a bakkie on a neighbour's say-so."
6. **DO (15:20) — my animal plan:** On their own garden map from Day 1, each learner marks:
   where animals are or would go, how the pen moves across the land, where manure goes, and a
   hive site if they want one. 15 minutes.
7. **DO (15:35):** Two volunteers hold up their map and talk through it.

### Debrief
1. Where did you put your animals, and why there?
2. What do you still need to find out before you start?

### Key messages
- Pollinators help many crops; the benefit depends on the crop and conditions.
- Safe site first, sun second. Away from paths, homes and children.
- Learn from a local beekeeper. Register for the activities the law covers. Check before moving bees.

### If things go wrong
- **Someone wants a hive today:** slow them down. The answer is the local beekeeper, not a hive.
- **Rain:** do the siting on the maps indoors, pointing at the poster instead of the ground.

### Language & inclusion
The plan is drawn, not written — nobody has to read or write to finish this session.
