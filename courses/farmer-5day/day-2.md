## F-D2-S1 · 09:30–10:30 · The water journey on a homestead (60 min)

**By the end, learners can:** explain *slow, spread, sink, store* and show on their own garden
map two places to catch water.

**Poster:** F-P07 Slow · Spread · Sink · Store · **Slides:** F-D2-02 → 05
**Clips:** F-ANI-07 (REUSE roof rain), F-ANI-08 (REUSE swale infiltration)
**Materials:** a watering can or 2 ℓ bottle with holes, a tray of soil on a slope (or a
spade's-width of real slope), 5 small stones, dry grass; learners' garden maps

| Time | Step | Teacher talk |
|---|---|---|
| 09:30 | Story + question: where did last summer's storm go? | 3 |
| 09:40 | Tray demo: fast water vs slow water (groups take turns) | 4 |
| 09:55 | Poster F-P07: the four words | 6 |
| 10:10 | Groups mark two water catches on each person's map | 1 |
| 10:25 | Key messages | 2 |

### Say / ask / do
1. **ASK:** "In summer, when a big storm comes, where does the water in your yard go?"
   *(down the road, into the river, takes the soil)* "And in September, when you need water,
   where is it?" *(gone)*
2. **SAY:** "Most of our rain falls in summer, often in big storms. If it runs away, we lose
   the water **and** the soil. Today we learn to make the rain walk, not run."
3. **DO (09:40) — tray demo:** Pour water on the bare tray: it runs fast and carries soil.
   Now each group's Materials person places a line of stones across the slope and a handful of
   grass, and pours again. **ASK:** "What changed?" *(water slowed, spread along the stones,
   soaked in; less soil moved)*
4. **DO (09:55):** F-P07 from top to bottom:
   - **Slow** — stop it running: stones, grass strips, swales across the slope.
   - **Spread** — let it spread along a level line, not down the slope.
   - **Sink** — let it soak into the soil: mulch, compost, pits. Soil is the biggest tank.
   - **Store** — keep it: tanks from the roof, ponds, and in the soil itself.
   Point to the homestead: roof → gutter → tank → overflow → swale → fruit trees → beds with
   mulch → greywater to the banana circle.
5. **ASK:** "What is the biggest water tank on your land?" *(the soil! Healthy soil holds
   water like a sponge.)*
6. **GROUPS (10:10):** On every person's garden map, the group helps draw: blue arrows where
   water comes from, and **two** places to catch it (tank, swale, pit, mulch, banana circle).
7. **SAY:** key messages.

### Debrief
1. What happened when we added stones and grass?
2. Why is soil a water tank?
3. Which catch will you make first at home?

### Key messages
- Make the rain walk, not run.
- Slow · spread · sink · store — in that order.
- The soil is the biggest tank you own. Cover it.

## F-D2-S2 · 10:50–12:30 · Mark a contour, dig a swale (100 min)

**By the end, learners can:** mark a level (contour) line with an A-frame and dig a swale with
the soil on the downhill side.

**Posters:** F-P06 A-frame · F-P09 A swale on the level line · **Slides:** F-D2-06 → 10
**Clips:** F-ANI-06 (REUSE A-frame), F-ANI-09 (REUSE swale overflow)
**Materials (per group of 5):** their A-frame, 15 pegs (sharpened sticks), 2 spades, 1 pick
or mattock, 1 rake or hoe, 1 bucket; gloves if available. Class: 2 ℓ bottles of water ×
5 for the test, dry grass for mulching the berm.
**Room:** the slope chosen on Day 0. Each group gets a 4–5 m section of the same contour line
(so the class makes one 20–25 m swale together), or separate short swales at stations.

| Time | Step | Teacher talk |
|---|---|---|
| 10:50 | Safety + poster F-P09 cross-section | 5 |
| 11:00 | Mark the contour (every learner places the frame at least once) | 1 |
| 11:20 | Check lines + join them | 2 |
| 11:25 | Dig: trench above, soil below (rotate every 10 min) | 1 |
| 12:05 | Level test with water | 2 |
| 12:15 | Mulch the berm; debrief | 2 |

### Say / ask / do
1. **SAY — safety:** "Carry spades and picks point-down. Nobody stands behind someone
   swinging a pick. Drink water every 20 minutes. Swap jobs every 10 minutes — nobody digs the
   whole time and nobody watches the whole time."
2. **DO:** F-P09 cross-section. Point: ① the **ditch** on the level line, about 40 cm wide and
   30 cm deep ② the dug soil goes **below** it (downhill) as a **mound** (berm) ③ rain runs down,
   stops in the ditch, soaks in ④ roots of trees and plants on the berm drink it for weeks
   ⑤ a **spillway** at one end — a low, stone-lined place where extra water leaves safely.
3. **GROUPS (11:00) — mark the contour:**
   1. Put the first peg at the start of your section.
   2. Put one A-frame foot on the peg. Swing the other foot up or down the slope until the
      string hangs on the **level mark**.
   3. Peg where the second foot is.
   4. Move the frame along: back foot on the new peg. Repeat.
   5. Every person in the group places the frame at least once. The Recorder counts turns.
4. **DO (11:20):** Walk the pegs with the class. If the line zig-zags, smooth it gently —
   "follow the average". Join each group's pegs to the next group's so the line is continuous.
5. **GROUPS (11:25) — dig:** Dig the ditch **on the uphill side** of the pegs. Throw the soil
   downhill to form the berm. Keep the ditch bottom **flat** (use the A-frame on the bottom).
   Rotate: 2 dig, 1 rakes the berm, 1 checks depth with a stick marked at 30 cm, 1 fetches
   water and times the swaps.
6. **DO (12:05) — test:** Each group pours two 2 ℓ bottles into its section. **ASK:** "Does it
   stay spread along the ditch, or run to one end?" If it runs, that end is low — take soil off
   the high end's floor, not the low end.
7. **DO (12:15):** Cover the berm with dry grass. Mark where fruit trees or pigeon pea / fodder
   plants will go on the berm (Day 4). **SAY:** "In the next big rain, come and look."

### Debrief
1. What did the A-frame show you?
2. Why does the soil go downhill of the ditch?
3. Where is a slope at home that needs this?

### Key messages
- A swale is a ditch on the level line with its soil mound below it.
- It slows, spreads and sinks the rain — and feeds the plants on the mound.
- Always make a safe spillway. Never dig swales on steep slopes (more than a steep walk) —
  use stone lines, grass strips or terraces there instead.

### If things go wrong
- **Ground too hard:** wet it the evening before (Day 0 / Day 1); or make **infiltration pits**
  (40 × 40 × 40 cm, every 2 m along the contour, filled with dry grass) instead of a full
  ditch — same principle, less digging.
- **Flat land:** dig **sunken beds** or **half-moons** (a crescent bank, open side uphill) —
  both catch water with no slope.
- **Heat:** stop at 12:00, move the test and mulching into S3's start.
- **Only one A-frame survived:** carousel — two groups mark while three dig.

### Language & inclusion
- A learner who cannot dig: holds the A-frame, checks levels, times swaps. These are real
  jobs — say so.

## F-D2-S3 · 13:15–14:30 · How much rain from a roof? (75 min)

**By the end, learners can:** measure a roof and calculate roughly how many litres it catches
in one storm and in a year, and explain a first-flush.

**Poster:** F-P08 How much rain from your roof? · **Slides:** F-D2-11 → 14
**Clips:** F-ANI-10 (NEW first-flush tank)
**Materials:** 2 tape measures (or a 1 m string with knots every metre per group), a 1 ℓ
bottle, a 20 ℓ bucket, bottle tops as counters (100 per group), flipchart, one calculator
or phone per group (optional)
**Room:** start at the venue roof, finish in the shade

| Time | Step | Teacher talk |
|---|---|---|
| 13:15 | Energiser *Rain Storm* | 1 |
| 13:20 | The magic rule: 1 mm on 1 m² = 1 litre | 5 |
| 13:30 | Groups measure the venue roof | 1 |
| 13:45 | Calculate one storm, then one year (counters) | 3 |
| 14:05 | Tanks, gutters, first-flush | 5 |
| 14:20 | Each learner: my roof at home | 1 |

### Say / ask / do
1. **SAY — the magic rule:** "Take a square one step by one step — one metre by one metre. If
   one millimetre of rain falls on it, that is **one litre** of water. One litre!" Hold up the
   1 ℓ bottle.
2. **ASK:** "A good summer storm gives 20 mm. How many litres on that one square?" *(20)* "A
   20 ℓ bucket!" Hold up the bucket.
3. **GROUPS (13:30):** Measure the venue roof: length and width along the ground under the
   roof edge (not the slope of the roof). Knotted string counts in metres.
4. **DO (13:45) — worked example (write on the flipchart):**

   | Step | Example |
   |---|---|
   | Roof length × width | 10 m × 6 m = **60 m²** |
   | × rain in one storm (mm) | 60 × 20 mm = 1 200 ℓ |
   | × 0.8 (some is lost: splash, evaporation, first flush) | 1 200 × 0.8 = **960 ℓ** ≈ **48 buckets** |
   | One year: × yearly rain (mm) | 60 × 800 mm × 0.8 = **38 400 ℓ** |

   **SAY:** "Ask your extension officer or the weather office for your area's yearly rain.
   Many parts of KZN get 700–1 000 mm; drier areas less. **Check your own area.**"
5. **GROUPS:** Calculate for the venue roof. For learners who don't use numbers easily: use
   the bottle tops — each top = 1 m² of roof; lay out the roof as a rectangle of tops; each top
   gives 1 bucket (20 ℓ) in a 20 mm storm, less a little. Count the buckets.
6. **DO (14:05):** Poster F-P08, lower half:
   - **Gutter** on the low edge of the roof, sloping gently to the tank.
   - **First-flush:** the first rain washes dust, bird droppings and leaves off the roof — let
     it go into a separate pipe that fills first; then clean water runs to the tank.
   - **Tank:** covered (no mosquitoes, no light = no green slime), on a strong flat base,
     tap above a bucket's height, **overflow** pipe to a swale or banana circle.
   - A 5 000 ℓ tank fills from a 60 m² roof in about five 20 mm storms.
7. **ASK:** "How much does your family use in a day?" *(maybe 5–10 buckets)*. **SAY:** "Even a
   small roof can give a garden many buckets a month in summer."
8. **GROUPS (14:20):** Each learner estimates their own house roof (steps = metres) and writes
   or draws the storm number on their garden map.

### Debrief
1. How many buckets from the venue roof in one storm?
2. Why do we let the first rain go?
3. Where will your roof water go now?

### Key messages
- 1 mm of rain on 1 m² of roof = 1 litre.
- Roof area × rain × 0.8 = the water you can catch.
- Gutter → first-flush → covered tank → overflow to the garden.

### If things go wrong
- **Roof can't be reached safely:** pace it out on the ground under the roof edge. Never
  climb.
- **The numbers are too much for the class:** use the counter method only; skip the yearly
  total. ✂ Cut the tank details to one sentence.

## F-D2-S4 · 14:45–15:40 · Every drop twice: mulch, bottles, greywater, banana circle (55 min)

**By the end, learners can:** make a bottle drip-waterer, mulch correctly, and explain how to
use greywater safely.

**Poster:** F-P07 (the lower part: mulch, greywater, banana circle) · **Slides:** F-D2-15 → 18
**Clips:** F-ANI-11 (NEW greywater & mulch), F-ANI-12 (REUSE mulch protection)
**Materials (per group):** 5 empty 2 ℓ bottles, a candle + matches + a nail and pliers (or
a hot nail from a fire), dry grass/leaves (a wheelbarrow), 1 spade. Class: a basin of soapy
water (washing water).
**Room:** outside, near the swale and a planting spot

| Time | Step | Teacher talk |
|---|---|---|
| 14:45 | Four stations, 10 min each (carousel) | 2 |
| 15:25 | My water plan on my map | 1 |
| 15:35 | Key messages | 2 |

### Stations (groups rotate every 10 minutes; one facilitator or Reporter per station)

**Station A · Bottle drip-waterer.** Heat the nail, hold it with pliers, make **2 small holes**
near the bottom of a 2 ℓ bottle. Bury it up to its neck beside where a plant will go. Fill it.
Water goes to the roots, not the air. *Safety: hot nail — pliers only, one person at a time.*

**Station B · Mulch right.** Cover a bare patch with 5–10 cm of dry grass/leaves (a hand's
width deep). Leave a **gap around stems** (a fist's width). Test: next to it, dig a hand's
depth in bare soil and in mulched soil from yesterday's demo — which is moist?

**Station C · Greywater.** The rules, said in turn by each learner:
- ✔ Washing-up, bath and laundry water (mild soap).
- ✘ Never toilet water, nappy water, or water with bleach, paraffin or oil.
- Pour it onto **mulch, around trees and bananas**, never on leaves you eat raw.
- Change the place you pour; don't let it stand and smell.
Pour the basin of soapy water onto the mulched berm.

**Station D · Banana circle (look and plan).** Mark on the ground with pegs: a circle 1.5–2 m
across. Dig the centre as a bowl ~50 cm deep (explain; a full dig is optional), pile the soil
as a ring around it. Fill the bowl with dry grass and prunings. Plant bananas (or pawpaw, or
sweet potato and comfrey in cooler areas) on the ring. Greywater and kitchen scraps go into the
middle.

### Say / ask / do (after the stations)
1. **GROUPS (15:25):** On their garden maps: mark where greywater will go and where mulch is
   needed first.
2. **SAY:** key messages.

### Key messages
- Every drop twice: water used in the house can water the trees.
- Mulch halves the watering. Keep it off the stems.
- Water the roots, not the leaves — bottles and basins do this.

### If things go wrong
- **No bottles:** use tins with holes punched with a nail and hammer (or rock).
- **Fire or candle not allowed:** push a sharp nail through with a hammer or rock.
