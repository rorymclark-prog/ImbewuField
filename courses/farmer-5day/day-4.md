## F-D4-S1 · 09:30–10:30 · Grow strong seedlings (60 min)

**By the end, learners can:** mix seedling soil, sow at the right depth, and say how to care
for and harden off seedlings before transplanting.

**Poster:** F-P14 Grow strong seedlings · **Slides:** F-D4-02 → 05
**Clip:** F-ANI-19 (REUSE seed or seedling)
**Materials (per group):** sieve (or a piece of shade-net / mesh over a bucket), 1 bucket
each of compost, sand and topsoil, 1 mixing basin; containers — 1 seedling tray per learner
*or* 6 yoghurt tubs / an egg box / toilet rolls in a tin (holes in the bottom); seed of 2–3
crops (e.g. spinach / Swiss chard, cabbage, tomato, lettuce, onion — open-pollinated); a
pencil-thick stick; a watering can with a fine rose or a bottle with pin-holes in the cap;
labels (cut from 2 ℓ milk bottles) + marker.
**Room:** a table or level ground in light shade

| Time | Step | Teacher talk |
|---|---|---|
| 09:30 | Seed or seedling? (poster) | 4 |
| 09:40 | Mix: 1 compost : 1 sand : 1 soil, sieved | 1 |
| 09:50 | Every learner fills and sows a tray | 2 |
| 10:10 | Label, water, shade | 1 |
| 10:15 | Care, hardening off, transplanting | 4 |
| 10:25 | Key messages | 1 |

### Say / ask / do
1. **ASK:** "Which crops do you sow straight in the bed, and which do you grow as seedlings
   first?" Sort together with F-P14's left panel:
   - **Straight in the bed:** beans, peas, maize, pumpkin, carrots, beetroot*, radish.
   - **Seedlings first:** cabbage, spinach/Swiss chard, lettuce, tomato, onion, peppers,
     kale. (*beetroot works both ways*)
2. **GROUPS (09:40):** Sieve and mix **1 compost : 1 sand : 1 topsoil** in the basin. Damp,
   not wet. Add a handful of worm castings if the class worm farm has any.
3. **GROUPS (09:50):** Every learner fills their own tray, presses it lightly, and sows:
   - Make a hole with the stick **2 times as deep as the seed is big** (tiny seed: just
     cover it).
   - **2 seeds per hole**; later keep the stronger one.
   - Cover, press gently.
4. **GROUPS (10:10):** Label (crop + date), water gently with the fine rose. Place in light
   shade. Learners take their tray home on Day 5.
5. **SAY (10:15) — care (point to F-P14 right panel):**
   - Water gently every morning; damp, never soaking. Protect from hot sun and heavy rain.
   - **Harden off:** the last week before planting out, put them in more sun each day and
     water a little less. They get tough.
   - **Transplant** at 4–6 true leaves (about 4–6 weeks), late afternoon or on a cloudy day.
     Water the tray first, lift with the soil ball, plant at the same depth, press firm,
     water, mulch.

### Debrief
1. How deep did you sow?
2. What will kill a seedling fastest? *(drying out; too much sun; drowning)*
3. Where will your tray live at home?

### Key messages
- 1 compost : 1 sand : 1 soil, sieved.
- Sow twice as deep as the seed is big.
- Harden off for a week, then transplant in the late afternoon.

## F-D4-S2 · 10:50–12:30 · Plant the beds to a plan and spacing (100 min)

**By the end, learners can:** draw a planting plan for a 1 m × 2 m bed, make and use a
spacing stick, and transplant seedlings at the correct distances.

**Poster:** F-P15 Planting plan: spacing & rotation (top half: spacing) · **Slides:** F-D4-06 → 10
**Clip:** F-ANI-20 (NEW spacing stick)
**Materials (per group):** one 1 m stick or a 1.2 m dropper + knife/marker (to make the
spacing stick), string, 10 pegs, a trowel or 2 hand-hoes, seedlings (2 nursery trays: e.g.
spinach/Swiss chard, cabbage, onion, lettuce), seed to sow direct (beans / beetroot / carrot),
a few marigold or herb seedlings for the edge, watering can, dry grass mulch, A4 + pencil.
**Room:** the trench beds built yesterday

| Time | Step | Teacher talk |
|---|---|---|
| 10:50 | Why spacing matters | 4 |
| 10:55 | Make a spacing stick | 1 |
| 11:05 | Draw the bed plan (paper) | 2 |
| 11:20 | Facilitator checks each plan | 2 |
| 11:30 | Plant: mark with the stick, transplant, sow | 1 |
| 12:10 | Water, mulch, check spacing | 1 |
| 12:20 | Bed walk + key messages | 2 |

### Say / ask / do
1. **ASK:** "What happens when cabbages are planted too close?" *(small heads, fight for
   water, disease)* "Too far?" *(wasted space, weeds, soil dries)* **SAY:** "The right
   distance gives the biggest harvest from the same bed."
2. **GROUPS (10:55) — spacing stick:** On a 1 m stick, cut or mark a notch every 10 cm. Mark
   15, 30 and 45 cm clearly (three colours if you have them).
3. **DO — the spacing table (F-P15):**

   | Crop | Plant to plant | Plants across a 1 m bed |
   |---|---|---|
   | Onion, beetroot, carrot (thin to) | 10 cm | 9–10 |
   | Beans (bush) | 15 cm | 6 |
   | Spinach / Swiss chard, lettuce | 30 cm | 3 |
   | Cabbage, tomato | 45–50 cm | 2 |
   | Maize (in a field) | 30 cm in rows 75–90 cm apart | — |
   | Pumpkin | 1–1.5 m | at the edge / on the swale berm |

   Plant in a **triangle pattern** (each row shifted half a space) — more plants, less bare soil.
4. **GROUPS (11:05) — plan on paper first:** Each group draws its 1 m × 2 m bed from above
   and plans: a middle row of tall/big crops, smaller crops at the sides, onions or herbs at
   the ends (they confuse pests), marigolds at the corners. Mix at least **3 different crops**
   in one bed.
   *Example plan:* 2 cabbages … 6 spinach … 20 onions … beetroot row … 2 marigolds.
5. **DO (11:20):** Facilitators check each plan before planting: spacing right? 3+ crops?
6. **GROUPS (11:30) — plant:** Mark holes with the spacing stick and pegs/string. Water the
   seedling trays first. Transplant (as in S1), sow the direct-sow seed in short rows, press
   firm. **Everyone plants at least 3 plants.** The Recorder ticks names.
7. **GROUPS (12:10):** Water gently (1–2 buckets per bed); mulch between plants, not touching
   stems. Check each other's spacing with the stick.
8. **DO (12:20):** Bed walk — Reporters explain their plan.

### Debrief
1. How many plants did your bed take?
2. Why did we plan on paper first?
3. What will you plant first at home, and how far apart?

### Key messages
- Plan on paper, then plant.
- Use the spacing stick: 10 · 15 · 30 · 45 cm.
- Mix crops in every bed; triangle pattern.

### If things go wrong
- **Seedlings didn't arrive:** direct-sow beans, beetroot, carrots and spinach seed to the
  same spacing — the lesson is the spacing, not the seedlings. Transplant the learners' own
  trays in 5 weeks at the first mentor cluster visit.
- **Too hot:** plant only the shaded end now; finish at 15:00 in S4.

## F-D4-S3 · 13:15–14:30 · Healthy garden, fewer pests + make a natural spray (75 min)

**By the end, learners can:** name the steps of the healthy-garden ladder in order, find and
identify a pest and a garden friend, and make a garlic-chilli spray safely.

**Poster:** F-P16 Healthy garden, fewer pests (the ladder) · **Slides:** F-D4-11 → 14
**Clip:** F-ANI-21 (REUSE helpful insects)
**Materials (per group):** 1 garlic bulb, 5–10 hot chillies, 1 onion, a knife and board or a
stone to crush, a 2 ℓ bottle with ~1 ℓ of water, a cloth to strain, a bar of plain soap (not
dish-washing liquid) and a grater/knife, a funnel (cut bottle top), a spray bottle or a
bundle of leaves to flick with, a label. Class: gloves, a basin of clean water for eyes and
hands, 5 toilet-roll tubes (collars), a jar for collected pests.
**Room:** outside in the shade; a visit to a neighbour's garden or the venue's edges for the
pest walk

| Time | Step | Teacher talk |
|---|---|---|
| 13:15 | Energiser *Web of Life* (pests and friends) | 1 |
| 13:20 | Bug hunt: find one pest and one friend per group | 1 |
| 13:35 | The ladder (poster F-P16) | 6 |
| 13:50 | Make garlic-chilli spray (groups) | 2 |
| 14:10 | Collars, hand-picking, traps | 2 |
| 14:20 | Key messages | 2 |

### Say / ask / do
1. **GROUPS (13:20) — bug hunt, 15 min:** "Find one creature that eats our plants and one that
   helps us. Bring them (or a leaf with them) in the jar — or photograph them." Common finds:
   *pests* — aphids, caterpillars, cutworms, snails/slugs, whitefly; *friends* — ladybirds and
   their larvae, spiders, wasps, lacewings, frogs, lizards, birds, praying mantis.
2. **DO (13:35) — the ladder, bottom to top (F-P16):**
   1. **Healthy soil** — strong plants resist attack.
   2. **Many plants together** — pests can't find their food; flowers feed the friends.
      Rotation (Day 4 S4) breaks their life cycle.
   3. **Look twice a week** — catch problems small. Under the leaves!
   4. **Hands and barriers** — pick off caterpillars and snails, toilet-roll collars against
      cutworms, net or old curtains over cabbages, a board to trap slugs overnight.
   5. **Friends** — birds, ladybirds, frogs, chickens (after harvest). Leave a flowering corner
      and a water dish.
   6. **Home-made sprays** — garlic, chilli, soap.
   7. **Bought poison — last, if at all.** Ask the extension officer first; follow the label
      exactly, use protective clothing, keep children and animals away, and wait the days the
      label says before eating.
   **ASK:** "Where do most people start?" *(at the top — with poison)* "Where do we start?"
   *(at the bottom)*
3. **GROUPS (13:50) — make the spray:**
   1. Crush 1 garlic bulb, 5–10 chillies and 1 onion.
   2. Put into the bottle with 1 ℓ water. Leave overnight (we use one made yesterday by the
      facilitators to show the finished result).
   3. Strain through the cloth.
   4. Add a small spoon of grated plain soap (it helps it stick). Shake.
   5. To use: **1 cup of mix to 4 cups of water.** Test on a few leaves first. Spray in the
      late afternoon, under the leaves. Repeat after rain. Wash vegetables before eating.
   6. **Label it** — "PEST SPRAY — NOT FOR DRINKING". Keep away from children.
   **Safety:** gloves if you have them; never touch your eyes; wash hands with soap.
4. **DO (14:10):** Each group puts a toilet-roll collar round one young seedling in its bed
   (pushed 2 cm into the soil) and lays one board/sack between plants as a slug trap.

### Debrief
1. What did you find? Pest or friend?
2. Why does the ladder start with soil?
3. What will you try first at home?

### Key messages
- Start at the bottom of the ladder: healthy soil, many plants, look often.
- Every pest has an enemy — invite the friends.
- Home-made sprays are strong too: label them, gloves, wash hands.

### If things go wrong
- **No bugs found:** use the pest and friend picture cards (F-IMG-40 → 45).
- **Someone gets chilli in the eyes:** rinse with lots of clean water for 10 minutes; no rubbing.

## F-D4-S4 · 14:45–15:40 · Rotation, food-forest layers, plant a fruit tree · my planting plan (55 min)

**By the end, learners can:** explain the 4-step rotation, name the layers of a food forest,
plant a fruit tree correctly, and plan their own first beds.

**Posters:** F-P15 (bottom: rotation wheel) · F-P17 Food-forest layers & planting a fruit tree
**Slides:** F-D4-15 → 18 · **Clips:** F-ANI-22 (NEW rotation wheel), F-ANI-23 (REUSE seven
layers)
**Materials:** 1 fruit tree seedling (suited to the area — ask the local nursery), the pit
dug on Day 0 (60 × 60 × 60 cm), compost/old manure (1 bucket), a stake + soft tie, mulch,
2 × 20 ℓ water. Learners' garden maps.

| Time | Step | Teacher talk |
|---|---|---|
| 14:45 | Rotation wheel (walk it as a group) | 4 |
| 14:55 | Food-forest layers (poster) | 4 |
| 15:05 | Plant the fruit tree (all groups a step) | 2 |
| 15:20 | My planting plan on my map | 1 |
| 15:35 | Key messages | 1 |

### Say / ask / do
1. **DO — rotation wheel:** Four learners stand in a circle holding cards: **Beans (legumes) →
   Leaves (cabbage, spinach) → Fruits (tomato, pumpkin, maize) → Roots (onion, carrot,
   beetroot) → back to Beans.** Walk them one step. **SAY:** "Each bed moves one step each
   season. Beans feed the soil for the hungry leaves; roots come last and use what's left.
   Pests that live in the soil lose their food."
2. **DO — food-forest layers (F-P17 left):** Tall trees · small fruit trees · bushes · herbs ·
   ground cover · roots · climbers. **ASK:** "In the forest, who waters and fertilises?"
   *(nobody — leaves fall, roots hold water)* **SAY:** "Around your fruit trees, plant the other
   layers: pigeon pea, herbs, sweet potato, a climbing bean."
3. **GROUPS (15:05) — plant the tree (each group one step, F-P17 right):**
   1. Pit 60 × 60 × 60 cm (dug Day 0), topsoil kept separate.
   2. Mix topsoil with a bucket of old compost/manure (never fresh manure against roots).
   3. Refill so the tree sits **at the same depth** as in its bag.
   4. Cut away the bag; don't break the soil ball. Plant, firm with hands.
   5. Make a basin (a ring of soil) to hold water; stake on the windy side; tie loosely.
   6. Water 20 ℓ; mulch — a gap around the trunk.
   **SAY:** "Best planted at the start of the rains. Water weekly for the first dry season."
4. **GROUPS (15:20):** On each garden map: *which beds · which crops first (this season) ·
   where a fruit tree could go (north side, near greywater or the swale)*.

### Key messages
- Rotate: beans → leaves → fruits → roots.
- A food forest has layers that look after each other.
- Plant trees at the same depth, in a basin, mulched, at the start of the rains.

### ✂ Cut if short
Food-forest layers: show the poster and the clip only; do the tree planting.
