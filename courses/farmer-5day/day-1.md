## F-D1-S0 · 09:00–09:30 · Welcome, register, consent, picture quiz (30 min)

**By the end, learners can:** name the two facilitators, find their group, and say what the
week will give them.

**Poster:** F-P01 (blank agreement, up but empty) · **Slides:** F-D1-01 → 03
**Materials:** register, POPIA consent forms, name tags, markers, 8 picture-quiz cards
(F-IMG-70 → 77), 5 group role cards, green stickers ("no photo")
**Room:** chairs in a circle; registration table at the door

| Time | Step | Teacher talk |
|---|---|---|
| 08:30 | Facilitators ready at the door. Greet every person by name as they arrive | — |
| 09:00 | Register + consent (co-facilitator reads consent aloud to small groups as they arrive) | 3 |
| 09:10 | Welcome circle + call-and-response | 4 |
| 09:15 | Picture quiz (start of course) | 3 |
| 09:25 | Groups of 5 + the promise of the week | 3 |

### Say / ask / do
1. **DO:** Co-facilitator at the door. Each learner signs the register (AM column), gets a
   name tag (first name, big letters) and the consent form. Read the consent aloud in the
   local language to each small cluster. Thumbprint or mark is fine. Green sticker on the name
   tag for anyone who ticks "no photos".
2. **SAY (09:10):** "Welcome. My name is … I grow … at home. This is … Together we will be
   with you for five days, 9 to 4."
3. **DO:** Teach the **call and response** (e.g. you call "*Imbewu!*", they answer
   "*Iyamila!*"). Use it three times so it sticks. Explain: "When you hear this, we come back
   together."
4. **ASK:** Round the circle: "Your name, where you live, and one thing you grow or want to
   grow." (Right to pass.) Keep it moving — 20 seconds each.
5. **DO (09:15):** Picture quiz (forms.md §5). Hold up each card. Point to three corners of
   the room: A, B, C. "Walk to the answer you think is right. Nobody is wrong today — this
   tells us what to teach." Co-facilitator counts people per corner and writes the totals.
   ✂ If late, do only cards 1, 2, 3, 5.
6. **DO (09:25):** Form **groups of 5** (facilitation-toolkit.md §4). Mix ages, men and women,
   readers and non-readers; put near-neighbours together. Hand each group a plant name
   (Bean, Pumpkin, Maize, Banana, Spinach, Marula) and a role card. Roles today: the group
   chooses.
7. **SAY:** "By Friday every one of you will go home with: your own seedlings, your own seed,
   a map and plan of your garden, a record book — and a certificate."

### If things go wrong
- **Many learners arrive late:** start at 09:00 with whoever is there. Late arrivals register
  at tea; the co-facilitator reads them the consent then.
- **More than 30 arrive:** register everyone, but talk to the programme office at tea. Above
  30, practicals stop being hands-on — split into a second course, don't squeeze.

### Language & inclusion
- Say everything in the local language first. English only for the key words.
- Sit elders on chairs, not mats. Ask an elder to open with a word or a prayer if that is the
  custom of the place.

## F-D1-S1 · 09:30–10:30 · Hopes, class agreement, the three ethics (60 min)

**By the end, learners can:** say their own hope for the week, agree how the class works, and
explain the three ethics with an example from home.

**Posters:** F-P01 Our class agreement · F-P02 Earth care · People care · Fair share
**Slides:** F-D1-04 → 06 · **Clip:** F-ANI-01 (NEW three ethics, optional)
**Materials:** flipchart, markers, A4 paper + crayons (1 per learner), prestik

| Time | Step | Teacher talk |
|---|---|---|
| 09:30 | Hopes: draw it, share in the group | 2 |
| 09:45 | Hopes gallery: Reporters present | 1 |
| 09:50 | Class agreement — built by the class onto F-P01 | 4 |
| 10:05 | The three ethics: story + poster | 6 |
| 10:15 | Groups: find one example of each ethic at home | 1 |
| 10:25 | Report back + key message | 2 |

### Say / ask / do
1. **SAY:** "Draw your hope for your garden at the end of this year. No writing needed."
   Hand out A4 + crayons. 5 minutes.
2. **GROUPS:** "Show your drawing to your group. Each person, one minute." (Timekeeper
   times it.)
3. **DO:** Each Reporter holds up one drawing from their group and says whose it is and what
   it shows. Stick all drawings on the wall under a heading "**Our hopes**". They stay up all
   week — you will come back to them on Day 5.
4. **ASK (09:50):** "We will be together five days. What do we need from each other so that
   everybody can learn?" Write (or draw) their answers on F-P01 in their words. Guide them
   towards these if they don't come up:
   - *Phones on silent; answer calls outside.*
   - *Everyone speaks, everyone listens. One voice at a time.*
   - *Right to pass — nobody is forced to speak.*
   - *No laughing at anyone's question or language.*
   - *We all do the practicals — no watching.*
   - *Start on time; back on time after breaks.*
   - *What is said personally here stays here.*
   - *Respect the land: no rubbish, no burning, care for the plants.*
5. **DO:** Ask everyone to come and put a mark / thumbprint / signature on the agreement.
   **SAY:** "If we forget, anyone can point to this poster."
6. **SAY (10:05) — the story:** "Gogo Thandi's neighbour sold all his cabbages and bought
   fertiliser bags every season. After five years, his soil was hard and grey. Gogo Thandi fed
   her family first, gave extra to her neighbour when her son was sick, and put all her peels
   back into the soil. Her soil got darker every year."
7. **ASK:** "Who was caring for the soil?" *(Gogo Thandi)* "For people?" *(her family and the
   neighbour)* "Who shared?" *(Gogo Thandi)*
8. **DO:** Reveal F-P02. Point to each circle:
   - **Earth care** — look after the soil, water, plants and animals. They are alive.
   - **People care** — your family eats first; then your community.
   - **Fair share** — take what you need, give back the extra: food, seed, water, knowledge,
     and peels back to the soil.
9. **GROUPS (10:15):** "Find one thing at home for each ethic — something you already do."
   Recorder draws three circles and puts a drawing in each. 8 minutes.
10. **DO:** One ethic per group Reporter (rotate so all three are covered twice).
11. **SAY:** "You already farm with these ethics. This week we add the *how*."

### Debrief
1. What did you draw in each circle?
2. Why does caring for the soil also care for people?
3. What will you do this week that is *fair share*? *(share seed on Day 5, share what you
   learn with a neighbour)*

### Key messages
- Earth care, people care, fair share — together, not one at a time.
- Your family eats first; the surplus is shared or sold.
- Everything you take from the soil must be returned.

### If things go wrong
- **Nobody wants to draw:** draw your own hope first, badly, and laugh at it. Then ask again.
- **The agreement turns into a list of rules from you:** stop, hand the marker to a learner.

### Language & inclusion
- Ethics words: ask the group for the best local words for "care" and "share" and write them
  on F-P02 in marker under the English.

## F-D1-S2 · 10:50–12:30 · Two gardens · six garden principles (100 min)

**By the end, learners can:** explain three differences between a chemical, bare-soil garden
and an agroecological garden, and choose a picture that shows each of the six garden
principles.

**Posters:** F-P03 Two gardens · F-P04 Six garden principles
**Slides:** F-D1-07 → 11 · **Clips:** F-ANI-02 (NEW diversity), F-ANI-03 (NEW two gardens
in a drought)
**Materials:** per group — 1 envelope with 10 magazine pictures, 1 flipchart sheet, markers,
prestik, glue stick. Class — 2 buckets, 2 bricks of soil (1 bare & compacted, 1 mulched &
crumbly — dig them on Day 0), 2 ℓ bottle of water.
**Room:** start inside at the posters; the water demo outside

| Time | Step | Teacher talk |
|---|---|---|
| 10:50 | Energiser *Water Drop* | 1 |
| 10:55 | Two gardens: picture compare in groups | 1 |
| 11:10 | Water pour demo: bare vs mulched soil (each group pours) | 3 |
| 11:25 | What is agroecology? — poster F-P03 | 5 |
| 11:35 | Six principles: poster F-P04 walk-through | 6 |
| 11:50 | Magazine hunt: match pictures to principles | 1 |
| 12:10 | Gallery walk + Reporters | 2 |
| 12:25 | Key messages | 2 |

### Say / ask / do
1. **DO:** Energiser *Water Drop* (toolkit §6). Ends with: "The mulch kept the rain in."
2. **DO (10:55):** Cover the right half of F-P03 with a flipchart sheet. Show only the left
   garden (bare soil, rows of one crop, bags of fertiliser, spray can, water running off).
   **GROUPS:** "Look for 3 minutes. What do you see? What will this garden look like in a
   drought?" Then reveal the right garden (mulch, many crops together, trees, compost, a swale,
   a tank). "What is different?" Recorders list or draw the differences.
3. **DO (11:10) — water pour demo, outside:** Put the two soil bricks side by side on a slope.
   Each group's Materials person pours a cup of water on each. **ASK:** "Where did the water
   go?" *(bare: runs off, takes soil; mulched: soaks in)*.
4. **SAY (11:25):** "**Agroecology** means farming *with* nature, not against it. We use what
   nature already does — soil life, rain, sun, many plants helping each other — so we buy
   less and our food is healthy. **Permaculture** is a way of *designing* a garden or farm so
   it works like this. Same family."
   Point to F-P03's four markers of an agroecological garden: ① soil always covered ② many
   kinds of plants together ③ water slowed and kept ④ nothing wasted — waste becomes food for
   soil.
5. **ASK:** "Which of these did your grandmother do?" *(many: mixed maize-beans-pumpkin,
   manure, saving seed)*. **SAY:** "This is not new. It is old knowledge plus new
   understanding."
6. **DO (11:35):** Walk through F-P04. For each principle: point to the picture, say the
   principle, ask for one local example. Keep each to ~2 minutes.

   | # | Principle | Example to draw out |
   |---|---|---|
   | 1 | **Watch first, then act** | Watch where rain runs for a season before you dig |
   | 2 | **Catch and keep** (water, sun, seed, knowledge) | A tank on the roof; saving seed |
   | 3 | **Many plants, many helpers** (diversity) | Maize, beans and pumpkin together |
   | 4 | **Nothing is waste** | Peels → compost; greywater → banana |
   | 5 | **Start small, grow slowly** | One well-kept bed beats five bare ones |
   | 6 | **Use the edges** | Beds along a path; where the tap drips; the fence for climbing beans |

7. **GROUPS (11:50) — magazine hunt:** Each group opens its envelope. "Put each picture next
   to the principle it shows — or shows the *opposite* of. Glue them on your flipchart under
   six headings (draw the six symbols from F-P04). You have 20 minutes. Everybody must place
   at least two pictures."
8. **DO (12:10):** Groups stick their sheets up. Gallery walk: all groups walk round. Each
   Reporter explains *one* surprising match.
9. **SAY (12:25):** Key messages (below). Leave F-P03 and F-P04 up for the whole week.

### Debrief
1. Which picture was the hardest to place? Why?
2. Which principle do you already use at home?
3. Which one will you start with this week?

### Key messages
- Agroecology = farming with nature; permaculture = designing with nature.
- Bare soil loses water and soil. Covered soil keeps both.
- Six principles: watch first · catch and keep · many helpers · nothing is waste · start
  small · use the edges.

### If things go wrong
- **No magazines:** use the 12 printed picture cards from the kit (F-IMG-80 → 91) — two per
  principle — shuffled into each group's envelope.
- **Rain:** do the pour demo under the roof edge — even better, it shows real run-off.

### ✂ Cut if short
The gallery walk: take one Reporter only, and leave the sheets up for learners to look at
over lunch.

### Language & inclusion
- The magazine hunt needs no reading. Make sure non-readers hold the glue and place pictures.

## F-D1-S3 · 13:15–14:30 · Read the land — site walk (75 min)

**By the end, learners can:** find north, show where the sun, cold wind and water come from on
the venue land, and name the 5 zones of a homestead.

**Poster:** F-P05 Read your land · **Slides:** F-D1-12 → 14 · **Clips:** F-ANI-04 (NEW
sun and shadows), F-ANI-05 (NEW water movement)
**Materials:** cardboard house + torch, compass or phone compass (optional), 5 clipboards
with A3 paper, pencils, 20 pegs, ribbon in 3 colours (yellow = sun, blue = water, red = wind)
**Room:** outside, walking the whole venue land

| Time | Step | Teacher talk |
|---|---|---|
| 13:15 | Energiser *Sun and Shade* | 1 |
| 13:20 | Cardboard house: where does the sun go? | 5 |
| 13:30 | Site walk in groups with ribbons | 2 |
| 13:55 | Zones: the homestead from 0 to 5 | 5 |
| 14:05 | Groups draw the venue site map | 1 |
| 14:20 | Show maps + key messages | 2 |

### Say / ask / do
1. **DO:** Energiser *Sun and Shade* (toolkit §6).
2. **DO (13:20):** Hold up the cardboard house in the real sun (or shine the torch). **ASK:**
   "Where is the sun at midday?" *(north)* "Where is the shadow?" *(south)* "In winter the sun
   is lower. What happens to the shadow?" *(longer)* Move the torch lower to show it.
   **SAY:** "In South Africa the sun is always in the north. A garden on the **south** side of
   a house is in shade most of winter. Put your vegetables **north** of the house."
3. **GROUPS (13:30):** Each group gets 3 colours of ribbon and 4 pegs. "Walk the land for 20
   minutes. Put a **yellow** peg where it is sunny all day, a **red** peg where the cold or
   strong wind comes from, and a **blue** peg where water runs or sits after rain. Look for
   clues." Clues to look for (tell them): bare channels, silt fans, wet or green patches,
   bent trees, where people's washing is hung, where the soil is washed off roots.
4. **DO (13:55):** Gather at F-P05. Explain **zones** with the homestead picture:
   - **Zone 0** the house · **Zone 1** right by the door: herbs, spinach, seedlings, the
     things you pick every day · **Zone 2** beds and fruit trees you visit every few days,
     chickens · **Zone 3** field crops: maize, beans, pumpkins · **Zone 4** grazing, wood,
     wild food · **Zone 5** wild land you leave alone and learn from.
   **ASK:** "Why is spinach close to the kitchen and maize further away?" *(you pick spinach
   every day; the closer, the more you look after it)*
5. **GROUPS (14:05):** "Draw the venue land from above: the building, north arrow, your
   pegs, where water flows (blue arrows), where you would put Zone 1." 15 minutes.
6. **DO (14:20):** Two groups show their maps. Point out anything important they missed.

### Debrief
1. Where does water come from and go on this land?
2. Why does the north matter for our garden?
3. At home tonight, where will you look?

### Key messages
- Watch first: sun, wind, water, slope.
- The sun is in the north; plant north of buildings and trees.
- Things you use every day go closest to the door.

### If things go wrong
- **Cloudy day:** use the torch and the cardboard house; ask where the sun *usually* rises
  (east) and sets (west).
- **The land is flat:** water still moves — look for where puddles form and where the roof
  drains.

### Language & inclusion
- Pair a slow walker with the Recorder role; bring the site to them by asking groups to
  report back to them.

## F-D1-S4 · 14:45–15:40 · Build an A-frame · my garden map (55 min)

**By the end, learners can:** build an A-frame and calibrate it; draw a first map of their own
garden with north, sun, water and zone 1.

**Poster:** F-P06 Build an A-frame · **Slides:** F-D1-15 → 17 · **Clip:** F-ANI-06 (REUSE
A-frame)
**Materials (per group of 5):** 2 straight poles 2 m (droppers / gum poles / bamboo),
1 crossbar 1.2 m, 3 m soft wire or strong string, pliers (or a rock), 1 m string + a stone /
bolt for the plumb-bob, 1 marker or knife, 2 pegs. Class: A4 paper + pencil per learner.
**Room:** outside, 5 stations on reasonably flat ground

| Time | Step | Teacher talk |
|---|---|---|
| 14:45 | Show the poster: what an A-frame does | 3 |
| 14:50 | Groups build (roles) | 1 |
| 15:10 | Calibrate: mark, flip, mark, middle | 3 |
| 15:20 | Check every group's frame | 1 |
| 15:25 | My garden map — home task | 2 |

### Say / ask / do
1. **ASK:** "How can we find a line across the slope where water will stand still and not
   run?" *(listen; someone may know the A-frame or a water level)* **SAY:** "Tomorrow we dig a
   ditch on that line to catch rain. Today each group builds the tool."
2. **DO:** Point to F-P06 ①–⑥.
3. **GROUPS (14:50):** Build. Everybody has a job: two hold poles, one ties, one makes the
   plumb-bob, one checks the poster. Tie the two poles together at the top (a cross, with the
   tops sticking out 10 cm). Open the legs about 2 m apart at the bottom. Tie the crossbar
   halfway up, the same height on both legs. Hang the string + stone from the top so it
   passes the crossbar.
4. **DO (15:10) — calibrate (show once, then every group does it):**
   1. Stand the frame on the ground. Put a peg at each foot.
   2. Mark where the string crosses the crossbar. Mark **1**.
   3. Lift and turn the frame round so the legs swap pegs exactly.
   4. Mark where the string crosses now. Mark **2**.
   5. The **middle between mark 1 and 2 is LEVEL.** Make a big mark there.
   6. Check: when the string hangs on the middle mark, both feet are at the same height.
5. **DO (15:20):** Facilitators visit each station and check the level mark. Frames go into
   the store for tomorrow with the group's name.
6. **SAY (15:25):** "Tonight, walk round your yard. Draw it from above like we drew the
   venue: your house, north arrow, the tap or tank, where water runs when it rains, where the
   sun is strong, and where you want Zone 1. Bring it tomorrow — any drawing is good."
   Hand out A4 + pencils.

### Debrief (quick, at the stations)
1. What was hardest in building?
2. Why do we flip it?
3. Where could you use this at home?

### Key messages
- An A-frame finds the level line. Water on a level line stops and soaks in.
- Always calibrate: mark, flip, mark, middle.

### If things go wrong
- **Poles are crooked:** fine — the calibration fixes it, as long as the legs don't move
  against each other. Tie tightly.
- **Wind blows the string:** use a heavier stone; shield it with your body.
- **Only one A-frame kit:** build one and in the carousel each group calibrates it on a
  different spot while the others start their garden maps. Order kits before the next course.
