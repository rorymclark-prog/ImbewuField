## F-D5-S1 · 09:30–10:30 · Save your seed + seed swap (60 min)

**By the end, learners can:** choose plants to save seed from, save seed by the dry and wet
methods, store it, and test it for germination.

**Poster:** F-P18 Save your seed · **Slides:** F-D5-02 → 06
**Clips:** F-ANI-24 (REUSE selecting parents), F-ANI-25 (REUSE dry processing), F-ANI-26
(REUSE storage jar vs bag), F-ANI-27 (NEW tomato ferment)
**Materials (per group):** dry bean pods (or dry maize cob), a ripe tomato, a spoon, a small
jar with a little water (plus the class ferment started 2 days ago), a sieve, newspaper or a
plate for drying, 2 paper envelopes, a marker, a clean jar with lid, a spoon of wood ash,
10 bean or maize seeds + a damp cloth + a plastic bag (germination test). Class: small paper
bags or envelopes for the swap (5 per learner).
**Room:** tables in the shade; the seed table

| Time | Step | Teacher talk |
|---|---|---|
| 09:30 | Why save seed? Open-pollinated vs hybrid | 5 |
| 09:40 | Choose the parents | 2 |
| 09:45 | Dry method (beans/maize) — groups | 1 |
| 09:55 | Wet method (tomato) — groups; show the 2-day ferment | 2 |
| 10:05 | Store and test | 2 |
| 10:15 | Seed swap | 1 |
| 10:25 | Key messages | 1 |

### Say / ask / do
1. **ASK:** "Where did your grandmother get her seed?" *(saved it, swapped it)* "Where do we
   get it now?" *(buy it every year)*
2. **SAY (F-P18 top):** "There are two kinds of seed. **Open-pollinated** seed — like your
   grandmother's — grows children that look like the parents. You can save it for ever.
   **Hybrid (F1)** seed gives a good crop once, but its children are mixed and weaker. Read
   the packet: *F1* or *hybrid* means don't save it. Treated seed (coloured pink/blue) is poisoned
   — never eat it, never feed it to animals."
3. **SAY (09:40) — choose the parents:** Save from the **best** plants: healthy, strong, tasty,
   the ones that did well in drought — never from the first early plant that bolted. Mark them
   with a ribbon. Save from **many plants** (at least 5–10), not one.
4. **GROUPS (09:45) — dry method:** Pods/cobs dry on the plant until they rattle. Pick, dry
   more in the shade, shell. Seed is dry when it **snaps or cracks** when bitten or hit — it
   doesn't bend.
5. **GROUPS (09:55) — wet method:** Scoop tomato seed and juice into the jar, add a little
   water, cover with cloth. Show the class ferment: a white skin on top after 2–3 days; good
   seed sinks. Pour off the top, rinse the seed in the sieve, spread thin on a plate or
   newspaper to dry in the shade (not paper towel — it sticks).
6. **SAY (10:05) — store (F-P18 bottom):** **Cool, dark, dry.** A clean jar with a lid, a
   spoon of wood ash or dry chilli to keep insects away. **Label:** crop, variety, date,
   where from. **Test:** 10 seeds on a damp cloth in a plastic bag, somewhere warm, 5–7 days.
   *8 or more sprout = good seed. 5–7 = sow thicker. Fewer = don't use.*
7. **DO (10:15) — seed swap:** Everyone takes up to 5 small packets from the seed table,
   labelling each. "Take only what you will plant. Next season, bring back double." (Fair share.)

### Debrief
1. How do you know seed is dry enough?
2. Why save from many plants and the best ones?
3. Who will you share seed with next season?

### Key messages
- Open-pollinated seed can be saved for ever; F1 hybrid seed cannot.
- Save from the best, from many plants.
- Store cool, dark, dry, labelled. Test 10 before you plant.

## F-D5-S2 · 10:50–12:30 · Eat the rainbow · harvest, weigh and record · garden handover (100 min)

**By the end, learners can:** plan a year of vegetables for the family plate by colour and
season; weigh and record a harvest (eaten, shared, sold); and agree who cares for the course
garden.

**Poster:** F-P19 Eat the rainbow — the family plate · **Slides:** F-D5-07 → 10
**Clip:** F-ANI-28 (REUSE surplus routes)
**Materials:** food picture cards (F-IMG-50 → 65: 16 local foods), 5 paper plates, a crate of
fresh vegetables (bought, or from learners' gardens), 1 hanging or kitchen scale per group (or
one for the class), learners' record books (the harvest page), pencils. Garden care roster
sheet (flipchart).

| Time | Step | Teacher talk |
|---|---|---|
| 10:50 | The family plate (poster) | 5 |
| 11:00 | Plate game: groups build a day's plates from cards | 1 |
| 11:15 | A year of colour: which crops, which season | 2 |
| 11:40 | Harvest, weigh, record (eaten / shared / sold) | 3 |
| 12:05 | Garden handover: care roster | 2 |
| 12:25 | Key messages | 1 |

### Say / ask / do
1. **DO (F-P19):** "A healthy plate is half vegetables and fruit, a quarter starch (pap,
   rice, bread, potatoes), a quarter beans, eggs, fish, chicken or meat. And **many colours**."
   - **Dark green leaves** (spinach, morogo/imifino, pumpkin leaves, cabbage, kale) — iron,
     vitamins, strong blood.
   - **Orange and yellow** (pumpkin, butternut, carrots, orange sweet potato, pawpaw, mango) —
     vitamin A: strong eyes and fighting sickness, especially for children.
   - **Beans, peas, peanuts** — protein: growing bodies.
   - **Red and purple** (tomato, beetroot) and **fruit** — more vitamins.
   **SAY:** "Many of our children are too short for their age because they don't get enough
   variety in the first years. The garden is the medicine."
2. **GROUPS (11:00) — plate game:** Each group gets 16 food cards and a paper plate. "Make
   three plates for one day for a family with a small child: breakfast, lunch, supper. Use at
   least 4 colours across the day." Reporters show one plate.
3. **GROUPS (11:15) — a year of colour:** On a flipchart, draw two columns — **Summer (Oct–Mar)**
   and **Winter (Apr–Sep)** — and fill in which crops from each colour group they could grow
   in each season in their area. *(summer: beans, pumpkin, maize, tomato, sweet potato,
   amaranth/morogo; winter: cabbage, spinach, onion, carrot, beetroot, peas — check the local
   planting calendar; frost areas differ)*
4. **GROUPS (11:40) — harvest, weigh, record:** Each group takes a bundle from the crate
   (e.g. spinach, tomatoes). Weigh it. Then decide: "Of this harvest, how much do you **eat**,
   **share**, **sell**?" Split and weigh each part. Everyone writes (or draws marks) on their
   record book's harvest page:

   | Date | Crop | Total kg | Eaten | Shared | Sold | R received |
   |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
   | 12/3 | Spinach | 2.0 | 1.0 | 0.5 | 0.5 | R10 |

   **SAY:** "If you don't write it down, you can't know if the garden is paying. Even the food
   you eat is money you didn't spend in the shop."
   *[APP] twin (only if learners have the app):* in **Records/Field Journal** log the same
   harvest; pairs help each other. Same 25 minutes.
5. **DO (12:05) — garden handover:** "Who will look after our beds, compost and tree after
   today?" Make a roster on the flipchart: names, days, the watering, the compost turning,
   the stirring of the liquid manure. The community group's contact person signs it. The
   mentor (Course 2) will visit here.

### Key messages
- Half the plate vegetables and fruit, many colours — dark green and orange every day.
- Plan crops for summer *and* winter so there is always something to pick.
- Weigh and write: eaten, shared, sold.

## F-D5-S3 · 13:15–14:05 · Money in, money out (50 min)

**By the end, learners can:** record money in and money out in a record book, keep garden
money separate, and work out a simple price for a bunch of spinach.

**Poster:** F-P20 Money in, money out · **Slides:** F-D5-11 → 14
**Clip:** F-ANI-29 (REUSE farm record)
**Materials:** play money (per group: 10 × R10, 5 × R20, 2 × R50, R5 and R2 coins), 3 tins
per group labelled with pictures (house · garden · savings), learners' record books (money
page), 5 "market day" scenario cards (below).

| Time | Step | Teacher talk |
|---|---|---|
| 13:15 | Energiser *Pass the Clap* | 1 |
| 13:18 | Two pockets: house money and garden money | 4 |
| 13:25 | Market-day role-play with play money | 2 |
| 13:40 | Write it in the book | 2 |
| 13:50 | What should a bunch cost? | 3 |
| 14:00 | Key messages | 1 |

### Say / ask / do
1. **SAY (F-P20):** "The garden needs its own pocket — a tin, an envelope, or a page in the
   book. When you sell, money goes **in**. When you buy seed, manure, a hosepipe, money goes
   **out**. If it all goes in one pocket, you never know if the garden helps you."
2. **GROUPS (13:25) — market day:** Each group gets R200 play money in the *garden* tin and
   one scenario card. Act it out, moving the money between tins:
   - Card 1: *You buy seed for R45 and a bag of manure for R30. You sell 8 bunches of
     spinach at R10.*
   - Card 2: *A neighbour buys 2 kg tomatoes for R40 but will pay next week.* (Owed — not in
     the tin yet!)
   - Card 3: *You pay R60 taxi fare to take vegetables to the school.*
   - Card 4: *You take R50 from the garden tin to buy bread.* (Is that a garden cost? No —
     write it as *taken for the house*.)
   - Card 5: *You put R20 in savings.*
3. **GROUPS (13:40):** Everyone writes (or draws with tally marks) the card's lines on the
   money page:

   | Date | What | Money in | Money out |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | 12/3 | Seed | | R45 |
   | 12/3 | 8 bunches spinach | R80 | |

   Add up in and out. **ASK:** "More in, or more out?"
4. **DO (13:50) — price (worked example on the flipchart):**
   - Seed R45 + manure R30 + water & transport R25 = **R100** spent on one spinach bed.
   - The bed gives about 40 bunches over the season.
   - R100 ÷ 40 = **R2.50 cost per bunch.**
   - The spaza sells a bunch for R15 — **check your local prices**. Sell at a fair price
     between your cost and the shop price, e.g. R10.
   **SAY:** "Your own time is worth something too. The Farmer Money course goes deeper."
   *[APP] twin:* in **Finances** add one *Money in* and one *Money out*; show **scan a till
   slip** on a real receipt. Same 20 minutes.

### Key messages
- Garden money gets its own pocket.
- Write every money in and money out, on the day.
- Know your cost per bunch before you set a price.

## F-D5-S4 · 14:05–14:45 · My 90-day garden plan — make it, show it (40 min)

**By the end, learners can:** present a 90-day plan for their own garden to their group.

**Poster:** F-P21 My 90-day garden plan · **Slides:** F-D5-15 → 16
**Materials:** each learner's garden map (from Days 1–4), a printed 90-day plan sheet (A4:
three columns — *first 2 weeks · by 6 weeks · by 90 days*, with picture prompts: water ·
soil · beds · seedlings · seed · records), pencils, crayons.

| Time | Step | Teacher talk |
|---|---|---|
| 14:05 | Show the poster; one example plan | 3 |
| 14:10 | Everyone fills their plan (pairs help) | 1 |
| 14:25 | Present to the group (2 min each) | 0 |
| 14:40 | Group chooses one "buddy" per person | 1 |

### Say / ask / do
1. **SAY — example:** "First 2 weeks: start compost, mulch the bare patch by the tap, put the
   bath water on the peach tree. By 6 weeks: dig one trench bed north of the house; plant my
   seedlings out with the spacing stick. By 90 days: harvest spinach every week; save bean
   seed; write every sale in my book."
2. **GROUPS:** Fill in 2–3 actions per column (drawing is fine). Then each person presents to
   their group; the group gives **two stars and a wish** (toolkit §8).
3. **DO:** Each learner chooses a **buddy** from their group to check on them in 2 weeks
   (phone or visit). Write the buddy's name on the plan.
4. **SAY:** "Your mentor will ask to see this plan on the first visit. Keep it with your
   record book."

## F-D5-SC · 15:15–16:00 · Certificate ceremony + closing circle (45 min)

1. **DO:** Invite families, ward councillor, programme staff, the venue owner if present. Seat
   learners in their groups.
2. **SAY:** A short word (3 min) from the lead facilitator and a programme representative.
3. **DO:** Call each learner by full name — clearly, correctly pronounced. Hand the
   certificate personally, shake hands, photograph (only if consented). Applause for every
   person. Each learner also collects: their seedling tray, seed packets, garden map & 90-day
   plan, record book.
4. **DO:** A song or a word from an elder learner.
5. **Closing circle:** one word about the week. Register PM signatures (final day). Tools back
   and counted.
6. **SAY:** "Your mentor will visit within 2 weeks. Your buddy will call you. Look after our
   garden here — it is yours."

Facilitators after the course (60 min): final register photos, skills checklist complete,
evaluation totals, daily report + a one-page course summary (numbers present each day,
certificates issued, practicals all hands-on Y/N, top 3 "want to learn more" topics) to the
programme office. Hand the "want to learn more" list to the mentor.
