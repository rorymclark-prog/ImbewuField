## F-D3-S1 · 09:30–10:30 · Soil is alive: hand tests (60 min)

**By the end, learners can:** tell sandy, loamy and clay soil apart by hand; read a jar test;
count worms; and say what their soil needs.

**Poster:** F-P10 Soil is alive — hand tests · **Slides:** F-D3-02 → 05
**Clips:** F-ANI-13 (REUSE living soil), F-ANI-14 (REUSE soil observation)
**Materials (per group):** 1 spade, 1 clear 1 ℓ jar with lid, a 2 ℓ bottle of water, a
teaspoon of salt or dish soap (helps the jar settle), a tin with both ends cut off, a
30 cm ruler or marked stick. Class: the jar set up on Day 0 (already settled), a hand lens
(optional).
**Room:** outside, 5 stations; each group digs its own sample

| Time | Step | Teacher talk |
|---|---|---|
| 09:30 | Hook: a handful of good soil vs dead soil | 3 |
| 09:35 | Poster F-P10: what lives in soil | 4 |
| 09:45 | Four tests in groups | 1 |
| 10:15 | Groups report: what does our soil need? | 2 |
| 10:25 | Key messages | 2 |

### Say / ask / do
1. **DO:** Hold up two handfuls — dark crumbly soil from under old mulch, and pale hard soil
   from a path. **ASK:** "Which one is alive? How do you know?" *(smell, colour, crumbs,
   roots, worms)*
2. **SAY (09:35):** "One handful of healthy soil has more living things than there are people
   in South Africa: worms, beetles, fungi, and tiny bacteria we can't see. They eat dead
   plants and manure and turn them into food for plants. **Feed the soil, and the soil feeds
   the plant.**" Point to F-P10's cut-away: worms, roots, fungus threads, air, water, crumbs.
3. **GROUPS (09:45) — four tests (7 minutes each, Timekeeper calls):**
   - **Test 1 · Squeeze.** Wet a small handful and squeeze. *Falls apart = sandy (drains fast,
     holds little). Makes a ball that crumbles when poked = loam (best). Makes a sticky
     ribbon between thumb and finger = clay (holds water, drains slowly, hard when dry).*
   - **Test 2 · Jar.** Fill the jar ⅓ with soil, add water to ¾, a pinch of salt, lid on,
     shake hard for 2 minutes. Leave it. Look at the Day 0 jar: *sand at the bottom (settles
     in a minute), silt in the middle (an hour), clay on top (a day), and bits of dead plants
     floating.* More floating bits = more organic matter.
   - **Test 3 · Smell and look.** Sweet earthy smell = alive. Sour or no smell = needs
     feeding. Dark = organic matter. Crumbs that hold together = good structure.
   - **Test 4 · Worm count + water soak.** Dig one spade-deep square. Count the worms:
     *0–2 hungry soil · 3–9 getting there · 10+ healthy.* Push the tin into the ground and
     fill with water; count how long to soak away. Compare with a mulched spot.
4. **DO (10:15):** Each Reporter: "Our soil is ___. It needs ___." *(nearly always: more
   organic matter — compost, manure, mulch, and never left bare)*

### Debrief
1. What did your soil tell you?
2. Why does clay soil and sandy soil need the *same* answer? *(organic matter: it helps sand
   hold water and helps clay drain)*
3. What will you test at home?

### Key messages
- Soil is alive. Feed the soil, the soil feeds the plant.
- Whatever your soil is — sandy or clay — the answer is organic matter.
- Worms are your free workers. Count them to see if you are winning.

### Language & inclusion
- Tests need no reading. Let elders lead the squeeze test — they often know soil by hand
  better than anyone.

## F-D3-S2 · 10:50–12:30 · Beds that hold water: build a trench bed (100 min)

**By the end, learners can:** dig and layer a trench bed 1 m wide that holds water and food
for years, and explain why beds are never walked on.

**Poster:** F-P13 Beds that hold water · **Slides:** F-D3-06 → 09
**Materials (per group):** 2 spades, 1 fork, 1 pick, 1 rake, 1 wheelbarrow or 2 buckets,
1 tape or the 1 m stick, 4 pegs + string; bed layers — a pile of dry stalks/sticks, 1 bag of
kraal manure, a pile of green cuttings, a pile of dry grass, cardboard/newspaper, 1 bag
compost (if available), water (4 × 20 ℓ).
**Room:** the bed area marked on Day 0, below the swale; 5 beds side by side with 50 cm paths

| Time | Step | Teacher talk |
|---|---|---|
| 10:50 | Poster F-P13: trench bed and keyhole bed | 5 |
| 11:00 | Peg out 1 m × 2 m | 1 |
| 11:05 | Dig: topsoil to one side, subsoil to the other (rotate) | 1 |
| 11:40 | Layer: coarse → manure → green → dry → soil, repeat | 2 |
| 12:10 | Finish: topsoil + compost, basin edges, mulch, water | 1 |
| 12:25 | Walk the beds + key messages | 2 |

### Say / ask / do
1. **DO:** F-P13. Point to the cut-away: ① 1 m wide — you reach the middle from both sides,
   so you **never step on it** ② dug about 50–60 cm deep ③ layers like a sandwich: coarse
   sticks and cardboard at the bottom, then manure, greens, dry grass, a little soil, water —
   again and again ④ topsoil and compost on top ⑤ mulch ⑥ the bed stays a little lower at
   the edges so water stays in.
   **SAY:** "You dig this once. It feeds your plants and holds water for three to five years."
   Point also to the **keyhole bed** (round, 2 m across, with a compost basket in the middle
   and a path to it) — "good near the kitchen door, for older people: no bending to the
   ground if you build it waist high."
2. **GROUPS (11:00):** Peg and string a 1 m × 2 m rectangle.
3. **GROUPS (11:05) — dig:** Topsoil (the dark top layer) on one side, the pale subsoil on the
   other. Aim for 50 cm deep (a forearm and a hand). Rotate every 10 minutes. Break up the
   bottom with the fork — don't make a smooth floor.
4. **GROUPS (11:40) — layer (≈10 cm each):**
   1. Coarse: sticks, maize stalks, cardboard (wet it).
   2. Manure — a thin layer.
   3. Greens: fresh cuttings, weeds without seeds, kitchen peels.
   4. Dry grass and leaves.
   5. A sprinkle of topsoil. **Water it well.**
   Repeat 2–5 until 15 cm from the top. Mix the subsoil into the middle layers — never leave
   it on top.
5. **GROUPS (12:10):** Top 15 cm: topsoil mixed with compost/old manure. Shape a small lip at
   the edges. Mulch with dry grass. Water 2 buckets. Peg a group sign on it. **SAY:** "The
   bed will sink as the layers rot — that's food being made. Top up with mulch."
6. **DO (12:25):** Walk along the five beds. Each Reporter says one thing their group would
   do differently at home.

### Debrief
1. What was in your sandwich?
2. Why don't we walk on the bed?
3. Where at home will your first bed go? (Zone 1, north of the house, near water.)

### Key messages
- Deep, layered beds hold food and water for years.
- 1 metre wide: reach the middle, never step on it.
- Subsoil goes in the middle, never on top.

### If things go wrong
- **Rock or very hard ground at 20 cm:** build **up** instead — a raised "no-dig" bed: lay wet
  cardboard on the ground, and pile the same layers 40 cm high inside a border of logs, stones
  or bottles filled with sand.
- **Not enough manure:** use more greens and a watering of liquid manure later (S3).
- **Running late:** finish the top layer and mulch; skip the bed walk.

### Language & inclusion
- Layering is a great seated/standing job for those who can't dig.

## F-D3-S3 · 13:15–14:30 · Build a compost heap + start liquid manure (75 min)

**By the end, learners can:** build a compost heap with the right mix of green, brown, air
and water, check if it is working, and make liquid manure.

**Posters:** F-P11 Build a compost heap · F-P12 Liquid manure & worm farm (top half)
**Slides:** F-D3-10 → 14 · **Clips:** F-ANI-15 (REUSE compost materials), F-ANI-16 (REUSE
compost heap), F-ANI-17 (NEW liquid manure drum)
**Materials (per group):** browns (dry grass, leaves, maize stalks — 2 wheelbarrows), greens
(fresh cuttings, peels — 1 wheelbarrow), manure (½ bag), 1 bucket of soil, water (3 × 20 ℓ),
1 fork, 1 spade, a 1.5 m stick. Class: one 20 ℓ drum with lid, a hessian/onion sack, string,
1 bag kraal manure, a stirring stick.
**Room:** the compost corner — in part-shade, near water, close to the beds

| Time | Step | Teacher talk |
|---|---|---|
| 13:15 | Energiser *Compost Heap* | 1 |
| 13:20 | Poster F-P11: the four needs | 4 |
| 13:30 | Groups build a heap each | 1 |
| 14:00 | Squeeze test + stick thermometer | 2 |
| 14:05 | Liquid manure drum (each group adds a step) | 3 |
| 14:20 | Key messages + what never goes in | 3 |

### Say / ask / do
1. **DO:** Energiser *Compost Heap* (toolkit §6) — it teaches the four needs.
2. **DO (13:20):** F-P11. The **four needs**:
   ① **Greens** (fresh, wet, "food"): cuttings, peels, fresh manure.
   ② **Browns** (dry, "bread"): dry grass, leaves, stalks, cardboard. **About 2–3 buckets of
   brown for every 1 of green.**
   ③ **Water**: as damp as a squeezed-out cloth.
   ④ **Air**: sticks at the bottom, and turning.
   Size: about **1 m × 1 m, 1 m high** — big enough to get hot.
3. **GROUPS (13:30) — build:**
   1. Loosen the ground. Lay a bed of sticks/stalks (air).
   2. Browns 15 cm → greens 5 cm → a little manure → a sprinkle of soil → **water**.
   3. Repeat until knee-to-waist high.
   4. Push the long stick into the middle. Cover with a thick layer of dry grass or a sack.
4. **DO (14:00):** Each group does the **squeeze test** on a handful from the middle: a few
   drops = right; running water = too wet (add browns); dry = add water.
   **SAY:** "Pull out the stick in 3 days. Warm = working. Hot = working very well. Cold = add
   greens or water. **Turn** the heap after a week and then every week or two: outside to the
   middle. Ready in 2–3 months when it is dark and smells like forest soil."
5. **DO (14:05) — liquid manure (one drum for the class, each group does a step):**
   1. Group A: put ~5 ℓ (a spade-full or two) of manure in the sack; tie it.
   2. Group B: hang the sack in the drum from a stick across the top.
   3. Group C: fill with water; add a handful of green leaves.
   4. Group D: lid on (keeps flies out).
   5. Group E: agree who stirs every 2–3 days.
   **SAY:** "In 2 weeks it looks like strong tea. Use **1 cup of it to 10 cups of water** —
   the colour of weak tea. Pour on the soil around plants every 2 weeks. Not on leaves you
   will eat soon."
6. **SAY (14:20):** Never in the heap: meat, bones, fat and oil, dairy, plastic, glass, tins,
   dog/cat/human waste, sick plants, and weeds with seeds (unless the heap gets hot).

### Debrief
1. What happens if we use only green? *(it smells, slimy)* Only brown? *(stays dry, slow)*
2. How will you know your heap is working?
3. What will you put in your compost at home this week?

### Key messages
- Green + brown (about 1:2 or 1:3) + water + air = compost.
- A heap 1 m × 1 m × 1 m gets hot and works fast.
- Liquid manure: 1 cup to 10 cups of water, on the soil every 2 weeks.

### If things go wrong
- **Not enough material for 5 heaps:** build 2–3 large heaps, two groups per heap, each group
  building alternate layers.
- **No manure:** use more greens and a few handfuls of old compost or forest soil to "start" it.

## F-D3-S4 · 14:45–15:40 · Worm farm, green manure · my soil plan (55 min)

**By the end, learners can:** set up a small worm farm, name two green-manure crops for summer
and winter, and write/draw a soil plan for their own garden.

**Poster:** F-P12 (bottom half: worm farm) · **Slides:** F-D3-15 → 17
**Clip:** F-ANI-18 (NEW worm farm layers)
**Materials:** class demo — a worm farm made of 2 stacking crates/buckets (top one with
holes) or an old bath/tyre stack, moist shredded newspaper and cardboard, a handful of
kitchen scraps, 100–250 g red compost worms (from a supplier or another farmer — *not* garden
earthworms), a lid or sack. Seed of cowpea and oats or similar to show.

| Time | Step | Teacher talk |
|---|---|---|
| 14:45 | Set up the worm farm together (groups do steps) | 5 |
| 15:05 | Green manure: plant to feed the soil | 4 |
| 15:15 | My soil plan on my garden map | 1 |
| 15:35 | Key messages | 1 |

### Say / ask / do
1. **DO — worm farm (each group does one step):** holes in the bottom of the top container;
   moist shredded paper bedding 10 cm; worms in; a handful of chopped peels on one side;
   cover with damp sack; bottom container catches **worm tea** (dilute 1:10 like liquid
   manure). **Rules:** shade, damp, no citrus/onion/meat/oil, feed a little every few days.
   Worm castings are the richest compost there is — sprinkle in seedling mix.
2. **SAY (15:05) — green manure:** "Some plants feed the soil. Beans, cowpeas and lablab (in
   summer) and oats or peas (in winter) take food from the air and store it in their roots.
   Grow them on a tired bed, cut them before they flower, and lay them as mulch." Show the
   seed.
3. **GROUPS (15:15):** On each learner's garden map: *where my compost goes · where my first
   bed goes · where mulch comes from (whose grass? what leaves?)*. Group checks each map.

### Key messages
- Red compost worms turn scraps into the best plant food.
- Plants can feed the soil: beans and cowpeas in summer, oats or peas in winter.
- Never leave soil bare — plant it or mulch it.

### ✂ Cut if short
Skip green manure detail; it comes back on Day 4 (rotation).
