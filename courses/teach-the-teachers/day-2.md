## T-D2-S1 · 09:00–10:20 · Outcomes that start with a doing word · the question ladder (80 min)

**By the end, trainees can:** **write** a session outcome a farmer could *show* by the end,
**make** questions on three rungs of the question ladder, and **wait** 5 seconds after asking.

**Poster:** T-P05, T-P08 · **Slides:** T-D2-03 → T-D2-07
**Materials (per group of 5):** 6 "foggy outcome" cards (list below); 5 sticky notes; 1 marker;
each trainee's Round 2 lesson plan (from last night). Plus 20 "5-second" cards (a big hand
with 5 fingers — hand-drawn is fine).
**Room:** Room A, groups at tables.

| Time | Step | Trainer talk |
|---|---|---|
| 09:00 | Look — two outcomes: which can you see? | 3 min |
| 09:06 | Talk — T-P05 doing words, the "see it" test | 5 min |
| 09:13 | Try — fix six foggy outcomes | 1 min |
| 09:28 | Try — fix your own Round 2 outcome; partner checks | 1 min |
| 09:38 | Look — three questions, three kinds of answer | 3 min |
| 09:43 | Talk — T-P08 the question ladder | 4 min |
| 09:50 | Try — ladder on a poster (pairs) | 1 min |
| 10:00 | Try — wait time and bounce-back drills | 2 min |
| 10:12 | Take home — 3 ladder questions for Round 2 | 0 min |

### Say / ask / do

1. **DO (09:00):** Write two outcomes on the flipchart:
   **A:** "Farmers will understand compost."
   **B:** "Farmers can build a compost heap one metre wide in layers of green and brown."
   **ASK:** "At 12:30, how would you know if outcome A happened? And B?"
   *(A — you can't; B — you can walk to the heap and look.)*
2. **DO (09:06):** Reveal **T-P05**. Point ①–③:
   ① **A doing word** — make, show, find, measure, explain, name, choose, draw, plan, record.
   ② **What** — the thing they do it to (a compost heap).
   ③ **How well** — enough detail to check (one metre, layers).
   **SAY:** "Foggy words hide learning: *understand, know, learn about, be aware of,
   appreciate*. The test: **could I see it or hear it before they go home?**"
3. **GROUPS (09:13, 12 min):** Give 6 foggy cards. Rewrite each on a sticky note. Stick next
   to T-P05. **DO:** Read three aloud; the room gives a thumbs up or a "fix it".

   | Foggy card | A clear version (one of many) |
   |---|---|
   | Know about mulch | Cover a bed with 10 cm of dry grass and say why |
   | Understand the three ethics | Name the three ethics and give one garden example of each |
   | Learn the roof-water sum | Measure a roof and calculate how many litres 10 mm of rain gives |
   | Be aware of pests | Find two pests and two helpful insects on a crop |
   | Appreciate seed saving | Dry, label and store seed from one bean plant |
   | Understand money records | Record three sales and one cost in a money in / money out book |

4. **Pairs (09:28, 10 min):** "Swap lesson plans. Read your partner's outcome. Can you see it by
   the end of *ten minutes*? If not, help them fix it." **SAY:** "Ten minutes = **one** small
   outcome. 'Build a compost heap' is too big. 'Sort a pile into greens and browns' fits."
5. **Look (09:38):** Ask the room three questions, one after the other. Wait after each.
   - "Is soil alive?" *(Yes. — one-word answers.)*
   - "What lives in soil?" *(Worms, ants, roots, fungi, bacteria… many answers.)*
   - "How could you find out if **your** soil is alive, this afternoon?" *(Dig and count worms;
     smell it; a jar test; put a cloth in the soil… thinking, arguing.)*
   **ASK:** "Which question got the best thinking?"
6. **Talk (09:43):** Reveal **T-P08**. Point ①–③ and the two habits:
   ① **Closed** — one right answer. Good for checking. *(Is it north? How many litres?)*
   ② **Open** — many answers. *(What do you see? How did your grandmother do it?)*
   ③ **Deeper** — thinking and deciding. *(Why do you think? How do you know? What would
   happen if…? What will you do at home?)*
   Habit ④: **Wait 5 seconds.** Habit ⑤: **Bounce it back** — "What do others think?"
   **SAY:** "Never say 'Any questions?' Say 'What questions do you have?' And never answer your
   own question — wait."
7. **Pairs (09:50, 10 min):** Each pair chooses a Course 1 poster on the wall. Write one
   question on each rung for it. Stick them beside the poster.
8. **Drills (10:00, 12 min):**
   - **Wait time (4 min):** pairs; one asks a deeper question; the other must **count five
     fingers silently** before answering. Swap. **ASK:** "How long did 5 seconds feel?"
     *(Very long.)* "That's how long a farmer needs to think — and to translate in their head."
   - **Bounce-back (8 min):** trainer plays a farmer: "Which is better, kraal manure or chicken
     manure?" Trainees take turns replying **without answering** — bouncing it to the room.
     *("Who here has used both? What happened?")* Do 4 farmer questions:
     "Why are my seedlings dying?" · "Is it true that mulch brings snakes?" ·
     "When must I plant maize?" · "Can I use Jik water on the garden?"
     Then the trainer gives the short correct content answers (see notes).
9. **Take home (10:12, 8 min):** Everyone writes three questions — one per rung — into their
   Round 2 lesson plan.

> **Short content answers for the bounce-back drill:**
> Kraal vs chicken manure: both good; chicken is stronger (more nitrogen) — compost it or
> use less so it doesn't burn plants. · Seedlings dying: often too wet, too much sun too soon,
> or cutworm — look at the stem. · Mulch and snakes: keep mulch away from the house door and
> paths; snakes want cover and rats — keep the yard tidy. · Maize planting: in summer-rainfall
> areas usually after good spring rains (Oct–Dec); check the ImbewuField **Calendar** or ask
> local farmers — say "check locally". · Jik (bleach) water: no — it harms soil life. Greywater
> from bathing and washing (mild soap) is OK on mulch around fruit trees, not on leafy greens.

### Debrief (the three questions)
1. What did you do? 2. Why do clear outcomes and deeper questions change the lesson?
3. What will you change in your Round 2 plan?

### Key messages
- An outcome starts with a **doing word** and passes the **"see it" test**.
- Climb the ladder: **closed → open → deeper**. Wait **5 seconds**. Bounce it back.
- Ten minutes = one small outcome.

### If things go wrong
- **Trainees argue over verbs:** the test settles it — "Could you see it by 12:30?"
- **A trainee answers every question:** thank them, then "Let's hear from someone who
  hasn't spoken yet."

### Language & inclusion
- Doing words translate well; ask groups to write the ten verbs in their languages beside T-P05.
- Wait time matters more when people think in one language and answer in another.
- ✂ **Cut if short:** the ladder pairs activity (step 7) to 5 minutes.

## T-D2-S2 · 10:40–12:10 · Model lesson under a tree: read the land & water — then unpack (90 min)

**By the end, trainees can:** **find** north and **show** where shade falls in winter and
summer; **measure** how mulch changes runoff; **calculate** roof water; and **name** five rules
for teaching outside ("tree talk") and five improvised teaching aids.

**Poster:** F-P05, F-P07, F-P08, T-P12 · **Slides:** T-D2-08 → T-D2-11 · **Clips:** T-ANI-13, T-ANI-14, T-ANI-15 (slide mode only)
**Materials (per group of 5):** 1 cardboard house (shoebox + roof, windows on all sides);
1 stick 1 m + 5 small sticks; 2 halved 2 ℓ bottles with soil (one bare, one mulched with 5 cm
dry grass); 1 ℓ water in a jug; 2 cups; 1 measuring jug (or a marked 500 ml bottle);
1 clipboard with a **ladder tally card** (for the observer). Class: 1 torch, 1 phone compass,
tape measure 30 m, cable ties, F-P05 and F-P07 rolled.
**Room:** the **tree-talk spot**, then the **sun spot** 20 m away. Lead trainer teaches.

**Observers:** before going out, each group's **Recorder** gets the ladder tally card: one mark
in the right row every time the teacher asks a closed, open or deeper question.

| Time | Step | Trainer talk |
|---|---|---|
| 10:40 | Walk out; hang F-P05 on the tree | 1 min |
| 10:45 | MODEL — Look: "stand where you'd be warmest at 07:00 in June" | 2 min |
| 10:50 | MODEL — Try 1: cardboard house and stick shadow | 3 min |
| 11:03 | MODEL — Try 2: the bottle runoff test + water signs walk | 2 min |
| 11:15 | MODEL — Talk: slow · spread · sink · store (F-P07), roof sum (F-P08) | 5 min |
| 11:23 | MODEL — Take home | 0 min |
| 11:25 | UNPACK — the ladder count | 3 min |
| 11:33 | UNPACK — tree talk: what made it work outside? (T-P12) | 3 min |
| 11:45 | UNPACK — improvised aids brainstorm | 2 min |
| 11:58 | CONTENT — roof-water sum practice + questions | 3 min |

### Say / ask / do — model lesson (lead, trainees are farmers)

1. **DO (10:40):** Walk out together. Hang **F-P05** on the tree trunk at seated eye level with
   cable ties. Seat the circle so **the sun is behind the learners** and not in their eyes;
   the trainer faces the sun. Elders get the shade first.
2. **Look (10:45):** **SAY:** "Stand where you'd be warmest at seven o'clock on a June morning."
   People move. **ASK:** "Why there?" *(Morning sun, out of the wind, the north side of a wall.)*
   **ASK:** "Where is the sun at midday in winter?" *(Low in the north.)* "In summer?" *(Almost
   straight up, a little north — in parts of SA in mid-summer it is nearly overhead.)*
3. **Try 1 (10:50, 12 min):** **GROUPS:** "Put your house on the ground with this window facing
   north. Use the sun (or my torch, low from the north for winter, high for summer). Push small
   sticks where the shade falls in winter. Where would you plant spinach? Tomatoes?"
   Then: stand the 1 m stick upright; mark the tip of the shadow; **ASK:** "Which way does the
   shadow point at noon?" *(South.)*
   Walk between groups; ask "why there?", "what if the house had a tall tree on the north?".
4. **Try 2 (11:03, 12 min):** **GROUPS:** "Tilt both bottles like a slope. Pour 500 ml on each,
   slowly. Catch what runs out in a cup. Measure." *(The bare one loses much more water, and
   it is muddy; the mulched one loses little and it is clear.)*
   Then a 5-minute walk: "Find three places on this site where water runs or sits."
   *(A gully, a silt fan, a wet patch, a downpipe splash, a bare path.)*
5. **Talk (11:15):** Back at the tree. Hang **F-P07**. Reporters say what they measured.
   **ASK:** "Where was water slowed? Spread? Sunk? Stored?" Point to the poster as they answer.
   Then **F-P08**: **ASK:** "This roof is about 10 m by 6 m. How many litres from 10 mm of rain?"
   Let a group try first. *(60 m² × 10 mm = 600 ℓ; less some loss — about 480–540 ℓ.)*
6. **Take home (11:23):** Each group: "One place at your home where water runs away, and what
   you'll do about it."

### Say / ask / do — unpack (lead, trainees are teachers again)

7. **DO (11:25):** "Step out." Recorders report their ladder counts. Total on a flipchart:
   closed / open / deeper. **ASK:** "What was the balance? Which question made you think most?"
   *(Aim: more open and deeper than closed.)*
8. **GROUPS (11:33, 12 min):** "What made teaching outside work? What could go wrong?" Stick
   answers on **T-P12**. Then reveal the **tree-talk rules** (T-P12 labels):
   ① Sun behind the learners · ② Poster on the trunk at eye level · ③ Circle or horseshoe,
   everyone sees the object · ④ Your back to the wind, voice to the group ·
   ⑤ Stop-and-talk stations — never talk while walking.
   *(Also from trainees: noise from road, dogs, children; rain plan; shade moves — check at 11
   and 14:00; ants; the need for a signal to call people back.)*
9. **GROUPS (11:45, 13 min):** "Improvised aids" brainstorm. Each group lists **8 things you
   could teach with that cost nothing**, and what they teach. Gallery-read in 2 minutes.
   *(Cardboard house = sun & shade · 2 ℓ bottles = runoff, drip irrigation · jar + soil = soil
   texture · beans in tins = money in/out · a rope with knots every 30 cm = spacing · a hose
   + clear water = level line · a sponge = soil holding water · a bucket with holes = leaky
   soil · a maize cob = seed selection · a torch = the sun · a plate = the family plate ·
   people standing = zones · the ground itself + a stick = maps.)*
10. **Content (11:58, 12 min):** Pairs do two roof sums (below), then questions.
    **SAY:** "1 mm of rain on 1 m² of roof = 1 litre. Area × rainfall = litres. Take off about
    a fifth for splash and first flush."
    Pair sums: (a) roof 8 m × 5 m, 25 mm storm → 40 × 25 = 1 000 ℓ, about 800 ℓ caught.
    (b) same roof, a year of 750 mm → 30 000 ℓ, about 24 000 ℓ — "Check your local average
    rainfall with the SA Weather Service or local farmers."

### Content refresh notes

- **Sun (southern hemisphere):** the sun is always in the **northern** half of the sky at noon
  in South Africa; shadows point **south**. Winter sun is low → long shadows; plant tall
  crops on the south side so they don't shade short ones. North-facing slopes are warmer.
- **Wind:** know the direction of hot dry winds and cold winds on your site; windbreaks on
  that side protect crops (up to about 5–10 times the windbreak's height downwind).
- **Frost & cold air:** cold air flows downhill and pools in low spots — frost pockets.
- **Water:** slow it (mulch, plants, stones), spread it (along contours, swales), sink it
  (pits, swales, mulch, trench beds), store it (tanks, soil, ponds). Start at the top of the
  slope. Water on contour (a level line, found with an A-frame) soaks in; water down the
  slope runs away and takes topsoil.
- **Roof water:** litres = roof area (m²) × rain (mm); first-flush diverter keeps the dirty
  first flow out of the tank.
- **Greywater:** bath and washing water with mild soap can go onto mulch around trees; not
  kitchen water with fat, not bleach, not onto leaves you eat raw.

### Debrief (the three questions)
1. What did you do? 2. Why did real objects outside teach more than a talk inside?
3. Which improvised aid will you use first?

### Key messages
- Take the lesson to the land: **tree talk** has rules — sun behind learners, poster at eye
  level, stop-and-talk.
- Anything can teach: a box, a bottle, a torch, a stick.
- Count your questions. More open and deeper than closed.

### If things go wrong
- **Rain:** teach under a roof edge or veranda; the bottle test works on a table; sun with the
  torch and cardboard house indoors.
- **Overcast — no shadow:** torch and cardboard house only; ask "where would the shadow be?"
- **Road noise:** move the circle closer; you face the noise so your voice goes to the group.

### Language & inclusion
- Standing and moving (step 2) — elders may point from their chair instead.
- The runoff test needs no reading; the numbers can be shown with fingers.
- ✂ **Cut if short:** the water-signs walk (step 4, 5 min) and pair sum (b).

**write** a 5-prompt *Teach it* card, and **choose** one change so a named learner can take part.

**Materials:** F-P06 with its A5 *Teach it* card; 10 sticky notes; per group: 6 inclusion

### Say / ask / do

## T-D2-S4 · 14:00–15:50 · Micro-teaching Round 2: ten minutes, solo, talk measured (110 min)

**By the end, trainees can:** **teach** a 10-minute lesson to a written outcome, from a poster
only, with **measured** teacher talk, and **record** their own talk % on the scoreboard.

**Poster:** T-P04, T-P08, T-P10 · **Slides:** T-D2-15 → T-D2-16
**Materials (per group of 5):** 5 full observer sheets (micro-teaching-bank.md); 1 timer;
time cards ("2 min", "1 min", "STOP"); posters, objects, *Teach it* cards.
**Room:** 4 group corners (2 inside, 2 outside is fine). **No devices** — poster mode only.

| Time | Step | Trainer talk |
|---|---|---|
| 14:00 | Briefing: the full observer sheet; how to tally talk | 6 min |
| 14:08 | ROUND 2: teachers 1–3 × (10 teach + 6 feedback) | 0 min |
| 14:56 | Stretch (trainee leads) | 0 min |
| 15:01 | ROUND 2: teachers 4–5 | 0 min |
| 15:33 | Scoreboard: write your talk % | 1 min |
| 15:38 | Plenary: what helped you talk less? | 5 min |
| 15:48 | Mode tracker (Poster) | 0 min |

### Say / ask / do
1. **SAY (14:00):** "Ten minutes each. **Reverse order** from yesterday — the last teacher goes
   first. Poster and objects only." Walk through the full **observer sheet** (see
   micro-teaching-bank.md): outcome seen? · talk tally (one mark a minute) · questions by rung
   · did every learner do something? · *Look · Try · Talk · Take home* ticks · two stars and a wish.
2. **SAY:** "Talk % = marks ÷ 10 × 100. Three marks = 30%. That's the target."
3. **Feedback (6 min):** teacher (1) · observer ⭐⭐🌱 with the numbers (3) · one more star (1) ·
   "Thank you, next time I will…" (1).
4. **DO (14:08):** Groups start. Trainers each sit in on **two full teaches** in different
   groups and complete their own observer sheet (to compare with the trainee observer's).
5. **DO (15:33):** Each trainee writes their talk % on the **Talk scoreboard**. No comments on
   others' numbers.
6. **ASK (15:38):** "What helped you talk less?" *(Giving the task in one sentence; handing out
   materials first; asking and waiting; letting them read the poster.)* Add to *Our teaching tips*.
   "Whose talk was over 50%? What happened?" (volunteers only) *(Explained before they tried;
   answered my own questions; told a long story.)*
7. **DO (15:48):** Tick **Poster** on the Mode tracker.

### Debrief (the three questions)
1. What did you do? 2. What helped you talk less, and why does it matter?
3. What is your one wish for Round 3?

### Key messages
- Talk is **counted**, and it can come down fast.
- The biggest cut: give the task **before** the explanation.
- Numbers are for your own growth — not a competition.

### If things go wrong
- **Observer forgets to tally:** estimate together honestly; mark it "estimated".
- **A teach runs over:** timekeeper says STOP at 10:00. Feedback still gets its full 6 minutes.
- **A trainee is upset by their number:** trainer talks privately at the break: "Round 1 of
  anything is high. Watch it drop."

### Language & inclusion
- Observers who don't share the teaching language still tally talk (they can hear who talks).
- ✂ **Cut if short:** plenary to 5 minutes.

2. **Course craft (16:00):** **SAY:** "POPIA — the Protection of Personal Information Act —
   means we only collect what we need, say why, keep it safe, and ask before photographs."
   **GROUPS (8 min):** 8 situation cards — OK or not? Why?

   | Situation | OK? |
   |---|---|
   | Photo of a group at the compost heap; two have green dots | Not until the two are out of frame |
   | Photo of a learner's child in the garden, parent not asked | No — guardian consent for children |
   | Posting the register photo on a public Facebook page | No — only on the programme's closed channel |
   | Photo of a bed, no people | Yes |
   | Sharing a learner's phone number with a seed seller | No — not what they agreed to |
   | A learner changes their mind on Day 3 | Yes, respect it; remove photos where you can |
   | Programme WhatsApp group for the register photo | Yes, if the group is the programme's and closed |
   | Photos kept on your personal phone forever | No — send to the programme; delete from personal phone |
