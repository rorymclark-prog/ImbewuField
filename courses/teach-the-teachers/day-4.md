## T-D4-S1 · 09:00–10:20 · Stories, songs, role play — and difficult moments (80 min)

**By the end, trainees can:** **tell** a 2-minute teaching story with a question at the end,
**make** a short song or chant that carries a key message, and **show** five calm steps for a
difficult moment in a role play.

**Poster:** T-P22, T-P11 · **Slides:** T-D4-02 → T-D4-06
**Materials:** per group: 3 story-topic cards (from pool A), 1 flipchart; 10 difficult-moment
role-play cards (list below); a printed list of local help lines (checked on Day 0).
**Room:** Room A; groups spread out for role plays (outside is fine).

| Time | Step | Trainer talk |
|---|---|---|
| 09:00 | Look — a story (2 min), then "what did it teach?" | 4 min |
| 09:08 | Talk — the story shape | 2 min |
| 09:12 | Try — groups make and tell a 2-minute story | 1 min |
| 09:27 | Try — make a song or chant | 1 min |
| 09:40 | Talk — T-P22 calm in a difficult moment | 4 min |
| 09:45 | Try — role plays in threes (3 rounds) | 1 min |
| 10:05 | Plenary — what worked? help lines | 4 min |
| 10:15 | Take home | 0 min |

### Say / ask / do
1. **Look (09:00):** Tell this story (2 minutes, no poster):
   > "Two brothers each got a field from their father. The first burned the grass and
   > ploughed it bare every year — neat, clean. The second left the grass on top, planted
   > beans between his maize and dug small pits along the slope. The neighbours laughed:
   > 'Your field is messy.' Then came the dry year. The first brother's soil blew away in the
   > August wind. The second brother's maize was short — but it stood, and his beans fed the
   > family. Which brother would you ask for seed next year?"
   Wait. Take answers. **ASK:** "What did the story teach?" *(Cover the soil; mixed crops;
   catch water; being laughed at doesn't mean you're wrong.)* "Why will you remember it?"
   *(People, a problem, a surprise, a question.)*
2. **Talk (09:08):** The **story shape** (write on flipchart):
   Someone like us · a problem · what they tried · what happened · **a question to the room**.
   **SAY:** "Local proverbs work too — *Izandla ziyagezana* (hands wash each other). Ask the
   room for their own."
3. **GROUPS (09:12, 15 min):** Pick one pool-A topic card. Make a 2-minute story in the shape.
   Tell it inside the group; the group chooses one teller to tell the room (4 × 2 min).
4. **GROUPS (09:27, 13 min):** "Make a short song or chant that carries a key message — use a
   tune everyone knows, or call and response." Examples: *"Slow it, spread it, sink it, store
   it!"*, *"Green and brown, green and brown, make it wet and turn it round."*
   Each group sings theirs once; the room joins in the second time.
   **SAY:** "Songs are memory. A learner who can't read can carry a song home."
5. **Talk (09:40):** Reveal **T-P22 Calm in a difficult moment**. Point ①–⑤:
   ① **Breathe.** Slow down; lower your voice.
   ② **Name it kindly.** "I can see you feel strongly about this."
   ③ **Listen — ask, don't argue.** "Tell me more. What happened on your farm?"
   ④ **Offer a way on.** A choice, a role, the Question Tree, a talk at tea.
   ⑤ **Follow up in private.** Never shame anyone in front of the group.
   Plus: **safety first** — if anyone is at risk, stop and get help.
6. **Role plays (09:45, 3 rounds × 6 min):** Groups split into threes (one pair of two joins a
   three). Roles: **facilitator**, **learner** (reads the card secretly), **observer** (watches
   for ①–⑤). 3 minutes play, 3 minutes talk. Rotate roles and cards each round.

   | Role-play card (the learner's secret) | A good response (for the plenary) |
   |---|---|
   | You talk over everyone; you know a lot | Thank; "let's hear from someone new"; give a role (Timekeeper); talk at tea |
   | Your group is silent — shy, and younger than the others | Pairs first, then groups; ask open questions to the group, not a person; give roles |
   | You're an elder: "This is nonsense. My father ploughed and we ate." | Honour: "Tell us how your father did it." Find what's shared (he rested land? used manure?). Offer a side-by-side trial |
   | You're on your phone the whole time | Give the phone a job (photos, timer); agreement reminder to the whole room, not him |
   | You arrive 40 minutes late every day | Welcome, sign the register, catch up with your group; private talk about why (transport? childcare?) |
   | You arrive smelling of alcohol and are loud | Stay calm; don't argue in front of the group; ask a co-facilitator to talk privately; safety first; they may come back tomorrow |
   | You're angry: "Where is our transport money? You people eat our money!" | Listen; don't promise; "I'll ask the programme office and answer you by tomorrow" — and do |
   | You start crying during the "family plate" lesson | Pause the group task; the co-facilitator offers a quiet place; listen; know the help lines; don't push for details |
   | You say "women shouldn't be group Leaders" | Calm; refer to the agreement; roles rotate for everyone; don't debate it for 20 min |
   | You campaign for your party / church during the break and the lesson | Agreement: this course is for everyone; the space isn't for campaigning; talk privately |

7. **Plenary (10:05, 10 min):** **ASK:** "Which card was hardest? What worked?" Two groups
   show 30 seconds of their best response. **DO:** Hand out the help-lines list.
   **SAY:** "You're a facilitator, not a counsellor. Listen, keep people safe, and refer."
8. **Take home (10:15):** Journal: "The difficult moment I fear most — and my first sentence."

> **Help lines to check and print on Day 0** (numbers change — confirm before each course):
> GBV Command Centre **0800 428 428** · SADAG suicide crisis line **0800 567 567** ·
> Childline **116** · the local clinic, police station and social worker numbers.

### Key messages
- A story needs **someone like us, a problem, a surprise and a question**.
- Songs carry key messages home — for readers and non-readers.
- In a difficult moment: **breathe, name it kindly, listen, offer a way on, follow up in private.**
- You're not a counsellor. Keep people safe and refer.

### If things go wrong
- **A role play gets real** (someone is upset): stop that group; the trainer sits with them.
- **Nobody wants to sing:** trainer starts, badly and loudly. Laughter is the energiser.

### Language & inclusion
- Stories and songs in any language — encourage mother tongue.
- Religion: some learners won't sing certain songs; offer chants or clapping rhythms.
- ✂ **Cut if short:** songs to one group performing; role plays to 2 rounds.

## T-D4-S2 · 10:40–12:10 · Model lesson: a gallery walk on plants, pests & seed — then unpack (90 min)

**explain** the healthy-garden ladder (IPM), plant spacing, food-forest layers and seed saving.

**Poster:** F-P14, F-P15, F-P16, F-P17, F-P18, T-P21 · **Slides:** T-D4-07 → T-D4-10 ·
**Clips (slide mode):** T-ANI-20, T-ANI-21, T-ANI-22, T-ANI-24
**Room:** 6 stations, 3 m apart (walls, trees, fence). Each station: its F-poster, a
**picture task card** (no reading needed), the materials, and a **"leave your mark" flipchart**.
The lead trainer teaches.

| Station | Poster | Task (6 min) | Materials |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 · Seedlings | F-P14 | Mix a handful of seedling mix (sand + compost + soil); sow 2 seeds in a tray cell at twice their depth; label | 1 tray, 3 tubs of ingredients, seeds, labels |
| 2 · Spacing | F-P15 | Use the knotted string (knots every 30 cm) and a 50 cm stick to mark 6 cabbage spots on a 1 m × 2 m patch | string, sticks, 6 bottle tops |
| 3 · Pest or friend? | F-P16 | Sort 12 insect picture cards into *pest* / *friend*; put the 5 ladder cards in order | cards, 2 trays |

| 5 · Food-forest layers | F-P17 | Stack 7 layer cards on the ground from tall tree to roots; place a real fruit-tree pit marker on the north side of a wall | layer cards, stick |

| Time | Step | Trainer talk |
|---|---|---|
| 10:40 | MODEL — Look: a leaf with holes | 2 min |
| 10:45 | MODEL — how the gallery walk works | 3 min |
| 10:48 | MODEL — Try: 6 stations × 7 min (6 task + 1 move) | 0 min |
| 11:30 | MODEL — Talk: the ladder, and the marks on the walls | 6 min |

### Say / ask / do — model (lead; trainees are farmers)
1. **Look (10:40):** Hold up a cabbage leaf full of holes (or a photo). **ASK:** "Who did this?
   What would you do?" *(Caterpillars; spray poison; pick them off; chickens…)* "Hold that
   answer — we'll come back to it."
2. **SAY (10:45):** "Six stations. At each, a job — not just reading. Six minutes, then I ring
   the bell (or clap) and you move clockwise. Recorder leaves your group's mark on the
   flipchart: a tick, a drawing, a question."
3. **DO (10:48):** Start groups at stations 1, 2, 4 and 5 (so no station has two groups).
   Groups visit all six in turn (two stations are empty at any time — that's fine).
   Trainer stands at the **hardest station** (3 · pest or friend), asking "how do you know?"
   Call "1 minute" and ring the bell every 7 minutes.
4. **Talk (11:30):** Everyone at F-P16. **ASK:** "Now — the leaf with holes. What's the first step
   on the ladder?" *(Healthy soil and mixed plants.)* Walk the ladder from the bottom:
   ① healthy soil, strong plants · ② mixed crops, rotation, flowers for helpers · ③ look often,
   catch it early · ④ pick off, barriers, traps · ⑤ home-made spray, last. Read the marks
   on two flipcharts aloud.
5. **Take home (11:40):** Each Reporter: "One thing we'll do before we ever spray."

### Say / ask / do — unpack (co-trainer)
6. **GROUPS (11:43, 8 min):** "What made the gallery walk work? What would break it?"
   *(A **task** at each station, picture instructions, a **signal** to move, one group per
   station, the teacher at the hardest station, "leave your mark", time called. Break it: only
   reading, groups bunching, no bell, too many words on task cards.)*
   **ASK:** "When would you use a gallery walk in Course 1?" *(Revising on Day 5; Day 4 plant
   topics; the poster wall at the end of the course.)*
7. **Content (11:55, 15 min):** Questions. Use the notes.

### Content refresh notes
- **Healthy-garden ladder = IPM (integrated pest management):** prevent first (soil, diversity,
  timing), watch often, act early with the gentlest method, spray last. Even natural sprays
  kill helpers — use them only on the problem plants.
- **Helpers:** ladybirds (eat aphids), lacewings, hoverflies, spiders, praying mantis, wasps
  that parasitise caterpillars, birds, lizards, frogs. Flowers (e.g. marigold, coriander,
  dill, cosmos-type daisies) feed helpers.
- **Spacing:** roughly — cabbage 45–60 cm; spinach/Swiss chard 30 cm; onions 10 cm; tomatoes
  50–60 cm; beans 10–15 cm in rows 45 cm. Check the seed packet; closer spacing in rich
  deep beds, wider in poor soil.
- **Seedlings:** sow at about twice the seed's width deep; keep moist not wet; harden off by
  giving more sun and less water for a week before transplanting; transplant in the late
  afternoon or on a cloudy day.
- **Rotation:** leaf → fruit → root → legume (beans/peas) is a simple 4-bed rotation.
- **Food forest layers:** tall tree, small tree, shrub, herb, ground cover, climber, root. Plant
  fruit trees where they won't shade the vegetable beds (usually south of the beds — the sun is
  in the north).
- **Seed:** open-pollinated varieties grow true from saved seed (if not crossed); F1 hybrids
  don't grow true. Dry seed crops (beans, maize, pumpkin): dry fully, store cool and dry in a
  sealed jar. Wet seed (tomato): ferment 2–3 days in water, rinse, dry on a plate. Label with
  crop, variety, date. Germination test: 10 seeds on a damp cloth — count after 7–10 days.
- **GMO question:** SA allows some GM maize, soya and cotton. Saving seed from GM or hybrid
  maize is not advised (it won't grow true, and GM seed may carry contract terms — check
  the packet). Open-pollinated seed avoids both issues.

### Debrief (the three questions — unpack)
1. What did you do? 2. Why does a task at each station beat reading? 3. Where will you use a
gallery walk?

### Key messages
- A gallery walk is a **walk of tasks**, not a walk of reading.
- The ladder: **prevent first, spray last**.
- Open-pollinated seed can be saved; hybrids can't grow true.

### If things go wrong
- **Groups bunch up:** ring the bell firmly; say the next station number for each group.
- **A station has missing kit:** turn it into a "draw it" station on the flipchart.
- **Rain:** stations indoors on tables and walls.

### Language & inclusion
- Task cards are **pictures only**; numbers for steps.
- Station 4: anyone with asthma or sensitive skin handles the recipe cards, not the chilli.
- ✂ **Cut if short:** 5 stations (drop station 5), 6 minutes each.

### [APP] ImbewuField on phones / tablets

**Materials:** 4 tablets or trainees' phones (at least 1 per group) with the ImbewuField app
signed in, lessons saved via **My Studies → Study offline → Save available lessons to this
phone**; 1 phone with the **Mentor** workspace; the leaf with holes from S2; a small bag of
harvested spinach (or any vegetable) and a kitchen scale.

1. **Look & talk (13:00):** Point to **T-P13** app column. **SAY:** "The app does three jobs for
   a teacher": ① **Show** — lessons and animations in **My Studies**, even offline. ② **Record**
   — **Mentor → Training**: attendance register, signatures, photos, venue, feedback; saved
   offline and sent later. ③ **Ask and look** — **Lima** (the assistant bar) and **Lima Vision**
   (photo → crop, a yield estimate, weigh a harvest).
2. **Stations (13:05, 3 × 11 min), groups rotate:**
   - **A · My Studies, offline.** Switch on **flight mode**. Open a lesson that matches a Course 1
     session (e.g. the soil-health or water-harvesting module). Play its animation to the group.
     Practise **watch → do → talk**: show the 10–20 s clip once, groups do a 3-minute task,
     then talk. **ASK** at the end: "What did the clip show that a poster can't?"
     *(Movement: water soaking, a heap heating, seeds sprouting.)*
   - **B · Mentor → Training.** Create a training event (venue, date, course name
     "TtT practice"). Mark attendance for your group; each person signs on the screen; take
     one group photo — **check green dots first**; add feedback (one star, one wish). Save
     with flight mode on; then turn it off and watch it sync. **Delete the test event** at the end.
   - **C · Lima Vision & Lima.** Photograph the leaf with holes and the spinach. Read the result
     **together**. **ASK** the group: "Do we agree? How could we check?" Weigh the spinach on
     the scale and compare with the weigh-harvest reading. Then ask **Lima** one farmer question
     from the bounce-back drill (Day 2) and compare the answer with the Day 2 notes.
     **SAY:** "Lima Vision starts a conversation. It's not the final word."
3. **Talk (13:40):** Rules for teaching with a phone (write on a flipchart):
   - Phones come out **for a task**, then go away.
   - **One device per group of 5** — the Recorder holds it.
   - **Offline first**: save lessons before you leave signal. Test in flight mode on Day 0.
   - **Consent** before any photo — every time.
   - AI can be **wrong**: check with your notes, the poster, an experienced farmer.
   - **Paper backup always**: the register on paper if the phone dies.

## T-D4-S4 · 13:45–16:10 · Micro-teaching Round 4: twenty minutes, pairs, choose your mode (145 min)

**By the end, trainees can:** **teach** a 20-minute lesson with slides (big screen or tablet) or
the app, **switch** to poster or paper without losing the group when a device fails, and keep
teacher talk ≤ 30%.

**Poster:** T-P13, T-P14, T-P10 · **Slides:** T-D4-15 → T-D4-16
**Materials (per stream):** 10 observer sheets + 5 practical-check cards; timer; time cards;
devices: Stream A — laptop + TV/projector and 2 tablets; Stream B — 2 tablets + phones with
the app; flip-books and printed stills as paper twins; pairs' kit.
**Streams:** as on Day 3 (A = Bean + Pumpkin with the lead · B = Maize + Marula with the co-trainer).

**Mode rule:** each pair uses **slides** (big screen or tablet) **or** the **app**. Each partner
must use a mode they haven't used yet (check the Mode tracker). Every teach still includes a
**hands-on** moment and the poster stays on the wall.

**Device-fail drill:** once in each stream, the trainer may quietly switch off a screen or
take a tablet mid-lesson (**tell trainees at the briefing that this may happen**). The pair
switches to the poster or flip-book and carries on. It happens in real courses.

| 13:45 | Briefing in streams: modes, device-fail drill, order | 5 min |
| 13:50 | Pair 1 (20 teach + 7 feedback) | 0 |
| 14:17 | Pair 2 | 0 |
| 14:44 | Pair 3 | 0 |
| 15:11 | Stretch (5 min) | 0 |
| 15:16 | Pair 4 | 0 |
| 15:43 | Pair 5 | 0 |

### Say / ask / do
1. **SAY (13:45):** "Twenty minutes. Your chosen mode. Set up during the previous feedback —
   test the screen **from the back**. One device may fail on purpose; switch and keep going."
2. **Feedback (7 min)** as on Day 3, plus Observer 1 answers: "Did the device help learning,
   or just decorate?" and "How fast was the switch to paper?" (if the drill happened).
3. **DO:** Trainer observes silently; runs the device-fail drill **once** in a teach where the
   pair is confident (never on a nervous trainee's first device lesson).
4. **DO (16:10):** Streams return; each trainee ticks the Mode tracker.

### Key messages
- The device must **teach something a poster can't** — otherwise use the poster.
- When a device fails: **switch in 30 seconds, don't apologise for 5 minutes.**

### If things go wrong
- **Two pairs want the TV at once:** order is fixed at the briefing; the second pair uses tablets.
- **Power cut:** everyone is in paper mode — note it on the Mode tracker as "paper twin".
- **A pair runs over:** STOP at 20:00; feedback is never cut.

### Language & inclusion
- Slides with **pictures and ≤ 3 short bullets** work for mixed literacy; read nothing aloud
  from the screen that learners can't see.
- ✂ **Never cut** Round 4.
