## T-D1-S0 · 08:30–09:30 · Arrive, register, consent, our learning agreement (60 min)

**By the end, trainees can:** name everyone in their group, **explain** why the register and
POPIA consent come first, and **make** a learning agreement with a group.

**Poster:** T-P01, T-P06, T-P11 · **Slides:** T-D1-01 → T-D1-03
**Materials:** register + 2 pens at the door; 24 POPIA forms; name tags; green dots; 2 pads of
sticky notes (2 colours); 4 group cards; 20 role cards (5 per group); T-P01 on the wall with
space to write; 3 markers.
**Room:** chairs in one circle, no tables. Register table at the door.

| Time | Step | Trainer talk |
|---|---|---|
| 08:15 | Register at the door (co-trainer), name tags | — |
| 08:30 | Welcome and the day's promise | 3 min |
| 08:33 | Name-and-object circle | 2 min |
| 08:48 | POPIA consent, read aloud | 5 min |
| 08:58 | Hopes and worries | 1 min |
| 09:10 | Groups of 5 and our learning agreement | 2 min |
| 09:25 | Our attention signal; role cards | 1 min |

### Say / ask / do

1. **DO (08:15):** Co-trainer sits at the register table. Each trainee signs **AM**, takes a
   name tag and writes their first name big. Say: "Sign the register first — always."
2. **SAY (08:30, lead):** "Welcome. For five days you are learners and teachers. We will teach
   this course the way you will teach farmers. Watch *how* we teach, not only *what*."
   Point to the day's promise, then to T-P01's empty space.
3. **DO (08:33):** Name-and-object circle.
   **SAY:** "Say your name, where you farm or work, and hold up one thing from your pocket or
   bag that you could teach with." Go first: hold up a coin. "I could teach money in, money
   out with this." Then round the circle, 30–40 seconds each.
   *(Expected: a phone, a pen, a key, a seed, a water bottle, a hat.)*
   **ASK** after the circle: "What did we just learn about teaching?" *(Any object can teach;
   we remember people by their object.)*
4. **DO (08:48, co-trainer):** POPIA consent. Hand out forms. Read forms.md §2 aloud in English,
   then ask a trainee volunteer to read it in isiZulu / Sesotho as fits the room.
   **SAY:** "You will do this with farmers on Day 1 of Course 1, before any photograph.
   A thumbprint counts." Green dot on the tags of anyone who ticks "No photos".
5. **DO (08:58):** Hopes and worries. Green sticky note: "one hope for this week". Orange: "one
   worry about teaching". One idea per note, words or a drawing. 4 minutes to write.
   Trainees stick them on two flipcharts; two volunteers cluster similar notes (5 min).
   **ASK:** "What do most of us worry about?" *(Forgetting content, languages, big groups,
   being asked a question I can't answer, elders not respecting young facilitators.)*
   **SAY:** "Every worry here has a session this week." Point to where (e.g. questions you
   can't answer → Day 1 S2; elders → Day 4 S1).
6. **DO (09:10):** Hand out group cards (Bean, Pumpkin, Maize, Marula). Groups sit together.
   **GROUPS (6 min):** "Agree three rules that would help *you* learn this week. One per
   sticky note."
   **DO:** Reporters stick them on T-P01. Lead clusters and reads back (4 min). Add any
   missing must-haves (see box). Everyone signs or marks the poster (3 min).
7. **DO (09:25):** Agree the call-and-response signal in the local language (the room chooses;
   example from the toolkit: "*Imbewu!*" / "*Iyamila!*"). Practise it twice.
   Hand out role cards (T-P06). Each group decides today's Leader, Timekeeper, Materials,
   Recorder and Reporter in 1 minute. **SAY:** "Roles rotate every morning."

> **Must-haves for the agreement (add if the room doesn't):**
> Phones on silent · Start on time · Right to pass · One voice at a time ·
> No correcting anyone's language or accent · Feedback is a gift: say thank you ·
> What is said about a person stays in the room · Questions that don't fit go on the Question Tree.

### Debrief
Skip a formal debrief. Ask one question: "What did we do in the last hour that you will do on
Day 1 of your own course?" *(Register first, consent, names, agreement, groups, a signal.)*

### Key messages
- The register and consent come **before** anything else.
- A learning agreement made **by** the group is kept **by** the group.
- Groups of 5 and roles start in the first hour.

### If things go wrong
- **Trainees arrive late:** start on time; late arrivals sign at the door and join the circle.
  Never restart. Their object can be shown at tea.
- **Someone will not sign POPIA:** they don't have to. They can attend. No photos, no data in
  the app beyond the register (explain the register is needed for attendance).
- **More than 20 arrive:** see Day 0. Stay kind; take names for the next course.

### Language & inclusion
- Read the POPIA form aloud; never ask a trainee to read aloud unless they volunteer.
- Drawing is fine on every sticky note.
- ✂ **Cut if short:** hopes and worries to 6 minutes (no clustering; read 5 aloud).

## T-D1-S1 · 09:30–10:20 · How adults learn (50 min)

**By the end, trainees can:** **name** the four steps of the learning cycle, **show** where a
lesson they experienced fits on it, and **name** the four steps of *Look · Try · Talk · Take home*.

**Poster:** T-P02, T-P03 · **Slides:** T-D1-04 → T-D1-07 · **Clip:** T-ANI-01 (optional)
**Materials:** 40 sheets of A4 scrap paper (for seed envelopes), 1 finished seed envelope,
flipchart + markers, sticky notes.
**Room:** Groups Bean + Pumpkin stay in Room A with the lead. Maize + Marula go outside or to
Stream B space with the co-trainer for step 3.

| Time | Step | Trainer talk |
|---|---|---|
| 09:30 | Look — best teacher, worst lesson (pairs) | 2 min |
| 09:41 | Try — fold a seed envelope, taught two ways | 3 min |
| 09:51 | Talk — the learning cycle (T-P02) | 5 min |
| 10:03 | Talk — Look · Try · Talk · Take home (T-P03) | 2 min |
| 10:13 | Take home — journal | 1 min |

### Say / ask / do

1. **ASK (09:30):** "Think of the best teacher you ever had. Not only at school — maybe a gogo
   who taught you to plant, a coach, a boss. What did they *do*?" Pairs, 3 minutes.
2. **DO:** Harvest on a flipchart, one idea per pair, 4 minutes. *(Showed me, let me try,
   was patient, told stories, asked what I thought, believed in me, let me make mistakes.)*
3. **ASK:** "Now the worst lesson you sat through. What happened?" Pairs, 2 minutes; harvest
   fast. *(Talked all the time, used big words, made me feel stupid, no break, read from
   the paper.)*
4. **DO (09:41):** Two ways to teach the same thing: **fold a seed envelope from A4**.
   - Room A (lead) — **tell only**: explain the folds in words for 2 minutes. Hands stay on
     laps. Then give paper: "Now make one." 2 minutes.
   - Outside (co-trainer) — **show and try**: give paper first. Fold one step at a time; they
     copy each step; partners help each other. 4 minutes.
   Both groups return to the circle with their envelopes.
5. **ASK (09:49):** "Hold up your envelopes. Which group did better? Why?"
   *(The show-and-try group, usually. They did it with their hands; they saw each step;
   they helped each other.)*
6. **DO (09:51):** Reveal **T-P02 How adults learn**. Point to ①→④ as you ask:
   - ① **Do** — "What did you *do*?" *(Folded paper.)*
   - ② **Look back** — "What did you notice?" *(It tore; my corner was wrong.)*
   - ③ **Make sense** — "What's the rule?" *(Fold the bottom first; press the edges.)*
   - ④ **Use it** — "When will you use it?" *(Saving bean seed on Day 5.)*
   **SAY:** "Adults learn round this cycle. You can start anywhere, but you must go all the
   way round. Most bad lessons stop at step ③ — the teacher gives the rule and nobody does."
7. **ASK:** "What do adults bring that children don't?" *(Experience, their own problems,
   responsibilities, pride.)* **SAY** the four adult-learning facts (below), one line each.
8. **DO (10:03):** Reveal **T-P03 Look · Try · Talk · Take home**.
   **GROUPS (6 min):** "Map the *show-and-try* envelope lesson onto these four steps. Where
   was *Look*? *Try*? What was missing?" *(Look = the finished envelope; Try = folding;
   Talk = this discussion; Take home = missing!)*
   **SAY:** "*Look · Try · Talk · Take home* is the learning cycle turned into a lesson plan.
   Every session in Course 1 is written this way."
9. **DO (10:13):** Journal. "Write or draw one thing your best teacher did that you will do."
   Two groups share one each.

> **Four facts about adult learners** (plain words, from adult-learning research):
> 1. Adults want to know **why** before they spend time on something.
> 2. They learn from their **own experience** — so ask about it first.
> 3. They learn best from **real problems** they already have (water, pests, money).
> 4. They need **respect**: being made to feel stupid stops learning.
>
> **Myth to avoid:** the "learning pyramid" (we remember 10% of what we read, 90% of what we
> teach…) is **not** backed by research. Don't teach those numbers. The real finding is
> simpler: people remember what they **did** and **talked about**.

### Debrief (the three questions)
1. What did you do? 2. Why did the show-and-try group learn faster? 3. What will you change in
your own teaching?

### Key messages
- Adults learn by **doing, looking back, making sense and using it** — all four.
- A lesson is *Look · Try · Talk · Take home*. **Try** comes before the long explanation.
- Ask about their experience first. Respect is part of the method.

### If things go wrong
- **The tell-only group does better:** great discussion — ask why. (Usually a paper-folding
  expert is in the group.) The point stands: they had to *try* before they could show it.
- **No second space:** do both versions in Room A, one after the other; groups watch.

### Language & inclusion
- Folding needs no reading. Pair anyone with limited hand movement with a partner who folds
  while they direct.
- Let pairs talk in any language; harvest in the room's common language.
- ✂ **Cut if short:** the worst-lesson harvest.

## T-D1-S2 · 10:40–12:10 · Model lesson: two gardens & the 10 elements — then unpack (90 min)

**By the end, trainees can:** **explain** the three ethics and the FAO 10 elements of
agroecology with an example from their area, **name** what the trainer did in each step of
*Look · Try · Talk · Take home*, and **measure** teacher talk with a tally.

**Poster:** F-P02, F-P03, T-P19, T-P20, T-P04 · **Slides:** T-D1-08 → T-D1-12 · **Clip:** T-ANI-11 (three ethics, optional)
**Materials (per group of 5):** 1 set of 10 element cards (name + icon, from T-P19 labels);
1 set of 20 practice photo cards (list below); 1 flipchart sheet; 1 marker. Plus 4 talk-tally
cards (one per group) and 1 for the co-trainer.
**Room:** Room A, groups at 4 tables or floor spaces.

**Who teaches:** the **co-trainer** teaches the model lesson (45 min) as if trainees are
Course 1 farmers. The **lead trainer** keeps the talk tally. Then the lead runs the unpack.

| Time | Step | Trainer talk |
|---|---|---|
| 10:40 | Set up: who counts talk | 2 min |
| 10:42 | MODEL — Look: two gardens | 3 min |
| 10:47 | MODEL — three ethics in the good garden | 2 min |
| 10:52 | MODEL — Try: 10-element card sort | 1 min |
| 11:10 | MODEL — Talk: reporters; reveal T-P19; link to T-P20 | 6 min |
| 11:20 | MODEL — Take home | 0 min |
| 11:25 | UNPACK — talk tally and the meter (T-P04) | 4 min |
| 11:33 | UNPACK — what did the teacher *do*? | 2 min |
| 11:45 | CONTENT — "which element am I unsure of?" | 5 min |
| 12:05 | Key messages | 2 min |

### Say / ask / do

**Set-up (10:40, lead):**
1. Give each group's Recorder a **talk-tally card**. **SAY:** "Every minute the teacher talks
   to the whole room, make one mark. Don't count when groups talk." Lead does the same.
2. **SAY:** "For the next 45 minutes you are farmers in Course 1. Be yourselves. Then we'll
   step out and look at how it was taught."

**Model lesson (co-trainer):**

3. **Look (10:42).** Uncover **F-P03 Two gardens**. **ASK:** "What differences do you see?"
   Wait. Take one from each group. *(Bare soil vs mulch; one crop vs many; bags of fertiliser vs
   compost; no trees vs trees; tank on the roof; chickens.)*
   **ASK:** "In a drought, which garden would you rather eat from? Why?"
4. **Three ethics (10:47).** Point to **F-P02**. **ASK:** "Find one thing in the good garden that
   cares for the earth. One that cares for people. One that shares fairly."
   *(Mulch/compost = earth care; food near the house, shade for the gogo = people care; seed
   swap, surplus given to neighbours = fair share.)* Groups call out.
5. **Try (10:52).** Hand each group the 10 element cards and 20 photo cards.
   **GROUPS (15 min):** "Put each photo under the idea it shows. Some fit two places — argue
   about it! Then choose the **three ideas your village is strongest in** and **one that is
   weakest**. Recorder draws or writes them on the flipchart."
   Co-trainer walks and asks, doesn't tell: "Why there?" "Could it also go here?"
   Call "5 minutes left" at 11:02.
6. **Talk (11:10).** Each Reporter: "our strongest three and weakest one" (1 minute each).
   Reveal **T-P19**. Read each element with its icon; groups point to a photo that fits.
   **SAY (short):** "FAO — the UN's food and farming body — agreed these ten ideas in 2018 to
   describe agroecology. It is farming *and* the whole food system: knowledge, culture,
   fair markets, who makes decisions."
   Point to **T-P20**: "Permaculture gives us a design toolbox — twelve ways to design.
   Agroecology gives us the science, the practice and a movement. We use both."
7. **Take home (11:20).** **ASK** each group: "Which element will you look for in your village
   this week?" Reporter answers in one sentence.

**Unpack (lead) — "step out of the farmer role":**

8. **DO (11:25):** Everyone stands, turns round once, sits. "Now you are teachers again."
   **ASK** each Recorder: "How many marks?" Write them up. Average them.
   *(Usually 10–14 minutes of 45 = 22–31%.)*
   Reveal **T-P04 teacher-talk meter**. **SAY:** "≤ 30% is our rule. In a 60-minute session,
   18 minutes. From Round 2 tomorrow, an observer will count *your* talk."
   **ASK:** "Where did the talking happen?" *(Setting up the task; revealing the poster.)*
   "Where could it have been shorter?"
9. **GROUPS (11:33, 12 min):** Sticky notes on **T-P03**. "What did the teacher *do* in each step?"
   *(Look: uncovered a poster and asked, didn't tell. Try: gave materials to every group,
   walked and asked "why there?". Talk: one answer per group; poster revealed **after** the
   activity. Take home: each group said an action.)*
   **ASK:** "What was the teacher doing while you sorted?" *(Walking, listening, asking — not
   explaining.)*
10. **Content confidence (11:45, 20 min).** Hand every trainee 2 dot stickers.
    **SAY:** "Put a dot on the element or principle you'd be least sure to explain to a farmer."
    Take the **two** with most dots. For each: **ASK** "Who can explain it with a village
    example?" — a trainee explains (2 min), others add, the trainer fills gaps from the notes
    below (2 min). Other questions → Question Tree, answered by 16:00 or tomorrow.
11. **Key messages (12:05).** Say them; ask one trainee to repeat them in isiZulu or Sesotho.

**Practice photo cards (20, cut from magazines or printed):** mulched bed · mixed intercrop ·
seed swap table · compost heap · chickens under fruit trees · a roof tank · a stokvel meeting ·
a gogo cooking imifino · a youth selling at a taxi rank · a ward councillor meeting · a
farmers' WhatsApp group · a farmer showing a farmer · worm farm · bees near crops · a swale ·
maize + beans + pumpkin · a community market · a family meal · a seed bank shelf · a school
garden.

### Content refresh notes (for trainers — and for trainees to copy)

**The three ethics:** *Earth care* (look after soil, water, living things) · *People care*
(look after ourselves, family, community) · *Fair share* (set limits, share surplus, return
waste to the soil).

**FAO's 10 elements of agroecology — say it simply:**

| Element | Say it like this | Village example |
|---|---|---|
| Diversity | Many crops, animals and trees together | Maize, beans, pumpkin + chickens + a fruit tree |
| Co-creation & sharing of knowledge | Farmers and scientists learn together and teach each other | A lead farmer shows neighbours her trench bed |
| Synergies | Things that help each other | Chickens eat pests and give manure |
| Efficiency | More from less bought-in stuff | Compost instead of bought fertiliser |
| Recycling | Waste becomes food for the soil | Kitchen scraps → compost → cabbage |
| Resilience | The garden bounces back after drought, pests or a price rise | Mulch + tank + many crops |
| Human & social values | Dignity, fairness, women's and youth voices | Youth and gogos plan the garden together |
| Culture & food traditions | Honour local foods and ways of eating | Imifino, amadumbe, sorghum |
| Responsible governance | Fair rules about land, water and seed | A garden committee with a water roster |
| Circular & solidarity economy | Money and food stay local and fair | Selling to neighbours, a stokvel, a seed swap |

**Twelve permaculture design principles (T-P20), with a garden example each:**
1. Observe & interact — watch the site for a season before big changes.
2. Catch & store energy — a tank, mulch, seed, compost.
3. Obtain a yield — plant something you can eat soon, even while building.
4. Self-regulate & accept feedback — if the bed floods, change it.
5. Use & value renewables — sun, rain, manure, not bought inputs.
6. Produce no waste — every output is someone's input.
7. Design from patterns to details — see the slope and water first, then choose the bed.
8. Integrate rather than segregate — chickens next to the garden, not far away.
9. Use small & slow solutions — one good bed before ten weak ones.
10. Use & value diversity — many crops; nobody's "wrong" plant.
11. Use edges & value the margins — the fence line, the wet corner, the quiet learner.
12. Creatively use & respond to change — a drought year → plant drought-tolerant crops.
(These twelve are David Holmgren's widely taught set. Course 1 uses six simpler garden
principles on F-P04; trainers should know how the two fit.)

**Farmers' questions you will hear — good short answers:**

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Is agroecology just organic farming? | No. Organic is a rule set for what you may use. Agroecology is also about knowledge, fairness, culture and markets. Many agroecological farmers are not certified organic. |
| Can I never use fertiliser? | Build the soil first; many farmers find they need less or none. The course won't tell you what you *must* never do — it shows you cheaper ways that last. |
| Why not hybrid seed? | Hybrid (F1) seed can yield well but won't grow true if you save it; you must buy each season. Open-pollinated seed can be saved. Many farmers use both. |
| Is permaculture from overseas? | The word is Australian (1970s). Many of the practices — mixed crops, mulch, seed saving — are old African practice. Ask the elders. |

### Debrief (the three questions — for the unpack)
1. What did the teacher do? 2. Why did the card sort work better than a talk about 10 elements?
3. What will you copy tomorrow in Round 2?

### Key messages
- Agroecology = the practice **and** the food system (10 elements). Permaculture = the design
  toolbox (3 ethics, 12 principles). We teach both.
- Reveal the poster **after** learners have tried — the poster names what they found.
- Teacher talk is **measured**, not guessed. ≤ 30%.
- If you don't know an answer: "Good question — I'll find out by tomorrow." Then do.

### If things go wrong
- **Trainees won't "play farmer":** say "just be yourself — you are a farmer too." Keep going.
- **A permaculture-trained trainee lectures the group:** give them the Recorder role and ask
  them to draw, not talk. In the unpack, thank them for the knowledge and ask "how would you
  help a farmer find that out for themselves?"
- **Card sort takes too long:** stop at 11:07, whatever the state. Report what they have.

### Language & inclusion
- The photo cards need no reading. Element cards carry an icon as well as the words.
- Offer element names in isiZulu/Sesotho if a trainee can translate; write both on the flipchart.
- ✂ **Cut if short:** content-confidence session to one element (10 min).

## T-D1-S3 · 12:55–14:00 · Poster studio 1: a poster that teaches (65 min)

**By the end, trainees can:** **name** six poster rules, **make** a rough teaching poster for
their Round 1 micro-teach, and **check** a partner's poster from 6 m.

**Poster:** T-P09, T-P11 · **Slides:** T-D1-13 → T-D1-15
**Materials:** 1 "bad poster" (trainers make it on Day 0: an A1 sheet covered in small text,
three ideas, a clip-art cabbage, no question); F-P11 printed; per trainee: 1 half-flipchart
sheet (A2) or A3 card, 1 pencil, eraser, access to kokis (4 sets of 10 colours — one per group);
the **Round 1 card pool** MT-01 → MT-10 (2 sets); 20 "6-metre check" cards.
**Room:** poster studio corner + floor.

| Time | Step | Trainer talk |
|---|---|---|
| 12:55 | Energiser (trainer models it) | 1 min |
| 13:00 | Look — bad poster vs good poster at 6 m | 2 min |
| 13:05 | Talk — T-P09 rules | 5 min |
| 13:15 | Choose your Round 1 card | 1 min |
| 13:18 | Try — draw your poster | 3 min |
| 13:43 | 6-metre check | 1 min |
| 13:51 | Take home — fix one thing; prep | 1 min |

### Say / ask / do

1. **DO (12:55):** Run *Compost Heap* from **T-P11** (5 min). **SAY:** "From tomorrow **you** run
   the energisers — two volunteers a day."
2. **DO (13:00):** Hold the bad poster up at the far wall. **ASK:** "From your chair — what does
   it teach?" Then hold up F-P11. "And this one?"
   *(Can't read the bad one; F-P11 — big picture, few words, we can see the layers.)*
3. **GROUPS (13:05, 6 min):** "List what's wrong with the bad poster. Each problem becomes a rule."
   Reporters call one each. Reveal **T-P09**, point ①–⑥ and match their rules:
   ① One idea · ② ≤ 25 words · ③ Headline ≤ 6 words · ④ Big picture, no words inside it ·
   ⑤ Numbered labels · ⑥ One question for the room.
   **SAY (short):** "Also: sun in the north, people who look like our farmers, local materials,
   black-and-white must still work. The full rules are in the poster standards."
4. **DO (13:15):** Each group takes one Round 1 pool (MT-01 → MT-10). Each member picks a
   **different** card. They read it (or a partner reads it to them).
5. **SAY (13:18):** "Draw a poster for your five-minute lesson. Pencil first. Headline, big
   picture, up to three numbered labels, one question at the bottom. You have 25 minutes."
   **DO:** Walk and ask, don't draw for them: "What's the one idea?" "Can I read it from the door?"
   Call "10 minutes left", "5 minutes left".
6. **DO (13:43):** 6-metre check. Pairs swap posters; one holds it at the far wall; the other
   ticks the card: can I read the headline? count the words? one idea? a question? (4 min each way).
7. **SAY (13:51):** "Fix one thing. Then plan your five minutes: what will they **do**?"
   Use remaining minutes to prepare objects.

### Debrief
One question: "Which rule was hardest?" *(Usually ≤ 25 words.)*

### Key messages
- A teaching poster has **one idea, few words, a big picture and a question**.
- The picture has **no words inside** so it can be translated.
- Check it from **6 metres**, not from your lap.

### If things go wrong
- **"I can't draw":** stick figures and simple shapes are fine; the rules matter more than art.
  Offer tracing from a printed F-poster.
- **Kokis run out:** pencil and one dark koki for outlines is enough.

### Language & inclusion
- Headline may be in any language; encourage trainees to write it in the language they will teach in.
- Trainees who don't write comfortably can draw only; a partner writes the labels they say.
- ✂ **Cut if short:** the 6-metre check becomes a quick hold-up from the front.

## T-D1-S4 · 14:00–15:50 · Micro-teaching Round 1: five minutes, solo (110 min)

**By the end, trainees can:** **teach** one idea for 5 minutes using a poster and one real
object, and **give** *two stars and a wish*.

**Poster:** T-P10, T-P03 · **Slides:** T-D1-16 → T-D1-17
**Materials (per group of 5):** 5 observer sheets (Round 1 short version); 1 timer (phone);
2 time cards ("1 min left" orange, "STOP" red); the group's objects; their posters; Prestik.
**Room:** 4 corners of Room A (or 2 inside, 2 outside), each group in a small circle facing
its own wall.

| Time | Step | Trainer talk |
|---|---|---|
| 14:00 | Briefing: how a micro-teach works | 6 min |
| 14:10 | Model: trainer teaches 5 minutes, co-trainer gives feedback | 2 min |
| 14:20 | ROUND 1: 5 teachers × (5 teach + 5 feedback) in each group | 0 min |
| 15:10 | Stretch (a trainee leads *Rain Storm*) | 0 min |
| 15:15 | Plenary: what worked? | 8 min |
| 15:40 | Mode tracker + journal | 4 min |

### Say / ask / do

1. **SAY (14:00, lead):** "Each of you teaches your group for **five minutes**. Your group are
   farmers. Then five minutes of feedback. Five teachers per group, fifty minutes."
2. **Roles in each teach:** the **teacher**; the **observer** (the person who teaches *next*),
   who fills the observer sheet; the **timekeeper** (the person who taught *last*), who shows
   "1 min left" at 4:00 and "STOP" at 5:00 — and the teacher stops mid-sentence.
3. **Feedback in 5 minutes (point to T-P10):**
   - Teacher first (1 min): "What went well? What would I change?"
   - Observer: ⭐ ⭐ 🌱 — two specific stars, one wish (2 min).
   - One more star from anyone (1 min).
   - Teacher says only: "Thank you. Next time I will…" (1 min). **No defending, no arguing.**
4. **Round 1 observer sheet (three ticks only):** Did learners **do** something with their
   hands? Did the teacher **ask** a question and wait? Did it end with a **take home**?
5. **DO (14:10) — model it.** Lead teaches 5 minutes: MT-03 *Mulch holds water* (two 2 ℓ
   bottle halves, one bare, one mulched; a trainee pours 500 ml on each). **Deliberately**
   talk too long at the start. Co-trainer runs the feedback exactly as in step 3 — the wish:
   "Next time let them pour the water before you explain."
6. **DO (14:20):** Groups start. Order: alphabetical by first name. Trainers walk, stand
   behind the circle, **never interrupt**, and make notes of one star to share in plenary.
7. **DO (15:10):** A trainee leads *Rain Storm* (T-P11).
8. **ASK (15:15):** "How did it feel to teach? To be taught?" (3 answers each)
   "What made five minutes work?" Build an **"Our teaching tips"** list on a flipchart.
   *(Start with a question; give them something to hold; say less; stop on time; a take home.)*
   Trainers add two stars they saw (named, with permission).
9. **DO (15:40):** Each trainee ticks **Poster** on the Mode tracker. Journal: "My wish for
   myself for Round 2."

### Debrief (the three questions)
1. What did you do? 2. What made five minutes work? 3. What will you do in Round 2?

### Key messages
- Five minutes is enough to teach **one** idea well.
- The observer is a gift. Say "thank you", not "but".
- Stop on time — farmers' time is precious too.

### If things go wrong
- **A trainee freezes:** the timekeeper gives them the poster question to ask. If still stuck,
  they may start again after the next person. Never skip them.
- **Feedback turns harsh:** trainer steps in: "Let's make that a wish: 'next time maybe…'".
- **A group finishes early:** they re-teach the shortest lesson with the wish applied.

### Language & inclusion
- Teach in the language you'd use with farmers. Observers who don't share the language watch
  the hands, the objects and the faces.
- A trainee who cannot stand long may teach seated.
- ✂ **Cut if short:** plenary to 15 minutes; never cut the feedback inside the groups.
