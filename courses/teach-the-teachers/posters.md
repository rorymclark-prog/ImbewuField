# Posters · Course 3 · Teach the Teachers

Two sets:

1. **Teaching posters T-P01 → T-P22** — this course's own posters about *how to teach*, plus
   three content posters for trainers (T-P19 10 elements, T-P20 design principles, T-P21 the
   side-by-side trial). Fully specified below.
2. **The content poster library** — the Course 1 set **F-P01 → F-P21**, used in model lessons and
   micro-teaching, listed with one line each (full specs in `farmer-5day/posters.md`).

All posters follow `shared/poster-standards.md`: A1 portrait unless stated, ≤ 25 words, headline
≤ 6 words, text-free art, numbered callouts, one question for the room, Paper background,
Forest top strip with the ID in Leaf. Word counts below include headline + labels + question.

**Print guide for a Course 3 kit:** 20 × A1 portrait + 2 × A0 landscape (T-P19, T-P20) of the T set,
plus the 21 F posters. Paper for indoor venues; PVC banner for field kits.

## T-P01 · Our learning agreement
- Size: A1 portrait
- Used in: T-D1-S0, T-D2-S0, T-D5-SC
- Art: T-P01-ART

**Headline:** Our learning agreement
**Art brief (text-free):** Top 45% only. Eight adults of mixed ages sit in a circle on low chairs
and a bench under a wide shade tree; a gogo in a headscarf, a young man in a cap, a woman with
a notebook, an older man with a walking stick, two young women, an extension officer in a
work shirt. In the centre, all their hands rest together on a large blank sheet of paper on the
ground. Warm afternoon light from the upper left (north). Lower 55% of the poster is left as
plain Paper with faint Hairline ruled lines for writing the group's rules and signatures.
**Labels on the poster (11 words):**
1. We made it
2. We keep it
Question: *What helps you learn?*
**Layout:** Headline band; art in the top 45%; labels ① ② as two small ochre tags at the left of
the ruled area; ruled writing area 50%; question strip at the bottom.
**Teach it — back-of-poster card (A5):**
```
T-P01  Our learning agreement                     (≈ 15 min)
1. ASK: What would help you learn this week? One idea per sticky note.
2. DO: Groups agree 3; Reporters stick them on the lines.
3. DO: Cluster and read back. Add missing must-haves (phones, time,
   right to pass, no correcting language, feedback = thank you,
   Question Tree).
4. DO: Everyone signs or marks. A thumbprint counts.
5. ASK (every morning): Are we keeping it? What needs to change?
KEY: An agreement made by the group is kept by the group.
```

## T-P02 · How adults learn — the learning cycle
- Size: A1 portrait
- Used in: T-D1-S1, T-D1-S2, T-D5-S4
- Art: T-P02-ART

**Headline:** How adults learn
**Art brief (text-free):** A large circular loop with four thick Mid-green arrows running
clockwise, and a scene in each quarter. Top: a young woman folds a paper seed envelope (doing).
Right: she and a friend look closely at her torn envelope, the friend pointing (looking back).
Bottom: a group of four crouches around a simple drawing scratched in the soil with a stick,
one nodding (making sense). Left: at home, the same woman pours bean seed into a finished
envelope at a kitchen table (using it). Centre of the loop: empty Paper.
**Labels on the poster (15 words):**
1. Do
2. Look back
3. Make sense
4. Use it
Question: *Where do your lessons stop?*
**Layout:** Headline top; loop fills 60%; ①–④ ochre circles on each arrow; labels band below.
**Teach it — back-of-poster card (A5):**
```
T-P02  How adults learn                          (≈ 12 min)
1. DO first: any short hands-on task (fold a seed envelope).
2. POINT ①: What did you do?
3. POINT ②: What did you notice? What went wrong?
4. POINT ③: What is the rule? Why does it work?
5. POINT ④: When will you use it at home?
6. SAY: Adults want the why, bring experience, learn from real
   problems, need respect. Go all the way round.
KEY: Most bad lessons stop at "make sense". Finish with "use it".
```

## T-P03 · Every lesson: Look · Try · Talk · Take home
- Size: A1 portrait
- Used in: T-D1-S1, T-D1-S2, T-D1-S4, T-D5-S2, all micro-teaching
- Art: T-P03-ART

**Headline:** Look · Try · Talk · Take home
**Art brief (text-free):** Four horizontal bands stacked top to bottom, one scene each, same
people throughout. ① A facilitator holds up a real cabbage leaf with holes; seated learners
lean forward, two pointing. ② A group of five kneels at a bed, every pair of hands in the soil,
the facilitator standing back, hands behind her back. ③ The group sits in a circle beside a
poster on a fence, one learner speaking, others listening. ④ A learner walks along a village
path toward a house carrying a seedling tray. Each band has a thin Hairline divider.
**Labels on the poster (16 words):**
1. Hook them
2. Hands on
3. Three questions
4. One action
Question: *Who talks most in each step?*
**Layout:** Headline top; four bands 15% each; ①–④ ochre circles at the left edge of each band;
labels at the right edge of each band; question strip at the bottom.
**Teach it — back-of-poster card (A5):**
```
T-P03  Look · Try · Talk · Take home             (≈ 10 min)
1. ASK: Think of a lesson you just had. What came first?
2. POINT ①: Look — a question, object, story or poster reveal.
3. POINT ②: Try — every learner's hands, in groups of 5.
4. POINT ③: Talk — What did you do? Why does it work?
   What will you do at home?
5. POINT ④: Take home — each group says one action.
6. ASK: Who talks most in each step? (Learners, except a
   short set-up.)
KEY: Try comes before the long explanation.
```

## T-P04 · The teacher-talk meter
- Size: A1 portrait
- Used in: T-D1-S2, T-D2-S4, T-D3-S0, all micro-teaching
- Art: T-P04-ART

**Headline:** Teacher talk: 30% or less
**Art brief (text-free):** Top half: a big half-circle gauge, like a fuel gauge, split into ten
equal wedges — three Ochre wedges on the left, seven Leaf-green wedges on the right (the Leaf
wedges also carry small dot pattern so it works in black and white). A thick Ink needle points
into the third Ochre wedge. Bottom half: a facilitator stands to one side with an open hand
gesturing toward a busy group of five who are talking and working; beside the facilitator a
co-facilitator makes tally marks on a clipboard (marks drawn as simple strokes).
**Labels on the poster (20 words):**
1. Teacher: 3 in 10
2. Learners: 7 in 10
3. One mark a minute
Question: *Who counted today?*
**Layout:** Headline top; gauge 35%; people 25%; ① on the Ochre wedges, ② on the Leaf wedges,
③ on the clipboard; labels band; question strip.
**Teach it — back-of-poster card (A5):**
```
T-P04  The teacher-talk meter                    (≈ 8 min)
1. ASK: How much of that lesson was the teacher talking? Guess.
2. DO: Read the tally — one mark per minute of talk to the room.
3. SAY: Talk % = marks ÷ minutes × 100. 60-min session = 18 min max.
4. ASK: Where did the talk happen? Where could it be shorter?
5. SAY: Biggest cut: give the task first, explain after the Try.
KEY: Talk is counted, not guessed. ≤ 30%.
```

## T-P05 · Outcomes start with a doing word
- Size: A1 portrait
- Used in: T-D2-S1, all micro-teach planning
- Art: T-P05-ART

**Headline:** Start with a doing word
**Art brief (text-free):** A young farmer stands proudly beside a finished, neatly layered
compost heap about waist high, pointing to its layers. A facilitator beside her holds a
clipboard and draws a tick shape. In the background, a clock shape on a wall shows the hands
near twelve (midday). Three big ochre callout circles sit on: the farmer's pointing hand (①),
the heap (②), and the heap's visible layers (③).
**Labels on the poster (17 words):**
1. A doing word
2. What
3. How well
Question: *Could you see it by lunch?*
**Layout:** Headline top; art 60%; labels band; question strip. A blank flipchart hangs beside
it for the verbs list in local languages.
**Teach it — back-of-poster card (A5):**
```
T-P05  Start with a doing word                   (≈ 12 min)
1. SHOW two outcomes: "understand compost" vs "build a heap
   1 m wide in green and brown layers".
2. ASK: At lunch, how would you know each one happened?
3. POINT ①②③: doing word · what · how well.
4. SAY the verbs: make, show, find, measure, explain, name,
   choose, draw, plan, record.
5. SAY foggy words to avoid: understand, know, learn about,
   be aware of, appreciate.
6. DO: Fix three foggy outcomes in groups.
KEY: If you can't see it or hear it, it isn't an outcome.
```

## T-P07 · A practical, not a demo
- Size: A1 portrait
- Used in: T-D3-S1, T-D3-S2, T-D3-S4, T-D5-S2
- Art: T-P07-ART

**Headline:** Hands on, not a demo
**Art brief (text-free):** Two panels. Small top-left panel (one third width), drawn in faded
greys: one facilitator digging while a crowd of about twenty stands watching, arms folded; a
thick Ochre diagonal bar crosses the panel (a "no" sign made of a shape, not a letter). Large
main panel: four groups of five at four stations spaced apart on open ground, every person
working — digging, carrying, mixing, measuring; each station has its own small pile of tools
and a crate. The facilitator walks between stations with her hands clasped behind her back.
**Labels on the poster (24 words):**
1. Kit per group
2. Demo: 3 minutes
3. Groups of 5
4. Stations apart
5. Hands behind back
6. One kit? Carousel
Question: *Who watched?*
**Layout:** Headline top; art 60% (①② near the crates and facilitator, ③④ on the stations,
⑤ on her hands, ⑥ on an arrow looping between stations); labels in two rows; question.
**Teach it — back-of-poster card (A5):**
```
T-P07  Hands on, not a demo                      (≈ 10 min)
1. STORY (1 min): In 2022, practicals became one person digging while
   25 watched — even with kit for four groups in the car.
2. ASK: Why does it happen? Which reasons can we fix?
3. POINT ①–⑥: count kit per group · show 3 min max · groups of 5
   with roles · stations 3 m apart · hands behind your back ·
   one kit = carousel, nobody watches more than 10 min.
4. SAFETY: tools points down; nobody behind a swing; gloves or soap;
   hats and water; first aid kit open; lightning = stop.
5. DO: Plan a carousel for one A-frame and 25 learners.
KEY: If you take the spade, the practical is over.
```

## T-P08 · The question ladder
- Size: A1 portrait
- Used in: T-D2-S1, T-D2-S2, all micro-teaching
- Art: T-P08-ART

**Headline:** Climb the question ladder
**Art brief (text-free):** A tall wooden ladder with three rungs leans against a tree. On the
bottom rung, a small figure beside one short speech-bubble shape (empty). On the middle rung,
two figures with several branching bubble shapes. On the top rung, a figure looks out over a
whole garden, hand on chin, with a bubble shaped like a sprouting seed. At the ladder's foot:
an open hand with five fingers spread (wait), and a group of three passing a ball between them
(bounce back).
**Labels on the poster (19 words):**
1. Closed
2. Open
3. Deeper
4. Wait 5 seconds
5. Bounce it back
Question: *What do others think?*
**Layout:** Headline top; ladder art 60%, ①–③ at each rung, ④ on the hand, ⑤ on the ball;
labels band; question.
**Teach it — back-of-poster card (A5):**
```
T-P08  Climb the question ladder                 (≈ 12 min)
1. ASK three: "Is soil alive?" · "What lives in soil?" ·
   "How could you find out if YOUR soil is alive?"
2. ASK: Which got the best thinking?
3. POINT ①: Closed — one right answer; for checking.
   ②: Open — many answers: what, how, tell me.
   ③: Deeper — why? how do you know? what if? what will you do?
4. POINT ④: Count five fingers silently before you speak again.
5. POINT ⑤: "What do others think?" — never answer your own question.
6. SAY: Not "Any questions?" — "What questions do you have?"
KEY: Climb the ladder, then wait.
```
