## R-D3-S1 · 09:30–10:30 · Who buys, what they want, fair price, selling together (60 min)

**By the end, learners can:** name at least four kinds of buyers and what each wants, choose
the best buyer for a case family, show what "good quality" means, and explain how selling
together helps.

**Poster:** R-P14 · **Slides:** R-D3-02 → R-D3-05 · **Clip:** R-ANI-08, R-ANI-09 (optional)
**Materials (per group of 5):** 6 buyer cards (R-IMG-10, facts on the back — see printables
P16), family card, flipchart, markers. **Whole class:** price board; two bunches of spinach — one
fresh, clean, even; one wilted, muddy, mixed sizes.
**Room:** tables.

| Time | Step | Teacher talk? |
|---|---|---|
| 09:30 | Hook: the price board (homework) | 2 min |
| 09:38 | R-P14 · GROUPS: the best buyer for our family | 4 min |
| 09:58 | Good bunch, bad bunch | 3 min |
| 10:05 | Selling together | 5 min |
| 10:20 | Debrief + key messages | 3 min |

Teacher talk: **17 of 60 min (28%).**

### Say / ask / do
1. DO: Price board. ASK: "What prices did you see for one bunch of spinach?" Recorder writes
   each price called out, and where. *(spaza R15, hawker R10, neighbour R12, supermarket R25)*
2. ASK: "Why are they different?" *(the spaza has to make a profit; the hawker pays for
   transport; the supermarket packs and chills; bunch sizes differ)* SAY: "So **who** you sell to
   changes your price."
3. DO: Point to **R-P14 Who buys? What do they want?**: ① neighbour / at the gate ② spaza shop
   ③ hawker ④ creche or school kitchen ⑤ market stall ⑥ selling together as a group.
   (Tablet: R-ANI-08, surplus going three ways.)
4. GROUPS (15 min): "Read the back of each buyer card — or ask us to read it. For **your
   family**, put the six cards in order: best buyer on top. Be ready to say **why**, using:
   how many they want, what price, how and when they pay, what quality, how far to take it."
   *(Dlamini often chooses neighbours + creche; Mokoena: spaza + hawker, with care about
   month-end credit; Sizanani: creche for a steady order, neighbours for the rest)*
5. DO: Each Reporter gives the top buyer and one reason (30 seconds each).
6. DO: Hold up the two bunches. ASK: "Which would you buy? Why?" *(fresh, clean, same size,
   no holes)* SAY: "The spaza owner thinks the same. Quality **is** price."
   Write the checklist beside R-P14: **Picked early · Cool and shaded · Clean · Same size ·
   On time · Same every week.**
7. SAY: "One more buyer: yourselves, together." Tell the story (Tablet: R-ANI-09, or show
   R-IMG-13):
   *"Five neighbours near Lerato each have about 20 bunches a week. The creche wants 100 bunches
   every Monday. Alone, nobody can supply it. Together they can. They also share one taxi: R60
   for all, not R30 each — R150."*
8. GROUPS (7 min): "What must the five neighbours agree on to sell together?"
   *(same quality and bunch size; who delivers; one person keeps the book; every member gets a
   receipt for what she brought; when and how they share the money; what happens if someone
   brings bad spinach)*
   SAY: "That is a co-operative in its simplest form. Some groups later register as a co-op —
   ask your programme or the local agriculture office how. Start with the records."

### Debrief (the three questions)
1. What did you find? *(each buyer wants different things; quality matters; together we can
   reach bigger buyers)*
2. Why not just sell to whoever comes? → R-P14. *(the price, the payment time and the transport
   change what you keep)*
3. What will you do at home? *(ask one buyer what they want and when; tie even, clean bunches)*

### Key messages
- **Who you sell to changes your price** and when you are paid.
- **Quality is price:** fresh, clean, same size, on time, every week.
- **Selling together** reaches bigger buyers and shares transport — if the records are clear.

### If things go wrong
- **Learners have no buyers yet:** that's fine — the neighbours and the family are buyers too.
  "Selling surplus" starts with what the family doesn't eat.
- **Someone asks about selling to supermarkets:** SAY: "Big buyers need steady amounts,
  packing, certificates and often long payment times. It starts with selling together and keeping
  records. Put it on the Question Tree for your mentor."
- **Questions about NSNP / school feeding contracts:** SAY: "Schools often buy through appointed
  suppliers. Ask the school who buys, and whether the supplier buys from local growers."

### Language & inclusion notes
- Buyer cards have a picture on the front. Facts on the back use numbers and small icons
  (clock, coin, truck) for non-readers.

✂ **Cut if short:** step 5 (reports) — ask only two groups.

## R-D3-S2 · 10:50–12:30 · Market day: order → deliver → invoice → receipt → paid (100 min) **[APP/PAPER]**

**By the end, learners can:** follow a sale from order to payment, write an invoice and a
receipt, and keep track of money owed.

**Poster:** R-P15 · **Slides:** R-D3-06 → R-D3-12 · **Clip:** R-ANI-03 (optional)
**Materials (per group of 5):** 20 paper "bunch" tokens (green card strips), play money bag,
1 duplicate invoice book (P6) and 1 receipt book (P7) — or photocopied pads, 1 money book with
an *Owed to me* page, carbon paper if the books are not carbonless. **Buyer groups:** 1 buyer
role card each (P16); each buyer group counts a R1 500 wallet out of its own bag.
**Whole class:** 3 twist cards. **App ON:** 1 phone per group.
**Room:** the market corner — 3 buyer tables; seller groups at their own tables.

| Time | Step | Teacher talk? |
|---|---|---|
| 10:50 | Hook · R-P15: what each paper proves | 5 min |
| 10:58 | 3-minute demo: one full sale | 3 min |
| 11:01 | Set up roles | 2 min |
| 11:05 | Market round 1 | 2 min |
| 11:25 | Swap: market round 2 | 2 min |
| 11:45 | [APP] / [PAPER] — my own invoice and receipt | 3 min |
| 12:05 | Who still owes? | 2 min |
| 12:10 | Debrief, key messages, language time | 8 min |

Teacher talk: **27 of 100 min (27%).**

### Say / ask / do
1. ASK: "The creche phones Lerato: 'Bring 30 bunches on Monday.' She brings them. The cook
   says 'We pay at month-end.' At month-end the cook says: 'It was only 20 bunches.'
   What can Lerato show?" *(nothing — unless she has paper)*
2. DO: Point to **R-P15**, the five steps (Tablet: R-ANI-03):
   ① **Order** — what they want, how many, what price, when.
   ② **Deliver** — the buyer counts and signs.
   ③ **Invoice** — the bill: what was delivered and what they must pay, by when.
   ④ **Paid** — the money comes, cash or into the bank.
   ⑤ **Receipt** — proof they paid. The buyer keeps the top copy; you keep the copy.
   SAY: "Each paper proves one thing. The invoice says *you owe me*. The receipt says *you paid
   me*. Keep the copies in your book."
3. DO (3-min demo): Facilitator is the seller, co-facilitator is the creche cook. Take an order
   (30 bunches at R12, Monday), deliver 30 tokens, the cook counts and signs the invoice, cook
   pays R360 in play money, seller writes a receipt. Speak each step out loud. **Then stop — the
   groups do it.**
4. DO: Set up. Groups Bean, Pumpkin, Maize are **sellers** (Round 1). Groups Banana, Marula,
   Spinach are **buyers** — each goes to a table: SPAZA, CRECHE or HAWKER, with its role card
   and a wallet of **R1 500** counted out of its own play-money bag.
5. GROUPS (Round 1, 20 min): "Buyers: read your role card. Visit **every** seller once and
   place a real order. Sellers: take the order, deliver tokens, write an invoice, get paid (now
   or later — the buyer decides from the card), write a receipt when paid. Any money not paid
   goes on your **Owed to me** page."
   DO: At 11:22 ring the bell and call **"Month-end!"** — buyers who promised to pay at
   month-end now pay; sellers write receipts and tick the Owed page. "Next Friday" and "next
   week" stay owed — that is the point.
   Role cards (P16) say, for example:
   - SPAZA: "Order 20 bunches. Offer R9. Accept R10. Pay **half now, half next Friday**."
   - CRECHE: "Order 30 bunches for Monday at R12. You need an invoice for the committee. Pay
     **at month-end**."
   - HAWKER: "Order 15 bunches. Bargain hard — start at R7. Pay **cash now**. You don't want paper."
6. DO (at 11:15): Play **twist cards** — one to each buyer table:
   - "5 of the bunches you received are wilted. Return them and ask for a new invoice amount."
   - "The committee treasurer is away — you can only pay next week."
   - "You paid, but the seller forgot to give a receipt. Ask for it."
7. DO (11:25): Swap: sellers become buyers, buyers become sellers. Round 2, same rules, 20 min.
   New buyer groups count a fresh R1 500 wallet out of their own bag. Call "Month-end!" at 11:42.
8. SAY (step 11:45): "Now each person writes their own invoice and receipt for the Sizanani
   garden: **Monday order from the creche, 30 bunches at R12 = R360, due at month-end.** Then on
   the 30th the creche pays R360."

### [APP] 11:45–12:05 · The Invoice builder
9. GROUPS (17 min): "In each group, each person with a phone makes one invoice; others watch
   and check, then swap.
   - Open **Invoice** (top bar) or **My Records → Sold → New sale & invoice**.
   - *What are you recording?* → **A new invoice**.
   - *Invoice issue date*: today.
   - *Has the buyer paid?* → **Not yet — payment outstanding**.
   - Bill to: *Choose a customer* or type the creche name. Customer type: creche/school.
   - Item: spinach · Quantity 30 · Unit: bunch · Price each R12. Check the **Total due R360**.
   - Payment due: month-end. Add a note: *'Delivered Monday, counted and signed by the cook.'*
   - **Save invoice**. Tap **Share PDF** to see how you would send it on WhatsApp — **don't send
     it to a real person today.**
   - Now pretend it is month-end: open the saved invoice, change *Has the buyer paid?* to
     **Yes — paid in full**, add the *Payment received date*, save."
   SAY: "Unpaid invoices stay **outstanding** in the app — that is your *Owed to me* page.
   When you mark it paid, the sale goes into **My Records** once. Don't also type it in *Sold* —
   that would count it twice."
   Note for facilitators: bunches are not turned into kilograms by the app — that is fine.

### [PAPER] 11:45–12:05 · The duplicate invoice book and receipt book
9. GROUPS (17 min): "Each person, in your own books:
   - **Invoice** (P6): number, date, your name and phone, *Bill to* (the creche), what
     (spinach), how many (30), unit (bunch), price each (R12), total (R360), due date
     (month-end), buyer's signature on delivery. Press hard — the copy stays in your book.
   - Write the R360 on the **Owed to me** page.
   - Now it is month-end: the creche pays. Write a **receipt** (P7): number, date, *received
     from*, amount in numbers and words (*three hundred and sixty rand*), for (invoice number),
     cash or bank, your signature.
   - Tick the R360 off the Owed page. Write R360 **in** in the money book."
   Readers pair with non-readers. Numbers and signatures are what matter.

10. DO (12:05): **Who still owes?** Each seller group reads its Owed page aloud: who, how much,
    by when. ASK: "How will you remind a buyer politely?" Practise one line in the local
    language: *"Good day Mme. Just a reminder: invoice 12, R200, was due on Friday. When can
    you pay?"*

### Debrief (the three questions)
1. What happened? *(some paid cash, some paid half, some owe; the wilted bunches changed the
   invoice; the hawker wanted no paper)*
2. Why the papers? → R-P15. *(each proves one step; you can show what was delivered and what
   was paid; nobody argues later)*
3. What will you do at home? *(buy a duplicate book; write a receipt for every sale over R50;
   keep an Owed page)*

### Key messages
- **Order → deliver → invoice → paid → receipt.** Each paper proves one step.
- **Money owed is not money in the tin.** Keep the Owed page and follow up politely.
- **Credit rules:** agree the pay date in writing; no new credit until the old is paid; only
  give credit you could afford to lose.
- Write a sale **once** — when it is paid, tick it off; don't enter it again.

### If things go wrong
- **Market round becomes chaos:** ring the bell; freeze; ask each seller group "How many bunches
  have you sold? How much is owed?" Then restart.
- **Buyers ignore the role card and pay everything cash:** the co-facilitator plays the
  treasurer who "can only pay next week" for every group.
- **Duplicate books didn't arrive:** use two sheets with carbon paper, or write the invoice
  twice. The habit matters, not the book.
- **Someone asks if the app invoice is a tax invoice:** SAY: "It's a clear invoice. For VAT or
  tax questions, a registered seller must follow SARS rules — most small growers are far below
  the VAT line. Ask SARS or your mentor if your sales grow big." (App Finance unit 7 covers this.)

### Language & inclusion notes
- Amounts in words on a receipt are hard: accept numbers only, with the buyer's signature.
- Hawker bargaining can get loud and fun — keep it; but make sure quiet learners get a turn as
  the seller who writes.

✂ **Cut if short:** Round 2 (step 7) — go straight to the individual invoice.

## R-D3-S3 · 13:15–14:30 · Borrow wisely + the reserve tin (75 min)

**By the end, learners can:** work out the total cost of a loan, compare four ways to get the
money for a tool, spot an illegal lender, and plan a reserve.

**Poster:** R-P16 · **Slides:** R-D3-13 → R-D3-16 · **Clip:** R-ANI-04 (optional)
**Materials (per group of 5):** 4 loan offer cards (P14), play money bag (at least R5 000),
the Sizanani season card (monthly garden cash), 1 small tin labelled RESERVE, flipchart.
**Room:** tables.

| Time | Step | Teacher talk? |
|---|---|---|
| 13:15 | Energiser: Pass the Clap | 1 min |
| 13:20 | Hook: Bongani wants a pump | 2 min |
| 13:23 | R-P16: five questions before you borrow | 5 min |
| 13:28 | GROUPS: four ways to get R3 000 | 4 min |
| 13:50 | Spot the illegal lender | 4 min |
| 14:00 | The reserve tin | 3 min |
| 14:15 | Debrief + key messages | 3 min |

Teacher talk: **22 of 75 min (29%).**

### Say / ask / do
1. DO (energiser, 5 min): *Pass the Clap* (facilitation-toolkit §6).
2. SAY: "Bongani and the Sizanani Youth Garden carry water in buckets from the church tap. A
   small petrol pump and pipe costs about **R3 000** (check locally). Bongani wants it **now**."
   ASK: "Where can he get R3 000?" *(borrow from a mashonisa, a lender, the stokvel, family;
   save; share a pump)*
3. DO: Point to **R-P16 Borrow wisely** — five questions before you borrow:
   ① **What for?** Will it help earn or save money — or only spend it?
   ② **Total cost:** what you pay back minus what you borrowed.
   ③ **Can I pay?** Every payment, from my money book and season calendar — not from hope.
   ④ **Is the lender registered?** An NCR registration number (NCRCP…) on the paper.
   ⑤ **What if** something goes wrong — sickness, frost, the pump breaks?
4. GROUPS (20 min): "Four offer cards. For each one, **count it out with play money**:
   - Put down what Bongani **gets** (R3 000).
   - Add notes on top until the pile equals what he **pays back** in total.
   - The notes you added are the **cost of the loan**. Move them to a separate pile and count.
   - Then check with the Sizanani card: the garden has about **R400 a month** left after
     costs. Can they pay?"

   | Offer | Gets | Pays back | Cost of the loan |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | A · Man in the street: "R3 000 now, R4 500 at month-end. I keep your SASSA card." | R3 000 | R4 500 in 1 month | **R1 500** |
   | B · Registered lender (NCR number on the form, gives a written quote): 6 payments of R640 | R3 000 | R3 840 over 6 months | **R840** |
   | C · Savings group loan: 3 months, the group charges 5% a month on R3 000 (R150 a month) | R3 000 | R3 450 over 3 months | **R450** — and it goes back into the group, shared out to members |
   | D · Save R500 a month in the FARM tin; meanwhile hire a neighbour's pump at R50 a day when needed | R3 000 after 6 months | R3 000 | **R0** (plus hire days) |

   ASK: "Can the garden pay offer B — R640 a month — from R400 a month?" *(no — R240 short
   every month; they would have to cut household money or borrow again)*
   ASK: "Offer C — R1 150 a month (R1 000 back + R150) for 3 months?" *(no — too big; they'd
   need to borrow less, e.g. R1 000 for the pipe and share a pump)*
   ASK: "Offer A?" *(never — R4 500 in one month; and keeping the card is against the law)*
   *(Most groups choose D, or C for a smaller amount, or a group-shared pump. All are fine if
   they can explain the cost and the payments.)*
5. DO: Reporters say their choice and why (30 seconds each).
6. SAY: "**Spot the illegal lender.** Any one of these, walk away:" (write on R-P16's sheet)
   - Keeps your ID, SASSA card, bank card or asks for your PIN — **against the law**.
   - No NCR registration number; no written quote showing the total cost.
   - Threatens you or your family, or takes goods without a court order.
   - "No questions asked", "blacklisted? no problem".
   - Asks for a fee **before** you get the loan.
   SAY: "Check a lender with the National Credit Regulator: **0860 627 627** or ncr.org.za. If you
   are drowning in debt, an NCR-registered **debt counsellor** can help. The numbers are on the
   wall."
7. SAY: "Three golden rules:"
   - **Never borrow with interest for food, parties or to pay another loan.** Interest makes
     today's meal cost more tomorrow.
   - **Borrow for something that earns or saves** — and only if the book shows you can pay.
   - **Borrow less, for less time.** The longer and bigger, the more it costs.
8. SAY: "Now the best loan of all: the one you **don't** need. That is the **reserve tin**."
   Show the tin. "Farmers call it insurance of the mind — you sleep better." (Tablet: R-ANI-04
   shows how a debt grows; then the reserve stops it.)
   ASK: "What surprises come to a farming family?" *(funeral, frost, a broken pump, sickness,
   a stolen phone, taxi strike)*
9. GROUPS (10 min): "Your family starts a reserve of **R20 a week**. How many weeks to reach
   R500? Count it with play money." *(25 weeks — about 6 months)* "Where will they keep it?"
   *(SAVE tin, savings group, bank — not the FARM tin, not the purse)* "What is it **for**, and
   what is it **not** for?" *(for real emergencies; not for Christmas — plan Christmas in the
   budget)*

### Debrief (the three questions)
1. What did you compare? *(four ways to get R3 000 — costs R1 500, R840, R450, R0)*
2. Why does the total cost matter more than "only R640 a month"? → R-P16. *(small payments hide
   a big cost; the payment must fit the book)*
3. What will you do at home? *(start the reserve with R10 or R20 a week; never give a lender my
   card)*

### Key messages
- **Cost of a loan = pay back − borrowed.** Compare that, not the monthly payment.
- **Can I pay?** Check the money book and the season calendar first.
- **Nobody may keep your card or ask for your PIN.** Check the lender's NCR number.
- **A reserve** is the loan you never need. Start small, start now.

### If things go wrong
- **A learner defends their mashonisa ("she helped me when nobody else would"):** don't argue
  or shame. SAY: "She did help. Now let's count what the help cost." Let the play money speak.
- **A learner discloses a lender is holding their card:** speak privately after the session.
  They can report to the NCR and SAPS; SASSA can stop and replace a grant card. Give the numbers.
  Do not confront any lender yourself.
- **Someone asks "Which lender should I use?"** SAY: "We never name lenders. Use the five
  questions, get written quotes from two or three, compare the total cost."

### Language & inclusion notes
- Use the local word for a loan shark (*mashonisa*, *umashonisa*) — learners know it. Never
  describe a lender by nationality or ethnicity.
- Percentages are hard: always turn them into rands with play money ("5% of R3 000 is R150").

✂ **Cut if short:** step 9 — say the R20 × 25 weeks answer and move on.

## R-D3-S4 · 14:45–15:30 · My money plan · picture quiz (45 min)

**By the end, learners can:** present their own money plan to their group.

**Poster:** R-P17 · **Slides:** R-D3-17 → R-D3-19 · **Clip:** none
**Materials (per learner):** money plan sheet (P10), pencil, crayons. **Whole class:** picture
quiz cards (R-IMG-20), corner signs A · B · C, the Day 1 tally.
**Room:** tables; then the three corners.

| Time | Step | Teacher talk? |
|---|---|---|
| 14:45 | R-P17: the five parts of my plan | 3 min |
| 14:50 | Everyone makes their own plan | 2 min |
| 15:05 | Share in groups (2 min each) | 1 min |
| 15:15 | Picture quiz — end | 4 min |
| 15:27 | Wrap | 1 min |

Teacher talk: **11 of 45 min (24%).**

### Say / ask / do
1. DO: Point to **R-P17 My money plan**: ① my goal (draw it) ② my three tins (how I split
   garden money) ③ my book (when I write it — every evening? every Sunday?) ④ my pinch month
   and my reserve (R___ a week) ⑤ one step next month (a buyer, a price, a saving, a cost to cut).
2. SAY: "This plan is **yours**. Nobody will take it. You don't have to write real amounts —
   pictures and ticks are fine. Your mentor may ask *if* you are following it, not *what* is in it."
3. DO (15 min): Everyone fills in their own plan (P10). Readers help non-readers **only if asked**.
   Facilitators walk round and ask: "What's your one step next month?"
4. GROUPS (10 min): Each person shares **the goal and the one step** — 2 minutes each. Amounts
   are private. The group gives one star each ("I like that you…").
5. DO: **Picture quiz — end.** Same 8 cards, same corners. Co-facilitator writes the totals next
   to Day 1's. Now show the answers and celebrate the change.
6. SAY: "Look what changed in three days. That's you."

### Debrief (the three questions)
Folded into the closing — the plan *is* the take-home action.

### Key messages
- A plan you can see is a plan you can follow.
- One step next month is better than ten steps never.

### If things go wrong
- **A learner has no garden income yet:** their goal can be "start the three tins" or "sell my
  first surplus"; the plan still works.
- **A learner says "my husband / family decides":** SAY: "Then your step can be *show my family
  the three tins*." Don't push further in the group.

### Language & inclusion notes
- The plan sheet (P10) has a picture for each part. Plans can be drawn entirely.

## R-D3-SC · 15:30–16:00 · Evaluation · certificates · closing (30 min)

**Slides:** R-D3-20
**Materials:** evaluation sheet with 6 questions (forms.md §6, questions 1–6), stickers,
two flipcharts ("understand best" · "want to learn more"), certificates in name order, camera
(consent only), the register.

| Time | Step | Teacher talk? |
|---|---|---|
| 15:30 | Evaluation — fingers and stickers | 3 min |
| 15:40 | Certificate ceremony | 3 min |
| 15:55 | Closing words, money back, register PM | 3 min |

1. DO: Read the six evaluation questions (forms.md §6). Learners show 1–5 fingers; the
   co-facilitator counts. Then each learner puts 2 stickers on "Which topics do I now understand
   best?" and 2 on "Which topics do I want to learn more about?" — the topics are the 17 poster
   titles written on the flipcharts. The gap is the mentor's plan.
2. DO: Certificates (forms.md §8 wording in the README): say each name clearly, hand it over,
   shake hands. Photos **only** of learners without a green dot.
3. SAY: "Keep writing the book. Your mentor will ask to see if the tin matches the book — not
   how much is in it."
4. DO: **Money back** — the last count. Every group's play money and beans back in the bag.
   Register PM. Facilitator daily report + course report: register photos, quiz totals,
   evaluation totals, sticker counts.
5. DO: Hand over the play money and card sets to the programme for the next course.
