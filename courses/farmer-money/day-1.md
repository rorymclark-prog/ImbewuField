## R-D1-S0 · 09:00–09:30 · Opening circle (30 min)

**By the end, learners can:** name their group, their role for today, and the course promise.

**Poster:** — · **Slides:** R-D1-01 → R-D1-02
**Materials:** register (forms.md §1), POPIA consent forms (forms.md §2), pens, green "no photos"
stickers, name tags, group role cards, 30 paper "seeds" for Seed Swap.
**Room:** chairs in one circle; register table at the door.

| Time | Step | Teacher talk? |
|---|---|---|
| 09:00 | Register at the door + POPIA consent read aloud and signed | 3 min |
| 09:10 | Greeting, agree a call-and-response | 2 min |
| 09:13 | Seed Swap energiser | 1 min |
| 09:18 | Form groups of 5, take role cards | 2 min |
| 09:25 | Today's promise | 1 min |

### Say / ask / do
1. DO: Co-facilitator at the door: every learner signs the register (AM column). A mark or
   thumbprint is fine. Hand each a name tag.
2. DO: Read the POPIA consent aloud in the local language, slowly. Learners tick and sign.
   Stick a green dot on the name tag of anyone who ticks "No photos".
3. SAY: "Good morning. Welcome to *Farmer Money*. Three days. We start and end on time."
4. DO: Agree a call-and-response for attention (facilitation-toolkit §5). Try it twice.
5. DO: *Seed Swap* — each person gets a paper seed, swaps with 3 people. Say your name and
   **one crop you love to eat.**
6. DO: Form **6 groups of 5**: mix young and old, men and women, readers and non-readers,
   neighbours together. Name them *Bean, Pumpkin, Maize, Banana, Marula, Spinach*.
7. GROUPS: "Choose today's Leader, Timekeeper, Materials, Recorder and Reporter. Roles change
   every morning."
8. SAY: today's promise (above). Point to the posters: "By 4 o'clock we will have used all of these."

### Debrief (the three questions)
Not needed — this is the opening.

### Key messages
- We start on time. We sign the register every morning and afternoon.
- We work in the same group of 5 all course.

### If things go wrong
- **Late arrivals:** start anyway; they sign at tea and join a group with space.
- **More than 30 learners:** register all; ask the programme office for a second course date.
  Do not teach money to 40 people in one room.

### Language & inclusion notes
- Read the consent in the first language. Ask: "Any questions?" and wait 10 seconds.
- Elders may stay seated for Seed Swap — others come to them.

## R-D1-S1 · 09:30–10:30 · Our money agreement · meet the three families · picture quiz (60 min)

**By the end, learners can:** explain the money agreement, name their group's case family, and
show what they already know in the picture quiz.

**Poster:** R-P01 · **Slides:** R-D1-03 → R-D1-05 · **Clip:** none
**Materials (per group of 5):** 1 case-family card (P11), 1 flipchart sheet, 4 markers.
**Whole class:** picture quiz cards (R-IMG-20), three corner signs A · B · C, tally sheet.
**Room:** tables; the three corners of the room marked A, B, C for the quiz.

| Time | Step | Teacher talk? |
|---|---|---|
| 09:30 | Hook: the R100 note | 2 min |
| 09:35 | Our hopes for these 3 days (groups) | 2 min |
| 09:45 | Our money agreement (R-P01) | 5 min |
| 09:57 | Meet the three families | 4 min |
| 10:15 | Picture quiz — start | 3 min |
| 10:27 | Wrap | 1 min |

Teacher talk: **17 of 60 min (28%).**

### Say / ask / do
1. DO: Hold up a real R100 note. Say nothing for 5 seconds.
2. ASK: "When you hear the word *money*, what do you feel?" Pairs talk for 1 minute. Then
   take one word each from 8 people. *(worried, happy, tired, never enough, school fees, hope)*
3. SAY: "All of those are true. Money is about feelings as much as numbers. That is why we
   start with an agreement."
4. GROUPS (8 min): "What do you want to be able to do with money after these three days?
   Recorder draws or writes. Reporter shares one hope." Write hopes on the sheet beside R-P01.
   *(save for school fees, know if my garden makes money, stop running out before month-end,
   price my spinach, avoid loan sharks)*
5. DO: Uncover **R-P01 Our money agreement**. Point to each callout ①–⑤:
   ① We use made-up families — nobody shares their own money.
   ② You may say "pass".
   ③ What is said here stays here.
   ④ No selling in this room.
   ⑤ Every question is a good question.
6. ASK: "What else must be in our agreement?" Take 3 additions and write them on the poster
   sheet. *(phones on silent, no laughing at wrong answers, speak our language)*
7. DO: Everyone signs or marks the sheet beside the poster.
8. SAY: "Now meet three families. They are not real. They will be with us for three days."
9. DO: Each group's Leader draws one case-family card from a bag (2 groups per family).
   Read the card aloud for the whole room first, one family at a time (1 min each).
10. GROUPS (12 min): "Draw your family on the flipchart: the people, the house, the garden.
    Give each person a face." Recorder draws; everyone helps.
11. DO: Each Reporter introduces the family in 1 minute: "This is the Mokoena family. Lerato
    is 34…"
12. SAY: "Now a game. No reading, no writing. I show a card; you walk to corner A, B or C."
13. DO: Picture quiz (README table, cards R-IMG-20). Co-facilitator counts people in each
    corner and writes the totals. **Do not say the right answers.** SAY: "We will play again on
    Day 3 and see what changed."
14. SAY: "This afternoon we start with the money of *these* families — not yours."

### Debrief (the three questions)
1. What did we agree? *(families, pass, confidentiality, no selling)*
2. Why does a money class need an agreement? *(money is private, it can shame, it can cause fights at home)*
3. What will you do at home? *(nothing yet — "just notice how money comes and goes this evening")*

### Key messages
- Nobody has to share their own money here.
- The Dlamini, Mokoena and Sizanani families will carry our sums.

### If things go wrong
- **A learner starts telling their own debt story:** listen, thank them, and say: "Thank you.
  Let us speak at tea, just the two of us." Follow up privately (Day 0 referral list).
- **Someone asks "Will this course give us money / a loan?"** SAY clearly: "No. This course
  gives no money and no loans. It gives you tools to manage your own money."
- **A visitor or official arrives to sell something:** politely ask them to leave (agreement ④).

### Language & inclusion notes
- The family names are Zulu, Sotho and a mix — keep them; they help learners from all groups
  feel included. If your area is Xitsonga or Sepedi, you may rename the families on Day 0.
- In the quiz, elders may point from their chair — a helper walks to the corner for them.

✂ **Cut if short:** the 3 additions to the agreement (step 6) — use only the five callouts.

## R-D1-S2 · 10:50–12:30 · The money map + needs and wants (100 min)

**By the end, learners can:** draw a money map showing where a household's money comes from
and goes, and sort spending into needs and wants.

**Poster:** R-P02, R-P03 · **Slides:** R-D1-06 → R-D1-11 · **Clip:** R-ANI-01 (optional)
**Materials (per group of 5):** case-family card, tin of 200 beans, 1 flipchart sheet, 4
markers, 24 needs/wants picture cards (R-IMG-02), 3 small paper signs NEED · WANT · IT DEPENDS.
**Whole class:** a 2 ℓ plastic bottle with 5 small holes near the bottom, a jug of water, a
basin. "Shock cards" (P11 back page).
**Room:** tables; floor space for the bottle demo.

| Time | Step | Teacher talk? |
|---|---|---|
| 10:50 | Hook: the leaking bottle | 3 min |
| 10:55 | R-P02: the money map | 5 min |
| 11:00 | GROUPS: build the family money map with beans | 3 min |
| 11:30 | Gallery walk | 3 min |
| 11:42 | R-P03 + needs/wants card sort | 4 min |
| 11:57 | The month runs out (shock card) | 2 min |
| 12:07 | Debrief | 4 min |
| 12:20 | Key messages, language time, take home | 2 min |

Teacher talk: **26 of 100 min (26%).**

### Say / ask / do
1. DO: Pour water into the bottle with holes over the basin. Water runs out of five holes.
2. ASK: "Where did the water go?" *(out of the holes)* "How do you stop it?"
   *(find the holes, close some)*
3. SAY: "A household's money is like this bottle. Money comes in at the top. It leaks out at
   the bottom. Some holes are big — food. Some are small — airtime, sweets. Before we close any
   holes, we must **see** them. That is a money map."
   (Tablet/TV: play R-ANI-01, the leaking bucket, 15 s.)
4. DO: Point to **R-P02 The money map**: ① money comes in (left: grant, wages, piece jobs,
   garden sales) ② the household in the middle ③ money goes out (right: food, transport,
   school, electricity, airtime, church, stokvel, debts).
5. SAY: "Each bean is **R50**. Your family card tells you how many beans for each line.
   You do not need to calculate."
6. GROUPS (27 min): "Build your family's money map on the flipchart.
   - Draw the house in the middle.
   - Left side: put a pile of beans for each way money comes **in**. Draw a picture beside each pile.
   - Count all the 'in' beans into the house.
   - Right side: move beans **out** of the house, one pile for each spending line on the card.
   - Recorder draws an arrow and a picture for each pile.
   - What is left in the house at the end?"
   Walk between groups. Ask: "Which is the biggest pile going out? Which is the smallest?"
   *(Dlamini: food 40 beans is biggest; church 1 bean is smallest; 1 bean left)*
7. DO: At 11:25 call "5 minutes left".
8. DO: **Gallery walk** (12 min). Groups walk clockwise to the next map, 2 minutes each, and
   leave one sticky note: "Biggest leak I see: ___". Back to own table.
9. DO: Point to **R-P03 Needs first, wants later**: ① NEED — we cannot live well without it
   (food, water, school, clinic transport) ② WANT — nice, but can wait (new cap, takeaways,
   extra data for videos) ③ IT DEPENDS — airtime to call the clinic is a need; data for
   games is a want.
10. GROUPS (11 min): "Sort the 24 cards into three piles: NEED · WANT · IT DEPENDS. For every
    'It depends' card, say *when* it is a need." *(Airtime: need if for work or emergencies.
    Burial society: most say need. School uniform: need; the branded one: want.)*
11. DO (11:57): Read a **shock card** to each group — each group gets a different one:
    - "A funeral in the family. You must give R600 (12 beans)."
    - "The taxi fare goes up by R100 this month (2 beans)."
    - "The fridge breaks. R500 to fix it (10 beans)."
    - "A child needs R300 for a school trip (6 beans)."
12. GROUPS (8 min): "Find the beans. Take them from your map. Which piles will you make
    smaller? Only from wants first. Then which needs?" Recorder crosses out and redraws.
13. DO: Debrief (below), then **language time** (2 min): each person tells their neighbour
    in their own language what a money map is.
14. SAY (take home): "Tonight, **only if you want to**, make a money map of your own house with
    beans on the kitchen table. Nobody will ask you about it. It is for you."

### Debrief (the three questions)
1. What did you see on the maps? *(food is the biggest; grants carry most families; very little
   is left; small leaks add up — airtime, sweets, taxis)*
2. Why does a money map help? → point to R-P02. *(you cannot close a leak you cannot see; it
   shows who brings money and where it goes; it shows the garden's part)*
3. What will you do at home? *(make a bean map; watch one small leak for a week)*

### Key messages
- **See it before you fix it:** a money map shows where money comes from and goes.
- **Needs first, wants later.** When money is short, cut wants before needs.
- Small leaks add up.
- The garden is part of the map — the food you eat is money you do not spend.

### If things go wrong
- **A learner says "This is my life" and gets upset:** pause. SAY: "Yes. Many of us live this.
  That is why we are learning together." Offer a break; follow up privately.
- **Arguments about needs vs wants** (e.g. church, burial society): good! SAY: "Each family
  decides. There is no one right answer. The skill is to *choose*, not to follow my list."
- **Groups count beans slowly:** give the Materials person the job of counting in 10s into small
  piles of 10 first.

### Language & inclusion notes
- Many languages have no word for "budget" or "want" in this sense. Let groups choose their own
  words and write them on the poster sheet.
- Non-readers: every family-card line has a picture and a number of beans — no reading needed.
- Do not assume grants are "wasted". Research shows most grant money in SA goes on food and
  children. Never make grant-shaming jokes.

✂ **Cut if short:** the gallery walk — do a 3-minute "stand and look" instead.

## R-D1-S3 · 13:15–14:30 · A family budget + the three tins + who decides (75 min)

**By the end, learners can:** plan a month's money before it starts (budget), explain why farm
money is kept apart, and name one way a family can agree on money.

**Poster:** R-P04, R-P05 · **Slides:** R-D1-12 → R-D1-16 · **Clip:** R-ANI-02 (optional)
**Materials (per group of 5):** family card, the family's month income in **play money** (from
the bag), 1 budget sheet (P5) enlarged to A3 or drawn on flipchart, 3 tins or envelopes with
blank labels, 1 marker, R300 extra play money for "this week's garden sales".
**Room:** tables.

| Time | Step | Teacher talk? |
|---|---|---|
| 13:15 | Energiser: Stand for a Need | 1 min |
| 13:20 | Hook: planning a journey · R-P04 | 4 min |
| 13:27 | GROUPS: budget with play money | 3 min |
| 13:47 | The three tins · R-P05 | 5 min |
| 14:02 | Who decides? (the Dlamini story) | 4 min |
| 14:20 | Debrief + key messages | 4 min |

Teacher talk: **21 of 75 min (28%).**

### Say / ask / do
1. DO (energiser, 5 min): *Stand for a Need* — call out items; learners **stand** for a need,
   **stay seated** for a want, **wave** for "it depends". *(bread, a new phone, clinic taxi,
   takeaway chicken, seed, a school jersey, airtime)* Go faster and faster.
2. ASK: "Before a long journey to Durban or Joburg, what do you do?" *(save for the fare, pack
   food, know where to sleep)* "You plan **before** you go. A budget is a plan for the month,
   made **before** the month starts."
3. DO: Point to **R-P04 Plan the month before it starts**: ① count what will come in
   ② pay needs first ③ put some away — save ④ put the farm's money aside ⑤ wants with what is
   left ⑥ keep a little for surprises.
4. GROUPS (20 min): "Materials: count your family's money for one month out of your play-money
   bag (the amount is on the family card). Lay the budget sheet on the table. Put real notes on each row, **in
   the order on the poster.** Needs first. Recorder writes or draws the amount. You must keep
   **at least R100** in the *surprises* row."
   Walk round. ASK: "Where did you put the airtime? Why?" "How much did you save?"
   *(Dlamini groups usually find they must cut something to keep R100 for surprises.)*
5. DO: Each Reporter says one sentence: "We kept R___ for surprises by cutting ___."
6. SAY: "Now Lerato Mokoena's story." Tell it (1 min):
   *"On Monday Lerato sells spinach for R120. She puts it in her purse. Tuesday: bread. Wednesday:
   airtime. Friday: the children want chips. Next Monday she needs seed and compost — and the
   purse is empty. The garden made money, but the money disappeared."*
7. ASK: "What went wrong?" *(garden money mixed with home money; no plan for the farm)*
8. DO: Point to **R-P05 Three tins**: ① HOME ② SAVE ③ FARM. (Play R-ANI-02 if you have a tablet.)
   SAY: "Every rand from the garden goes into the FARM tin first, and into the book. Then, on
   one day each week, the family decides: this much stays in FARM for seed and compost, this much
   goes to SAVE, this much goes HOME. **The farm pays the home — on purpose, not by accident.**"
9. GROUPS (10 min): "Label your three tins. Take R300 play money — this week's garden sales.
   Decide how to split it. Be ready to explain why." *(A common split: FARM R120, SAVE R60,
   HOME R120. Any split is fine if they can explain it.)*
10. SAY: "One more question: in a family, **who decides** how the money is split?" Read the
    Dlamini scene (P11 back):
    *"Sipho wants the R400 from the garden for data and a taxi to look for work. Thandi, who
    does most of the garden work, wants to buy seed and a hosepipe. Gogo has her grant card and
    pays for the groceries. Nobody sat down together. On Sunday there was a big argument."*
11. GROUPS (12 min): "Talk about the Dlamini family, not your own.
    - Who does the work in the garden?
    - Who decides where the garden money goes?
    - Is that fair?
    - How could they agree?"
    *(a family meeting once a month; the three tins; everyone who works has a say; write the
    decision down; Sipho gets some if he helps in the garden)*
12. DO: Take one idea from each group. Write "ways to agree" on the sheet beside R-P05.

### Debrief (the three questions)
1. What did you do? *(planned a month; split garden money into three tins; talked about who decides)*
2. Why keep farm money apart? → R-P05. *(so there is money for seed next season; so you can
   see if the garden pays; so it does not disappear)*
3. What will you do at home? *(label three tins or envelopes tonight; suggest a family money meeting)*

### Key messages
- A **budget** is a plan made before the month starts: needs, save, farm, wants, surprises.
- **Three tins:** garden money goes to FARM first, then some to SAVE and HOME — on purpose.
- The people who work for the money should have a say in how it is spent.

### If things go wrong
- **A learner discloses that a partner takes their money or grant, or that they are afraid:**
  do not discuss it in the group. After the session, speak privately, listen, and give the
  referral list (GBV Command Centre, local social worker, SASSA for grant problems). Do not
  promise to fix it. Tell only the lead facilitator, and only that "a learner needs follow-up".
- **Men dominate the "who decides" talk:** ask the Reporter role (rotated) to report; or run the
  discussion in women-only and men-only groups if learners agree (decide by show of hands at the
  start of step 11).
- **A group says "the man decides, that is our culture":** do not argue. ASK: "In the Dlamini
  family, Thandi does the work. How will she buy seed next season?" Let the group find it.

### Language & inclusion notes
- "Budget" — use the local word the group chose in S2, or "the month plan".
- Learners who cannot read the budget sheet use the **pictures** on each row and put play-money
  notes on them — writing amounts is the Recorder's job.

✂ **Cut if short:** step 5 (reports on surprises) — go straight to Lerato's story.

## R-D1-S4 · 14:45–15:40 · Saving together + keeping money safe (55 min)

**By the end, learners can:** compare four places to save, and name four rules that keep
money safe (PIN, card, phone, "too good to be true").

**Poster:** R-P06, R-P07 · **Slides:** R-D1-17 → R-D1-20 · **Clip:** none
**Materials (per group of 5):** the four "ways to save" cards (on the back of R-P06 as A5
handouts, or drawn on flipchart), 16 beans, 1 red card and 1 green card (A5 coloured paper).
**Whole class:** 10 scam-or-safe cards (P15), a small tin with a few coins.
**Room:** tables.

| Time | Step | Teacher talk? |
|---|---|---|
| 14:45 | Hook: where did gogo keep money? | 2 min |
| 14:50 | R-P06 ways to save · group scoring | 5 min |
| 15:10 | R-P07 · Scam or safe? game | 7 min |
| 15:30 | Debrief + key messages | 2 min |

Teacher talk: **16 of 55 min (29%).**

### Say / ask / do
1. DO: Shake the tin of coins.
2. ASK: "Where did your grandmother keep her money?" *(in a tin, under the mattress, tied in a
   cloth, with the church, in a stokvel, in cattle)* SAY: "Our grandmothers were savers. Let us
   learn from them — and add some new places."
3. DO: Point to **R-P06 Ways to save**, the four pictures:
   ① **A savings group or stokvel** — people you trust save together; in a *savings group*
   (also called a VSLA) members save every meeting, can borrow small amounts from the fund and
   pay it back with a little interest, and share everything out at the end of the cycle; the box
   has three locks and three key-holders. In a *rotating stokvel* each member gets the whole pot
   in turn.
   ② **A bank or Postbank account** — money is kept in the bank; grants can be paid in.
   ③ **Money on the phone** (a cellphone wallet or banking app).
   ④ **A tin at home.**
4. GROUPS (13 min): "For each of the four ways, answer four questions. Put a bean on the card
   for every **yes**:
   - Is it safe from thieves and fire?
   - Can I get my money quickly in an emergency?
   - Is it free — no fees?
   - Does it help me **keep** saving (not spend it)?"
5. DO: Each Reporter gives the bean count for each way. Co-facilitator fills a table on the
   sheet beside R-P06. *(Typical: tin — quick and free, but not safe and easy to spend;
   bank — safe, but fees and far away; phone — quick, but fees and a lost phone is a risk;
   savings group — helps you keep saving, trust is key, money is locked till share-out.)*
6. SAY: "There is no perfect place. Many families use **two**: a savings group for the big
   goals, and a tin for this week."
   Add: "Money in a registered bank is protected by law up to R100 000 per person per bank if the
   bank fails. A stokvel is **not** — so choose people you trust, keep written records at every
   meeting, and have two people count the money."
7. SAY: "Now: keeping money safe." Point to **R-P07 Keep your money safe**:
   ① Your PIN is yours only — never tell anyone, not even SASSA, the bank or family.
   ② Cover the keypad. Never take help from strangers at the ATM.
   ③ Never give an OTP (the number the bank SMSes you) to anyone who phones you.
   ④ Too good to be true? It is a scam.
   ⑤ Nobody may keep your SASSA or bank card.
8. DO: *Scam or safe?* Read each of the 10 cards (P15) aloud. Groups talk for 20 seconds, then
   hold up **green** (safe) or **red** (scam / unsafe). Ask one group to explain. Give the
   answer. *(Answers on the card backs: 1 red, 2 red, 3 green, 4 red, 5 red, 6 green, 7 red,
   8 green, 9 red, 10 red.)*
9. SAY: "If money is taken from your account or grant: phone the number on the back of your card
   **at once**, then go to SAPS. For grant problems: SASSA, 0800 60 10 11 — it is free."
   Point to the referral list on the wall.

### Debrief (the three questions)
1. What did you compare? *(tin, bank, phone, savings group)*
2. Why do scams work? → R-P07. *(they rush you; they sound official; they promise big money;
   they ask for your PIN or OTP)*
3. What will you do at home? *(change a PIN that is a birthday; tell gogo never to give her
   card to anyone; ask about a savings group)*

### Key messages
- Save somewhere **safe** and somewhere **quick** — often two places.
- **Your PIN and OTP are yours only.** Nobody real will ever ask for them.
- **Nobody may keep your card.** Not a shop, not a lender, not a relative.
- If it is too good to be true, it is a scam.

### If things go wrong
- **Learners ask "Which bank is best?"** SAY: "We don't name banks or products in this course.
  Ask three places for their monthly fees and compare — you know how now."
- **A learner is in a pyramid or 'investment' WhatsApp group now:** do not shame them. Speak
  privately; suggest they stop paying in; give the referral list.
- **Someone knows a lot about stokvels:** invite them to explain their stokvel's rules in
  2 minutes — elders and savings-group members often teach this better than we do.

### Language & inclusion notes
- Use the local names: *umshayelo*, *stokvel*, *mogodišo*, *umgalelo* — ask the room.
- Older learners may not use ATMs or phones: pair them with a youth for the ATM questions; make
  sure the "never give your card" rule is heard by everyone who collects a grant for someone else.

✂ **Cut if short:** play only 6 of the 10 scam cards (1, 3, 4, 5, 7, 10).

## R-D1-SC · 15:40–16:00 · Closing circle (20 min)

**Slides:** R-D1-21

| Time | Step | Teacher talk? |
|---|---|---|
| 15:40 | One word each (right to pass) | 1 min |
| 15:47 | Group take-home actions | 1 min |
| 15:52 | Question Tree | 2 min |
| 15:55 | Money back + register PM | 1 min |

1. DO: One word about today from each learner.
2. DO: Each Reporter says the group's take-home action. Write them beside R-P05.
   Suggested: **"Label three tins or envelopes: HOME · SAVE · FARM."**
3. DO: Answer Question Tree notes, or promise a time tomorrow.
4. DO: **Money back:** Materials count play money and beans back into the bag and check the
