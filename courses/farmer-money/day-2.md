## R-D2-S1 · 09:30–10:30 · The money book (60 min) **[APP/PAPER]**

**By the end, learners can:** record money in and money out, find the balance, check the book
against the tin (reconcile), and explain the difference between a sale and cash.

**Poster:** R-P08 (+ F-P20) · **Slides:** R-D2-02 → R-D2-08 · **Clip:** R-ANI-07 (optional)
**Materials (per group of 5):** 10 transaction cards "Lerato's week" (P12), a tin with R150 in
play money + the group's play money bag, 1 money book page enlarged to A3 (P1) or drawn on
flipchart, markers. **Per learner:** their own money book (P1–P3) and a pen.
**App ON:** 1 phone per group with ImbewuField open at *My Records*.
**Room:** tables.

| Time | Step | Teacher talk? |
|---|---|---|
| 09:30 | Hook: "Did Lerato make money?" | 2 min |
| 09:35 | R-P08: how the money book works | 5 min |
| 09:42 | GROUPS: Lerato's week, card by card | 3 min |
| 10:02 | Reconcile + sale is not cash | 4 min |
| 10:10 | [APP] / [PAPER] — my first entries | 2 min |
| 10:25 | Debrief + key messages | 1 min |

Teacher talk: **17 of 60 min (28%).**

### Say / ask / do
1. DO: Hold up a tin. SAY: "Lerato's farm tin has **R315** in it on Sunday night."
   ASK: "Did her garden make money this week?" *(we can't tell; we don't know what she started
   with; we don't know what she spent)* SAY: "Right. The tin alone cannot tell you. The **book**
   can."
2. DO: Point to **R-P08 The money book** (and F-P20 from Course 1):
   ① Date ② What happened (a picture is fine) ③ Money **in** ④ Money **out** ⑤ **Balance**
   — what should be in the tin now. At the back: ⑥ **Owed to me** page.
   SAY: "Every rand that goes into or out of the FARM tin gets **one line**. The same day."
3. GROUPS (20 min): "Materials: your tin has R150 — that is the **opening balance**. Write it
   on the first line. Now take the 10 cards in order. For each card:
   - The Leader (or a volunteer) reads it; the facilitator reads it if you ask.
   - Materials moves the play money into or out of the tin.
   - Recorder writes one line: date, picture or word, in or out, the new balance.
   - Card 3 and card 9: think — does money move?"
   Walk round. At card 3 ASK: "Did money come into the tin?" *(no)* "Did Lerato sell?" *(yes)*
   "Where do we write it?" *(on the Owed to me page: Spaza, 20 bunches, R200, to pay month-end)*
   At card 7 ASK: "Is R50 for bread a farm cost?" *(no — it is money taken home; write it as
   'taken home' so we can see it)*
   At card 9 ASK: "Money book?" *(no money moved — it goes in the harvest record, which we do next)*
4. DO (card 10): "Count the tin. Does it match the last balance?" *(R315 = R315)*
   If a group's count is different: "Good! That is what reconciling is for. Go back line by line
   and find the mistake." *(usually a missed card 7, or card 3 put in as cash)*
5. ASK: "How much did Lerato **sell** this week?" *(R590)* "How much **cash** came in?" *(R390)*
   "Where is the other R200?" *(the spaza owes it)*
   SAY: "**A sale is not cash until you are paid.** Keep the *Owed to me* page. On month-end, when
   the spaza pays, write R200 **in** — and tick it off the Owed page. Do not write the sale twice."
6. SAY: "And did she make a **profit**? We still don't know. The tin grew from R150 to R315, but
   she also took R50 home, and the compost will feed many weeks of spinach. Profit needs a full
   season. That is this afternoon."

### [APP] 10:10–10:25 · My first entries in My Records
7. DO: One phone per group. Open **My Records**. Show the tabs: **Picked · Sold · Spent · Charts**.
8. GROUPS (12 min): "Enter three lines of Lerato's week:
   - Card 1 → **Sold** tab → *Quick sale entry*: spinach, 10 bunches, R120.
   - Card 2 → **Spent** tab → *New entry* (Money you paid out): What for *Onion seed*,
     Amount (R) 35, Supplier optional.
   - Card 3 → **Sold** → *New sale & invoice*, and choose *Not yet — payment outstanding* — the
     app keeps it as owed until you mark it paid. (We practise invoices tomorrow.)
   Every person in the group taps at least one entry."
9. SAY: "The app adds up for you. **But keep the paper book too** — phones break, get stolen,
   run out of data. The app works offline and sends later when you have signal."

### [PAPER] 10:10–10:25 · My first entries in my own money book
7. DO: Hand each learner their own money book. Write their name on the cover (or help them).
8. GROUPS (12 min): "In **your own** book, write the Sizanani Youth Garden's Monday — practice
   lines, not your own money:
   - Opening balance R80.
   - Sold 12 bunches to the creche at R12, cash (R144 in).
   - Bought a packet of seed R35 (out).
   - Taxi R20 (out).
   What is the balance?" *(80 + 144 − 35 − 20 = R169)*
   Readers help non-readers; pictures instead of words are fine.
9. SAY: "From tonight, the next page is for **your** garden. Nobody here will read it."

### Debrief (the three questions)
1. What did you do? *(wrote a week of money in and out; counted the tin; found the owed money)*
2. Why does the book matter? → R-P08. *(the tin can't tell you what happened; you find mistakes;
   you see money owed; you see money taken home)*
3. What will you do at home? *(write every farm rand, the same day, for 7 days)*

### Key messages
- **One line for every rand**, in or out, the same day.
- **Reconcile:** the tin must match the balance. If it doesn't, find why — don't hide it.
- **A sale is not cash until you are paid.** Keep the *Owed to me* page.
- Write money taken home as "taken home" — it is not a farm cost, but it must be seen.

### If things go wrong
- **Arithmetic is hard for a group:** do the balance with play money only — count the tin after
  every card; the Recorder writes the count. The tin *is* the calculator.
- **App won't open / no data:** switch that group to [PAPER] at once. The app saves offline;
  sync is not needed today.
- **A learner says "I don't write anything down, I remember":** honour it. ASK: "Can you remember
  what the spaza owed you in March?" Then: "The book remembers for you."

### Language & inclusion notes
- Non-readers draw: a spinach leaf for a spinach sale, a taxi for transport, a bag for compost.
  The balance can be drawn as notes and coins. The money book (P1) has picture headings.
- Use the local words for *in*, *out*, *owed* chosen on Day 0 — write them on R-P08's sheet.

✂ **Cut if short:** the practice lines in step 8 — do only the opening balance and one sale.

## R-D2-S2 · 10:50–12:30 · Harvest record + count every cost + keep every slip (100 min) **[APP/PAPER]**

**By the end, learners can:** record where a harvest went (eaten, shared, sold, lost), explain
the value of food eaten, list every cost of a crop including "free" ones, and keep proof of a cost.

**Poster:** R-P09, R-P10 · **Slides:** R-D2-09 → R-D2-16 · **Clip:** R-ANI-06, R-ANI-07 (optional)
**Materials (per group of 5):** a basket or crate, knife or scissors, 10 rubber bands, kitchen
scale (or share), 1 harvest record (P4) at A3, 12 cost cards (R-IMG-09), 1 cost sheet (P9) at A3,
2 till slips (real, collected on Day 0 — or printed mock slips from P2), glue stick, 1 envelope.
**App ON:** 1 phone per group.
**Room:** outside at the garden bed for the first 30 minutes; then tables.

| Time | Step | Teacher talk? |
|---|---|---|
| 10:50 | Hook: the basket · R-P09 | 4 min |
| 10:55 | GROUPS outside: harvest, bunch, weigh, record | 4 min |
| 11:20 | Food eaten is money saved | 5 min |
| 11:30 | R-P10 · GROUPS: find every cost of Lerato's bed | 7 min |
| 11:55 | [APP] / [PAPER] keep every slip | 4 min |
| 12:15 | Debrief, key messages, language time | 4 min |

Teacher talk: **28 of 100 min (28%).**

### Say / ask / do
1. DO: Put a full basket of spinach (or any harvest) in the middle.
2. ASK: "Where does this basket go?" *(we eat it; give to neighbours; sell; some rots or the
   goats eat it)* Point to **R-P09 Where did the harvest go?**: ① eaten ② shared ③ sold ④ lost.
   (Tablet: R-ANI-06, the basket splitting four ways.)
   SAY: "All four matter. Only one brings cash. But all four came from your work and your costs."
3. GROUPS outside (25 min): "At the bed:
   - Harvest 5 bunches — outer leaves only, leave the heart so it grows again.
   - Tie each bunch with a band. Make them the same size.
   - Weigh one bunch. Write its weight. *(about 300–500 g)*
   - Now fill the harvest record for your case family's week — the numbers are on your family
     card: picked, eaten, shared, sold, lost."
   *(Dlamini week: picked 30 → eaten 8, shared 2, sold 18, lost 2. Mokoena: picked 50 → eaten 5,
   shared 5, sold 35, lost 5. Sizanani: picked 40 → eaten 4, shared 0, sold 34, lost 2.)*
   ASK at each group: "Do the four add up to what was picked?" *(they must — if not, something
   is missing)*
4. DO: Back to tables. Bunches go to the host / lunch pot.
5. ASK: "The Dlamini family ate 8 bunches this week. If they had bought them at the spaza for
   R12 each, what would it cost?" Use play money: Materials counts out 8 × R10 + 8 × R2 = **R96**.
   SAY: "That R96 stayed in Gogo's purse. **Food eaten is money not spent.** Write it at the
   bottom of the harvest record: *value of food eaten*."
   SAY: "Over a season, Lerato's family ate 40 bunches — that is **R480** she did not spend at the
   shop. When someone says 'the garden makes no money', show them this line."
6. DO: Point to **R-P10 Count every cost**: ① seed ② compost and manure ③ water ④ transport
   ⑤ bands, bags, tools ⑥ your time. SAY: "Some costs take money out of the tin. Some are
   'free but not free' — like your time, your manure, rainwater."
7. GROUPS (20 min): "Lerato's spinach bed, one season. Take the 12 cost cards.
   - Put them in two piles: **cash costs** (money left the tin) and **free but not free**.
   - For every cash cost, write the amount on the cost sheet. Use the numbers on the back of
     each card.
   - Add them up with play money first. Check with the calculator last."
   *(Answer: seed R35 + compost R220 + water R60 + bands R30 + transport R120 + spray R15 +
   watering-can share R30 = **R510**. Free but not free: manure, rainwater, own seedlings, her
   time 44 hours. The 'airtime to buyers' and 'fence' cards: Lerato had none this season — R0.)*
   ASK: "Why only R30 for a R90 watering can?" *(it lasts three seasons — each season pays a third)*
8. DO: Take the answer from one group; other groups check theirs. Leave the R510 on the flipchart
   — we need it after lunch.

### [APP] 11:55–12:15 · Keep every slip — scan a till slip
9. GROUPS (16 min): "One real till slip per group.
   - Open **My Records** → **Spent** → *New entry*.
   - Under *Receipt photo*, tap **Take photo** and photograph the slip flat, in good light.
   - Tap **Read a receipt with Lima**. Wait.
   - **Check** what Lima read: the amount, the date, what for. Fix anything wrong. Lima can
     make mistakes — you are the boss of your book.
   - *Crop this cost was for*: choose only if the cost was just for that crop; leave blank if it
     served the whole garden.
   - Save. Then **keep the paper slip too**, in the envelope."
   SAY: "The photo stays on this phone. Reading it needs data. If there is no signal, type the
   amount yourself and read it later."

### [PAPER] 11:55–12:15 · Keep every slip — the slip envelope
9. GROUPS (16 min): "Take your two till slips.
   - Find the date, the shop and the total on each. Circle the total.
   - Glue one slip onto the slip page at the back of the money book (P2). Write the line number
     from the money book beside it.
   - Put the other in the envelope marked with the month.
   - No slip (taxi, hawker)? Write a *home-made slip*: date, what, how much, who — and sign it."
   SAY: "A slip is **proof**. When you or a buyer, a funder or your family asks 'where did the
   money go?', the slip answers."

### Debrief (the three questions)
1. What did you do? *(harvested and recorded where it went; found all the costs; kept a slip)*
2. Why count 'free' costs and food eaten? → R-P09, R-P10. *(so we see the real value of the
   garden and the real cost; so we don't fool ourselves)*
3. What will you do at home? *(start a harvest record; keep every slip in an envelope)*

### Key messages
- Record **every** harvest: eaten · shared · sold · lost. They must add up.
- **Food eaten is money not spent.** Put a value on it.
- **Count every cost** — cash costs, and "free but not free": your time, manure, water.
- **Keep every slip.** No slip? Write one.

### If things go wrong
- **No garden bed or nothing to harvest:** use the bought bunches: groups untie, re-bunch, weigh
  and record. The learning is the record, not the picking.
- **Rain:** do the harvest record at tables with the bunches brought inside.
- **Lima reads the slip wrong:** good teaching moment. SAY: "This is why *you* check. The app
  helps; you decide."
- **Learners argue that time shouldn't count:** don't fight it now — it is the heart of S3.
  SAY: "Hold that thought until after lunch."

### Language & inclusion notes
- Seated role at the bed: the Recorder with the harvest record, and anyone who prefers not to
  bend — they weigh and bunch at a table.
- Cost cards carry a picture and an amount on the back, so non-readers can add with play money.

✂ **Cut if short:** weighing (step 3) — count bunches only.

## R-D2-S3 · 13:15–14:30 · One bunch: cost, price, profit, break-even (75 min)

**By the end, learners can:** work out the cost of one bunch, choose a fair price, work out a
season's profit (before and after their own time), and find a break-even number with counters.

**Poster:** R-P11, R-P12 · **Slides:** R-D2-17 → R-D2-21 · **Clip:** none
**Materials (per group of 5):** play money bag, tin of 200 beans, 1 cup or small tin marked
"COSTS", 10 bunches (paper strips or the bands from S2 as tokens), calculator (to check only),
flipchart, markers. The R510 flipchart from S2. Day 0 price sheet.
**Room:** tables.

| Time | Step | Teacher talk? |
|---|---|---|
| 13:15 | Energiser: Price Is Right | 1 min |
| 13:20 | Cost of one bunch · R-P11 | 5 min |
| 13:35 | A fair price: three checks | 4 min |
| 13:45 | Profit — and your time | 5 min |
| 14:00 | Break-even with beans · R-P12 | 5 min |
| 14:25 | Debrief + key messages | 2 min |

Teacher talk: **22 of 75 min (29%).**

### Say / ask / do
1. DO (energiser, 5 min): *Price Is Right* — hold up (or name) a bunch of spinach, a cabbage,
   a 10 kg bag of maize meal, a packet of seed. Groups shout their price guess. Closest group
   gets a clap. Use the Day 0 price sheet for the answer. *(Warms up numbers and shows prices vary.)*
2. SAY: "Before we set a price, we need the **cost of one bunch**." Point to **R-P11**: ① all
   costs ÷ ② all bunches = ③ cost of one bunch.
3. DO — small example first, with play money: put **R40** on the table and **10 bunch tokens**.
   GROUPS: "Share the R40 fairly onto the 10 bunches." *(R4 each)* SAY: "If 10 bunches cost R40,
   one bunch costs R4. That is the cost of one bunch."
4. GROUPS (5 min): "Lerato: costs R510, bunches 150 — **all** 150, because the eaten and shared
   bunches cost the same to grow. Use the calculator: 510 ÷ 150."
   *(**R3.40** a bunch)* SAY: "So if Lerato sells a bunch for less than R3.40, she **loses** money
   on it."
5. SAY: "A fair price passes **three checks**." Point to R-P11 lower band:
   ① **Floor** — never below the cost of one bunch (R3.40).
   ② **Market** — what do others charge? *(Day 0 sheet: spaza R12–R20, hawker R10–R15 — check locally)*
   ③ **Buyer** — what does *this* buyer value? Fresh, big bunches, delivered, reliable?
   ASK: "Lerato sells at R12. Does it pass the three checks?" *(yes: above R3.40; near the market;
   neighbours are happy)* "Could she ask R15 from the creche if she delivers every Monday?"
   *(maybe — reliability has value)*
6. SAY: "Now: **profit**." Write on the flipchart and let groups do it with play money:
   - Sales: 90 bunches × R12 = **R1 080**
   - minus cash costs **R510**
   - = **R570** cash profit for the season.
   ASK: "Is that all the garden gave?" *(no — food eaten R480, and 10 bunches shared)*
   SAY: "So the garden gave Lerato's family **R570 cash + R480 of food** = **R1 050** of value
   in one season, from one bed."
7. ASK: "And her **time**? 44 hours. If someone paid her the national minimum wage — about R30 an
   hour (check the current figure) — what would that be?" Groups: 44 × R30 = **R1 320**.
   ASK: "So — is R570 cash enough to pay her time?" *(no)*
   SAY: "This is the honest truth. Selling from one bed does **not** pay a wage. But it feeds
   the family, it gives some cash, and she works at home near her children. **Only Lerato can
   decide if it is worth it.** Our job is to see the numbers clearly."
   ASK: "How could Lerato's time earn more?" *(sell at the gate — less taxi; bigger bunches;
   more beds with the same water; make own compost — saves R220; sell to one steady buyer)*
8. SAY: "Last one: **break-even** — how many bunches must we sell before we stop losing?"
   Point to **R-P12 Break-even with beans**: ① costs that stay the same all season go in the COSTS
   cup ② each bunch sold leaves a little money after its own cost ③ fill the cup — then you
   are at break-even ④ after that, each bunch is profit.
9. DO: Tell the Sizanani story: *"The youth garden sells spinach to the creche at R12 a bunch.
   Each bunch uses about R2 of seed, compost and band. The season also has costs that don't
   change however many bunches they sell: fixing the fence R200, two watering cans R100, and
   transport to the creche R300. That is R600."*
10. GROUPS (15 min): "Each bean is **R10**.
    - How much does each bunch leave after its own R2? *(R12 − R2 = R10 = one bean)*
    - The COSTS cup needs R600. How many beans is that? *(60)*
    - Now sell bunches: for every bunch sold, drop one bean in the cup. Count the bunches until
      the cup is full." *(**60 bunches** = break-even)*
    - "Bunch 61: where does its bean go?" *(out of the cup — profit)*
    - "Now the creche says R10, not R12. What does each bunch leave now?" *(R8)*
      "Put the beans away. Use play money: make a pile of R8 for each bunch sold (a R5 and three
      R1s, or four R2s) until the piles add up to R600. How many piles?" *(600 ÷ 8 = **75 bunches**)*
    Let groups find it their own way; check with the calculator last.
11. ASK: "The youth garden expects to sell about 200 bunches this season. Are they safe?"
    *(yes — 200 is well above 60, or 75 at the lower price)*

### Debrief (the three questions)
1. What did you work out? *(cost of one bunch R3.40; profit R570; with food R1 050; time R1 320;
   break-even 60 bunches)*
2. Why does it matter? → R-P11, R-P12. *(you know your floor price; you see if selling pays; you
   know how many bunches before you stop losing)*
3. What will you do at home? *(count costs and bunches for one bed; find my cost of one bunch)*

### Key messages
- **Cost of one bunch** = all costs ÷ all bunches. Never sell below it.
- A **fair price** passes three checks: floor · market · buyer.
- **Profit** = sales − costs. Look at it before *and* after your own time. Add the food you ate.
- **Break-even:** fill the COSTS cup first; after that, each bunch is profit.

### If things go wrong
- **Division is too hard:** always use play money shared into piles (step 3). For 510 ÷ 150,
  show it is "a bit more than R3" by putting R3 on each of 10 tokens, then say the calculator
  gives the exact R3.40.
- **A learner is upset that the garden "doesn't pay":** SAY: "Your garden already saves you
  money on food — that is real. And now you know where to make it better. Most businesses
  don't know this."
- **Someone says "the NGO should pay us for our time":** don't argue or promise. SAY: "That's a
  question for the programme — I'll put it on the Question Tree and pass it on."

### Language & inclusion notes
- Break-even is new in every language. Use the picture: *the cup must be full before the water
  runs over.* Let groups name it.
- Elders often calculate fast in their heads with money — let them lead the play-money counting.

✂ **Cut if short:** the R10 price change in step 10.

Teacher talk: **15 of 55 min (27%).**

### Say / ask / do
1. DO: Point to **R-P13 Money through the season**: 12 months in a row, spring on the left
   (September). ① money out comes early (seed, compost) ② money in comes later (harvest)
   ③ big family months (December, January school) ④ the tin level goes up and down.
   (Tablet: R-ANI-05.)
2. ASK: "In your area, which month is the hardest for money?" *(January — school uniforms and
   stationery after December; September — spring seed before any harvest; winter if frost kills
   the garden)*
3. SAY: "Every group now does the **Dlamini farm tin**, the same numbers, so we can compare."
4. GROUPS (25 min): "Start: the FARM tin has **R200** on 1 September. For each month, put the
   month card on the calendar. Add the money in, take out the money out, and any money the family
   takes home. Use play money in the tin. Recorder writes the balance at the end of each month.
   Colour a month **red** if the tin goes below zero."

   | Month | Garden money in | Garden money out | Taken home | Tin at month-end |
   |---|---|---|---|---|
   | Sep | R100 | R350 (seed, compost, seedlings) | — | **−R50 (red)** |
   | Oct | R150 | R100 | — | R0 |
   | Nov | R300 | R50 | — | R250 |
   | Dec | R450 | R50 | R200 (Christmas) | R450 |
   | Jan | R300 | R250 (autumn seed) | R400 (school) | R100 |
   | Feb | R350 | R100 | — | R350 |

   (Groups mark the red month with a red marker or a red sticker.)
   ASK: "In September the tin is short by R50. What can the Dlamini family do?"
   *(save from winter sales in August; buy seed in two steps; make their own compost; share seed
   with neighbours; sell seedlings early)*
   ASK: "January has only R100 left. What if the school costs are R600 this year?"
   *(keep money in the SAVE tin from December; start saving in October)*
5. DO: Each group writes one **plan** on the calendar: "In ___ we will save R___ for ___."

### [APP] 15:15–15:30 · The month-end check — Financial sheet
6. GROUPS (12 min): "Open **My Records** → **Financial sheet**. Choose **Month**.
   - Read the four boxes: **Income**, **Expenses**, **Recorded cash margin**, **Yield logged**.
   - Find Lerato's three entries from this morning in the list.
   - Is the onion seed under *Out*? Is the spaza sale shown as owed, not cash?
   - Optional: **Export** → *Export this month (CSV)* — a file you can send to a mentor or
     print."
   SAY: "*Recorded cash margin* is money in minus money out **that you recorded**. It is not the
   same as profit for the season — it is only as good as what you typed in."

### [PAPER] 15:15–15:30 · The month-end check — the summary page
6. GROUPS (12 min): "Turn to the **month summary page** at the back of the money book (P3).
   Fill it for Lerato's week as if it were the whole month:
   - Total money in: *(R390)*
   - Total money out: *(R225)*
   - Opening + in − out = balance: *(150 + 390 − 225 = R315)*
   - Tin count: *(R315)* — ✔ matches?
   - Owed to me: *(R200 — spaza)*
   - I owe: *(nothing)*
   - Taken home this month: *(R50)*"
   SAY: "Do this on the last day of every month. Ten minutes. Show it to your mentor."

### Debrief (the three questions)
1. What did you see? *(the tin is empty in spring; January is thin; the garden pays back later)*
2. Why plan the season? → R-P13. *(money goes out before it comes in; you can save ahead for the
   pinch month instead of borrowing)*
3. What will you do at home? *(mark my family's hard months on my own calendar; do a month-end
   check on the last day of this month)*

### Key messages
- **Money goes out before it comes in.** Plan for it.
- Find the **pinch month** and save for it months ahead.
- **Month-end check:** in, out, balance, tin count, owed to me, I owe — every month.

### If things go wrong
- **Negative numbers confuse:** use play money: when the tin is short, the group must **borrow**
  R50 from the facilitator's "bank" — and pay it back in October. It shows why a shortfall
  matters (and prepares tomorrow's borrowing session).
- **App shows other data:** if a phone was used with sample data, the totals will be large —
  point only to Lerato's lines.

### Language & inclusion notes
- Use local names for months and seasons. In many areas people count the season by crops
  ("when the mealies are green") — accept it and write it on the calendar.

✂ **Cut if short:** do only September to December.
