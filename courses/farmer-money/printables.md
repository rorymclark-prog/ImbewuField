# Printables · Farmer Money

Everything learners write on or hold, ready to lay out and photocopy. Rules for every sheet:
- **Pictures first, words second.** Every column and row has an icon (Lucide-style line icons or
  the course art), so a learner who does not read can still use it.
- Black and white must work. Big boxes for big handwriting. Public Sans, 12 pt minimum on
  handouts, 16 pt for headings; numbers in Newsreader.
- The words on each sheet are in English here; retype in the local language (poster-standards.md
  → Translation). Leave the icons as they are.
- **No real learner data** on any printed example. All examples use the three case families.

## P1 · Money book page

A5 portrait, one page = 12 lines. The strip of picture headings is also printed alone so it can
be pasted into a cheap exercise book.

```
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ MONEY BOOK · FARM TIN      Month: ________   Page ___            │
├───────┬──────────────────────┬──────────┬──────────┬─────────────┤
│ [cal] │ [leaf/taxi]          │ [coin →] │ [→ coin] │ [tin]       │
│ Date  │ What happened        │ Money in │ Money out│ Balance     │
├───────┼──────────────────────┼──────────┼──────────┼─────────────┤
│       │ Opening balance      │          │          │             │
│       │                      │          │          │             │
│  …12 lines…                                                       │
├───────┴──────────────────────┴──────────┴──────────┴─────────────┤
│ Tin count today: R______   Matches? ☐ yes ☐ no → find the line   │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

**Rules printed inside the front cover (with icons):**
1. One line for every rand in or out of the FARM tin — the same day.
2. Sold but not paid? Write it on the **Owed to me** page, not here.
3. Money taken home? Write "taken home" — so you can see it.
4. Food eaten or shared? Harvest record, not here.
5. Count the tin every Sunday. It must match the balance.
6. Keep every slip at the back.

## P9 · Cost sheet — count every cost

A4 (A3 for groups). Crop: ______ Bed / plot size: ______ Season: ______

| Icon | Cost | Cash? (money left the tin) | Free but not free | Amount R |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| seed | Seed / seedlings | ☐ | ☐ own seed | |
| compost | Compost, manure | ☐ | ☐ own compost / manure | |
| water | Water | ☐ bill | ☐ rain / tank | |
| taxi | Transport | ☐ | | |
| bands | Bands, bags, crates | ☐ | | |
| can | Tools (share: price ÷ seasons it lasts) | ☐ | | |
| spray | Pest control | ☐ | ☐ home-made | |
| fence | Fence, repairs | ☐ | | |
| phone | Airtime to buyers | ☐ | | |
| clock | **My time:** ___ hours × R___ | | ☐ | *(write separately)* |

```
A  Total cash costs                         R_______
B  Bunches (or kg) picked — ALL of them       _______
C  Cost of one bunch  = A ÷ B               R_______   ← never sell below this
D  Sales (bunches sold × price)             R_______
E  Profit before my time  = D − A           R_______
F  Food eaten (bunches eaten × price)       R_______
G  Value to the family  = E + F             R_______
H  My time (hours × R per hour)             R_______   Does E pay for H?  ☐ yes ☐ no
```

Worked example (Lerato's bed): A R510 · B 150 · C **R3.40** · D 90 × R12 = R1 080 · E **R570** ·
F 40 × R12 = R480 · G **R1 050** · H 44 × R30 = R1 320 → no, selling alone does not pay her time.

**Break-even box** (bottom of the sheet):
```
Season costs that stay the same (fence, cans, transport)    R_______  (the cup)
Price per bunch R____ − own cost per bunch R____ = each bunch leaves R____
Break-even = cup ÷ what each bunch leaves =  ______ bunches
```
Worked example (Sizanani): R600 ÷ (R12 − R2) = **60 bunches**. At R10: R600 ÷ R8 = **75**.

## P14 · Loan offer cards

A4 → 4 cards. The same need on each: **Bongani needs R3 000 for a pump.**

**A · The man in the street**
"R3 000 today. Pay me **R4 500** at month-end. I keep your SASSA card until you pay."
*Back (facilitator):* cost R1 500 in one month. Keeping the card is against the law. Walk away.

**B · A registered lender**
Office in town. NCR registration number on the form. Gives a written quote.
"R3 000 now. **6 payments of R640** a month."
*Back:* pays back R3 840 · cost R840 · R640 a month is more than the garden's R400 a month.

**C · Our savings group**
Bongani is a member. The group agrees to lend R3 000 for 3 months. The group's rule: 5% a month
on the amount borrowed = R150 a month.
"Pay back **R1 150 a month for 3 months**."
*Back:* pays back R3 450 · cost R450 — and the interest goes back into the group fund, shared
out to members at the end of the cycle · R1 150 a month is too much for R400 — borrow less.

**D · Save first, share for now**
Save **R500 a month** in the FARM tin (from garden money + part of his piece jobs). Meanwhile hire
a neighbour's pump at **R50 a day** on the days it is really needed.
*Back:* R3 000 after 6 months · cost of credit R0 · plus hire days · the pump is his, no debt.

## P15 · Scam-or-safe cards

A4 → 10 cards. Front: a picture and the words (read aloud). Back: **red** (scam / unsafe) or
**green** (safe) and one line why.

| # | Card | Answer | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | An SMS: "SASSA: your grant is stopped. Click this link and type your ID number and PIN." | Red | SASSA never asks for your PIN. Don't click. |
| 2 | Your card is stuck in the ATM. A kind man says: "Type your PIN again, I'll help." | Red | A trapped-card trick. Phone the bank's number on your card. |
| 3 | At the ATM you cover the keypad with your other hand. | Green | Nobody can see your PIN. |
| 4 | A caller says he is from your bank. "Read me the number we just SMSed you." | Red | That is an OTP. Never give it to anyone. |
| 5 | A WhatsApp group: "Pay in R500, get R2 000 in two weeks!" | Red | Too good to be true — a pyramid scheme. |
| 6 | Your stokvel writes every payment in a book, and two members count the money together. | Green | Records and two counters keep a group safe. |
| 7 | The spaza owner says: "Leave your SASSA card with me — I'll take what you owe on pay day." | Red | Nobody may keep your card. |
| 8 | An SMS says R1 000 left your account. You didn't do it. You phone the number on the back of your card. | Green | Right — the number on your own card, at once. |
| 9 | "You won a prize! Pay R200 to receive it." | Red | Real prizes don't cost money. |
| 10 | Gogo writes her PIN on the back of her card so she won't forget. | Red | Anyone who finds the card can use it. Learn it, or keep it hidden at home — never with the card. |
