# Posters · Farmer Money (R-P01 → R-P17)

All posters follow `shared/poster-standards.md`: A1 portrait unless stated, max 25 words,
headline ≤ 6 words, labels ≤ 4 words, numbered callouts ①②③ that link art to labels, text-free
art, Paper background, Forest top strip with the poster ID in Leaf.

**Money-specific art rules (add to the standard rules):**
- **No real banknote designs.** Money in art is drawn as simple generic notes (a rectangle with
  a plain oval and a colour band) and plain round coins. No faces, no animals, no SARB marks —
  counterfeit rules and translation both need this.
- **No brand names or logos** of banks, lenders, phone networks or shops. A bank is "a small
  building with a counter"; a phone is a plain phone.
- **No stereotypes.** Lenders are never drawn by nationality or ethnicity. Poor families are
  drawn with dignity: tidy, working, capable.
- Numbers are **typeset on the layout**, never drawn into the art, so they can be changed for
  local prices.

Word counts below include headline + labels + question.

## R-P01 · Our money agreement
- Size: A1 portrait
- Used in: R-D1-S1, R-D2-S0, R-D3-S0
- Art: R-P01-ART

**Headline:** Our money agreement
**Art brief (text-free):** A circle of eight adults of mixed ages (two gogos, a young man with a
cap, a young woman with braids, a middle-aged man, others) seated on chairs and a bench around
a woven mat. In the centre of the mat a closed tin with its lid on. Callouts: ① one person
holding up a card showing a simple drawn family (three stick figures) ② a young woman raising a
flat open palm, calmly (passing) ③ an older woman with one finger to her lips, smiling
④ a hand gently pushing away a bag of goods (no selling) ⑤ a young man with his hand up, the
others turned to listen. Warm, safe, daylight under a tree or in a hall.
**Labels on the poster (22 words):**
1. Made-up families only
2. You may pass
3. It stays here
4. No selling here
5. Every question counts
Question: *What else do we agree?*
**Layout:** Headline top band; art 60% with five callouts round the circle; labels in two rows;
blank space at the bottom (5 cm) for learners' signatures or marks — or a sheet beside it.
**Teach it — back-of-poster card (A5):**
```
R-P01  Our money agreement                     (≈ 12 min)
1. ASK: When you hear "money", what do you feel? One word each.
2. POINT ①: We use made-up families. Nobody shares their own money.
3. POINT ②: You may say "pass". Nobody asks why.
4. POINT ③: What is said here stays here.
5. POINT ④: Nobody sells anything in this room — not us, not visitors.
6. POINT ⑤: Every question is a good question.
7. ASK: What else must we agree? Add 3. Everyone signs or marks.
KEY: Money is private. Here we learn with made-up families.
```

## R-P02 · The money map
- Size: A1 portrait
- Used in: R-D1-S2
- Art: R-P02-ART

**Headline:** Where does money go?
**Art brief (text-free):** A simple house (tin roof, one door) in the centre, drawn like a
container with a small coin pile inside. **Left side:** three thick arrows flowing **into** the
house from ① a grant card held in an older woman's hand, a hand with a wage packet, and a bunch
of spinach being handed over for coins. **Right side:** arrows flowing **out** to ③ a food
basket with maize meal, a minibus taxi, school shoes, a light bulb, a phone, a church, a small
cash box (stokvel). The food arrow is the thickest. ④ At the bottom of the house several small
drips of coins leak out (sweets, airtime, a cold drink shown very small). Beans are scattered
beside each arrow like counters.
**Labels on the poster (21 words):**
1. Money comes in
2. Our household
3. Money goes out
4. Small leaks add up
Question: *Which is your biggest leak?*
**Layout:** Landscape-style art on a portrait sheet: in-arrows left, out-arrows right; labels
under each side; ④ callout at the drips.
**Teach it — back-of-poster card (A5):**
```
R-P02  The money map                    (≈ 10 min + 30 min practical)
1. DO: Pour water into a bottle with holes. ASK: Where does it go?
2. POINT ①: Money comes in — grant, wages, piece jobs, garden sales.
3. POINT ②: The household in the middle.
4. POINT ③: Money goes out — food is the biggest arrow.
5. POINT ④: Small leaks add up — airtime, sweets, cold drinks.
6. GROUPS: Build your family's map with beans. 1 bean = R50.
7. ASK: Which pile is biggest? What is left at the end?
KEY: See it before you fix it.
```

## R-P03 · Needs first, wants later
- Size: A1 portrait
- Used in: R-D1-S2, R-D1-S3
- Art: R-P03-ART

**Headline:** Needs first, wants later
**Art brief (text-free):** A traditional balance scale made of a stick and two woven baskets.
① The lower, heavier basket holds a bag of maize meal, a 20 ℓ water drum, a pair of school
shoes, a clinic card, a candle, soap. ② The higher basket holds a takeaway box, a fancy cap, a
bag of sweets, a phone showing a game (simple shapes). ③ Between them, hanging from the middle,
a plain phone with a small question mark shape above it (airtime — it depends). A woman and a
teenage boy stand either side, thinking.
**Labels on the poster (18 words):**
1. Need: must have
2. Want: can wait
3. It depends
Question: *When is airtime a need?*
**Layout:** Scale centred; labels under each basket; ③ in the middle.
**Teach it — back-of-poster card (A5):**
```
R-P03  Needs first, wants later                (≈ 5 min + 15 min sort)
1. ASK: What can a family NOT live without?
2. POINT ①: Needs — food, water, school, clinic, soap.
3. POINT ②: Wants — nice, but can wait.
4. POINT ③: It depends — airtime to call the clinic is a need; for games, a want.
5. GROUPS: Sort 24 cards into Need / Want / It depends.
6. DO: Give each group a shock card. What will they cut? Wants first.
KEY: When money is short, cut wants before needs. Each family decides.
```

**Labels on the poster (23 words):**

2. ÷ all bunches
3. Cost of one bunch
4. Floor
5. Market
6. Buyer
Question: *Is your price above the floor?*
**Layout:** Two bands: the division (top) and the three price checks (bottom).
**Teach it — back-of-poster card (A5):**
```
R-P11  One bunch: cost, price, profit     (≈ 10 min + 15 min practical)
1. DO: R40 and 10 bunch tokens. Share the money onto the bunches. (R4 each)
2. POINT ①②③: all costs ÷ all bunches = cost of one bunch. Lerato: R510 ÷ 150 = R3.40.
3. POINT ④: Floor — never sell below R3.40.
4. POINT ⑤: Market — what others charge (check locally).
5. POINT ⑥: Buyer — what this buyer values: fresh, delivered, on time.
6. SUMS: Sales R1 080 − costs R510 = R570 profit. Plus food eaten R480.
7. ASK: Her time — 44 h × R30 = R1 320. Does selling pay her time? Be honest.
KEY: Know your cost of one bunch. Price between the floor and what the buyer values.
```

6. DEMO 3 minutes, then MARKET DAY: groups buy and sell with role cards.
7. ASK: Who still owes you? Write it on the Owed page. Practise a polite reminder.
KEY: Each paper proves one step. Money owed is not money in the tin.
