# Course 4 · Farmer Money — financial literacy for farming households

**For:** programme farmers who have done Course 1 · household gardeners who are starting to sell
surplus · youth micro-enterprises · savings-group and stokvel members.
Many learners have low literacy and numeracy. Many households live on SASSA grants plus
informal income. Most sell informally: to neighbours, spaza shops, creches, schools, hawkers.

**Class size:** 20–30 learners (25 is ideal), in groups of 5. **Two facilitators.**
**Hours:** 09:00–16:00, three consecutive days, plus a **Day 0** preparation day.
*Or* six weekly half-days with homework between (see [below](#alt-6-weekly-half-days)).
**Venue:** a hall, church or school room with tables (money work needs flat surfaces) and,
if possible, a garden bed nearby to harvest and weigh on Day 2.

> **The promise of this course:** *"You will know where your money comes from, where it goes,
> and what your garden really gives your family — and you will leave with a plan."*
> We never promise income. We speak of **saving money on food** and **selling surplus**.

## What learners leave with

By the end of Day 3 every learner can:

1. **Draw** a money map of a household: where money comes in and where it goes.
2. **Sort** spending into needs and wants, and **plan** a simple monthly budget.
3. **Keep** farm money separate from household money (the three tins).
4. **Choose** a safe place to save and **name** four rules that keep money safe (PIN, card, SMS).
5. **Record** money in and money out in a money book and **check** it against the cash (reconcile).
6. **Record** a harvest: eaten, shared, sold, lost — and **explain** the value of food eaten.
7. **Count** every cost of a crop, including seed, water, transport and their own time.
8. **Work out** the cost of one bunch, a fair price, the profit, and a break-even number.
9. **Plan** money through the season on a cash calendar and **find** the pinch month.
10. **Follow** a sale from order to delivery, invoice, receipt and payment, and **track** money owed.
11. **Compare** loan offers by total cost and **spot** an illegal lender.
12. **Present** their own money plan to their group.

They take home: a money book, a receipt book, a harvest record, three labelled tins (or
envelopes), their own budget sheet, a season cash calendar, their money plan, and a
certificate of attendance.

## The three case families (used all course)

Learners **never** have to share their own household's money. Every exercise uses one of three
made-up families. Each group of 5 "adopts" one family for the whole course. Full cards are in
[printables.md](printables.md#case-family-cards).

| Family | Who | Money comes from | Garden |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Dlamini household** (rural KZN) | Gogo Khanyisile (71), her daughter Thandi (45), grandson Sipho (22), two grandchildren (8, 12) | Gogo's older person's grant, child support grants, Sipho's SRD grant, garden sales | 10 m × 15 m: trench beds, a 2 500 ℓ tank, spinach, cabbage, beans, maize |
| **The Mokoena household** (peri-urban Free State) | Lerato (34), her children Karabo (11) and Dineo (6) | Domestic work 2 days a week, two child support grants, garden sales | 3 beds + a small plot: spinach, onions, green mealies; sells to spaza shops and a hawker; in a stokvel |
| **Sizanani Youth Garden** (Mpumalanga) | Bongani Nkosi (24), Nomsa Mahlangu (21), Tebogo Mashego (27) | Bongani's SRD grant, Tebogo's piece jobs, seedling and spinach sales | Borrowed plot at a church: seedling trays, spinach; sells to a creche; wants a water pump |

All amounts in the family cards are **exercise figures**. Grant amounts change every April —
say so each time, and check the current amounts with SASSA.

## Protecting learners

This is the most personal course in the pathway. Money talk can shame people, and in some
homes it can cause conflict. These rules are part of the course, not extras:

| Rule | What it means in the room |
|---|---|
| **Made-up families** | Exercises use the Dlamini, Mokoena and Sizanani cards. Nobody is asked for their own income, debts or grant amounts. |
| **Right to pass** | Anyone may say "pass" at any time. Nobody is asked why. |
| **What is said here, stays here** | The group agrees this on Day 1 (poster R-P01). Facilitators never repeat a learner's story, even to the mentor. |
| **No selling** | Facilitators do not sell, recommend or sign people up for any bank, loan, insurance, funeral plan, investment or phone product. We name *types* of places, never brands. Visitors who want to sell are not allowed in the room. |
| **No pushing credit** | We teach how to *judge* credit. We never say "you should borrow". |
| **No income promises** | We show real sums with honest results, including when a crop does not pay for the grower's time. |
| **Power and gender** | Money decisions in a home are not always shared. We discuss this through the case families (Day 1 S3), never by asking about a learner's own partner. Women and men can be in separate groups for this session if learners prefer. |
| **Help, not advice** | If a learner is in debt trouble or being abused over money, the facilitator speaks to them privately and gives the contacts in [resources.md](resources.md#who-to-call) (SASSA, NCR, a debt counsellor, local social worker). We do not give personal financial advice. |
| **POPIA** | Consent (forms.md §2) is signed before any photo or record. Learners' money plans belong to them: we photograph them only with consent, and never the amounts. |

## App on / app off

The ImbewuField app is **useful but never needed**. Four sessions are written as pairs with the
same time and outcome:

| Session | [APP] — ImbewuField | [PAPER] twin |
|---|---|---|

| R-D2-S2 · Keep every slip | *Spent* → *Receipt photo* → **Take photo** → **Read a receipt with Lima** (scan a till slip) | Receipt envelope + slip glued in the money book (P2) |
| R-D2-S4 · The month-end check | *My Records* → **Financial sheet** (and *Export this month (CSV)*) | Month summary page at the back of the money book (P3) |
| R-D3-S2 · Invoice and receipt | **Invoice builder** (/invoice, or *Sold* → *New sale & invoice*): *A new invoice*, items, *Has the buyer paid?*, *Share PDF* | Duplicate invoice book + receipt book (P6, P7) |

**How to switch the app track for a whole programme**

- **App OFF (default):** teach every `[PAPER]` block. Skip slides marked `Track: app`
  (the build marks them). Nothing else changes.
- **App ON:** at least **one smartphone per group of 5** with ImbewuField installed and the
  learner signed in (onboarding + POPIA consent done on Day 0 or before). Teach the `[APP]`
  block. **The paper money book is still handed out** — the app is a copy, not a replacement,
  because phones get lost, stolen, broken and run out of data.
- **Mixed room:** groups with a phone do `[APP]`; groups without do `[PAPER]`; the debrief
  compares both. This is often the best version — learners see that the thinking is the same.
- **Screen names:** there is no separate *Finances* screen any more — /finances opens the one
  book at **My Records** (/records, shown as "Records" in some menus) with the tabs
  **Picked · Sold · Spent** (+ Charts). **Sold = money in, Spent = money out.** The till-slip
  scanner is inside a Spent *New entry*. The desktop **Financial sheet** and the **Invoice
  builder** (/invoice) sit on the same book. Use these names in the room.

## Alt: 6 weekly half-days

For savings groups, stokvels or working learners. Each meeting is **3 hours** (e.g. 09:00–12:00
or 14:00–17:00): 20-min opening (register, homework check), two sessions, 10-min close. The
homework is what makes it work — learners try each tool at home for a week.

| Week | Sessions (from the day files) | Homework for the next week |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | R-D1-S1 agreement + families · R-D1-S2 money map & needs/wants | Make a money map at home with beans (privately). Notice one "leak". |
| 2 | R-D1-S3 budget + three tins · R-D1-S4 saving & safety | Set up three tins or envelopes. Change a weak PIN. |
| 3 | R-D2-S1 money book · R-D2-S2 harvest & costs | Write every farm rand in the money book for 7 days. Keep every slip. Record one harvest. |
| 4 | R-D2-S3 cost, price, profit, break-even · R-D2-S4 season calendar | Price check: visit 2 spaza shops and 1 hawker, note the price of spinach and one other crop. |
| 5 | R-D3-S1 who buys · R-D3-S2 market day & invoices | Write one real receipt or invoice for a sale. Ask one buyer what they want. |
| 6 | R-D3-S3 borrowing & reserve · R-D3-S4 my money plan · ceremony | Keep going — the mentor checks the money book at the next visit. |

Certificates in the half-day version: attend at least **5 of 6** meetings.

## Picture quiz — money version (start of Day 1, end of Day 3)

Same method as forms.md §5: picture cards, learners stand in corner A, B or C. Class totals only.
Cards are R-IMG-20 in [media.md](media.md).

| # | Question (said aloud) | A | B | C |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Money from spinach sales goes… | Into my purse with everything | Into the farm tin, written in the book ✔ | To whoever asks first |
| 2 | Which is a need? | Airtime for games | Maize meal ✔ | A new cap |
| 3 | Someone phones and asks for your SASSA PIN. You… | Give it — they are from SASSA | Give half of it | Never give it, hang up ✔ |
| 4 | The spaza takes 20 bunches and pays next month. Today you have… | Sold 20 bunches, no cash yet ✔ | Got nothing | Lost the bunches |
| 5 | Seed R35, compost R110, you sold R300. Profit before your time is… | R300 | R155 ✔ | R445 |
| 6 | Which loan costs least? Borrow R1 000 and pay back… | R1 500 in a month | R1 200 in 6 months ✔ | R2 000 in 2 months |
| 7 | The lender wants to keep your SASSA card. This is… | Normal | Against the law ✔ | Kind |
| 8 | The month with the least money is usually… | December | January ✔ (school costs) | June |

(Q8 depends on the area — accept any month learners can explain. January is the common answer.)

## Delivery rhythm

- Day 0 is never the same day as Day 1. Printing the play money and filling group bags takes
  most of Day 0.
- Max three courses back to back, then a week at home.
- Lead and co-facilitator swap on alternate sessions. The co-facilitator keeps the teacher-talk
  tally (forms.md §3). This course is **talk-heavy by nature** — the counters and play money are
  there so that learners *do* the sums. If you are explaining a sum for more than 3 minutes,
  stop and hand out counters.
- Plan for **one facilitator with good numeracy** and **one who speaks the learners' first
  language well** — ideally both in the same person, but at least one each.

- **Course 1** Day 5 S3 is the starter. The Course 1 record book is the same money book used here.
- **Course 2 (mentors):** the mentor visit form has a *records* support area. After this course,

- **Course 5 (AI literacy):** learners who use Lima to read a till slip here learn in Course 5
