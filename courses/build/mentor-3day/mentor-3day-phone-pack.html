Course 2 · Walking With Farmers — the mentor course

For: extension officers, community farmer-mentors, lead farmers and field supervisors on ACT·SEF-type food-security programmes. They will support the farmers who did Course 1 (Grow Food, Grow Strong) through one growing season. Class size: 16–24 is ideal (the farm visits need one host farmer per trio). Max 30. Groups of 5 for practicals; trios (Mentor · Recorder · Observer) for farm visits. Two facilitators per course, plus a driver/logistics person on Day 2. Two ways to run it (same outcomes):

Mode Length File
In person (default) Day 0 prep + 3 days, 08:30–16:00 this file, day-0 … day-3
Online / blended 6 weekly sessions of ~2 h + field tasks between online-programme.md

Why 08:30–16:00 and not 09:00? Mentors are programme staff and lead farmers, not a general public class. The extra 30 minutes pays for the Day 2 farm-visit travel without cutting a session, and the day still ends at 16:00 so people who use taxis, and women who care for children and elders, get home before dark. If most learners rely on taxis that arrive at 09:00, start at 09:00 and take the cuts marked ✂ 09:00 start in each day file (they add up to 30 minutes per day). Do not run later than 16:00.

What mentors leave with

By the end of Day 3 every mentor can:

  1. Explain the difference between walking beside a farmer (mentor) and checking on a farmer (inspector), and name the mentor's promise (boundaries and ethics).
  2. Listen actively and ask open questions that help a farmer find their own answer; start with what is working (a strength-based visit).
  3. Coach a practical skill from Course 1 with I do · We do · You do and record the farmer's skill as demonstrated or needs further support.
  4. Make an action agreement with a farmer: what · who · by when · how we'll know — and close it only with a date and evidence of what changed.
  5. Run a 45–60 minute individual visit using the Visit Hand (5 steps) on a real farm, and record it on the paper visit form or in the app (Mentor workspace → Record visit).
  6. Sort a caseload with the traffic light (red · amber · green), plan a week and a 6-month season for ~40 farmers in 4 clusters, and choose the right contact for each farmer: individual visit, cluster visit, field day or phone check-in.
  7. Plan and run a 2–3 hour cluster visit at a host garden for 8–12 farmers.
  8. Coach a farmer's garden and money records — on the app track (Records → Sold / Spent, till slip, Invoice builder, Reports) or the paper track (record book, money book, duplicate invoice book) — with a 10–15 minute on-visit script.
  9. Handle hard situations (drought, theft, conflict, drop-out, dependency) and know when and to whom to refer (safeguarding, gender-based violence, health, SASSA).

They take home: the visit playbook (printed), 20 paper visit forms, an observer checklist, their own traffic-light caseload sheet, a first-month week plan, a season plan, the A5 poster cards, and a certificate of attendance.

The visit model at the heart of the course: the Visit Hand

Five fingers, five steps. Every mentor has their hand with them on every visit.

Finger Step App field it fills (Mentor → Record visit)
Thumb 1. Look back — "Last time we agreed … Show me." Last agreed action → Completed on + Completion evidence
Pointing finger 2. Walk & look — walk the garden together; farmer leads Observations and issues, photos, Site location (GPS optional)
Middle (tallest) 3. Listen & ask — the farmer's own priority Support requested, support area (garden & water · soil & seedlings · harvest & food access · records · learning)
Ring finger 4. Try it together — coach one skill, hands on Skill observed + Observation result
Little finger 5. Agree & record — one action, one owner, one date Agreed action, Responsible person, Follow-up date

Switching the app track on or off

Every farmer exercise and every mentor record exists twice: an app way and a paper way. The outcome is the same. Decide on Day 0 (in person) or before Week 1 (online):

Switch Who decides What changes
Programme: APP ON Programme manager, after the phone check (app-track.md §2) Mentors record in Mentor workspace → Record visit; farmers who can use a phone follow the app exercises; others go paper
Programme: APP OFF Programme manager Everything on paper: visit forms, record book, money book, duplicate invoice book. No phones needed
Per farmer (inside an APP ON programme) Mentor and farmer together, at the first visit Column "Track: A / P / A+P" on the caseload sheet. A+P = the farmer keeps paper and a helper (often a youth in the house) enters it in the app

In the day files, every app session is written as a pair of equal length: [APP] and [PAPER]. Teach the one that fits your programme; if the class is mixed, teach the [PAPER] version to everyone first (it is the backbone) and add the [APP] version for the app-track mentors in the same time, in a separate group with a co-facilitator. The paper track is never second best. A programme with no phones is a complete programme. Full detail: app-track.md.

Delivery rhythm for facilitator teams

  • Day 0 is never the same day as Day 1. Host farmers are confirmed in person the week before.
  • Max three courses back to back, then a week at home. Day 2 is long for facilitators: the driver/logistics person handles transport, lunch and the cluster site.
  • Lead and co-facilitator swap lead on alternate sessions. The co-facilitator keeps the session sheet (teacher-talk tally) every session.
  • Certificates are for attending at least 2 of 3 days in person, or 5 of 6 weeks plus 3 of 6 field tasks online. They are attendance certificates, not a qualification.

1. One week before — host farmers (the most important job)

Day 2 sends learners in trios to real farmers who did Course 1 (or any programme farmer).

How many hosts? One per trio, plus two spares. 24 learners → 8 trios → 10 hosts. 30 learners → 10 trios → 12 hosts. Plus one cluster site (best: the Course 1 demonstration garden or a strong farmer's garden) where 8–12 local farmers are invited for the Day 2 afternoon cluster visit.

Choosing hosts - Within 15 minutes' drive of each other and of the cluster site. Hosts in 2–3 villages. - A mix: some strong gardens, some struggling ones, some just starting. Not only the best. - Hosts who want the visit and have something they would like help with. - Never a household in crisis (illness, recent death, conflict) — that is not a training visit.

Visit each host in person (not only a phone call). Say, in their language:

"On [date] a small group of three mentors-in-training would like to visit your garden for about one hour, from about 10:30. One will lead the visit, one will write, one will watch how the mentor does. They will ask about your garden and help with one thing you choose. They will not bring inputs or money. You can say no, and you can stop the visit at any time. May they come? May they take photos of the garden? Of you?"

Record on the host sheet (keep with the lead facilitator):

Host name & phone Village / GPS or directions Consent: visit / photos of garden / photos of people One thing they would like help with

Say / ask / do

  1. DO (facilitators, 2 min each): Visit A — the inspector. Co-facilitator is the farmer, sitting. Lead walks in with a clipboard and hat, does not greet properly, says "Why is this bed dry? You did not do what we taught. I will write this down. Next time I want to see mulch." Walks off. Visit B — the mentor. Greets, asks after the family, "Show me your garden — what are you proud of?", listens, kneels at the dry bed, "What do you think is happening here?", agrees one thing, "When shall I come back to see?"
  2. ASK: "Which visit would make you want to try again?" (B) "Why?" (respect, listened, asked, farmer decides, came back). "Which one do we often do?" (honest answers welcome — A is common when we are rushed and counting visits).
  3. PAIRS: "Tell your partner about the best helper you ever had — a teacher, a gogo, a coach, a boss. What did they do that helped you?" 3 min each. Timekeeper calls swap.
  4. GROUPS: each person writes or draws 2 things their helper did on sticky notes (one idea per note). Recorder can write for others.

GROUPS: put each note on the flipchart under in front / beside / behind. Most will land under beside. ASK: "When is in front right?" (teaching a new skill, danger, safety). "When is behind right?" (almost never — maybe a deadline for a programme form).
  6. DO: Reveal M-P02 The mentor's promise. Read the 5 promises with the room:
  7. I listen first. 2. I promise only what I can do (no promises of inputs, money, jobs, stipends). 3. I keep your story private (POPIA; only what the programme needs).
  8. I take no gifts that change how I help (a cup of tea is hospitality; a chicken is not). 5. I keep you safe (and I report harm to a child or adult).

Help them plan the next 3 |

Debrief (the three questions)

  1. What did you see in the two visits? 2. Why does beside work better with adults? (adults learn when they choose, when it links to their own life, when they are respected) 3. What will you do differently on your next visit?

Key messages

  • Walk beside: the farmer chooses the path; you bring questions, skills and company.
  • Adults learn when they decide it matters.
  • Promise only what you can do. Broken promises end trust.
  • What you see is what you write. True records protect the farmer and you.

If things go wrong

  • Groups argue about gifts ("our culture is to give"): honour it. The line is what changes how I help. A cup of tea: yes. Something with money value: kindly no.
  • An officer says "our job is inspection (compliance)": agree some programme checks exist (e.g. SEF participant attendance). Say: "Do the check honestly and quickly, then switch hats to mentor."

Language & inclusion notes

  • Let pairs tell stories in their own language. Recorders may draw.
  • Safeguarding can be upsetting. Say at the start: "You may step out if you need to."

Posters: M-P03, M-P04 · Slides: M-D1-07 → M-D1-10 · Clip: M-ANI-01 (optional) Materials (per group of 5): 1 set of 10 question cards (closed questions, below), 3 observer strips (listening checklist), pencils. For the room: a ball or rolled sock. Room: group stations; triads spread out so they can hear each other.

Time Step Teacher talk?
10:35 Hook — "the bad listener" game in pairs 3 min
10:45 M-P03 — listen with ears, eyes and heart: what it looks like 5 min

Say / ask / do

  1. PAIRS: A tells B "what I did last weekend". B must not listen: look away, check phone, interrupt. 1 minute. Then swap. ASK: "How did it feel?" (angry, small, stopped talking). SAY: "Farmers feel this when we are in a hurry."
  2. DO: Reveal M-P03. Point to the three parts: - Ears — let them finish; count to 3 before you speak; say back what you heard ("So the water runs out in the second week…"). - Eyes — look at them and at what they show you; sit or squat at their level; phone away. - Heart — notice feelings ("You sound tired of this.") without fixing them straight away. SAY: "Say it back, then ask. Don't jump to advice."
  3. GROUPS → TRIADS (a group of 5 makes a triad + a pair; the pair joins with the facilitator as observer, or does a 4-person round with 2 observers). Roles: Speaker talks about a real problem in their own garden or work (not private family matters). Listener only listens, says back, and asks one open question. Observer ticks the listening strip:
Observer strip ✔
Listener let the speaker finish
Said back what they heard
Asked an open question
Gave no advice
Body: facing, level, phone away

Round = 5 min talk + 3 min observer feedback (two stars and a wish, toolkit §8). Rotate 3×. 4. DEBRIEF: ASK "Which was hardest?" (giving no advice!). "What did the speaker feel?" (heard, found own answer). SAY: "When you stop giving advice, the farmer starts thinking." 5. DO: Reveal M-P04 the question ladder. Four rungs, bottom to top: 1. Closed — yes/no: "Did you water?" 2. Open — "How do you water the beds?" 3. Deeper — "What happens to the water after you pour it?" 4. Farmer's own plan — "What could you try? … What else?" DEMO: take "Are your seedlings healthy?" and climb it with the room shouting the next rung. Open questions start with What, How, Where, When, Who, Tell me about… "Why" can sound like blame — soften it: "What made you choose…?" 6. GROUPS: each group gets 10 closed-question cards. Recorder writes/draws the climbed ladder for each on the flipchart. Then each person practises asking one aloud in the local language.

Closed question cards: Did you make compost? · Is the tank full? · Did you plant at the right spacing? · Are you keeping records? · Do you have pests? · Did you save seed? · Did you sell anything? · Is your family eating from the garden? · Are you using the A-frame? · Will you come to the cluster visit?

  1. FISHBOWL — start with what's working. Two volunteers in the centre, farmer and mentor. Round 1: mentor opens with "What problems do you have?" (3 min). Round 2: mentor opens with "What is going well in your garden? Show me what you're proud of." then "What helped that work?" then "Where else could that work?" (3 min). The circle watches. ASK: "What changed in the farmer?" (energy, pride, she explains more, she finds her own solution). SAY: "This is called a strength-based start. We build from what the farmer already does well. Then the problem is easier to face."
  2. DEBRIEF and take home.

Debrief (the three questions)

  1. What did you notice when you only listened? 2. Why do open questions help adults learn? (they think, they own the answer, you learn what they know) 3. Take home: "Tonight, ask someone at home three open questions and only say back what you hear."

Key messages

  • Listen first. Say it back. Count to 3 before advice.
  • Climb the ladder: closed → open → deeper → farmer's own plan. Then ask "What else?"
  • Start with what's working. Pride opens the door to change.
  • The best answer is the one the farmer finds.

If things go wrong

  • Speakers bring very private problems (debt, marriage): SAY gently, "Choose a garden or work problem for practice." Remind everyone of the privacy rule.
  • Everyone gives advice in the triads: stop the room, ask observers "What did you tick?" Then re-run one round with the rule "no advice at all".
  • Only English-speakers talk: run the triads in home languages. Open questions translate well.

Language & inclusion notes

  • In isiZulu, "Ungangitshela kabanzi…" (tell me more about…) is a useful open opener; ask the room for equivalents in Sesotho/siSwati and write them on the flipchart beside M-P04.
  • Some cultures avoid direct eye contact with elders. "Eyes" means attention, not staring — looking at what the farmer shows you is enough.

  • I do · We do · You do — then talk.

M-D1-S4 · 14:45–15:40 · The action agreement and evidence of change (55 min)

By the end, learners can: write an action agreement (what · who · by when · how we'll know) and say what evidence closes it.

Posters: M-P06, M-P16 · Slides: M-D1-14 → M-D1-16 Materials (per group of 5): 5 blank action-agreement cards (A6, below), 2 weak agreements printed, pencils. Room: one pile of before/after photo pairs (print from M-IMG-06) or drawn pairs.

Time Step Teacher talk?
14:45 Hook — "I'll try to mulch" vs "I will mulch the 2 front beds with grass by Friday" 3 min

14:57 Groups: fix 2 weak agreements 1 min
15:07 Pairs: make a real agreement from this morning's triad problem 1 min

15:32 Debrief + key messages 2 min

Say / ask / do

  1. ASK: "Which of these will happen? A: 'I'll try to mulch.' B: 'I will mulch the two front beds with dry grass by Friday; my son will cut the grass; my mentor will see it on the 14th.'" (B) "Why?" (clear, small, a person, a date, someone checks).
  2. DO: Reveal M-P06. Four questions, one per corner: - What? One small, doable action (the farmer's words). Small wins build confidence. - Who? A named person — the farmer, a family member, the mentor, the group. - By when? A date — before the next contact. - How will we know? What we will see (evidence): mulch on the beds, 3 entries in the book, a photo, a tray of seedlings. SAY: "In the app these are Agreed action, Responsible person and Follow-up date. On paper they are the bottom box of the visit form. The farmer keeps a copy — the card."
  3. GROUPS fix two weak agreements: (a) "Farmer must improve the soil." (b) "Mentor will bring seeds." For (b) ASK: "Is that a promise we can keep? Who owns the garden?" (turn it into the farmer's action: "Thandi will save seed from 3 best bean plants by March; mentor will check the jar on the next visit.")
  4. PAIRS: take the problem from the morning triad. The listener asks the four questions; the speaker answers; write it on a card. Swap.
  5. DO: Reveal M-P16. Hand each group 4 photo/drawing pairs (before/after). ASK: "Which pair proves the action was done?" Discuss: a photo of a bed with mulch, taken on the date; the book with entries; the farmer showing you. SAY the rule: "An action is only closed with a date and evidence of what changed." In the app, Completed on and Completion evidence must be filled. On paper, write the date and what you saw.
  6. ASK: "What if the action was not done?" (no blame: "What got in the way? What would make it possible? Keep it, change it, or drop it?" — a new agreement).

Debrief (the three questions)

  1. What makes an agreement strong? 2. Why do we close with evidence, not a tick? (it proves change to the farmer, the programme and the funder; it shows the farmer their own progress)
  2. Take home: write one action agreement for yourself for tomorrow.

Key messages

  • What · Who · By when · How we'll know.
  • One small action the farmer chose beats five the mentor chose.
  • Show me what changed: close with a date and evidence.
  • Not done is information, not failure. Ask what got in the way.

If things go wrong

  • Learners write the mentor as "who" for everything: ASK "Whose garden is it?"
  • Dates far away: "Before the next contact — usually 2 to 4 weeks."

Language & inclusion notes

  • The card can be drawn: a picture of the action, a stick person, a calendar date circled.

The app track — switch it on, switch it off

Mentoring works with or without the ImbewuField app. This file explains:

  1. What the app track is (and what stays the same)
  2. How to switch it on or off — for the whole programme, or per farmer
  3. The season sequence: 9 farmer exercises, app and paper side by side
  4. The app exercises — a 10–15 minute "coach it on the visit" script and a check for each
  5. The paper twins — the exact same outcomes with books and a pencil
  6. What the mentor uses: Mentor workspace or paper forms
  7. Phones, data and privacy

1. What the app track is

Every farmer in the programme learns to keep garden records and money records through the season. The app track does it on a phone in ImbewuField. The paper track does it in a record book, a money book and a duplicate invoice book.

What stays the same on both tracks: - The same 9 exercises, in the same months. - The same coaching method (I do · We do · You do) and the same check for each exercise. - The same visit (the Visit Hand) and the same visit record fields. - The same evidence in month 6: what the farmer can do, and what their records show.

The paper track is never second best. A farmer who keeps a clean money book is as good a record keeper as one who uses an app. Many strong farmers will use both.

4. The app exercises — coach it on the visit

Every script follows I do · We do · You do. The farmer's own phone, the farmer's own hands. During You do the mentor's hands stay in their pockets. Use sample details if the farmer is not ready to enter real ones. If there is no signal, the app saves offline — show the farmer that. Screen names below are the ones in the app; if a label has moved in a newer version, follow the app's own guide in Studies and tell the programme office.

E1 · Set up profile & consent (month 1, 12 min)

  • Before: farmer has installed the app (or you help with the Play Store link and their own Google account — never yours).
  • I do (3 min): show the language picker; read the consent screen aloud in the farmer's language: what is saved, who sees it.
  • We do (5 min): farmer chooses language, reads/hears consent, types name; you point.
  • You do (3 min): farmer opens the app again from Home and finds their profile.
  • Talk (1 min): "Who can see your records?" (the farmer, their mentor/organisation as agreed).
  • Check: farmer opens the app alone, in their language, and can say who sees their records.

E2 · Map your garden (month 1, 15 min)

  • I do (3 min): on Map, show drawing a boundary with a finger, then one pin.
  • We do (6 min): farmer draws the garden boundary; adds the water storage (drum/tank) and a pin for the compost heap. Walk to the corners together if GPS helps.
  • You do (4 min): farmer adds one more pin (e.g. seedling nursery) alone and saves.
  • Talk (2 min): "Where does the water come from and go?" (link to Course 1 site reading).
  • Check: the map shows the boundary, water storage and at least 2 pins that match the real garden.

E3 · Garden Survey → Crop Plan (month 2, 15 min)

  • I do (3 min): open Garden Survey; show step 1 of the 5-step wizard.
  • We do (7 min): farmer answers the steps (beds, sun, water, what they want to grow). The survey gives beds and a 6-week plan.
  • You do (3 min): farmer opens Crop Plan and finds this week's tasks (Day/Week view).
  • Talk (2 min): "Does the plan match your garden and your family's plate? What would you change?" Compare with the Course 1 90-day plan and the spacing check.
  • Check: farmer can find this week's tasks and name the next thing to plant and where.

E4 · Field Journal (month 3, 10 min)

  • I do (2 min): add one journal entry: a photo of a bed and one line ("aphids on 3 cabbages").
  • We do (4 min): farmer adds today's entry.
  • You do (3 min): farmer finds last week's entry (or today's) again.
  • Talk (1 min): "When in the week will you write?" Agree a day. Optional: ask Lima one question about what the photo shows — and check the answer against what you both see; if unsure, ask the mentor or extension officer.
  • Check: at least one entry per week since the last visit (look back at the next visit).

E5 · Log a harvest (month 4, 12 min)

  • Before: have produce and a scale (kitchen or hanging scale).
  • I do (3 min): weigh a bunch; open My Records → Picked; check Recent harvests first so it is not counted twice; choose the crop; type Kg harvested; save.
  • We do (4 min): farmer weighs and records the next crop.
  • You do (3 min): farmer records one more alone and finds it in Recent harvests.
  • Talk (2 min): "Six bunches is not six kilograms — weigh first." The form uses today's date, so record on the day you pick (or keep the real date on paper). A harvest is not a sale. Optional: Lima Vision can help estimate or weigh from a photo — treat it as a helper, and use the scale for the record.
  • Check: the saved kilograms match the scale; the farmer can find the entry again.

E6 · Money in / Money out (Records → Sold / Spent) (month 5, 15 min)

  • I do (3 min): show the one book with three tabs: Picked · Sold · Spent. Sold is money in; Spent is money out. Record one sale (sample: 5 bunches spinach, R10 each = R50).
  • We do (5 min): farmer records a real cost under Spent → Log a cost (What for, Amount (R) with cents, Supplier optional). Check first that it isn't already there.
  • You do (5 min): farmer records one sale and one cost alone.
  • Talk (2 min): "What came in this week? What went out? What's left?" Use Edit to correct a wrong entry — don't add a second copy. Don't type 0 when you don't know the amount; find the slip first.
  • Check: one sale and one cost saved, amounts match the evidence, farmer can find both.

E7 · Scan a till slip (month 5, 10 min)

  • Before: a real till slip (seed, manure, a hose fitting).
  • I do (2 min): in Log a cost, add a Receipt photo (Take photo), then Read this photo with Lima.
  • We do (4 min): farmer photographs their slip (flat, good light, all of it in the frame); compare what Lima suggests with the slip line by line — item, supplier, total.
  • You do (3 min): farmer corrects anything wrong, saves, reopens it with View slip.
  • Talk (1 min): "Lima reads; you check. The slip is the proof. Keep the paper slip too — the photo stays on this phone."
  • Check: the saved cost equals the slip total to the cent; the slip opens from the entry.

E8 · Invoice builder — build and send (month 5, 15 min)

  • Before: a real or sample buyer (e.g. the crèche kitchen buys 10 bunches of spinach at R10, pays at month end). Buyer's name, what, how many, price, date, when they'll pay.
  • I do (3 min): open Invoice (/invoice); show the parts: your name and contact, the buyer, the line (item · quantity · unit · price), the total, the dates.
  • We do (6 min): farmer fills in the invoice; you read the total aloud together; save.
  • You do (4 min): farmer reopens it from Saved and uses Share PDF (WhatsApp) or Print.
  • Talk (2 min): "An invoice is not a payment. Mark it paid only when the money arrives. Another copy is not another sale — reopen the saved one." The VAT/tax-number field only prints a reference; it doesn't calculate VAT (most smallholders are not VAT vendors; check with SARS guidance or a bookkeeper).
  • Check: a saved invoice with correct total and dates, shared to the buyer (or a sample contact); the farmer can say whether it is paid.

E9 · Read a report (month 6, 15 min)

  • I do (3 min): open Reports; choose the season; show the parts (harvest, money in, money out). Read one number aloud and ask "Is that right?"
  • We do (6 min): read it together; check two numbers against what the farmer remembers or the paper slips. The report is written by AI from the records: it is only as good as the records, and it can be wrong — check it.
  • You do (4 min): farmer tells you, in their own words, three things the season gave and cost. Print or share if they want (to the programme, a buyer, a savings group).
  • Talk (2 min): "What will you grow more of next season? Less of?"
  • Check: farmer can say total kg harvested, money in and money out for the season (or say which records are missing).

7. Phones, data and privacy

  • The farmer's phone is the farmer's. Ask before touching it; never read their messages or photos; hand it back for You do.
  • Shared phones: a family phone may be used by several people. Show the farmer how to close the app; discuss who else can see the records.
  • Data costs: most exercises work offline and sync later. Share over WhatsApp only small files (a PDF invoice is small; videos are not). Programmes should budget a data allowance for mentors (resources.md).
  • Lost or stolen phone: switch to paper the same day (§2b). Tell the programme office so the account can be secured.
  • Consent: photos of people only with consent (and never of children without guardian consent). Money records are private: in group practice always use sample numbers.

Facilitating online — practical tips

  • Low bandwidth first. Audio only; cameras off; no video clips in the session (send clip links before, optional). Posters are printed cards, not screen shares.
  • Say names. In audio, call people by name to invite them in ("Thandi, what did you hear?").
  • Silence is fine. Count to 10 after a question (people unmute slowly).
  • Language time works online too: buddies retell the idea to each other in their language in a 3-minute phone call.
  • Late or missed session: a 5-minute voice-note summary goes to the group within 24 hours; the field task is still due.
  • Registers: screenshot the participant list (Mode A) or the replies (Mode B) — that is the register. Store it like a paper register.
  • Field tasks are the heart: comment on every one within 3 days (two stars and a wish, in a voice note, privately if it is critical).
  • Safeguarding online: no private messaging between facilitators and learners after hours except for course matters; farmer photos only with consent; any concern follows the same safeguarding route as in person (visit-playbook.md §11).
  • Fatigue: two facilitators; alternate who leads; the other watches the chat and the time.

Blended option (recommended when possible)

Weeks 1–2 online → one in-person day (the Day 2 farm-visit day, day-2.md) → weeks 4–6 online. The real, observed farm visit is the part that suffers most online; this puts it back.

1. The Visit Hand

Five fingers. Five steps. The same on every visit, every contact.

Finger Step What you do Ask
Thumb 1 Look back Greet properly (family, health). Then the last agreed action. Look at the evidence. "Last time you planned to… Show me how it went."
Pointing 2 Walk & look The farmer leads the walk. Start with what's working. Look with the stage checklist (§5). Count and measure. "Show me your garden. What are you proud of? What helped?"
Middle 3 Listen & ask Find the farmer's own priority. Say back what you heard. Climb the question ladder. No advice yet. "What would help you most right now? What have you tried? What else?"
Ring 4 Try it together Coach one skill: I do · We do · You do, then talk. Decide the result. "Shall we try it together, right here?"
Little 5 Agree & record One action: what · who · by when · how we'll know. Write it at the farm; read it back; leave the card. "So what will you do, by when? What will I see when I come?"

Why this order? Looking back first shows the farmer that their plan matters and it closes the loop (evidence of change). Walking before talking means you talk about what is real. Asking before coaching means you coach what the farmer wants. Agreeing last means every contact ends with a next step.

Where it comes from: adult experiential learning (do → reflect → understand → try again); farmer-to-farmer extension and farmer field schools (learning in the field, by doing); coaching and motivational interviewing (open questions, reflective listening, the person decides); appreciative inquiry and positive deviance (start from what works, find who is already succeeding with the same resources); goal-setting (specific, owned, dated actions, with feedback).

Rules of thumb - The farmer talks more than half the time. - One skill per visit. One action per visit (two at most). - Numbers beat words: "3 of 5 beds mulched", "2.5 kg spinach this week". - An action is only closed with a date and evidence of what changed. - No promises you cannot keep. No gifts that change how you help.

3. The cluster visit (2–3 hours, 8–12 farmers, host garden)

Why: a cluster visit reaches 8–12 farmers in the time of three individual visits, and farmers learn fastest from a neighbour with the same soil, rain and money. The host grows in confidence. It is the backbone of the season for green and amber farmers.

Cluster: 8–12 farmers who live within walking distance (about 30 min) of each other. Host: rotates monthly. Choose someone who does one thing well — not always the best garden. Ask the host a week before; agree what they will show.

5. What to look at, stage by stage

Use on step 2 (Walk & look). Don't check everything — pick the 3–5 that fit the stage and the farmer's priority. The Course 1 skills (forms.md §4) are marked ★.

Month 1 · Baseline and set-up

  • Water: source, storage (drums, tank), roof catchment possible? ★
  • Soil: hand test done? compost started? ★
  • The 90-day plan (F-P21): what's first?
  • Records: book or app set up; first entry made ★

Month 2 · Establishment

  • Planting check — is the planting plan followed? ★

Month 3 · Growth

  • Plant health; pests found early; the ladder followed (healthy soil and mixed plants first) ★
  • Natural spray made correctly and safely (kept from children, eyes) ★
  • Liquid manure / worm farm going ★
  • Water: tank levels, bottle drip, greywater used safely (no oily/bleach water on food)
  • Succession: seedlings ready for the next planting?
  • Records: diary / Field Journal entries this month

Month 4 · Harvest

  • Harvesting right (outer leaves of spinach; picking at the right size)
  • Weighing and recording harvests (kg, not bunches) ★
  • The family plate: is the garden feeding the household? Who eats first? ★
  • Seed plants chosen and marked (the best, not the leftovers) ★

Month 5 · Selling surplus

  • What is surplus after the family eats? Who buys? (neighbours, spaza, school/crèche, church)
  • Price: costs known (money out), local price checked ★
  • Money book or app Records (Sold / Spent) used; slips kept ★
  • Invoices for any buyer who pays later (duplicate book or Invoice builder)
  • Credit sales written down with a date to pay

Month 6 · Review and next season

  • Seed saved, dried, labelled, jarred; germination test ★
  • Before/after photos from the baseline spot
  • Records summary (paper month-end summaries, or app Reports)
  • Compare with the baseline and the "what would make you proud" answer
  • Next season plan: rotation, what to grow more/less of (F-P15)

6. The traffic light, caseload arithmetic, a mentor's week, routes

6a. The traffic light (re-sort on the first working day of each month)

Colour Who Contact
R — Red: needs me soon Not started / failing; missed 2 contacts; crisis (theft, illness, drought loss); new; asked for help Individual visit every 2 weeks + phone between
A — Amber: on the way Growing; some skills need further support; patchy records Individual visit monthly + cluster visit
G — Green: going well Skills demonstrated; records kept; could host or help Cluster visit monthly + phone check-in; invite to host/present
  • Nobody goes a calendar month without a contact.
  • The colour is private — a planning tool, not a label.
  • Move farmers up as well as down. Celebrate a farmer moving to green (privately, in words).
  • A farmer who says "no more visits" is recorded as withdrawn by choice with the date — respect it; invite them to cluster visits anyway.

6b. Caseload arithmetic (for any number of farmers)

Individual visits needed per month  = (Red × 2) + (Amber × 1)
Cluster visits per month            = number of clusters
Phone check-ins per month           = Green (+ Red between visits)

Individual visits you can do        = field days × visits per day
   visits per day: 4–5 if farmers are close (one village)
                   3   if spread out (rural, walking)
                   2   on a day with a cluster visit

Worked example — 40 farmers, 4 clusters of 10, 1 mentor, 5-day week:

Item Number
Red 8 × 2 16 visits
Amber 16 × 1 16 visits
Needed 32 individual visits + 4 cluster visits + ~16 phone check-ins
Available: 4 field days × 4 weeks, with 1 cluster day a week (2 visits) + 3 visit days (3 visits) 11 a week → 44 a month
Spare ~12 visits a month for new red cases, repeat visits, a field day, bad weather

If needed > available: make clusters bigger, move amber farmers to "cluster + phone" for one month, ask a lead farmer to co-host, or tell your supervisor (with this sum). Don't quietly do shorter visits.

6d. Route planning

  1. Mark every farmer on a map: the app Map/People & gardens (garden pins) or a hand-drawn village sketch with house numbers or landmarks ("blue gate after the church").
  2. Draw each cluster as a circle. Start the day at the farthest farmer and work back towards home or the taxi route.
  3. Put reds first in the day (energy and time for them); ambers after.
  4. Walk where you can; share programme transport by cluster; note taxi fares in your claim.
  5. Keep a "wet-weather route" (roads passable in rain) and a list of farmers you can phone instead if a road is closed.
  6. Always tell someone your route for the day (supervisor or buddy) — see §11.

10. Looking after mentors

  • Buddy observation: once a month a buddy mentor comes on one visit with the observer checklist (day-2.md) and gives two stars and a wish. Swap the next month.
  • Monthly peer meeting (2 h): all mentors in an area. (1) Wins — one each; (2) a case clinic on one hard situation (M-P15 four steps); (3) numbers — lights, visits, actions closed with evidence; (4) one skill refresh from the "want more help" list (Day 3 evaluation); (5) plan next month's cluster themes together.
  • Supervisor check-in: monthly, on the numbers and on how the mentor is coping.
  • Self-care: you will hear hard stories. Talk to your supervisor or buddy (not the village). Take your days off. You are a mentor, not a rescuer.

11. Safety and ethics

The mentor's promise (M-P02)

  1. I listen first.
  2. I promise only what I can do. No promises of inputs, fencing, tanks, money, jobs or stipends. Write requests under Support requested and pass them on — and say so.
  3. I keep your story private. Collect only what the programme needs; store forms safely; share with named people only (POPIA). Never discuss one farmer with another.
  4. I take no gifts that change how I help. Tea and water: yes, with thanks. Money, livestock, produce to take home, favours: kindly no. Never ask for anything. Never charge.
  5. I keep you safe — and I report harm.

Boundaries

  • Visit in daylight, at agreed times. Tell your buddy/supervisor your route.
  • Meet in the garden or yard, in view of others — not alone indoors with one person.
  • Don't lend or borrow money with farmers. Don't sell them things.
  • Don't take sides in family or community disputes; refer.
  • Relationships: no romantic or sexual relationships with farmers you mentor. Report any pressure or harassment to your supervisor.
  • Programme checks (e.g. attendance for a stipend scheme) are done honestly; you don't change records for anyone.

Gender and inclusion

  • Women often do the garden work but men may speak for the household: speak with the person who does the work. Ask "Who works in the garden? Who decides what is sold?"
  • Arrange visit times that suit caregivers. Offer women-only clusters if women prefer.
  • Youth: pair with elders; recognise their phone skills (A+P track).
  • Disability: adapt the walk (farmer sits and directs) and tasks.

Safeguarding (children and adults at risk)

  • Children: no photos of children (unless the guardian consents in writing and the programme allows); never be alone with a child; no physical contact beyond a greeting.
  • If you see or hear of harm (abuse, neglect, violence): stay calm, listen, don't investigate or promise secrecy, don't confront anyone. Tell your supervisor the same day and follow your organisation's safeguarding policy. Record only "referred to ___ on ___".
  • Danger now: SAPS 10111. Childline 116 (free). GBV Command Centre 0800 428 428 (24 h). (Check these numbers are current and add your local clinic, social worker and police station to the back of this book.)
  • Hunger or illness: refer to the clinic, local social development office or SASSA office (grant applications). Don't give money from your pocket.

Photos and data

  • Consent before every photo; respect the green sticker / "no photos" tick.
  • Photos of gardens, not faces, unless the farmer agreed.
  • App: records sync to the programme; don't keep copies on your personal WhatsApp longer than needed. Paper: forms in a closed folder, handed in weekly.

12. Hard situations

Four steps for every hard moment (M-P15): Stay calm · Listen · What can they do? · Who else can help?

Situation What to do What not to do
Drought / no water Protect the most important bed and seedlings first; thick mulch; water early/late at the roots; greywater (no bleach/oil); shade with branches or shade cloth; bottle drip; delay sowing until rain; record the loss; support request to programme. Move to red. Promise a tank or water delivery; blame the farmer
Theft Listen; offer to help report to SAPS for a case number (their choice); neighbour watch in the cluster; less attractive crops near the road; tools stored with a neighbour; replant plan Accuse anyone; name suspects in the record
Conflict (group garden, family, neighbours) Hear each side separately; meet together with the group; agree a written rule (water roster, bed allocation); involve the group leader, traditional leader or ward committee if needed Take sides; carry messages between people
Drop-out One visit or call with no blame: "We miss you. What's happening?" Offer a smaller goal (one bed, one keyhole); a neighbour buddy; invite to the cluster visit; accept a "no" and record withdrawn by choice Chase repeatedly; shame in front of the group
Dependency ("we wait for inputs") Be honest (promise 2); start with what they have: saved seed, seed swap at cluster, kraal manure, grass mulch, 2 ℓ bottles; visit a green farmer who started with nothing; celebrate the first small step Bring inputs from your own pocket; threaten
Pests/disease beyond you Photo; ask Lima (app ON) and check the answer with your supervisor or the local extension office; ask the cluster; don't guess a chemical Recommend poisons
Farmer money taken / GBV Listen and believe; share helpline numbers privately; supervisor same day; safeguarding route Confront the partner; record details in the visit form
You feel unsafe Leave politely; phone your buddy/supervisor; report Stay "to finish the form"

shared/facilitation-toolkit.md

Facilitation toolkit (all courses)

The day files refer to these by name ("run Groups of 5", "use Three-question debrief"). Read this once before any course; keep it on your phone.

1. The shape of every session: Look · Try · Talk · Take home

Step What happens Who talks
Look (hook) A question, an object, a story, a poster reveal Facilitator asks, learners answer
Try Learners do it — in groups of 5, with their hands Learners
Talk Three-question debrief; the poster fills in the "why" Learners, facilitator sums up
Take home One thing to do at home, said aloud by each group Learners

Teacher talk ≤ 30%. In a 60-minute session that is 18 minutes. The co-facilitator keeps a tally on the session sheet (see forms.md). If you are over, stop and ask a question.

2. Opening circle (every morning, 30 min)

  1. Register (10 min) — learners sign themselves in on the sheet at the door while the circle gathers. Late arrivals sign at tea. Never skip it: funders pay against it.
  2. Greeting and song or energiser (5 min).
  3. "What did you sleep on?" (10 min) — three or four learners say one thing they remember from yesterday, one question they woke up with. Questions go on the Question Tree.
  4. Today (5 min) — point to today's posters; say the day's promise in one sentence.

3. Closing circle (every afternoon, 20 min)

  1. One word — each learner says one word about today (right to pass).
  2. Group report — each group's Reporter says their Take home action.
  3. Question Tree — answer what's left or promise a time tomorrow.
  4. Tools back — Materials people count tools back into the kit. Nobody leaves until the count matches.

4. Groups of 5 (for every practical)

Form groups on Day 1 and keep them all course (they become support groups afterwards). Mix them: young and old, men and women, readers and non-readers, near neighbours together (so they can help each other at home).

Name each group after a plant: Bean, Pumpkin, Maize, Banana, Marula, Spinach.

Role Job Rotate?
Leader Keeps everyone doing, not watching Daily
Timekeeper Watches the time, calls "5 minutes left" Daily
Materials Collects, checks and returns the group's kit Daily
Recorder Draws or writes what the group found (drawing is fine) Daily
Reporter Speaks for the group in the debrief Daily

In the AI Literacy course the Materials person is also the Phone Keeper (charges, hands out and collects the group's shared phone).

Put the roles on a small card per group. Rotate every morning so everyone does every role.

Never demo-only. If you have only one set of materials: demonstrate in 3 minutes, then every group takes a turn while the others do a different task (a "carousel"). Nobody sits and watches for longer than 10 minutes.

5. Getting attention back without shouting

  • Hand up, mouth shut: raise your hand; anyone who sees it raises theirs and goes quiet.
  • Clap back: you clap a rhythm, the room claps it back.
  • Call and response: you call "Imbewu!", they answer "Iyamila!" (the seed grows) — agree your own call on Day 1 in the local language.
  • Whisper: talk very quietly. People lean in.

6. Energisers (5 minutes, when energy drops — after lunch always)

Use one whenever you see yawning, phones, or people leaving. Choose ones that teach.

Name How Teaches
Sun and Shade Facilitator is the sun, walks north side; learners must stand in the "shade" of a partner Southern hemisphere sun
Water Drop Circle holds hands = soil; one person is a raindrop trying to get in; "mulch" players protect gaps Infiltration, mulch
Seed Swap Each person gets a paper "seed"; swap with 3 people, say your name and one crop you love Names, sharing
Compost Heap Call "green!" (sit), "brown!" (stand), "water!" (wave), "air!" (jump) — faster and faster Compost needs
Web of Life Ball of string passed across the circle: "I am the worm, I feed the…" Connections
Zones Walk Call a zone number; learners run to the matching spot (0 = the door, 5 = far wall) Zones
Pass the Clap Clap passes round the circle; reverse on "hey!" Focus
Two Truths and a Crop Say two true things and one false about your garden; others guess Getting to know
Mirror Pairs mirror each other's slow movements Listening (mentor course)
Rain Storm Rub hands → click → pat knees → stamp → back again Calming, rain

Check first: nobody forced to touch or run. Elders may sit and call the game.

7. The three-question debrief (after every practical)

  1. What did you do / see? (facts)
  2. What did you learn — why does it work? (meaning) ← point to the poster here
  3. What will you do at home? (action)

Take one answer per group for each question. Write the home answers on the flipchart beside the poster.

8. Feedback that builds people ("two stars and a wish")

Used in micro-teaching (Course 3), mentor role-plays (Course 2) and on farm visits:

  • Star 1: one thing that worked, specific ("You let the group dig before you explained.")
  • Star 2: a second thing that worked
  • Wish: one wish, as a suggestion ("Next time, maybe ask them first where the water goes.")

The person who was taught speaks first about their own session. Never correct someone's language, spelling or accent.

9. Mixed literacy, mixed ages, mixed languages

  • Assume some learners do not read. Draw, point, do. Recorders can draw.
  • Never ask someone to read aloud unless they volunteer.
  • Speak the local language first; English words for key terms second, so learners can read seed packets and product labels.
  • Give language time: after you explain, groups retell the idea to each other in their own words for 2 minutes. This is the best check of understanding you have.
  • Elders hold knowledge: ask "How did your grandmother do this?" in every topic. Honour it, then add what's new.
  • Youth often have phones: pair them with elders for photographs, records and the app.
  • Women often carry the garden and the household: plan start and end times with them.
  • Disability: every practical has a seated role (sorting seed, mixing seedling soil, recording).

10. Safety

  • First aid kit open and visible; know the nearest clinic and have a vehicle plan.
  • Sun and heat: hats, water, shade breaks; heavy digging before 11:00.
  • Tools: carry spades and forks points down; nobody behind a swinging pick; count tools.
  • Manure and compost: gloves or wash hands with soap before eating.
  • Chilli/garlic sprays: keep away from eyes; wash hands.
  • Weather: lightning = everyone indoors, stop the practical.

11. The Question Tree

A flipchart sheet on a tree or wall, labelled with a drawing of a tree. Questions that don't fit the moment are written (or drawn) on sticky notes and hung there. Before the closing circle, answer them or say when you will. It keeps the session on track without ignoring anyone.

12. Timekeeping

  • Start on time with whoever is there — the opening circle is the reward for being early.
  • Say "5 minutes left" in every practical.
  • Never tell learners you are behind. Cut from the could know list, never from the practical. Each session in the day files marks what can be cut: ✂ cut if short.

Art rules (all courses)

  1. Southern hemisphere. Sun in the north, shadows fall south. Any compass shows N up.
  2. No text inside the artwork. Words are typeset on the layout so they can be translated.
  3. Generic plant shapes unless the poster is about telling crops apart (then the art brief names the crop and it is drawn accurately). Never draw a recognisable invasive species.
  4. People look like the learners: Black South African smallholders, mixed ages and genders, gogos and youth, work clothes, gumboots, headscarves, caps. Nobody in a lab coat.
  5. Flat, warm, instructional illustration. Thick dark outlines. Not photoreal, not 3D.
  6. One idea per poster. If you need two ideas, it is two posters.
  7. Show local materials: 20 ℓ drums, 2 ℓ bottles, tins, wire, droppers, kraal manure, grass mulch, a corrugated-iron roof, a JoJo-style tank, fencing with droppers.

Poster wall habits (teach these in Course 3)

  • Posters go up before the session and stay up for the whole course: by Day 5 the room is a map of everything learned.
  • Hang them at eye level of a seated learner, in the order they were taught, left to right.
  • Leave one blank sheet of flipchart beside each poster for the group's own words and drawings.
  • The Question Tree (a flipchart sheet on a tree or wall): any question that doesn't fit the session is written there and answered before the day ends.
