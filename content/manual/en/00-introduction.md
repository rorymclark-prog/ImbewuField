# Introduction

The richest smallholders are rarely those with the most land. They are those whose land works for them: soil that holds water, trees that feed and shelter, and animals that fertilise.

That is what permaculture offers a farmer in Southern Africa. Our rain is uncertain, droughts are growing longer, and bought seed, fertiliser and sprays are dear. Permaculture answers with design: we watch how nature works, then plan the farm so that water, soil, plants and animals support each other, using mostly what is already on the land.

Bill Mollison, one of the founders of permaculture, put it simply: "The greatest change we need to make is from consumption to production, even if on a small scale, in our own gardens."

**By the end of this introduction you will be able to:**

- Say why permaculture suits a small farm in Southern Africa
- Find the chapter for the job in front of you
- Know where to look up a new word

## Who this manual is for

Smallholder and homestead farmers in Southern Africa, and the mentors, students and field staff who work beside them. You need a piece of land, however small, and the will to start.

## Where this manual comes from

Rory Clark compiled this manual. It began as *The Permaculture Gardening Handbook* for the training course of the Reducing Vulnerability from Climate Change (RVCC) project, run by UNDP and the Government of Lesotho in 2020 and 2021. It has since been checked and adapted for South Africa.

Parts are adapted, with permission, from the African Conservation Trust's *Introduction to Permaculture and Homestead Gardening* (2014).

## How to use this manual

1. **Start with the ideas.** Chapter 1 explains permaculture and its twelve principles. Chapters 2 and 3 show how to plan your farm around sun, wind, water and fire.
2. **Then go to the work.** Chapters 4 to 11 cover crops, animals, trees, water, soil, ecology, pests and the home. Each stands on its own and ends with a "Try it" activity.
3. **Try one thing at a time.** Watch it work before you add the next.
4. **Use the Glossary.** Technical words such as swale and mulch are explained the first time they appear, and all together in the Glossary at the end.
5. **Find the notes at the end.** Notes on the law and on keeping people and animals safe are gathered in one place at the back.

The manual is also in isiZulu, Sesotho and Tshivenḓa, as drafts still being checked by fluent speakers. If something is unclear, compare the English.

## Key points

- Permaculture builds a farm that feeds you through hard seasons, by design rather than bought inputs.
- Start with Chapters 1 to 3, then go to the chapter you need.
- New words are in the Glossary; notes on the law and safety are at the end.

Start where you stand. Chapter 1 begins with the ideas that hold it all together.
