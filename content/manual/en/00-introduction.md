# Introduction

This manual is a practical guide to permaculture for smallholder and homestead farmers in Southern Africa. It was first written as a handbook for a five-day permaculture training course. You can use it together with a course, or on your own.

The aim is to help you start your permaculture journey. The manual takes you through the basics of permaculture design and how to lay out your garden or farm, so that your work and resources go further. It also covers soil building, water harvesting, animals, trees and natural pest control. You can use many of these methods straight away to improve the fertility and harvests of your garden.

Many of the methods use what you already have on your land, so they help to cut the cost of buying seed, fertiliser and chemicals. Where you grow more than your family needs, the manual has ideas for selling or adding value to your surplus.

We hope you enjoy the manual and that it inspires you on your path to abundance.

> **Note:** "The greatest change we need to make is from consumption to production, even if on a small scale, in our own gardens." — Bill Mollison, one of the founders of permaculture.

## Where this manual comes from

This manual was compiled by Rory Clark. It was first written as *The Permaculture Gardening Handbook* for the permaculture training of the Reducing Vulnerability from Climate Change (RVCC) project, run by UNDP and the Government of Lesotho in 2020–2021. It has now been checked, updated and adapted for South Africa and the wider Southern African region.

Parts of this manual are drawn from the African Conservation Trust (ACT) manual *Introduction to Permaculture and Homestead Gardening* (2014). They are used and adapted with permission.

## How to use this manual

- **Read one chapter at a time.** Each chapter stands on its own. Start with Chapter 1 (What is permaculture?) and Chapter 2 (Planning your farm). They explain the ideas the other chapters build on.
- **Try one thing, then the next.** Pick one method you can do this season, such as a compost heap or a mulched bed. Watch how it works before you add more.
- **Look for the boxes.** Boxes marked **Safety** warn you about real dangers to people, animals or the environment. Always read them. Boxes marked **Tip** or **Note** give extra help.
- **Check the "Key points".** Each chapter ends with a short list of the main ideas. Use it to revise, or to explain the chapter to others.
- **Adapt to your own place.** Southern Africa has many climates. Most of the interior of South Africa gets its rain in summer and has cold, dry winters, often with frost on the Highveld. The Western Cape gets most of its rain in winter. Adjust planting times to your own rain season, and ask local farmers and extension officers what works in your area.
- **Remember the sun is in the north.** In Southern Africa the sun moves across the northern part of the sky. North-facing slopes and walls are the warmest and sunniest; south-facing ones are cooler and shadier.
- **Choose other languages if you need them.** The manual is also available in isiZulu, Sesotho, Tshivenḓa and Xitsonga. These translations are drafts that are still being checked by fluent speakers, so compare with the English if something is unclear.

## Key points

- This manual is a practical, step-by-step guide to permaculture for Southern African smallholder farmers.
- It was compiled by Rory Clark and adapted in part, with permission, from the African Conservation Trust's *Introduction to Permaculture and Homestead Gardening* (2014).
- Start small: read one chapter, try one method, observe, then build on it.
- Always read the **Safety** boxes.
- Adapt timing and methods to your own rainfall season and local conditions.
